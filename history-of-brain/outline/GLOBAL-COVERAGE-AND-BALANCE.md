## How to read the matrix

- **Foundation** — opened sources support placement in the master outline.
- **Lead** — a credible route exists, but local and/or primary evidence is still thin.
- **Required gap** — the collection cannot responsibly claim balanced coverage until a
  dedicated research pass improves this cell.
- **Protocol gate** — research or publication requires community, archive, language, rights,
  or cultural-governance review beyond ordinary citation checking.

No cell marked “Foundation” means that a public article is sourced. It means the outline has
enough evidence to reserve and define the work.

## Pass 5 — Long-arc thematic paths

The collection needs twelve persistent paths. Each path is an authority guide with focused
pages underneath it; it is not a tag archive.

1. **Where did people think the mind lived?** Brain, heart, breath, soul, body, land,
   relationships, distributed personhood, and the danger of false equivalence.
2. **What counted as distress or difference?** Period terms, symptoms, disability, identity,
   danger, gift, sin, illness, and the limits of retrospective diagnosis.
3. **Who cared before—and beside—the professional?** Families, peers, healers, religious
   specialists, attendants, nurses, social workers, mutual aid, and ordinary coping.
4. **Treatment, relief, harm, and consent.** Remedies, regimens, ritual, medicines, surgery,
   psychotherapy, environment, rehabilitation, evidence, coercion, and refusal.
5. **Emotion has a history.** Named feelings, mood, temperament, expression, regulation,
   moral judgment, gender, class, race, and culturally specific emotion concepts.
6. **How psychology became many psychologies.** Philosophy, physiology, laboratories,
   schools, clinics, industries, empire, local adaptation, and critique.
7. **How psychiatry gained—and lost—authority.** Law, institutions, diagnosis, medicine,
   professional jurisdiction, users, families, and reform.
8. **Brain science and the person.** Injury, anatomy, localization, neurons, chemistry,
   imaging, genetics, cognition, prediction, and overclaiming.
9. **Trauma, violence, memory, and survival.** War, enslavement, colonization, genocide,
   disaster, testimony, compensation, cultural variation, and collective action.
10. **Addiction, compulsion, substances, and altered states.** Ritual and medical use,
    commerce, crime, dependence, mutual aid, medications, harm reduction, and drug policy.
11. **From charity and poor relief to social work and public systems.** Mutual aid,
    settlement, casework, welfare, public health, community organization, rights, and the
    older care histories professional origin stories omit.
12. **Wellness, coping, recovery, and the good life.** Rest, movement, ritual, art, nature,
    relationships, mindfulness, resilience, recovery, markets, and structural critique.

## Pass 6 — Lived-experience streams

These streams recur inside every period and become independent collection paths where the
evidence is strong enough:

- **People speaking for themselves:** letters, petitions, diaries, memoirs, testimony, oral
  histories, patient publications, art, blogs, and recorded speech.
- **Voices inside someone else's record:** reported speech in medical, legal, religious,
  school, welfare, military, and family documents.
- **Families and chosen families:** care, conflict, payment, commitment, discharge, advocacy,
  burden, and refusal.
- **Care workers beyond the famous expert:** nurses, attendants, social workers, community
  health workers, interpreters, domestic workers, peer workers, and ritual specialists.
- **Collective voice and resistance:** survivor, disability, Mad, neurodivergent, Indigenous,
  queer, feminist, antiracist, anticolonial, family, and labor organizations.
- **The archive is silent:** a visible component naming what cannot be known, who controlled
  preservation, and which searches or languages remain incomplete.

## Pass 10 — Balance and duplication audit

### Balance failures prevented in the mapped outline

- There is no “West plus sidebars” master chronology.
- Ancient Egypt does not represent Africa; India does not represent Asia; Jamaica does not
  represent the Caribbean; one modern health model does not represent Indigenous history.
- Premodern care is not reduced to demons, and modern care is not treated as automatically
  humane or effective.
- Psychiatry, psychology, and neuroscience do not absorb family care, social work, ritual,
  disability, peer support, coping, or wellness.
- Patient experience is a structural stream, not a quotation garnish.
- Every harm story has a consequence-and-response route; no “weird treatment” list is admitted.
- Search-language pages merge into substantial authorities rather than doorway pages.

### Duplications to prevent during page planning

- One strong **asylum authority guide** should coordinate regional institution pages; do not
  repeat a generic asylum introduction on every country page.
- One **terms-through-time guide** should bridge current queries to historical vocabulary;
  period pages should link rather than recreate the dictionary.
- “History of depression,” “melancholy,” “acedia,” and culture-specific distress cannot be
  identical pages; the first is a careful modern-query route through distinct histories.
- Trauma, shell shock, PTSD, historical trauma, and cultural concepts of distress need a
  parent path with clearly bounded children, not synonym cannibalization.
- Mindfulness, Buddhist cultivation, meditation research, and the wellness market are four
  connected but different historical jobs.
- Deinstitutionalization, community care, hospital closure, incarceration, and homelessness
  need one system map with country-specific evidence, not a single-cause story repeated five
  times.

### Blocking gaps before any “complete global history” claim

1. Locally authored and local-language histories for the required-gap cells above.
2. Nation- and community-led review for Indigenous knowledge, images, data, and testimony.
3. More direct experience from Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Caribbean, and Oceania.
4. Global histories of psychiatric nursing, occupational therapy, family caregiving, peer
   work, counseling, social work, and harm reduction.
5. Country-specific histories of political abuse, coercion, eugenics, colonial medicine,
   and reform.
6. Source validation for search demand and publishing priority; the current reader map is
   qualitative and English-language weighted, not keyword-volume research.
7. Claim-level evidence extraction for every selected public page.

## Audit result

**Passes 4–7 and 10 are complete enough to create the first actual master outline.** The
architecture is deliberately larger than the first publishing phase. The gaps above remain
visible and scheduled; they are not silently converted into universal claims.
