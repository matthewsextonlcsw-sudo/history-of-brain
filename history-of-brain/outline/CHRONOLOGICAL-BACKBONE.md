# Chronological Backbone — History of the Mind and Mental Health

**Pass:** 3 — Build the chronological backbone
**Status:** Mapped v0.2; source-backed outline input, not public copy
**Last updated:** Aug. 19, 2026
**Authority:** `../PROJECT-BRAIN.md`, `SCOPE-MAP.md`, and `../sources/EVIDENCE-MODEL.md`

## Editorial ruling

The backbone uses **overlapping global time bands**, not a universal march through
“ancient–medieval–Renaissance–modern.” Those labels can be useful inside a named region,
but they cannot organize the whole world. Each band has a historical change as its job,
several regional doors, at least one lived-experience or archive-silence route, and an
exchange question.

The dates below are navigation aids, not claims that every society changed at once.
Individual public pages will use place-specific chronologies.

Reviewed research packets enter this backbone only at their bounded internal gate. A
`PASS WITH GAPS` is not publication approval. A stopped or incomplete corpus remains an
acquisition task and cannot be disguised as a narrative node.

## B0 — Before writing: bodies, care, survival, and the limit of inference

**Working range:** Deep prehistory to the emergence of locally surviving written records.
**Backbone job:** Open with what material evidence can show—and what it cannot tell us about
fear, compassion, stigma, belief, or diagnosis.

- `B0.1` **How can we study minds before words survive?** Archaeology, paleopathology,
  burial context, disability, assistance, and the difference between an observed injury and
  a story about motive.
- `B0.2` **Trepanation: an opening in a skull is not a psychiatric diagnosis.** Compare
  places and techniques only where physical context supports the comparison
  ([Ramirez Rozzi and Froment](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-23914-1)).
- `B0.3` **Did prehistoric communities care for disabled people?** Use bounded cases such
  as Shanidar to teach competing interpretations of survival and assistance
  ([Pomeroy et al.](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2019.207);
  [Spikins et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00438243.2018.1433060)).
- `B0.4` **Evidence lab: from bone to claim.** Let readers separate documented alteration,
  strongly supported survival, plausible assistance, disputed motive, and unknown experience.

**Lived-experience rule:** No direct voice survives. Say so prominently.
**Transition:** Writing changes what can enter the archive; it does not mark the beginning of
distress, care, emotion, or ideas about the self.

## B1 — First written worlds: signs, bodies, hearts, spirits, households

**Working range:** c. 3200 BCE–c. 500 BCE, varying by place.
**Backbone job:** Show the first surviving written explanations without treating writing as
the birth of mental life or reducing care to either “magic” or “medicine.”

- `B1.1` **Mesopotamian signs and suffering.** Diagnostic-prognostic tablets, ritual and
  material remedies, divine and social relations, and the literary first person in
  *Ludlul bēl nēmeqi* ([Electronic Babylonian Library](https://www.ebl.lmu.de/corpus/L/2/2)).
- `B1.2` **Egypt and the Nile worlds: heart, brain, body, family, and the dead.** Put the
  Edwin Smith Papyrus beside heart-centered vocabularies and family petitions; do not turn
  one trauma text into “Egyptian neuroscience”
  ([National Library of Medicine](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/ma10/ma10_hmd_reprint_papyrus.html);
  [UCL Letters to the Dead](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/literature/religious/lettersdead.html)).
- `B1.3` **Nubia and Kush are not an Egyptian footnote.** Build a distinct evidence lane and
  mark the limits created by language survival and unequal excavation
  ([Smith](https://journals.librarypublishing.arizona.edu/jaei/article/1099/galley/1094/download/)).
- `B1.4` **Embodied selves in Levantine and Iranian texts.** Follow named words for breath,
  heart, life, spirit, agency, and suffering without translating them all as “mind.”
- `B1.5` **Evidence story: Is an ancient first-person poem a patient memoir?** Compare literary
  voice, scribal setting, and modern reconstruction.

**Missing voices:** Non-literate people, children, enslaved people, rural households, and most
women appear through elite or institutional records, if at all.
**Transition:** Written archives multiply, but philosophy, medicine, ritual, law, and ordinary
care remain intertwined.

## B2 — Many classical worlds: self, emotion, suffering, cultivation, and plural healing

**Working range:** c. 800 BCE–c. 600 CE, with locally specific chronologies.
**Backbone job:** Put Mediterranean, South Asian, East Asian, West Asian, African, and American
questions in parallel without pretending they shared one “classical age.”

- `B2.1` **Greek-speaking Mediterranean: the brain, soul, regimen, and sanctuary.** Pair
  *On the Sacred Disease* with Asclepian healing and competing philosophies; naturalizing
  arguments did not end ritual care
  ([Hippocrates](https://classics.mit.edu/Hippocrates/sacred.html);
  [Gregis](https://doi.org/10.14277/2532-6848/Axon-1-2-17-08)).
- `B2.2` **Roman imperial pluralism.** Follow household remedies, itinerant healers,
  temples, learned medicine, Galenic synthesis, law, status, and reported patient speech
  ([Celsus 3.18](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:latinLit:phi0836.phi002.perseus-lat5:3.18/?right=perseus-eng2);
  [Mattern](https://doi.org/10.1017/mdh.2024.41)).
- `B2.3` **South Asia: layered selves, *manas*, suffering, and transformation.** Distinguish
  Vedic and Upaniṣadic arguments, changing Ayurvedic corpora, Buddhist schools, and other
  philosophical traditions; never equate *unmada* with a current diagnosis
  ([Jain et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6198594/);
  [SEP, Indian Buddhist mind](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/)).
- `B2.4` **China: *xin* as heart-mind, emotion, conduct, and order.** Compare named schools
  and changing uses of *qing* rather than inventing a single Chinese psychology
  ([SEP, Chinese heart-mind](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-mind/);
  [SEP, early Chinese emotions](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotions-chinese/)).
- `B2.5` **Premodern Chinese madness was medically plural.** Trace *dian*, *kuang*, wind,
  phlegm-fire, emotional disturbance, drugs, needling, talking, and ritual without calling the
  whole past “TCM” ([Chen](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203740262-18)).
- `B2.6` **Mesoamerican and Andean research door.** Reserve a first-class lane for locally
  grounded concepts, material evidence, and later contact-zone sources; do not reconstruct
  preconquest experience from colonial accounts alone.
- `B2.7` **Who had a choice of healer?** A comparative page on status, gender, enslavement,
  household authority, cost, pilgrimage, and the limits of the “medical marketplace.”

**Exchange route:** Egypt, the Persian empires, Central Asian corridors, Indian Ocean ports,
pilgrimage, war, and diaspora connected people and knowledge; similarity alone does not prove
direct borrowing ([Thomas](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17152173/)).
**Transition:** Classical traditions do not disappear. They are translated, criticized,
combined, ritualized, institutionalized, and made vernacular.

## B3 — Translation worlds: monasteries, courts, ports, hospitals, shrines, and streets

**Working range:** c. 300–1200 CE, overlapping later regional developments.
**Backbone job:** Replace the “dark ages then rediscovery” story with multilingual networks and
locally changing practices.

- `B3.1` **Late-antique inner lives.** Augustine, ascetic disciplines, *acedia*, natural and
  demonic explanations, charity, and the disputed history of hospitals
  ([Crislip](https://doi.org/10.1177/00209643221146234);
  [Underwood](https://doi.org/10.1525/sla.2018.2.3.342)).
- `B3.2` **Greek → Syriac → Arabic was a web, not a relay race.** Translators such as Hunayn,
  patrons, families, physicians, editors, and libraries changed texts as they moved
  ([NCBI, *Why Translate Science?*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622612/)).
- `B3.3` **Sciences of the soul in Arabic and Persian.** Map perception, imagination, memory,
  sleep, emotion, body, ethics, and revelation through specific writers and debates—not a
  timeless “Islamic psychology”
  ([SEP, Arabic and Islamic mind](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-mind/)).
- `B3.4` **What did a bimaristan actually do?** Compare named institutions, endowments,
  staffing, household care, confinement, and later legend; reject unsupported “first
  psychiatric hospital” claims
  ([Dols](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025727300046287)).
- `B3.5` **Jewish thought inside Arabic, Hebrew, and Latin worlds.** Treat Jewish scholars as
  producers and readers, not merely intermediaries
  ([Freudenthal and Fontaine](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0957423915000090)).
- `B3.6` **Dunhuang and Central Asian crossroads.** Manuscripts in many languages can show
  contact and translation, not population-wide belief
  ([UNESCO Dunhuang](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/content/dunhuang)).
- `B3.7` **Buddhist mind and medicine on land and sea routes.** Track translators,
  monasteries, courts, ports, and changing terminology
  ([Salguero](https://www.pennpress.org/9780812246117/translating-buddhist-medicine-in-medieval-china/)).
- `B3.8` **Africa’s manuscript and oral worlds.** Build named North, West, East, Central, and
  Southern African lanes; Timbuktu manuscripts are one anchor, not a proxy for a continent
  ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/islamic-manuscripts-from-mali/about-this-collection/)).

**Lived-experience route:** Separate direct self-expression, reported speech, institutional
description, and modern reconstruction.
**Transition:** Knowledge networks expand while local households, shrines, healers, and courts
continue to decide what care and control look like.

## B4 — Regional syntheses and early global empires

**Working range:** c. 1100–1700 CE, with no universal “Renaissance.”
**Backbone job:** Show continuing Asian, African, American, Oceanic, Islamic, Jewish, and
European worlds colliding with print, conquest, slavery, mission, and empire.

- `B4.1` **Persianate and South Asian entanglements.** Follow Unani, Ayurveda, Siddha,
  Buddhist, court, shrine, household, and market practices rather than a single imported or
  indigenous line ([Attewell](https://doi.org/10.1524/para.2009.0008)).
- `B4.2` **East and Southeast Asian continuities and adaptations.** Research Song–Qing
  changes, Kampo, Korean and Vietnamese lineages, and vernacular care separately.
- `B4.3` **The babaylan/katalonan through a colonial archive.** Ask what early Spanish
  accounts reveal, distort, translate, and persecute
  ([Tremml-Werner](https://doi.org/10.1093/pastj/gtz031)).
- `B4.4` **Care, law, and coercion in medieval and early modern Europe.** Place Bethlem,
  family custody, charity, courts, medicine, religion, and alleged possession in specific
  jurisdictions; “everyone blamed demons” is false.
- `B4.5` **Acedia, melancholy, and semantic change.** Track words and practices without
  turning either into a timeless depression diagnosis.
- `B4.6` **Print, anatomy, and the mechanical body.** Vesalius, Burton, and Descartes are
  changes within European debates, not the moment humanity discovered the brain
  ([SEP, Descartes and the pineal gland](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pineal-gland/)).
- `B4.7` **Conquest as a contact zone in the Americas.** Nahua and other Indigenous concepts,
  colonial translation, mission, medicine, law, and the Hospital de San Hipólito
  ([Ramos](https://doi.org/10.1017/tam.2020.41)).
- `B4.8` **Atlantic Africa and the violent making of new healing worlds.** Track African,
  European, and Indigenous knowledge under slavery and commerce without sanitizing “exchange”
  ([Kananoja](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0E6627303E0A532A5C9A0232F5DD2D91/9781108491259c6_155-186.pdf/treating-their-symptoms.pdf)).
- `B4.9` **A person writes distress before psychiatry.** Build evidence stories from letters,
  petitions, spiritual autobiography, poems, and court records; show later cataloguing power
  ([Willis](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0080440125100340)).

**Transition:** Empires increasingly connect classification to administration, property,
labor, race, policing, and institutional confinement—but household and plural care persist.

## B5 — Classification, confinement, reform, race, and revolution

**Working range:** c. 1650–1870, overlapping the next band.
**Backbone job:** Explain why new taxonomies and institutions mattered without narrating a
simple rescue from superstition.

- `B5.1` **Enlightenments, sensibility, nerves, and moral treatment—in the plural.** Track
  national and imperial settings, predecessors, religious reform, household care, and labor.
- `B5.2` **The asylum as architecture, promise, workplace, and instrument of rule.** Compare
  public, private, charitable, penal, military, colonial, and family routes.
- `B5.3` **Did “moral treatment” feel moral to patients?** Put institutional ideals beside
  rules, labor, restraint, letters, escape, and discharge.
- `B5.4` **Enslavement, racial science, and the invention of diagnostic authority.** Trace who
  benefited from categories and how resistance was pathologized.
- `B5.5` **Colonial institutions in South and Southeast Asia.** Early asylums, lunacy law,
  armies, ports, policing, migration, repatriation, family economies, and patient labor
  ([Ernst](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199497780.003.0004);
  [Lin](https://doi.org/10.1215/18752160-3460689)).
- `B5.6` **Asylums in Africa, the Caribbean, Latin America, and Oceania.** Build a comparison
  that keeps local dates, institutions, race, labor, and law distinct.
- `B5.7` **Who gets believed in the Ann Pratt official dossier?** Read elicited examinations,
  reported experiences, worker evidence, administrative defense, hostile credibility framing,
  and bounded procedural agency as a constructed evidence chain—not an impartial verdict or
  Pratt's original publication
  ([R22 packet](../research/era-packets/R22-JAMAICA-OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTS-1860/FINDINGS.md)).
- `B5.8` **Psychology before the laboratory.** Faculty psychology, association, perception,
  education, mesmerism, phrenology, measurement, and public spectacle—plus the boundary
  between useful experiment and exploitative claim.
- `B5.9` **Six letters, six mediation chains in St. Thomas, 1738–1753.** Enslaved writers,
  dictators, translators, petitioners, missionaries, editors, and archives all shape what
  survives; authored agency and heavy mediation coexist
  ([R19 packet](../research/era-packets/R19-ST-THOMAS-ENSLAVED-LETTERS/FINDINGS.md)).

**Evidence card inside `B5.9`:** What “ill from sadness” can—and cannot—mean in one 1753
letter. Preserve the devotional, translated, relational, and archival setting; do not convert
the phrase into a modern diagnosis, cause, prevalence estimate, treatment, or outcome.

**Transition:** The nineteenth century makes professions, statistics, mass institutions,
colonial networks, and claims of scientific expertise more powerful—and more contestable.

## B6 — Disciplines, empires, mass institutions, and social reform

**Working range:** c. 1840–1914.
**Backbone job:** Tell the formation of psychiatry, psychology, neurology, psychiatric nursing,
and social work through institutions, workers, subjects, colonies, and movements—not founder
portraits alone.

- `B6.1` **How psychiatry became a profession.** Licensing, journals, associations,
  institutions, alienism, neurology, law, and public authority.
- `B6.2` **The psychology-laboratory story without the “single birthplace” myth.** Map
  experimental networks, philosophical predecessors, universities, instruments, subjects,
  and national traditions.
- `B6.3` **What happened to the people inside expanding asylums?** Admissions, chronicity,
  work, restraint, medication, abuse, community ties, death, discharge, and patient writing.
- `B6.4` **Empire makes psychiatric categories travel.** Compare British India, Japanese
  imperial circuits, French Indochina, the Dutch East Indies, African colonies, the Caribbean,
  settler states, and Latin American nation-building.
- `B6.5` **Race, degeneration, heredity, gender, sexuality, and class.** Show how scientific
  institutions made inequalities look natural—and where evidence or resistance challenged them.
- `B6.6` **From charity to social work.** Settlement work, casework, mutual aid, poor law,
  child welfare, public health, Black reform networks, labor, and the contested professional
  boundary.
- `B6.7` **Psychoanalysis is made by a network.** Freud belongs in a story of patients,
  correspondents, critics, women analysts, colonial adaptations, and rival theories.
- `B6.8` **Indigenous removal, residential and boarding schools, and family rupture.** Center
  testimony, sovereignty, language, land, and community-led healing; do not reduce the story to
  a clinical trauma label
  ([Canadian National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation](https://nctr.ca/records/reports/);
  [Australian Human Rights Commission](https://humanrights.gov.au/bringing-them-home/index.html)).
- `B6.9` **Law on paper and practice in reports: Dullunda, 1858–1872.** Separate the 1858
  legal design from later administrative records of custody, visitor action, labor and revenue,
  expenditure, mortality, release, and relatives' or friends' obligations
  ([R21 packet](../research/era-packets/R21-DULLUNDA-1858-1872-LAW-LABOR-FRIENDS/FINDINGS.md)).

**Evidence card inside `B6.9`:** Work, revenue, and the missing question of consent.
Institutional records can document tasks and money without proving voluntariness, coercion,
therapeutic intent, pay, safety, meaning, or benefit.

- `B6.10` **Who gets to make evidence? Illinois, 1867–1870.** Keep four connected but
  different acts of authority distinct: the 1867 statute's procedural design, the committee's
  private evidence selection and credibility gate, Packard's 1868 composite publication and
  editorial framing, and the Board's later account of contested commitment policy
  ([R28 packet](../research/era-packets/R28-ILLINOIS-PACKARD-1867-1870/FINDINGS.md)).

**Evidence cards inside `B6.10`:** Law on paper is not implementation; separate attribution
does not make contributors independent corroborators; and neither a committee report nor a
later official history settles every allegation, diagnosis, causal reform claim, or firstness
claim. `Counterpublic` and `counter-authority` remain held until the complete relevant modern
scholarship is opened.

- `B6.11` **One retained letter through one later publication: Grahamstown, 1908–1911.** A
  2014 descendant-scholar article publishes a complete letter transcription and reconstructs
  clinical, administrative, employment, pension, family, and retrospective-memory layers. The
  node is direct only for what that opened publication reports and transcribes
  ([R26 packet](../research/era-packets/R26-GRAHAMSTOWN-WILBRAHAM-1908-1911/FINDINGS.md)).

The manuscript, casebook, ledgers, estate file, register, family objects, and annual report
remain unopened. This one English-language white-settler case cannot represent Black or
isiXhosa-speaking patients, workers, the institution, South Africa, or colonial Africa.

**Transition:** War turns mental measurement, selection, shock, rehabilitation, and state
responsibility into mass public questions.

## B7 — War, testing, analysis, welfare, and mass persuasion

**Working range:** c. 1914–1945.
**Backbone job:** Connect world wars, colonial armies, revolution, migration, genocide,
propaganda, schools, workplaces, and clinics to changing accounts of mind and distress.

- `B7.1` **Shell shock was not simply PTSD by another name.** Follow bodily, psychological,
  moral, disciplinary, pension, gendered, and national interpretations.
- `B7.2` **Mental tests move from laboratories into armies, schools, immigration, and work.**
  Show standardization, opportunity, racial and class bias, disability, and resistance.
- `B7.3` **Psychotherapy proliferates.** Psychoanalysis, child guidance, counseling, social
  casework, occupational therapy, behavior experiments, and culturally reworked approaches.
- `B7.4` **Eugenics becomes policy.** Sterilization, immigration restriction, marriage law,
  institutionalization, racial hygiene, and survivor histories across multiple countries.
- `B7.5` **Somatic treatments: hope, urgency, experiment, risk, and consent.** Malaria therapy,
  insulin coma, seizure therapies, psychosurgery, and early electroconvulsive therapy must be
  separated by indication, evidence, setting, and patient experience.
- `B7.6` **Colonial psychiatry between wars.** Labor, race, crime, family, repatriation,
  agricultural colonies, professional ambitions, and local alternatives
  ([Edington](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8437878/)).
- `B7.7` **War and occupation across Asia.** Japanese empire, China, Southeast Asia, South
  Asian soldiers, partition’s approach, and changing national professions.
- `B7.8` **The Holocaust and psychiatric murder.** Eugenics, Aktion T4, professional
  participation, disabled victims, resistance, testimony, and postwar accountability.
- `B7.9` **Publishing an argument beside a still-unopened file chain.** Alberto Nicolat's
  deliberately published book preserves affected-person authorship and public argument; it
  does not verify every biographical, family, legal, clinical, or institutional event. The
  Oneto thesis and consequential case-file folios remain held
  ([R23 packet](../research/era-packets/R23-MEXICO-LA-CASTANEDA-NICOLAT-1918-1932/FINDINGS.md)).

- `B7.10` **A notebook, an unfinished novel, and the archive between them: Rio de Janeiro,
  1919–1920.** Two FBN autograph objects preserve different genres in one dependence chain:
  Lima Barreto's situated asylum notebook and an unfinished literary reworking. Track authored
  self-report, revision, posthumous editing, family-to-archive provenance, digitization, and
  later scholarship as separate layers
  ([R25 packet](../research/era-packets/R25-BRAZIL-LIMA-BARRETO-HNA-1919-1920/FINDINGS.md)).

The institutional observation page and January 1920 newspaper interview remain unopened.
Diary and fiction do not independently corroborate one another, and no diagnosis, causal
alcohol story, translation, portrait, facsimile, long quotation, representative population
claim, or public use is authorized.

**Transition:** After 1945, rights language, new drugs, decolonization, welfare states,
community experiments, and Cold War systems remake both care and control.

## B8 — Rights, drugs, decolonization, community care, and competing psychologies

**Working range:** c. 1945–1980.
**Backbone job:** Resist the tidy story that medicines emptied hospitals and communities took
over. Follow policy, housing, families, labor, budgets, prisons, activists, and regional
experiments.

- `B8.1` **The psychopharmacology turning point.** Antipsychotics, antidepressants,
  tranquilizers, lithium, marketing, trials, adverse effects, and the changing authority of
  diagnosis.
- `B8.2` **Deinstitutionalization was not one policy or one outcome.** Compare countries,
  hospitals, community services, housing, incarceration, family burden, and abandonment.
- `B8.3` **Patients, survivors, disabled people, and families organize.** Rights, refusal,
  peer support, independent living, recovery, mad liberation, family advocacy, and internal
  disagreements.
- `B8.4` **Decolonization and institutional inheritance.** Fann in Senegal, Fanon in Algeria,
  African national systems, South Asian institutions, Southeast Asian states, the Caribbean,
  and Pacific policy changes
  ([Antić](https://doi.org/10.1177/0952695121993702)).
- `B8.5` **Fanon's clinical and political route.** Martinique, France, Algeria, institutional
  practice, racism, violence, and the global afterlife of liberation thought
  ([IMEC Fanon archive](https://imec-archives.com/archives/fonds/178FNN)).
- `B8.6` **Community experiments beyond one model.** Nigeria's Aro village system, Fann,
  Jamaica, Nise da Silveira's workshops, therapeutic communities, sector psychiatry, and
  local critiques.
- `B8.7` **Psychologies multiply.** Behaviorism, cognitive approaches, humanistic and
  existential therapies, family systems, community psychology, liberation psychology, and
  culturally grounded approaches.
- `B8.8` **Trauma after genocide, war, dictatorship, and displacement.** Holocaust survivors,
  anticolonial wars, partition, Vietnam, Latin American dictatorships, Indigenous removal,
  and debates over memory and diagnosis.
- `B8.9` **WHO expert-committee authority and recommendation-making, 1949–1955** (internal
  node `T08-K` from R32, Batch 08). Institutional foundation for authority, recommendation,
  roster, edition, and bounded corpus-silence questions. No global consensus, WHO-policy,
  country-implementation, or public-voice foundation. Evidence labels per claim: §6 labels
  required. Gaps recorded in `GAPS-AND-HANDOFF.md`.
- `B8.10` **Japan's dual legal-administrative architecture and government program claims,
  1950–Oct 1 1965** (internal node `T08-L` from R31, Batch 08). Japanese law/program
  foundation for the 1950–1965 lane. No Korean evidence, no foundation for Japanese local
  implementation, direct patient/frontline voice, legal-language alignment, consent, or
  outcomes. Evidence labels per claim: §6 labels required. Gaps recorded in
  `GAPS-AND-HANDOFF.md`.
- `B8.11` **Attributed Aro pilot and operational descriptions, Oct 1954–1963** (internal
  node `T08-M` from R33, Batch 08). Attributed Aro organizer/admin program description.
  No participant, family, host, worker, healer, consent, cost, outcome, national, or
  representative regional foundation. Fann/Dakar remains a deferred feasibility lane.
  Evidence labels per claim: §6 labels required. Gaps recorded in `GAPS-AND-HANDOFF.md`.

**Transition:** Diagnostic manuals, insurance, markets, neuroscience, disability law, and
global policy networks increasingly shape what counts as a problem and a solution.

## B9 — Diagnosis, markets, neuroscience, recovery, and unfinished reform

**Working range:** c. 1970–2000.
**Backbone job:** Show how categories, evidence standards, imaging, pharmaceuticals, law,
economics, and social movements jointly changed mental health.

- `B9.1` **How diagnostic manuals acquire power.** Reliability, validity, insurance,
  research funding, pharmaceuticals, advocacy, and revisions; change is never only scientific.
- `B9.2` **The brain becomes visible—but an image is not a mind.** Neurochemistry, CT, PET,
  MRI, genetics, cognitive neuroscience, popular “chemical imbalance” stories, and limits of
  inference.
- `B9.3` **Addiction shifts among sin, crime, disease, habit, identity, and public health.**
  Include mutual aid, harm reduction, drug wars, racialized enforcement, and user-led action.
- `B9.4` **Social work, counseling, psychiatric nursing, psychology, and psychiatry in
  changing systems.** Managed care, schools, prisons, workplaces, hospitals, and community
  agencies reshape professional work.
- `B9.5` **The right to leave the hospital—and the right to real support.** Brazil's
  anti-asylum movement, the 1990 Caracas Declaration, Italy's influence, Ghanaian user
  organizing, and the gap between closure and care.
- `B9.6` **Culture enters diagnosis and service design.** Transcultural psychiatry,
  culture-bound categories, migration, racism, and the difference between community control
  and cosmetic “adaptation.”
- `B9.7` **Land, kin, culture, and social and emotional wellbeing.** Māori, Aboriginal and
  Torres Strait Islander, Pacific, Native Hawaiian, and Indigenous American frameworks must
  be Nation- and creator-specific, not collapsed into one holistic model
  ([Te Whare Tapa Whā](https://www.health.govt.nz/maori-health/maori-health-models/te-whare-tapa-wha)).
- `B9.8` **When reform fails catastrophically.** Use inquiries and testimony—such as Life
  Esidimeni—to examine outsourcing, neglect, accountability, and the meaning of community care.

**Transition:** The internet, platform economies, global health programs, climate disruption,
and new forms of surveillance expand the actors that define mental life.

## B10 — Networked minds: global mental health, digital life, neurodiversity, and new claims

**Working range:** c. 2000–present, with older roots visible.
**Backbone job:** Treat the present as contested history in motion, not the story's final and
correct answer.

- `B10.1` **Global mental health: access, scale, adaptation, and power.** WHO frameworks,
  task-sharing, donor priorities, local systems, measurement, language, and community control.
- `B10.2` **The Friendship Bench travels outward.** Tell the Zimbabwean development and later
  adaptations through local creators, participants, outcomes, credit, and limits
  ([Chibanda et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4865009/)).
- `B10.3` **Neurodiversity, mad pride, recovery, and disability justice.** Trace separate and
  overlapping movements, their arguments, internal disagreements, and institutional effects.

## Backbone-wide recurring components

Every band should offer:

1. a two-minute orientation;
2. a regional route chooser rather than a “rest of world” sidebar;
3. one object, text, place, or experiment readers can inspect;
4. one direct or near-direct voice when ethically available;
5. a visible archive-silence statement when it is not;
6. “what people called it then” terminology;
7. care **and** control consequences;
8. a named exchange route;
9. one credible dispute or misconception check;
10. a separate “what current evidence says” bridge; and
11. links forward, backward, and into long-arc thematic paths.

## Backbone acceptance check

- No period claims to be a universal stage.
- No region appears only as an imported-ideas recipient.
- Europe and the United States are regions, not the default narrator position.
- “Modern” is not used as a synonym for humane, scientific, or Western.
- Each band includes people subject to care or control, not only experts.
- The outline distinguishes direct voice, reported speech, institutional description, and
  modern reconstruction.
- “First,” “discovery,” and retrospective diagnosis require explicit review.
- Every public factual claim will still need claim-level extraction and verification; the
  links here establish an outline foundation, not article-ready sourcing.
