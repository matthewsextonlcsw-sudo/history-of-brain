Evidence model

A system that preserves the complete path from something a source contains to the exact sentence, caption, chart, or timeline entry a reader sees.

Design, not proof.
This document defines how research and citations will work. It does not claim that historical evidence has already been collected or verified.

The durable chain

A bibliography can look authoritative while hiding which source supports which claim—or whether anyone opened it. The unit we preserve is the entire reasoning path.

Every arrow stays inspectable. Public wording may change without losing the evidence underneath it.

Twelve rules

  1. Never cite a source that has not been opened and checked.
  2. Search results, snippets, AI responses, and citation lists are leads—not evidence.
  3. Record the exact page, folio, timestamp, table, figure, object, or passage.
  4. Separate what a source says from what the project concludes.
  5. Separate direct testimony from testimony reported by somebody else.
  6. Separate contemporary records from later historical interpretation.
  7. Preserve disagreement, uncertainty, translation, archival silence, and bias.
  8. Use the source appropriate to the claim; “primary” does not mean sufficient.
  9. Copied citation chains are not independent corroboration.
  10. A link is access, not proof. Record what was checked and when.
  11. Plausibility never makes a claim publishable.
  12. No research record becomes public wording without editorial approval.

Seven record types

01

Source

The work, object, dataset, recording, archival item, webpage, edition, or snapshot actually consulted.

02

Evidence

The exact passage, measurement, feature, testimony segment, finding, or bounded archival absence used in reasoning.

03

Claim

One precise proposition the project may assert, qualify, compare, dispute, or reject.

04

Relationship

A typed link showing whether evidence supports, qualifies, contradicts, contextualizes, illustrates, originates, or repeats a claim.

05

Page use

The exact public sentence, caption, chart, timeline entry, metadata field, or interactive element using a claim.

06

Disagreement

The question, credible positions, evidence behind them, reasons for difference, and approved fair wording.

07

Asset

An image, audio clip, video, map, diagram, facsimile, or quotation display with rights, provenance, and ethical review.

Special protocols

Prehistory

Separate observation from behavioral inference and belief inference. Material evidence cannot supply an undocumented diagnosis, motive, ritual meaning, or feeling.

Translation

Record original term, language, translator, edition, ambiguity, and mediation. An English near-equivalent may not preserve the historical category.

Patient testimony

Record whether testimony was voluntary, prompted, translated, edited, coerced, anonymized, or preserved by an institution holding power.

Institutional records

Evidence of what an institution recorded and did—not transparent access to patient experience, prevalence, or social reality.

Community knowledge

Name people, place, knowledge holder, provenance, access protocol, and community authority. Public access does not erase ethical limits.

Quantitative claims

Preserve numerator, denominator, population, dates, measure, method, uncertainty, missingness, and definition changes.

Current science

Use current synthesis suited to the question. Distinguish mechanisms, associations, outcomes, consensus, and policy.

Historical diagnosis

Describe documented words, behavior, context, and period categories. If scholars debate a diagnosis, the debate is the claim.

Harm and coercion

Separate intended purpose, claimed benefit, observed outcome, patient experience, consent, compulsion, and later assessment.

Living people

Use heightened privacy and reputation review. Sensitive health information does not become publishable merely because it is accessible.

Ten-stage workflow

DiscoverRecord the lead and original source to pursue. Leads cannot be cited.
ObtainSecure lawful, ethical access to the complete relevant source or reliable edition.
OpenA named researcher directly consults the source and records the date.
ExtractCreate evidence records with locators, context, limits, translation, and sensitivity.
Form claimsWrite small propositions with bounded time, place, population, and wording.
Link and challengeSeek support, qualification, contradiction, dependence, and missing voices.
VerifyRecheck the source, quotation, locator, scope, and high-risk wording.
UseRecord the exact public wording, visual, table, caption, or analogy.
ApprovePass citation, context, empathy, global-scope, readability, and editorial gates.
MaintainRecheck, repair links, propagate corrections, and preserve withdrawal history.

What the reader sees

Body layer

  • Descriptive links placed with the claim.
  • Dated badge when the check date carries weight.
  • No more than two pulled-out claim cards.
  • No single citation dangling after unrelated claims.
  • Plain language explaining how we know when it helps.

End-citation layer

  • Creator and title.
  • Editor or translator.
  • Container, publisher, archive, or repository.
  • Edition, version, and date.
  • Exact pages, timestamps, figures, or call numbers.
  • DOI, stable link, access date, and archive when applicable.

Pass 2 approval gate

  1. The seven record types preserve the path from source to public wording.
  2. Source and evidence records capture provenance, location, context, limits, and access.
  3. High-burden claims receive stronger evidence requirements.
  4. Testimony, institutional description, interpretation, and project inference cannot merge silently.
  5. Credible disagreement is representable without false balance.
  6. Special evidence situations have adequate protocols.
  7. Readable public citations retain the claim-level trail.
  8. Corrections can find and repair every use of a claim.
  9. The pilot—not this document alone—decides final storage and application schema.
