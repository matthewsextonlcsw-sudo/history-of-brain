# R05-ROMAN — independent evidence review

**Packet reviewed:** `R05-ROMAN`

**Review date:** August 18, 2026

**Reviewer role:** independent second reader

**Review scope:** research controls only; this is not public prose

**Verdict:** **PASS WITH GAPS**

## Decision

The packet may proceed to master-outline assembly after the mandatory repairs below are
made. It is not cleared for public drafting or quotation. Its central historical architecture
survives review: the checked evidence does not disclose one Roman mental-health system, and
the packet appropriately separates republican philosophy, Latin medical prescription,
Greek-language medicine within Roman imperial settings, sanctuary self-writing,
Justinianic legal compilation, and late-antique Greek-to-Latin medical transmission.

Pivotal ancient locators checked out for Celsus, Aretaeus, Galen, Caelius, Aristides, the
*Institutes*, and the three sampled *Digest* titles. The legal packet is particularly careful
about capacity versus diagnosis, law versus ordinary practice, exceptional prison/chains
versus household supervision, and sale law versus enslaved people's own voices. The
Pergamum material also keeps a healing sanctuary distinct from a custodial institution and
places both Aristides and Galen in a Greek-speaking Roman-imperial world.

This is not a full pass because the remaining defects affect claim status or reproducibility:

1. the Seneca ledger assigns Letter 78 an aggregate page range covering Letters 77–78, and
   the claim calls the account “rare” without evidence capable of establishing rarity;
2. Cicero's exact Latin/Greek lexical claim is attached only to an English translation even
   though the original-language passage was not recorded as a source;
3. the Galen and Caelius English evidence summaries are presented as direct support although
   the packet records no named translation or documented project translation;
4. one medical-disagreement claim adds a jurisdictional “who has authority” clause that the
   retained full-text evidence does not establish;
5. the asylum audit and two other single-chain claims are labeled more strongly than the
   evidence model permits;
6. one cached Jina artifact includes an entire modern Perseus page presentation while the
   ledger records a text/data license without documenting that it covers the whole captured
   page.

## Contrary scholarship, archive limits, and missing voices

- **The medical archive is plural but still prescriptive.** Celsus and Caelius disagree
  internally and with other schools, but their pages do not measure actual frequency,
  consent, outcomes or implementation. A criticism of beating proves controversy, not that
  beating was either universal or rare.
- **Galen's category is not “the Roman view.”** His Greek-language Pergamene/Roman location,
  multiple bodily models, dreams and Asclepian references complicate both a unitary Roman
  account and a religion/science split. Aretaeus, Celsus, Caelius, Cicero and law remain
  distinct evidence nodes.
- **The legal archive selects status and dispute.** Curatorship concerns people and property
  brought within legal administration; the matricide rescript is an exceptional violence
  case; sale law objectifies enslaved people through buyer/seller interests. None is a census
  of ordinary care or diagnosis.
- **The asylum negative remains bounded.** Laes, the checked law and Pergamum archaeology
  support household, guard, prison and sanctuary distinctions, but the packet did not perform
  a systematic empire-wide archaeology/institution search. Lost or unconsulted local evidence
  remains possible.
- **Patient voice is sharply unequal.** Seneca and Aristides are wealthy male authors engaged
  in philosophical or religious self-fashioning. Aretaean, Galenic, Celsian and Caelian
  sufferers are physician-shaped. Enslaved people appear through sale law. Women, children,
  poor, rural, nonliterate and most provincial people remain largely silent.
- **Aristides is one mediated account.** Diary/dictation references increase evidentiary
  interest but do not remove retrospective composition, praise, exaggeration or fictive
  shaping. Neither the text nor archaeology proves typicality or efficacy.
- **Self-harm wording is context-sensitive.** Seneca's rhetoric, Aretaean descriptions and
  sale-law classifications must not become inspirational quotation, modern advice, motive
  diagnosis or spectacle. The packet's safety gate is necessary.
- **Origin myths are tested, not measured as popular.** The packet correctly rejects universal
  restraint, humane-progress, humoral-certainty, Roman-asylum and Galen-as-consensus stories
  within its evidence. It did not conduct a reception sample capable of saying how common
  those stories are online.
