## Decision

R03-NUBIA-KUSH's principal conclusions survive risk-based replay. The Tombos article documents
complete union of fractures in one adult woman; assistance remains a conditional functional
inference rather than observed caregiving or compassion. The Amara West article supports
metastatic carcinoma as the authors' best differential for one individual, while leaving the
primary tumour, pain, treatment, and social response unknown. The 2016 Meroitic synthesis
supports the crucial distinction between deciphered scripts and only partial understanding of
language. The Musawwarat pages list “hospital” among competing historical proposals and do not
establish a hospital or healing institution. The restricted Nuri article and abstract-only Wadi
Halfa case are correctly quarantined.

The packet does not require wholesale return or a new research pass before it can contribute to
internal outline architecture. It does require exact repairs before coordinator acceptance as a
verified packet. The most important are:

1. correct a Tombos source-text inconsistency and stop describing a modern method framework as
   independent corroboration that care occurred;
2. replace abstract-inclusive and inaccurate locators for the Amara and Meroitic claims;
3. align claim confidence with single-study and same-specialist dependence;
4. date the Meroitic and Musawwarat state-of-knowledge claims and acquire current independent
   review before presenting either as present consensus;
5. attribute the UNESCO archive critique to its sources rather than turning it into an
   unqualified archive-wide fact;
6. add typed relationship records so source dependence and qualification are mechanically
   inspectable; and
7. preserve an explicit regional-review gate because modern Nubian testimony does not repair the
   packet's English/French-weighted ancient scholarship base.

## Independence, contradiction, and popular-claim audit

The packet's independence-group assignments are mostly accurate:

- the Tombos article is one substantive case study;
- the Amara differential is one study even though it uses several methods;
- Rilly 2016 and 2019 are one specialist chain;
- the two Musawwarat pages are one project chain;
- the Narrating Nubia overview and collaborator pages share an editorial/project route;
- UNESCO self-history, Lemos's critique, and named testimony have different evidentiary jobs;
- Haaland's article is one exchange synthesis, not proof of every cited underlying route; and
- the Nuri and Wadi Halfa leads do not count as corroboration.

The main independence defect is presentational: `CLM-004001` can be read as though the Index of
Care independently confirms assistance. It does not. The Meroitic and Musawwarat claims also need
dated/same-group wording so “later update” and “generally accepted” are not mistaken for
independent current consensus.

The Musawwarat hospital proposition is responsibly rejected within the opened corpus, but its
popular/upstream citation chain is incomplete. The project page proves that “hospital” has existed
as a proposal; it does not identify who originated it, what material was cited, how later scholars
answered it, or how widely the claim circulates. Until `GAP-004007` is completed, the outline may
say only that the opened evidence does not establish a hospital. It should not build a full
myth-history or “first hospital” correction box.

No credible counterargument was suppressed in the pivotal clinical cases: Tombos timing,
diagenesis, functional uncertainty, and care/motive alternatives are visible; Amara's differential,
taphonomy, infection, primary-site uncertainty, and sampling limitations are visible; Haaland's
speculative route is quarantined. The Musawwarat and Meroitic “current consensus” questions remain
open because the evidence is too dependent and dated, not because an opposing position was
established.

## Regional, heritage, and scope audit

The packet does not use “Nubian” or “Kushite” as a timeless undifferentiated people in its core
case descriptions. It distinguishes Tombos's late New Kingdom/early Napatan context, Amara West's
pharaonic colonial setting, Napatan/Meroitic materials, post-Meroitic and modern evidence, and
modern archive history. It also refuses to infer a regional mental-health system from two
palaeopathological cases.

The packet contains meaningful locally grounded material: named Nubian collaborators Yasmin and
Nabra, Sudanese archaeologist and curator Shadia Abdrabo, NCAM permissions/roles visible in the
Tombos and Amara records, and Lemos's critical archaeology synthesis. This improves the archive
account but does not complete the brief's regional-authority requirement for ancient claims.

The packet itself accurately records the remaining imbalance: scholarship on the ancient sites is
dominated by European/North American institutions, the pass is heavily English-language with one
French specialist route, the full Fushiya dissertation was not opened, and no systematic Arabic or
Nubian-language search was completed.

Internal outline structure may therefore use the packet only with a visible **regional review
pending** flag. Before any node is marked verified or ready for public prose, obtain:

1. Sudanese/Nubian archaeological review of site labels, periodization, and political geography;
2. a documented Arabic-language bibliography search and relevant Nubian-language/community
   archive search with qualified researchers or translators;
3. the complete Fushiya dissertation or another full locally grounded community-archaeology
   source for Amara West;
4. a Meroitic specialist's review of every translated term and certainty label; and
5. asset-specific NCAM/community/custody review before any human-remains or culturally sensitive
   image use.

Modern testimony must not be counted as independent support for ancient semantic, medical,
religious, or emotional claims. Conversely, the lack of ancient first-person evidence must not be
used to marginalize named modern speakers from archive and heritage sections.
