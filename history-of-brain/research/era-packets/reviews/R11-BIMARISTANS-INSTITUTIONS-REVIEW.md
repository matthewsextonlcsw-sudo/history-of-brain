# R11-BIMARISTANS-INSTITUTIONS — independent evidence review

**Packet reviewed:** `R11-BIMARISTANS-INSTITUTIONS`

**Review date:** August 18, 2026

**Reviewer role:** independent second reader

**Review scope:** research controls only; this is not public prose

**Verdict:** **RETURN**

## Decision

Return the packet for bounded repair before outline assembly. The packet has a strong core: it
keeps al-Nūrī and al-Manṣūrī distinct, separates buildings from rules and practice, refuses
the familiar first-hospital, first-psychiatric-hospital, music-ward, occupational-therapy, and
universal-humane-care formulas, and treats recipient experience as mediated rather than
transparent. The Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah case locators replay accurately, the waqf/chronicle
dependence is mostly disclosed, and the lived/archive audit is unusually careful.

The packet cannot pass yet because:

1. the typed relationship table violates the controlled schema in 13 predicates and seven
   object endpoints, leaving four claims without a valid incoming relationship;
2. Ibn Jubayr's page-image wording distinguishes the new and old Damascus hospitals, while
   `EVD-016027` and `CLM-016066` blend registrar/round and restraint evidence into one system-level
   sentence;
3. several pivotal `READY` claims treat unopened inscription, deed, chancery, and chronicle
   texts as if their L1 wording had been verified, although only modern conduits were opened;
4. the Nūrī music locator is wrong, the Prisse date/edition is unresolved, and the occupational-
   therapy "source" is not an auditable source record; and
5. current-use/custody language for Damascus is stale, while the current Egyptian authority's
   statement about ongoing eye/dentistry use is absent from the retained current-site evidence.

These are evidentiary and structural defects, not requests for public-facing prose or corpus
expansion.

## Materials and method

The reviewer read the packet's `BRIEF.md`, all five outputs, `EVIDENCE-MODEL.md`, and
`SOURCE-RATING-AND-CAPTURE-PROTOCOL.md` in full. Review was risk-based and prioritized the two
institutional evidence stacks, inscriptions, waqf and appointment chains, translation-sensitive
case passages, priority/myth claims, later-versus-foundation chronology, dependency, source
levels and fit, lived recipient/worker limits, current heritage claims, rights/cache integrity,
and every `016` identifier and relationship.

Agent Reach diagnostics found Jina Reader available and Exa unavailable. Full sources, page
images, direct authoritative records, and the approved primary/scholarly web-search fallback
were used. Abstracts, snippets, and AI summaries were not accepted as evidence. No R11 packet or
cache file was modified. A second research reader independently replayed the priority, music,
occupational-therapy, and humane-care chains and reached the same substantive dispositions.

## Evidence layers and independence

The packet's conceptual separation is strong but must be enforced mechanically:

- **Epigraphy:** `SRC-016002` and `SRC-016003` are modern conduits to al-Nūrī inscriptions,
  not two independent events and not direct L1 consultation.
- **Waqf:** `SRC-016008` and `SRC-016009` are independently authored but repeat the same
  *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* chain. They cannot be counted twice as medieval corroboration.
- **Chronicle:** al-Nuwayrī and al-Maqrīzī are later textual layers. Neither may be merged
  into foundation-year practice, architecture, or outcomes.
- **Appointments:** Fernandes is an accountable L2 guide to identified instruments, not the
  instruments themselves.
- **Architecture:** surviving fabric and reconstructed room use are not interchangeable.
  Substantial loss of the Manṣūrī hospital fabric sharply limits room-function inference.
- **Travel:** Ibn Jubayr is a primary report, but the page explicitly distributes observations
  across two hospitals. It is not recipient testimony or an administrative register.
- **Biography/case narrative:** Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah is direct evidence for what the author
  narrates. Its cases are not independent outcome measurements, patient transcripts, or a
  representative sample.
- **Modern heritage:** official catalog/use statements support current administrative
  representation only; they are not medieval sources and can contain unsourced historical
  claims.

No sampled retained historical proposition rested only on an abstract, snippet, preview, or AI
summary. The Ragab preview is correctly quarantined. The major problem is not summary reliance
but treating unopened upstream primary texts as already rated/verified evidence.

## Priority and popular-myth chains

### First hospital

The packet's safe conclusion survives: neither selected institution is first. Horden's account
places an attested Islamic foundation in ninth-century Baghdad under his defined category, and
Savage-Smith lists multiple Baghdad foundations before 1154. The sources partly overlap a
historiography but are not one citation chain. The packet must not turn Horden's category-bound
"first Islamic hospital" into one uncontested global origin.

### First psychiatric hospital

No foundation, deed, division list, or selected primary report establishes a psychiatric-only
institution. The correct public-history move is to reject the affirmative priority claim, not
declare that people in mental distress were absent. The negative and the rejected myth should be
separate claim records so that relationships can target exact propositions.

### Music and music ward

Dols's Manṣūrī sentence is one chain to a nineteenth-century work whose edition and upstream
authority remain unresolved. A stronger recent repetition located during review cites Dols or
later Ibn Iyās through modern editions and is not independent proof of a thirteenth-century music
ward. The Nūrī chain is a 1648 traveler report and belongs to an early-modern handoff. Neither
chain supports a dedicated ward, treatment selection, frequency at foundation, or effectiveness.

### Occupational therapy

No direct evidence was recovered. The modern label is anachronistic unless an institution-
specific activity, actors, purpose, and outcome are separately described. The negative may be
retained only as a bounded audit result with actual query/date/lead provenance, not a universal
absence claim.

### Humane care

The packet correctly refuses a total moral label. Learned medical ideals, charitable/legal
provisions, named treatments, restraints, chains, exclusion, and archive silence coexist. None
is recipient assessment of "humaneness." Later writing should describe the specific evidence and
avoid speaking for recipients.

## Lived experience, archive, workers, and scope

This section passes. The packet is within its two-institution/two-region scope and does not let a
single general model stand for all care in Syria, Egypt, or the Islamic world. It distinguishes:

- physician-mediated attributed speech from a transcript;
- legal beneficiary/exclusion categories from actual admission;
- traveler observation from recipient voice;
- appointment and patron visibility from the whole workforce;
- home-support norms from household labor and delivery; and
- anecdotal recovery/death from institutional outcomes.

The dropsy narrative is especially well handled: work, distrust, wife-mediated negotiation, and
death survive only in a physician's reputation-bearing, blame-bearing story. The `māniyā` case
preserves a label and intervention but no name, consent, feeling, safety report, or durable
outcome. The packet also names missing attendants, cleaners, cooks, water carriers, burial
workers, family caregivers, women, enslaved/unfree people, children, rural people, people refused
care, and Jewish/Christian people affected by Manṣūrī exclusion.

One attribution repair affects this otherwise strong audit: old-hospital restraint in Ibn
Jubayr must not be made an undifferentiated al-Nūrī or system-wide practice.
