## Decision

R01 is a strong internal calibration packet. It stays within its three-case corpus, distinguishes observation from diagnosis, function, assistance, motive, and experience, exposes copied citation chains, and refuses to invent a prehistoric patient voice. Its most important negative findings are sound: M487 does not establish postoperative survival or motive; assistance is not evidence of compassion; and no consulted source supplies first-person experience for M487, M9, or Shanidar 1.

The packet does not require return for wholesale re-research. It does require claim-level repairs before coordinator acceptance, chiefly because several **Strongly supported** labels exceed the project's own definition when the case-specific support comes from one author/evidence chain. One M487 timing sentence also overstates its source. The unresolved full-text Shanidar counter-reading remains a publication blocker, as the packet correctly says.

## Claim wording and evidence-label audit

### Required repairs

1. **Repair `CLM-001003` and every repetition of its timing claim.** Replace “probably made close to or after death,” “supports action very near death or after death,” and “deliberately opened close to or after death” with a formulation no stronger than: **“No apparent healing is compatible with perimortem or postmortem alteration; the evidence does not resolve timing or motive.”** The source says “could,” and the independent healing paper warns against exact clocks.

2. **Apply the project's definition of Strongly supported consistently.** That label requires multiple strong, sufficiently independent sources or strong scholarly consensus. The following claims currently lack that basis within the packet:
   - `CLM-001002`, deliberate drilling for M487: change to **Plausible / strongly favored by the case authors** unless independent case-specific corroboration is added.
   - the assistance portion of `CLM-001008`: change to **Plausible, pending independent functional review**, while leaving the directly observed anatomy documented. Do not count `SRC-001002` and `SRC-001003` twice.
   - `CLM-001012`, functional hearing loss: change to **Plausible / strong clinical inference**, pending independent specialist support. The exostoses themselves remain documented.
   - `CLM-001005`, if retained as a broad field claim, either add independent current methods scholarship/consensus or narrow it to what Partiot et al.'s review found and label it documented as that study's result.

3. **Keep M9 care tasks explicitly modeled.** Tilley and Oxenham's pp. 37–40 language often moves from clinical possibility to “must” and treats absent skeletal complications as evidence of particular care. `CLM-001009` handles this well, but FINDINGS and future handoffs must not promote water provision, diet, skin inspection, massage, dedicated carers, or continuous nursing from scenarios to events.

4. **Unbundle Shanidar functional claims.** In FINDINGS, “one functional upper limb, an altered gait, probable reduced vision/coordination, and likely hearing loss” combines observations and different-strength inferences. Give visual limitation, coordination, arm function, gait, and hearing separate locators and labels, or omit the elements not necessary to the ladder test.

5. **Make public-page ratings claim-relative.** Institutional branding alone is not L3 evidence for ancient claims:
   - `SRC-001011` is L1-A for the narrow historical claim that the Smith page publishes that wording, but L5-D for prehistoric doctors, indications, brain-tissue removal, and “brain surgery” because the chain breaks.
   - `SRC-001010` is L1-A for its own public wording and L4-D for the uncited necessity/compassion inference.
   - Apply the same distinction to `SRC-001009` and `SRC-001012`: they may directly document their own public framing, while remaining context/mismatch for ancient function, motive, or care.

6. **Add a ledger crosswalk for required source fields.** Geographic scope, time scope, people/population, provenance, source position, and cultural protocol are partly embedded in citations and limitations but are not consistently explicit as the protocol's minimum ledger row requires. Add them as columns or a clearly keyed supplement; use `unknown/not reported` rather than inference.

### Wording that passed

- M487 is not called the first trepanation, a medical patient, a successful operation, or evidence of care.
- M9's KFS diagnosis is “favored but not definitive”; the missing skeleton and modern-category problem are stated.
- The packet rejects Tilley and Oxenham's claims about M9's will, self-esteem, psychological adaptation, inspiring personality, and ungrudging emotional support.
- Assistance, compassion, moral worth, productivity, dependence, identity, and subjective experience remain separate.
- Shanidar 1 necessary-care language is held as disputed, not resolved from the restricted article.
- No patient voice is manufactured from skeletal survival, mortuary treatment, a modeled care regimen, modern clinical analogy, or disability expertise.

**Gap:** the M9 assistance conclusion and the Shanidar hearing/function interpretation each still depend on one case-specific scholarly chain. The packet openly requests clinical/functional second checks, but its confidence labels must reflect their absence now.

**Gap:** `SRC-001005` combines new auditory observations with an older Stewart/Trinkaus injury-analysis chain. For reproducibility, its independence note should distinguish the 2017 auditory examination from inherited/reanalyzed injury claims in S1.

**Pass:** serious counterpositions are not hidden. Dettwyler is used to challenge helplessness, non-productivity, compassion, and group-morality inferences. Kinkopf is preserved as a pivotal unresolved counter-reading rather than summarized beyond the abstract.

**Publication blocker:** obtain and read the complete Kinkopf 2025 article before making a public claim that Shanidar 1 necessarily required care, was incapacitated, or could not contribute. If it cannot be acquired lawfully, the claim remains Disputed/Unknown rather than being resolved from the abstract.

## Scope and handoff assessment

**PASS.** The packet is a calibration exercise, not a global prehistory catalog. It gives the three cases different methodological jobs, avoids prevalence and priority claims, does not retroject later beliefs or diagnoses, and stops expansion when further spectacular cases would not resolve the method gaps. The archive section is unusually careful about preservation bias, invisible conditions, missing caregivers, mortuary evidence, and the difference between modern disability expertise and invented ancient identity.

After the six repairs above, the packet may be used as an **internal outline and research-method handoff**. It must retain these visible gates:

- direct chronology review before exact timeline placement;
- independent clinical/osteological review before stronger M9 or hearing-function wording;
- full Kinkopf review before resolving Shanidar care necessity;
- repository/community protocol review before any remains image; and
- human editorial approval before any claim becomes public prose.

**Final disposition: PASS WITH GAPS.** The gaps are repairable and already substantially recognized by the packet; they are not permission to draft around the uncertainty.
