## Decision

R31 is a careful internal evidence packet. It does not mistake statutes or ministry directives for implementation, protector consent for the admitted person's consent, national administrative design for prefectural practice, one Kanagawa file sample for Japan, or later scholarly mediation for direct patient or family testimony. The 1950 and 1965 legal objects, both official directives, the 1956 and FY1965 white papers, the Japanese government's 1962 English report, and the two complete specialist articles are strong enough for the packet's bounded internal propositions.

The packet is not yet schema-clean. One causal relationship points to an evidence atom that does not record the causal proposition, one relationship invents a different independence group from its source, the evidence controlled-value casing is noncanonical, all four disagreement objects use noncanonical field names and omit required fields, and the Kanagawa archive-access claim overstates the authority of the opened rights source. These defects are repairable without expanding the historical scope or changing the packet's deliberate voice, translation, privacy, or public holds.

## Official law, administration, and system evidence

### 1950 law and directive

The official enacted text supports the packet's core legal distinctions:

- Article 4 imposed a prefectural hospital duty, subject to the statutory alternative; Article 7 authorized consultation offices rather than making them a universal duty.
- Articles 20–22 created a hierarchy of legally designated protectors and assigned duties that often implicated family members but could fall to a municipal mayor. The packet appropriately refuses to turn legal assignment into voluntary family caregiving or family testimony.
- Article 29 governor-ordered admission required agreement by two designated appraisers. Article 33 permitted hospital admission without the person's consent when the legally designated protector consented and the other statutory conditions were met. The packet correctly treats this as **protector consent, not patient consent**.
- Articles 38, 41, and 42 placed restriction, protector retrieval, and visiting guidance within the same legal system. The packet therefore does not presume that a visit was autonomous, supportive community care.
- Articles 43–44 and 48 created a bounded outside-hospital restraint exception and transition rather than proving that household confinement immediately disappeared.

The directive is correctly used as national administrative instruction, not compliance or outcome evidence.

### 1956 and 1962 government reports

The 1956 white paper supports the reported 269 hospitals; 37,044 psychiatric-hospital beds plus 8,605 psychiatric beds in general hospitals; 49,893 inpatients; 109% utilization; 1,062 specialists; and 2,373 nurses, assistant nurses, and attendants. It also reports 41 consultation offices, insufficient use, limited specialist staffing, and visiting guidance. These are attributed government aggregates and acknowledgments, not measures of quality, coercion, household burden, or outcome.

The Japanese government's 1962 English report is correctly separated from WHO authorship or validation. Its own wording describes public health centres as both community service and administrative supervision agencies. Its mental-health chapter says the law accelerated institutional care while also calling consultation, visits, and preventive work “community care.” Tables 26–27 support the reported bed, hospital, inpatient, outpatient-volume, occupancy, and centre figures. The packet properly treats this as national self-description, preserves the unclear outpatient unit, and does not infer deinstitutionalization.

### 1965 act, directive, and white paper

The complete amending act and directive support the packet's description of centres, public-health-centre consultation and visits, qualified staff, half-cost outpatient finance effective 1965-10-01, repeal of the prior home-restraint route, and confidentiality provisions. They also support the simultaneous expansion of police-linked notification and emergency examination/admission powers. The packet correctly refuses a one-sided “community reform” story and treats the endpoint enactment as program design, not implementation.

The FY1965 white paper's mental-hygiene section is retrospective and was published in 1966. The packet properly flags post-endpoint figures. Its p. 32 narrative explicitly says review of substantial amendment had already been underway and then describes the March 1964 stabbing of Ambassador Reischauer as an occasion that further matured momentum before the June 1965 amendment. That wording supports a government retrospective framing, not sole causation, legislative consensus, or motive.

The eugenics material is also responsibly bounded: the packet records the government's policy adjacency and the aggregate 29,468 FY1964 operations while explicitly refusing to relabel that number as a psychiatric subtotal.

### 4. Rebuild all four disagreement objects to the canonical schema

### 5. Recast or source the Kanagawa repository-authorization statement

`CLM-043026` says the Kanagawa files “cannot be opened or used at case level without repository authorization,” while `REL-043047` uses NDL reproduction guidance and gives it `claim_fit:"A"`. NDL is not the Kanagawa Prefectural Archives and cannot establish that repository's current access requirements. Goto and Ando establish only that their own viewing application was approved.

In the latter route, change `REL-043047` to contextual qualification with no A-grade claim that NDL controls Kanagawa. Keep the complete case-level public hold in either route.
