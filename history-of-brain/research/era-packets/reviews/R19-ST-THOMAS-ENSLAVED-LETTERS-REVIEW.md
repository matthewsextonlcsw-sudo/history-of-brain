## Edition method and transmission

The edition-method chain is accurately bounded apart from the mandatory attribution correction.
The 1996 preface states that the 1991–1993 project entered about 2,000 manuscript pages and 500
printed pages, attempted a diplomatic edition, and transcribed photocopies made from microfilms of
fragile Herrnhut manuscripts. It describes the book as an annotated **selection**. The letters
chapter says the Herrnhut corpus contains about 150 letters written between 1737 and 1768 and that
the chapter presents a selection. It also explains that sentence boundaries sometimes had to be
created in the free translation.

The packet therefore passes the source-layer test:

`St. Thomas item → Moravian custody/copying → Herrnhut grouping → microfilm → photocopy → diplomatic transcription project → selected 1996 anthology/gloss/free translation → DBNL rendering → packet`

No graph edge treats the edition, the six item records, Van Rossem's later dissertation, or the
original objects as independent witnesses to handwriting. Item-level primary records are always
qualified as edition-mediated, and `CLM-031023` keeps original object properties Unknown.

## Damma language and interpretation

The disagreement is handled responsibly. The 1996 edition explicitly says the African language
was unidentified. Gerbner's full 2025 monograph identifies the text as Gbe, prints the current
archive form `R.15.B.a.03.61_a-b`, reads `adga Tome` as “Aja country,” and attributes the work to a
named collaboration. The later digital project reports divergent Gbe and Creole layers,
incomplete sentences, collaborator disagreement, interpretive choices, and earlier inaccuracies.

Gerbner and the digital project are correctly placed in one dependence group. The packet does not
count them as two independent translations, infer ethnicity from Poppo/Aja/Gbe, or replace the
frozen Unknown public state with the newest claim. `DIS-031002`, `CLM-031008–031010`, and the
public holds survive review.
