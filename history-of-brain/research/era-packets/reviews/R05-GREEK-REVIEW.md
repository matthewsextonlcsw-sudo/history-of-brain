# R05-GREEK — independent evidence review

**Packet reviewed:** `R05-GREEK`

**Review date:** August 18, 2026

**Reviewer role:** independent second reader

**Review scope:** research controls only; this is not public prose

**Verdict:** **PASS WITH GAPS**

## Decision

The packet may proceed as an outline-foundation packet after the mandatory repairs below are
made. It should not yet be described as fully source-verified, and the affected claims should
not be promoted into quotation-ready or publication-ready copy.

The central finding survives review: the checked Greek-language record preserves competing
models and practices rather than a clean religion-to-science succession. Pivotal primary
locators for *On the Sacred Disease*, Epidaurian sanctuary inscriptions, Aristotle's
cardiocentrism, and the later testimony about Alcmaeon all checked out. The packet also
handles institutional mediation, retrospective diagnosis, Roman-period handoff material,
and the difference between a represented patient and a patient-authored voice unusually
carefully.

This is not a full pass because four defects affect evidentiary status rather than polish:

1. a Perseus catalog record is rated as though the Greek witness itself had been opened;
2. an English regimen claim rests on a machine-generated Humanitext rendering/summary;
3. part of the sanctuary-compilation claim is presented as independent corroboration even
   though the checked article explicitly depends on LiDonnici for that source-layer account;
4. four cached whole-page files do not have a sufficiently clear reuse basis for the exact
   presentation artifacts stored.

## Materials and method

The reviewer read the packet's five deliverables, `BRIEF.md`, `EVIDENCE-MODEL.md`, and
`SOURCE-RATING-AND-CAPTURE-PROTOCOL.md` in full. Verification used a risk-based sample rather
than re-performing every search. The sample prioritized thesis-bearing claims, quotations,
origin/priority language, first-person and patient-experience representations, full-text
status, translation chains, and cached artifacts.

Agent Reach's Jina route was used to open full webpages. Agent Reach reported Exa unavailable,
so the approved built-in primary-source search fallback was used for discovery. Search-result
snippets and abstracts were treated as leads only. Local SHA-256 values were independently
recomputed.

## Contrary scholarship and unresolved voices

- **Sacred/natural is not a binary.** Jouanna's reading materially contradicts popular
  “religion ended when rational medicine began” summaries. Laskaris's protreptic/clientele
  reading, currently available here only through Rocca's review, adds a competing explanation
  of the treatise's rhetoric. The packet keeps both visible and should continue to do so.
- **Iamata transmission remains disputed.** Barrenechea is cautious about compilation and
  sanctuary shaping; Nissen is more willing to reconstruct originating patient experiences;
  LiDonnici's full source-layer argument was not lawfully opened. The packet must not flatten
  these positions into one independent consensus.
- **Institutional text is not patient transcript.** Kleo, Ambrosia, Euphanes, enslaved people,
  servants, women, children, companions, and caregivers are visible only through uneven and
  often institutionally controlled records. Their feelings, consent, affordability, coercion,
  satisfaction, and outcomes remain largely unknown.
- **Greek priority claims remain unsafe.** The Alcmaeon chain is later testimony, and the
  packet did not compare every relevant Egyptian, Mesopotamian, South Asian, Chinese, or other
  neighboring record. The global “first” prohibition is therefore mandatory, not stylistic.
- **Old translations constrain emotional vocabulary.** Adams and Ogle are useful witnesses to
  their editions, but English words such as “madness,” “melancholy,” “fear,” or “understanding”
  should not be lifted into modern psychological categories without source-language review.
