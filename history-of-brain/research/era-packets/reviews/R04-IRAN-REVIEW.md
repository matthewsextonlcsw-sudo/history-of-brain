# Independent cross-review — R04-IRAN

**Review date:** August 18, 2026
**Reviewer:** independent packet cross-review
**Decision:** **RETURN**
**Public prose authorized:** no

## Decision

R04-IRAN is a careful and unusually self-aware first pass. It separates composition, redaction,
performance, manuscript, translation, and repository history; it does not turn prescriptive law,
royal rhetoric, an administrative ration record, a priest's monumental self-presentation, or a
museum catalog into transparent patient experience. PF 1224 and the packet's core archive warning
replayed especially well. The packet also preserves rather than erases Kartīr's coercive rhetoric
while acknowledging uncertainty over the verb, acts, groups, and scale.

The packet nevertheless must be returned before it can pass the internal-outline gate. Four
governance defects are material:

1. `CLAIM-MAP.md` replaces the project's approved evidence vocabulary with “Supported with
   limits” and “Held correction,” and repeatedly uses **Strongly supported** for propositions that
   rest on one ancient witness, one holder account, or one edition-based synthesis. Under the
   evidence model, direct bounded evidence is **Documented**; **Strongly supported** requires
   multiple sufficiently independent sources or strong consensus. Hold/review state is separate
   from evidence strength.
2. The cached Cantera article's redistribution status is asserted as settled even though the
   volume front matter says each contribution is copyright its author and “All rights reserved.”
   An older journal archive page grants redistribution with attribution, but the packet does not
   identify that page or establish that its permission governs this migrated Harvard-hosted issue.
   The checksum passes; the rights conclusion does not.
3. The brief-required reception-chain audits for “first hospital,” “first psychology,” free will,
   demonology, and modern diagnosis were not actually performed. The packet identifies no dated
   downstream claim, exact wording, source ID, upstream citation trail, or `repeats` / `originates`
   / `contradicts` relationship. Search-result quarantine is good discovery discipline, but it is
   not a completed citation-chain audit.
4. The Gondēšāpur field is represented by one opened synthesis and a generic Dols access gap. A
   full, directly opposed article is openly available: Touraj Nayernouri, “Gondeshapur Revisited;
   What Historical Evidence?” (2017). It argues for a pre-Islamic school and hospital, while also
   acknowledging that the only explicit school-and-hospital reference is eighth-century and using
   several circumstantial inferences. The current hold is prudent, but a packet that promises both
   sides must record and assess this disagreement before calling the negative chronology a
   completed audit.

These defects are repairable without discarding the packet. None of this review authorizes public
copy, direct quotation, image reuse, or a Gondēšāpur priority answer.

## Avestan, Middle Persian, and Pahlavi controls

The packet passes the most important conceptual test: it does not treat “the Avesta” as a single,
fully datable book or a medieval manuscript as a transparent ancient autograph. It also correctly
keeps contemporary Middle Persian inscriptions, later Pahlavi textual compilations, and modern
editions in separate source layers.

Three refinements are mandatory:

1. Cantera's manuscript-performance argument is specifically about the Long Liturgy. Do not extend
   its particulars to every Avestan genre without a source for that move.
2. Replace the unreconciled K7a year with a late-thirteenth-century formulation, or verify the
   manuscript identity and date in a current catalog/critical study.
3. Use terminology that prevents “Pahlavi” from blurring contemporary inscriptional Middle
   Persian and post-conquest Book-Pahlavi compilations. Kartīr is a third-century inscriptional
   witness. The extant literary forms of *Dēnkard*, *Bundahišn*, and Zādspram used by later
   syntheses are not direct Achaemenid or Sasanian transcripts and remain handoff material.

No timeless “Zoroastrian psychology,” national essence, or unbroken doctrine is established by
the opened corpus. The packet's current guardrails on that point should remain.

## Gondēšāpur: mandatory two-sided audit

The packet is right to block “the world's first hospital/university.” Its present source base is
not sufficient to convert that block into a definitive historical absence claim.

The contrary source now identified is:

- Touraj Nayernouri, “[Gondeshapur Revisited; What Historical
  Evidence?](https://journalaim.com/PDF/84_0013.pdf),” *Archives of Iranian Medicine* 20.4
  (2017): 254–260, PMID [28412832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28412832/).

The full article is useful because it states the disagreement directly. It argues that Syriac
Christian evidence and later historiography support a pre-Islamic school and hospital. It also
reveals important weaknesses that must be tested rather than silently accepted:

- p. 257 says the only extant explicit reference to both a medical school and a hospital at
  Gondēšāpur is eighth-century;
- the pre-Islamic hospital inference partly moves from the existence/etymology of `bimārestān` to
  the existence of the institution, which is not by itself sufficient;
- the dispatch of physicians to Nisibis is used circumstantially to infer a Gondēšāpur school;
- the article invokes a late *Dēnkard* account for Sasanian translation activity and sometimes
  treats possibility as likelihood;
- its conclusion claims certainty despite acknowledging sparse primary evidence.

This does not make Nayernouri useless or automatically wrong. It makes it a genuine contrary
interpretation whose cited Syriac sources, dates, locations, and inferential steps must be replayed.
Add it to the ledger as a full-text scholarly argument, rate its individual jobs separately, and
link it as contradicting/qualifying the current Gondēšāpur correction. Also open the upstream
Dols article, Richter-Bernburg's key c. 610 and 765 sources, and the Syriac sources used by
Nayernouri. Distinguish:

1. a city and important Church-of-the-East see;
2. court physicians and translators in the Sasanian realm;
3. medical learning or disputation at a named place;
4. an institution caring for sick people;
5. a medical school;
6. supervised bedside clinical education; and
7. a priority claim such as “first teaching hospital.”

These are not interchangeable. Until the chain is replayed, public status remains held.

## Priority, free-will, and diagnosis reception chains

The packet's current “search claims quarantined” row is not an origin audit. A usable audit must
identify who made the formulation, when, where, with what wording, and which source was cited. It
must then follow that citation upstream and record repetitions that only echo the same chain.

At minimum, repair the following:

- **First teaching hospital/university:** record a dated, accountable exemplar such as Mohammadali
  M. Shoja and R. Shane Tubbs, “[The history of anatomy in
  Persia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2100290/),” *Journal of Anatomy* 210.4
  (2007): 359–378, DOI [10.1111/j.1469-7580.2007.00711.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7580.2007.00711.x),
  which calls Gondēšāpur the first teaching hospital, and trace that formulation upstream. Also
  record current popular pages separately from scholarship; popularity and evidentiary strength
  are different jobs.
- **Free will / first psychology:** record at least one dated downstream priority formulation and
  trace its translation chain. Abraham Valentine Williams Jackson's 1920 [“The Zoroastrian
  Doctrine of the Freedom of the
  Will”](https://www.cais-soas.com/CAIS/Religions/iranian/Zarathushtrian/freedom_will.htm)
  is an important reception-history lead because it explicitly maps the modern philosophical
  category onto Avestan and later Pahlavi materials while also discussing disputed passages. It is
  not proof that Zoroaster “invented” free will.
- **Demonology / diagnosis:** identify a dated modern article or public page making the exact
  diagnostic equivalence the project intends to correct, open its evidence, and trace the
  translation/commentary chain. If no accountable claim is found, retain the general no-diagnosis
  method guardrail but do not claim an origin audit.
- **“Persians invented psychiatry” / “magic and science”:** either perform the same source replay
  or leave these as unverified discovery questions. Do not use search-result frequency as historical
  evidence or as proof of modern prevalence without a defined search sample.

Add source/evidence records and `repeats`, `originates`, `contradicts`, or `qualifies` relationships
for every retained correction. If the packet owner chooses not to complete a particular reception
chain now, remove the checklist implication that it was completed.

## Lived experience, power, and missing voices

The packet's mediation matrix is strong. It correctly states that no opened core source provides a
patient-authored account and refuses to invent feelings for postpartum women, incantation-bowl
clients, targeted religious groups, or people governed by *Vendīdād* prescriptions.

Required refinements:

- Recast Aphriduh's bowl as a **named-beneficiary, object- and catalog-mediated care trace**, not a
  strong lived-experience account. A name and cataloged purpose survive; symptoms, words, outcome,
  agency, and emotion do not.
- Keep PF 1224's women, infants, partners, household members, and administrators visibly absent.
  The unequal allocation is administratively documented; its meaning to recipients is not.
- Keep Kartīr's targeted communities as an asymmetric-archive problem. His inscription is the
  powerful actor's monumental voice. Counterarchives from Jewish Babylonian, Syriac Christian,
  Manichaean, Buddhist/Central Asian, Armenian/Roman, and archaeological routes remain a material
  gap, not optional color.
- Keep South-Iraqi bowls in a Mesopotamian as well as Sasanian imperial context. An Iranian personal
  name does not turn an Aramaic object into generic “Persian medicine.”
- Preserve provenance cautions. The BM records document modern acquisition chains but do not make
  an uncertain findspot secure or establish representative household practice.

This is a **PASS WITH GAPS** on empathy and archive analysis. The gaps must remain visible in any
outline scene.
