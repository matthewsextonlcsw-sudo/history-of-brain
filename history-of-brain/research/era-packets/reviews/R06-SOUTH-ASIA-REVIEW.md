# Independent cross-review — R06-SOUTH-ASIA

**Packet reviewed:** `R06-SOUTH-ASIA — Selves, Suffering, Medicine, and Cultivation`
**Review date:** August 18, 2026
**Reviewer role:** independent packet cross-review
**Disposition:** **PASS WITH GAPS**
**Internal-outline gate:** conditional; the packet may contribute bounded nodes after the mandatory repairs below, but it must not be marked verified before those repairs close
**Publication status:** no public prose or public claim promotion authorized

## Decision

The packet's central historical controls survive risk-based replay. The opened witnesses support
text-specific accounts of faculties, suffering, cultivation, `unmāda`, and prescriptive care. The
packet correctly refuses a single “South Asian psychology,” a literal ancient brain claim, a
one-to-one `unmāda`/psychosis identity, unchanged modern mindfulness or wellness continuity, and
the conversion of medical prescription into evidence of implementation. Its archive and
lived-experience note is unusually careful about literary voice, practitioner voice, coercion,
gender/status/body filters, and the absence of a checked `unmāda` patient account.

The packet does not require wholesale return or a new general research pass before it can inform
internal outline architecture. It does require targeted repairs. The principal defects are not
that the pivotal passages were invented; they are that several claims use **Strongly supported**
for one evidentiary chain, one weak anonymous translation is still allowed into an outline node,
two `REL` verbs point the wrong logical way, a modern reception claim bundles historical mapping
with a clinical-evidence conclusion, and some composition/manuscript wording exceeds the exact
scope of the source used.

The coordinator should therefore treat this as **PASS WITH GAPS**, with the mandatory repair list
as a precondition to a verified internal-outline handoff. Current critical editions,
regionally grounded social-history research, and a separate current clinical review remain later
publication gates; they are not silently supplied by this review.

## Claim-label and source-fit audit

### Claims that survive within their present boundaries

- `CLM-009000–009005` are usable as passage- or edition-specific statements with their current
  quotation, transmission, audience, and efficacy controls.
- `CLM-009008–009010` correctly distinguish an ancient textual category, multiple causal
  registers, and prescriptive care from a modern diagnosis or observed practice.
- `CLM-009011` survives as a negative witness comparison: “brain” is absent from the checked
  Sanskrit clause. No positive translation of `hṛdaya` is established.
- `CLM-009012` survives as a transmitted colophon attribution once its locator is corrected.
- `CLM-009016` is a bounded taxonomy/practice claim tied to Woods's witness and Maas's historical
  context, not clinical therapy.
- `CLM-009019` is a sound audit of the **checked corpus**, not a claim that patient voice never
  existed in South Asian history.

### Confidence labels that must be normalized

The evidence model reserves **Strongly supported** for multiple strong, sufficiently independent
sources or a strong scholarly consensus. Direct evidence from one good source may be
**Documented** without being weak. The packet should therefore make these exact changes unless it
adds independent evidence:

1. `CLM-009006` — **Documented as Sujato's source-critical account**; Allon is contextual, not an
   independent Therīgāthā manuscript/attribution study.
2. `CLM-009007` — **Documented in Soni's bounded analysis**.
3. `CLM-009011` — **Documented for the checked Sanskrit/English witnesses**.
4. `CLM-009013` — **Documented in Pecchia's chiefly Vimānasthāna sample**.
5. `CLM-009015` — **Documented as Maas's reconstruction**; do not imply field consensus.
6. `CLM-009017` — **Documented as Maas's reception argument**.
7. `CLM-009018` — after the required split, the existence of modern mapping is documented by two
   publications; the clinical-evidence limitations are documented by the one 2023 systematic
   review. Do not give both propositions one shared confidence label.

These are label/independence repairs, not findings that the source passages are false.

### Source-level correction

`SRC-009016` is the sole material rating failure. A webpage that gives neither a named translator
nor a scholarly edition is a discovery lead under the source-rating protocol. Displaying Sanskrit
does not by itself make the page a critical witness: the source of that Sanskrit, variant policy,
editorial interventions, and translation authority remain unknown. Rate it **L5/A for what the
page displays; D for ancient semantics, access, or implementation**. `CLM-009014` must remain out
of the supported outline until replaced or independently verified.

The remaining L1 ratings are appropriately limited to what a particular opened textual witness
contains; they are not ratings of historical implementation or semantic neutrality. The L2
ratings are appropriate for the opened specialist studies' own arguments/findings. `SRC-009010`
is properly L3-B as a translator-critical introduction rather than a substitute for the full
specialist literature.

## `unmāda`, `hṛdaya`, diagnosis, and modern wellness audit

The packet's most important negative controls survive:

- the checked Sanskrit clause contains `hṛdaya`, not a word for brain;
- the multi-faculty and multi-causal `unmāda` category is not coextensive with one modern
  diagnosis;
- Weiss and Kulamarva et al. document a modern act of diagnostic mapping, not an ancient identity;
- the 2023 review's limitations do not authorize efficacy, safety, or treatment advice;
- PYŚ and Caraka vocabulary does not create an unchanged commercial wellness lineage; and
- shared words across centuries must be treated as reception/history, not automatic continuity.

The required split of `EVD-009019`/`CLM-009018` is substantive record hygiene. “Modern authors
map X to psychosis” is a history-of-reception fact. “A 2023 review found the clinical evidence too
sparse and heterogeneous for a firm conclusion” is a current-review finding. They have different
sources, locators, fit ratings, risk, and future update cycles. Combining them makes a clinical
claim hard to update and obscures which evidence does which job.

No public or internal wording should turn “not a demonstrated identity” into the reverse
overclaim that no comparison is ever useful. The safe conclusion is category non-equivalence and
explicitly modern mapping, not a timeless diagnosis either way.

## Textual prescription versus practice

**PASS.** This boundary is one of the packet's strongest features. It consistently distinguishes:

- a professional text's classifications from sufferers' own categories;
- a prescribed action from a performed action;
- a performed action from prevalence or uniformity;
- stated therapeutic intent from consent, benefit, harm, or lived experience; and
- comfort and reassurance from a totalizing claim of compassionate care.

The packet names violent measures without spectacle or euphemism and does not use them to label
all ancient Indian medicine “torture.” It also refuses the opposite “holistic and compassionate”
totalization. The treatment locators should be sharpened so later writers can see precisely which
verses support comfort, coercion, ritual, and recovery propositions.

`REL-009024` correctly uses the late/selective manuscript archive to qualify social
implementation. No source in the packet supplies a denominator, access rate, outcome rate,
consent record, refusal record, patient satisfaction account, or direct observation of the
prescribed acts.

## Caste, gender, status, disability, and access audit

**PASS for caution; HOLD for the positive access claim.** The packet does not claim that only one
caste studied or received medicine. It describes the Siva.sh passage as a normative,
male-addressed ideal and refuses to convert it into uniform practice. It distinguishes medical
lineage/familiarity, Brahmin/physician ritual hierarchy, bodily/sensory ideals, politically or
socially disfavored categories, and rules involving husbands/guardians/male accompaniment.

However, those detailed propositions currently depend on the unnamed Siva.sh translation.
Because translation choices are central to words such as bodily “defect,” disability, social
disfavor, treatment exclusion, and guardianship, `CLM-009014` cannot be a supported outline node
yet. The source-language text may guide follow-up, but a named critical edition/translation and
specialist review must decide the exact wording.

The packet's larger archive conclusion remains sound without `CLM-009014`: the consulted core is
dominated by elite, monastic, philosophical, professional, and prescriptive voices. Pecchia's
documented preservation history shows courts, learned centers, libraries, physicians, patrons,
and copyists shaping what survives, but it cannot quantify ancient caste, gender, class,
disability, or geographic access.

importance of women's attributed renunciant verse without pretending mediation disappears.

ever existed.

**PASS.** Practitioner descriptions of grief, fear, anger, conduct, and disturbance are not
silently rewritten as patient self-description. Prescriber intent is not treated as benevolence,
and textual violence is not used to invent a victim's feelings.

**GAP.** No vernacular, legal, epigraphic, household, archaeological, or patient-authored archive

gender, status, disability, wealth, household position, or region. Those are genuine absences in
the research base and must remain visible in the outline.
