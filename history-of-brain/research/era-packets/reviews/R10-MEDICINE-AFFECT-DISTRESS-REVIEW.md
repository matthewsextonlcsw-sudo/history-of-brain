## Decision

Return the packet for bounded repair before Batch 03 outline assembly. The four-chain selection
is disciplined, the pivotal translated passages generally survive replay, the modern-diagnosis
and efficacy holds are strong, and the lived/archive audit is candid about coercion, hierarchy,
gender, status, and absent patient voice. The Badri/CBT and Niẓāmī mutation chains also show
useful dependence-aware work.

Four defects nevertheless affect retained reasoning rather than publication polish:

1. the Isḥāq first-book claim is too absolute: formal treatment is deferred, but the same opened
   book reports a lead-cap intervention at printed p. 106;
2. the Niẓāmī graph omits Browne's direct chronology contradiction: according to the note,
   Ibn Sīnā's own chronology places his arrival after Qābūs had already been deposed;
3. the reception audit has no accountable downstream “first music therapy” exemplar, and the
   “first psychotherapy” upstream chain is not fully replayed; and
4. the source levels, controlled source types, claim labels, and two `originates` relationships
   do not conform to the governing model.

The translated-text findings remain usable after those repairs. The return does not require a
fifth chain or a general survey of Arabic- and Persian-language medicine.

## Source level, fit, language, and edition audit

The packet distinguishes original author from modern conduit in prose, but then assigns a
“medieval work L1-A” score inside records where only a modern English/French translation was
actually consulted. Source level belongs to the consulted object and its claim job. The packet
opened no Arabic or Persian text and no diplomatic manuscript transcription for the four core
chains.

- `SRC-015000` is a full historical English translation and public-domain facsimile. It is
  L3-A for Arberry's English wording and B for bounded content pending the Arabic/critical
  edition. The unconsulted Arabic work cannot supply a second L1 rating inside this row.
- `SRC-015003` is a 26-page authorized extract from Badri's translation. It is L3-A for its
  displayed English and L4-A for Badri/IIIT reception framing; it is not L1 merely because the
  underlying work is medieval. The complete JSTOR translation is openly available, but was
  recorded only as landing-page/metadata access in `SRC-015004`.
- `SRC-015007` is the complete French first-book article, but it displays neither Arabic nor a
  critical apparatus, manuscript basis, or translation method. L3-A for the French wording and
  B/C for historical content is defensible; L1-A is not.
- `SRC-015009` is a modern critical translation of a later biographical compilation. L2-A for
  the edition's rendering and C for the reported events is appropriate. Its medieval compiler
  does not create an independently consulted L1 object.
- `SRC-015010` is a full 1921 English translation. It is L3-A for Browne's wording and notes,
  and C/D for historical event/practice/efficacy. No Persian critical text was opened, so the
  row cannot also claim L1-A.
- `SRC-015011–015012` are appropriately L2-A/B for specialist genre and textual context,
  and D for proving Niẓāmī's event.
- `SRC-015013` is correctly L4-A for a modern mutation and LX-D for the event, despite its
  journal venue.
- `SRC-015006` remains `LX-D` for body claims because only an abstract was available.

Three source types are outside the controlled vocabulary:

- change `SRC-015002` from `authoritative_reference` to `institutional_reference` with
  “specialist encyclopedia” as subtype;
- keep `SRC-015004` as `institutional_reference` and move “reception/book metadata” to subtype,
  rather than using “and reception” as a second type; and
- change `SRC-015013` from `secondary_synthesis` to `popular_secondary` with “medical-history
  vignette” as subtype.

These corrections do not demote the safe text-level claims. They make the consulted language
and source layer reproducible and prevent an unconsulted original from masquerading as an
opened L1 witness.

## Isḥāq treatment seam
