## Decision

Return the packet for bounded repair before outline assembly. Its two-chain core is unusually
good: the opened editions support the workshop complexity, the passage-local Arabic vocabulary,
the two *De anima* recensions, the omission at 413b25–27, and the stated manuscript and causal
uncertainties. The packet also correctly separates composition, translation, surviving
manuscript, modern edition, and later reception; avoids retrodiagnosis; identifies source
dependence; preserves the lack of patient voice; and lawfully handles the two cached books.

The return is required for four evidentiary reasons rather than stylistic polish:

1. the brief requires four myth **audits**, but the packet records only the propositions and
   chain-internal rebuttals. It does not identify dated downstream exemplars, preserve their
   exact wording, or trace where they obtained the claim;
2. `CLM-013213` and `REL-013323` generalize a demonstrated patron/reader purpose across both
   selected chains even though `GAP-013504` says the original date, patron, place, and direct
   context of Isḥāq's *De anima* remain incomplete;
3. the claim and evidence tables omit required structured fields, and one source uses a
   non-controlled source type; and
4. the relationship graph is complete by count but contains at least one wrong evidentiary
   verb: the presence of Arabic and English in an edition cannot **support** the researcher's
   failure to complete Greek alignment.

These defects are repairable without widening the two-chain corpus.

## Myth-audit assessment

The packet's historical corrections are directionally sound, but an audit asks a different
question from a rebuttal: who said the proposition, when, in what exact form, and on what
upstream authority?

|---|---|---:|

A bounded repair needs one accountable, dated exemplar for each distinct myth family, not an
internet-wide reception history. Search snippets may locate candidates but cannot become the
evidence. Open the full exemplar, capture its exact wording/version/date, trace any cited
authority upstream, rate it claim-relatively, and connect it to the disagreement with typed
relationships. If no accountable origin can be found, label the origin unknown and audit a
dated repetition rather than asserting where the myth began.

### Reception audit replay and exact remaining repairs

#### A. House of Wisdom composite

`SRC-013009` does say that al-Maʾmūn created the House of Wisdom and immediately describes it as
a center of learning (approximately 02:30–02:47). It then shifts subjects: “over the next
several hundred years these Abbasid caliphs” sponsor scholars and translation, with collection
in “libraries in cities like Baghdad” and finally “one place” (approximately 02:47–03:12).
`EVD-013118`, `CLM-013215`, and `DIS-013403` fuse these adjacent statements into the stronger
proposition that al-Maʾmūn created a House in which all of the multilingual translation and
collection occurred.

**Required repair:** describe this as a composite or adjacent 2017 framing, preserve the subject
shift, and replace “repeats this exact compressed proposition” with wording that does not turn
the researcher's synthesis into a quotation. `REL-013329` may remain **repeats** only after the
claim is normalized to what the timed source actually says.

#### B. Bridge-to-“West” fusion

The 04:31–04:38 segment specifically calls the movement of Hindu numerals into the Islamic
empire a bridge to bringing them “to the West.” The separate 08:50–09:05 segment calls the
Islamic world/its empires “a very powerful bridge of human knowledge,” names Indian, Chinese,
and ancient Greek knowledge, and places that bridge during Europe's “Dark Ages”; it does not
say in that segment that all such knowledge was carried to “the West.” `EVD-013119` records both
segments, but `CLM-013216` merges them and adds “primarily,” which neither segment says.

**Required repair:** remove “primarily” and either split the numeral-to-West statement from the
broader Dark-Ages bridge statement or explicitly label the combined slogan as the packet's
constructed audit proposition rather than the exemplar's exact wording. Carry the same
precision through `DIS-013404` and `REL-013331`.
