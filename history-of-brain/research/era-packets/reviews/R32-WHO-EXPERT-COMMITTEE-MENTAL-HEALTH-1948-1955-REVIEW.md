## Recommendations are not implementation or patient voice

The complete reports confirm the packet's wording ceilings:

- TRS 9 proposed program priorities, training and diagnostic/statistical work. It cannot establish that countries adopted the proposals or that the classifications were valid or globally accepted.
- TRS 31's definitions and applications are committee formulations shaped by period maternal, family, migration, gender, public-health, and professional assumptions. They are not present scientific consensus.
- TRS 73 remained hospital-centered while recommending outpatient, aftercare, rehabilitation and community links. Its former-patient-club passage is unattributed expert prose. It is not a former patient's account, evidence of consent, proof that clubs operated as described, or evidence of transferred authority or recovery.
- TRS 73's racialized and colonial-development comparisons are report rhetoric produced within unequal professional and imperial authority. They are not population facts, modern diagnostic categories, or evidence that J. C. Carothers authored a collective passage.
- TRS 98 recommended safeguards while also preserving strong medical authority and compulsory-treatment concepts. It is not a human-rights charter, and the absence of identified patient or legal-profession members remains material.

No relationship in the packet supports country implementation, service uptake, treatment effect, prevalence, coercion rate, national law, outcome, patient experience, or a global consensus. This is the correct ceiling.

## Membership, geography, profession, and voice

Independent roster replay confirms 32 session-seats: 17 European affiliations, 9 in the Americas, 4 in Asia, 1 in Africa, and 1 in Oceania. These are repeated seats across sessions and printed affiliations, not unique-person counts, citizenship, delegation, population weighting, or representative legitimacy.

The two seats cautiously identified as women from printed name/honorific—Elsa B. Nordlund and Mme J. Roudinesco—both occur in the second session. The packet does not convert this observation into self-identified gender data.

Medical, psychiatric, and health-administration authority dominates the printed member lists. Patients and former patients, families, nurses, social workers, aides, workers/labor, lawyers or judges, Indigenous authorities, and traditional healers are discussed, omitted, or assigned roles by professionals; none supplies direct testimony in the opened corpus. Consultants and secretariat staff are attendance/role evidence only. Affiliations do not make experts national delegates, and attendance does not establish passage authorship.

The lived-experience/archive file handles this absence appropriately: no composite affected-person story, imagined reaction, consent claim, resistance narrative, or professional-prose proxy is permitted. A future lived-experience question requires a separate local corpus.

## Rights replay

WHO's current open-access policy states that WHO publications issued since 12 November 2016 use its stated Creative Commons framework and that pre-2017 WHO publications will not be reissued under that licence. Its copyright page says materials not available under that licence remain permission-controlled, explicitly separates commercial use, and prohibits unauthorized logo use or endorsement implication.

The packet's link-first, permission-review posture for the 1949–1955 reports is therefore appropriate. Access through IRIS is not a reuse licence. No cover, table, scan, long excerpt, house translation, or emblem is cleared.

One source-level rights defect remains: PMC makes the Bertolote article free to read, but NCBI's OA service returns `idIsNotOpenAccess` for `PMC2408392`. `SRC-044014` therefore cannot be described as a “complete open article” with an unexplained `licensed` rights value.
