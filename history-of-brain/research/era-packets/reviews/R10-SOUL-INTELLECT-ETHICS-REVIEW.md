# R10-SOUL-INTELLECT-ETHICS — independent evidence review

**Packet reviewed:** `R10-SOUL-INTELLECT-ETHICS`

**Review date:** August 18, 2026

**Reviewer role:** independent second reader

**Review scope:** research controls only; this is not public prose

**Verdict:** **RETURN**

## Decision

Return this packet for bounded repair before outline assembly. The four-author design is
responsible and useful. The packet does not manufacture a unitary “Islamic psychology,” does
not turn philosophical or ethical prescription into psychotherapy, does not diagnose historical
people, does not count translators and specialist summaries as independent ancient witnesses,
and is unusually candid about its elite textual archive and missing lived voices. The al-Kindī,
al-Fārābī, Ibn Sīnā, and al-Ghazālī selections also remain within the brief's maximum of four
named works/case chains.

The return is required because several pivotal records do not yet meet the packet's own burden
for a `quotation_or_position` claim:

1. the al-Fārābī anthology excerpt directly contains the heart/body/city hierarchy but not the
   brain-as-secondary-ruler proposition; the latter is present only in López-Farjeat's specialist
   synthesis citing unconsulted Walzer pages;
2. the packet claims passage-specific control of *rūḥ* and *qalb* while its evidence record says
   those terms were not passage-specifically sampled, leaving an explicit brief requirement
   incomplete;
3. the Ibn Sīnā course packet misspells Jon McGinnis as “John,” suppresses the actual source
   title and page sequence, and is rated as though the upstream Hackett translation itself were
   in hand;
4. several “Documented” textual-position claims are established only through specialist
   paraphrase plus an Arabic scan that the researcher did not translate; and
5. the first-psychologist and psychotherapy priority quarantines are sensible, but their
   relationship graph does not attach the packet's actual bounded search audit to those claims.

These defects are repairable without adding another author or writing public prose.

### Arabic vocabulary and disciplinary boundaries

The packet handles *nafs* and *ʿaql* cautiously and correctly resists one-to-one mapping to
modern “mind,” “soul,” or “reason.” Rahman's translation provides passage-local internal-sense
labels, and López-Farjeat provides passage-mapped imagination vocabulary. Those sources also
support the packet's distinction between philosophical anthropology, political analogy,
natural philosophy, theology/prophecy, and ethical formation.

The packet does **not** yet meet the brief's passage-specific requirement for *rūḥ*. It also
names *qalb* in `CLM-014017` without a retained passage-local comparison. A general truth that
Arabic terms change by work and genre cannot stand in for the requested evidence. Either add
exact passages from the four selected corpora with edition, translator, locator, and contextual
meaning, or explicitly remove those terms from the retained supported claim and mark the brief
requirement unresolved. Because *rūḥ* is expressly mandatory in `BRIEF.md`, omission is not a
minor publication polish item.

No modern psychological, neurological, or clinical equivalence is licensed. “Internal
senses,” “imagination,” “estimation,” “heart,” “brain,” “spirit,” “habituation,” and “soul
discipline” must stay attached to the author, work, genre, edition, and translator.

## Geography, community, genre, and chronology

The anti-collapse rule is strong, but two labels need correction:

- Current SEP says reliable biographical information about al-Fārābī is scarce and that he was
  **probably** born around 870 in Farab or Farayb. Change “born in Farab in Central Asia” to a
  qualified formulation and separate probable birthplace from the well-supported Baghdad and
  Syrian career locations. Do not infer ethnicity from birthplace, later national borders, or
  an anthology titled “Philosophy in Persia.”
- “Ninth-century Baghdad/Iraq” is suitable for al-Kindī's intellectual/career context but should
  not silently function as a sourced birthplace or population identity.

The Ibn Sīnā and al-Ghazālī scope labels are broadly responsible: Ibn Sīnā's biography supports
Afshana/Bukhara and later movement through Khwarazm, Jurjan, Ray, Hamadan, and Isfahan; Griffel
supports al-Ghazālī's Tus/Nishapur/Baghdad/Damascus/Jerusalem career and his Ashʿarite,
juristic, philosophical, ethical, and Sufi engagements. The packet should name whichever of
those contexts a claim actually uses rather than make “eastern Iranian/Central Asian courts” or
“Tus/Khurasan” do all contextual work.

The genre distinctions pass. Political-philosophical model, natural-philosophical soul theory,
thought experiment, prophecy argument, theological appropriation, and ethical prescription are
not treated as clinical encounters or observed outcomes.

The work/edition/translation chronology needs a compact normalized repair. At minimum, record
separately for each pivotal work: approximate composition; known witness or transmission limit;
consulted Arabic edition; named modern translation; derivative excerpt or anthology; and
access/publication date. The al-Fārābī chain should read ancient work → Walzer critical
edition/translation (1985) → Nasr/Aminrazavi anthology reprint (2008 publisher record; a 2007

pp. 175ff. → unidentified Williams course extraction. Al-Kindī's single-manuscript dependence
and al-Ghazālī's missing modern critical translation must remain explicit.
