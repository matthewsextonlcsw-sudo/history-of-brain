# R14-EAST-AFRICA-SWAHILI-COAST — Protection, Healing, Adversity, and Archive Position

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `026000–026999`

## Research question

What can three different archives—excavated material at Unguja Ukuu, reconstructed
oral/linguistic/settlement history in the Mombasa hinterland, and Pate/Lamu Ajami epic
transmission—establish about protection, healing knowledge, household/community care, and
represented responses to adversity before 1800?

## Frozen settings

1. Unguja Ukuu, Zanzibar, late eighth–ninth century: one excavated Arabic-inscribed rock-crystal
   seal/amulet and its archaeological/object/custody chain.
2. Mombasa's Mijikenda-speaking hinterland, with strongest claims bounded to the sixteenth–
   seventeenth centuries and later oral, linguistic, ethnographic, and documentary reconstruction
   visibly dated.
3. Pate/Lamu Ajami manuscript culture through the *Utendi wa Tambuka* witness and edition chain,
   traditionally dated 1728, as literary/archive history rather than patient evidence.

## Required evidence and tests

- obtain the full Unguja object study and repository/custody record before substantive use;
- distinguish material inscription, religious affiliation, proposed protection, wearer/use,
  illness, treatment, and outcome; most remain Unknown;
- preserve Mijikenda-speaking community plurality, later ethnonym history, *uganga* semantic
  breadth, healer/rainmaker/protective-knowledge claims, forest/sacred-space authority,
  household reproduction, gender/status, fees, dispute mechanisms, and the later-source seam;
- name kaya custodians and access rules; do not disclose recipes, ritual techniques, sensitive
  sites, or restricted knowledge;
- distinguish Pate composition/commissioning tradition, Northern Swahili/Kiamu, Arabic-script
  transmission, later copies, editions, collectors, poets, scribes, translators, and variant
  stanza counts; and
- audit amulet-equals-treatment, spirit-possession-equals-mental-illness, timeless ethnic or
  religious systems, first-object/first-literature, and unchanged-tradition claims.

## Exclusions

Do not use post-1800 ethnography as direct earlier evidence. Exclude Mwana Kupona, uncertain
pre-1800 works, ancient-DNA/human-remains work, broad slavery institutions, “the Mijikenda,”
“the Swahili,” or one blended Islam/local system. European repositories are access points, not
proxies for Pate/Lamu or kaya community authority.

## Stop

The likely result is a bounded history of evidence and archive limits, not regional “mental
health.” Unguja and Pate remain HOLD unless their full-source, object/manuscript, language,
custody, and rights gates pass. Create five standard outputs and rights-safe ignored cache; no
public prose.
