# R14-WEST-AFRICA-SAHEL — Manuscripts, Households, Courts, Healing, and Archive Power

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `023000–023999`

## Research question

What can a bounded set of West African and Sahelian manuscripts, archaeology, inscriptions,
travel or court accounts, material evidence, and responsibly handled later-recorded traditions
from approximately 800 to 1600 establish about personhood, emotion, distress, disability,
healing, family/community care, spiritual practice, law, and political power?

## Boundary

Choose no more than three named polities, towns, language communities, manuscript corpora, or
archaeological settings connected by a defensible question. Arabic external accounts, local
Arabic/Ajami manuscripts, archaeology, material practice, and later-recorded oral traditions
remain different archives. This packet represents neither “African psychology” nor all West
Africa or the Sahel.

## Required evidence

- opened manuscripts/editions/translations, inscriptions, objects, archaeological reports,
  archival records, or historically bounded accounts with exact locators;
- African and regionally grounded historians, archaeologists, philologists, manuscript
  custodians, oral historians, disability/gender/social historians, and relevant communities;
- source-position audit: local/external author, language, patron, recorder, collector,
  translator, archive/custodian, and date;
- later-recorded oral tradition dated to performance/recording with transmission and consent
  controls; no verbatim medieval substitution;
- household, healer, scholar, court, enslaved/dependent, gender, age, class/status, migration,
  disability, cost, coercion, consent, outcome, and missing-voice analysis;
- named Islamicate, trans-Saharan, or local exchange evidence without conversion-erases-locality
  or isolated-tradition myths;
- colonial theft/collection, manuscript dispersal, conflict, digitization, sacred/restricted
  knowledge, current custody, image, and community protocol audit; and
- audits of demonology-only, no mental-health history before colonialism, universal communal
  care, and timeless “African spirituality” claims.

## Stop

Do not use North/West Africa interchangeably, make Timbuktu the entire region, or fill missing
medieval evidence with colonial ethnography. Create five outputs and rights-safe ignored cache;
no public prose.
