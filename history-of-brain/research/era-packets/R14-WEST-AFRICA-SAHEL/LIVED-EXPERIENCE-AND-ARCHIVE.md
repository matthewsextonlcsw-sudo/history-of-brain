# R14 West Africa / Sahel — Lived Experience and Archive Audit

**Status:** `PASS WITH GAPS` after independent closure; internal reconciliation only; no public prose

## Bottom line

The packet does **not** contain a direct local affected-person archive that independently evaluates care, coercion or outcomes. It contains a first-person external traveler's illness report; elite male biographies and family/political recollections; funerary names/titles; excavated material without personal voices; and a named twentieth-century oral performer speaking through a recorder/translator/publication chain. This is enough to recover bounded human situations, but not enough to invent patient satisfaction, interior states or representative care systems.

## Required population audit

### Households and kin

- The traveler group and an Egyptian helper form a mobile care network, not a documented household (EVD-023100).
- Ill-relative visiting and al-Saʿdi's father's bedside companionship are kin/status examples selected for praise (EVD-023105–106).
- Aisha al-Fulani appears through descendant genealogy; this preserves a name while subordinating her voice to male family history (EVD-023109).

**Gap:** no household account book, caregiver diary, recipe with use context, recipient letter or direct family testimony was opened.

### Healers and treatment actors

- The Egyptian helper is identified by origin but not profession, training or reasoning. Do not promote him to physician or assign the preparation to “Malian medicine” (EVD-023100).
- Saint-associated wood occurs in a cure narrative, but no named practitioner, selection rule, dose, safety process or independent outcome survives (EVD-023108).
- The 1684 *Shifa al-Asqam* is a medicine/magic manuscript lead for the next chronological packet, not R14 practice evidence (EVD-023123).

**Gap:** no opened 800–1600 local medical manuscript, healer casebook, pharmacological recipe with observed use, or recipient outcome series.

### Scholars, courts and status

- Most chronicle subjects are scholars, rulers, judges or their kin. Positive care examples are inseparable from this archive's elite selection (EVD-023105–106).
- The qadi advice explicitly recognizes that elite access could harm socially weaker people, but the survival of advice is not evidence that institutions corrected the problem (EVD-023110).
- Royal commemoration, court performance, detention and witness scenes expose status, dependence and coercion alongside hospitality (EVD-023101–103); the commemoration was for Abu al-Hasan, not the unnamed travel companion.

### Enslavement, dependency and custody

- Enslaved/dependent attendants appear in Ibn Battuta but rarely as named speakers (EVD-023102).
- The restrained woman in the Kasa narrative cannot be treated as a freely testifying subject (EVD-023103).
- Aisha al-Fulani's genealogy is anchored in capture and distribution of women (EVD-023109).
- The chronicle also praises freeing enslaved people in a saintly biography (EVD-023107). This does not cancel the archive's violence or establish a universal norm.

**Publication control:** never use these traces as atmosphere, shock hooks or evidence of patient satisfaction. Preserve status, restraint, mediation and absent speech in the same sentence-level use.

### Gender

- Women are disproportionately preserved as enslaved attendants, captive ancestors, court figures or funerary title holders rather than authors.
- Gao-Saney `al-Malika` inscriptions create a genuine counterweight to a male-only political record, but office-in-own-right remains an interpretation, not settled proof (EVD-023113–114).
- Do not convert several royal/funerary women into a claim about women's general authority, care work or equality.

### Age

- Boys and students/dependents appear, but ages and childhood perspectives are absent (EVD-023102, EVD-023105).
- Aisha is described in old age, but aging experience and support are not preserved (EVD-023109).

### Disability and distress/difference

- Aisha's blindness is a descendant's descriptor, not a diagnosis or first-person disability account (EVD-023109).
- The chronicle's French “mad” layer is lexically, hagiographically and textually unstable: Houdas says the preceding sentence portion is absent from A, B and printed Arabic and that G arrived after printing; Arabic/modern-edition replay remains required (EVD-023107; DIS-023400).
- Bereavement behavior is recordable; retrospective psychiatric diagnosis is not (EVD-023104, EVD-023111).

### Migration and exchange

- Ibn Battuta and the Egyptian helper were mobile outsiders in Mali (EVD-023100).
- Imported Almerian marble and locally made stelae show exchange and local production together (EVD-023113).
- Gao-Saney artifacts support material connections, but not a single route of ideas about mind or healing (EVD-023118).

## Archive-position audit

### Local versus external

| Layer | Locality and strength | Mediation that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Ibn Battuta | visited Mali; strong for what he reports seeing/experiencing | external elite traveler, later redaction, court access, moral judgments, French colonial-era translation |
| Gao-Saney inscriptions | created and placed locally; direct dated material/textual record | formulaic funerary genre, damaged/lost/displaced stones, uncertain readings, modern collection and editing |
| Gao-Saney archaeology | excavated locally by a team including Malian archaeologist Mamadou Cissé | looting, small sample, functional inference, external publication system |
| *Tarikh al-Sudan* | locally authored in Timbuktu by a scholar/administrator | later than many events; political/hagiographic genre; manuscript-copy history; Houdas's colonial acquisition/editing/translation |
| Malio performance | named Songhay narrator and accompanist, recorded at Saga | 1980–81 date, external recorder/translator/editor, web excerpting, permission scope unresolved |

### Manuscript and object custody

- Houdas reports colonial officers/scholars sending or commissioning chronicle copies; exact present manuscript shelfmarks and legal histories remain to be replayed (EVD-023112).
- Farias documents fragmented, transferred and lost Gao-Saney stones (EVD-023116).
- Ngom documents manuscript confiscation and the incompleteness of cataloguing outside owner communities (EVD-023119–120).
- UCT identifies both public and private/family Timbuktu libraries, but its undated page is not current custodian confirmation.
- The *Shifa al-Asqam* dossier associates the author/work with 1096 AH/1684 but dates the nominated non-autograph witness only broadly to the seventeenth century; it identifies the Mamma Haïdara Library/SAVAMA as owner/custodian at nomination time, while current custody and use require direct readback (EVD-023123).

### Translation

- The packet consulted French, not independently collated Arabic, for Ibn Battuta and al-Saʿdi. Public quotation and claims turning on categories such as grief, “spirit,” “mad,” “weak” or “unfortunate” require Arabic and a current named specialist translation.
- Personal and place names vary across transliteration systems. Do not silently normalize identity-sensitive readings.
- The Malio performance passes through Songhay-to-English translation/editorial selection; translation is part of the evidence, not invisible plumbing.

### Sacred, restricted and community-controlled knowledge

- No sacred/restricted item was cached.
- Funeral inscriptions and tomb imagery require dignity, site/custodian and image-rights review even where text is published.
- The Malio audio/transcription remains link-only pending source-specific current authority.
- Jansen's Kela case demonstrates why one-time permission and copyright do not exhaust relationship-based accountability, but it cannot dictate a universal protocol for Songhay communities (EVD-023122).

## Empathy controls

1. Describe what a source records before interpreting it.
2. Name coercion, capture, restraint and archive inequality plainly without spectacle.
3. Do not invent whether a medieval person was “thrilled,” traumatized, comforted or cured.
4. When a person's words are absent, say who speaks for them and why the archive may favor that speaker.
5. Positive care examples and violent/status-conditioned examples coexist; neither cancels the other.
6. Religious/hagiographic interpretation deserves contextual explanation, not ridicule or automatic clinical validation.

## Lived-voice publication gate

`CLM-023200–226` may support internal outlining only. Before public drafting, affected-person claims require:

- Arabic lexical/edition replay for all category-sensitive passages;
- an African/regionally grounded social historian and, for funerary/oral material, relevant heritage/community authority;
- direct custodian/rights readback for images, manuscripts and performance media;
- trauma, enslavement, disability and sacred-material review;
- explicit wording that reported outcomes are not measured outcomes; and
- preservation of the archive silence in CLM-023226 rather than narrative fill.
