## Bounded corpus and question

This packet compares three connected archives rather than claiming a single West African view:

1. **Fourteenth-century Mali**, as represented in the external Arabic travel account of Ibn Battuta.
2. **Gao and Gao-Saney**, through locally situated Arabic funerary inscriptions and excavated material contexts.
3. **Timbuktu / Songhay**, through al-Saʿdi's locally produced seventeenth-century Arabic chronicle about fifteenth- and sixteenth-century people and events.

The shared question is: **What do these archives record about embodied illness, grief, dependency and status, scholarly or household response, and political power—and what do they leave unknowable about interior distress, care, consent, or outcomes?** A Songhay performance recorded at Saga, Niger, in 1980–81 is retained only as a later oral archive and protocol case, not as a fourth medieval polity or a verbatim medieval witness.
