## Cross-archive findings

### What the opened evidence can carry internally

- Specific reports of acute illness, a helper's preparation, prolonged illness and one traveler's death, followed separately by Mansa Sulayman's commemoration of deceased Marinid sultan Abu al-Hasan in Ibn Battuta's Mali narrative.
- Chronicle representations of bereavement, visiting the ill, bedside companionship, reported abnormal behavior, epidemic/cure claims, status tensions and the violent genealogical trace of an enslaved and later blind woman.
- Dated Gao-Saney funerary inscriptions, including women titled `al-Malika`, alongside object loss, transfer and reading uncertainty.
- Excavated settlement and exchange material at Gao-Saney predating the dated royal epitaph series.
- A strict difference between the date of a later performance and the time its narrative depicts.

### What it cannot carry

- A modern diagnosis for any represented person.
- Treatment efficacy, prevalence, universal access, institutional care or a region-wide care system.
- The proposition that spiritual language was the only explanatory or care register.
- The proposition that community care was always humane, voluntary, egalitarian or available.
- A general West African or “African” psychology.
- Medieval Ajami content from the selected settings without an opened, dated item.

## Exchange-route anchors

1. The 1108 Gao-Saney epitaph uses imported Almerian marble, while other stelae are locally made (EVD-023113): material connection and local production coexist.
2. Gao-Saney's excavated glass, metals, lamps, crucibles and beads are material evidence that the authors interpret through wider exchange, but they do not specify belief or therapy (EVD-023118).
3. Ibn Battuta's episode includes a mobile traveler network and an Egyptian helper in Mali (EVD-023100), but the preparation cannot be assigned a national medical tradition.
4. Timbuktu chronicles and manuscript collections belong to Arabic learned networks while remaining locally produced archives; Islamicate form does not erase locality (EVD-023112, EVD-023119).
