## Proposed internal outline nodes

These are research-node titles, not headlines or drafted copy.

1. **Three archives, three clocks**
   - Gao-Saney five-sample radiocarbon result (early eighth–mid-tenth centuries) and separately extended late-tenth interpretation
   - funerary inscriptions (eleventh–twelfth centuries in selected examples)
   - Ibn Battuta's fourteenth-century travel report
   - fifteenth-/sixteenth-century people represented in a seventeenth-century Timbuktu chronicle
   - a Songhay performance recorded in 1980–81
   - Control: CLM-023215, CLM-023219, CLM-023224

2. **A traveler becomes ill: body, mobility and an uncertain preparation**
   - Acute group illness, death, helper, preparation, vomiting, two-month illness
   - Keep preparation, causal attribution and outcome separate
   - Control: CLM-023200

3. **A royal commemoration: gathering, recitation and what the narrator cannot tell us**
   - After the separate traveler-illness/death episode, Mansa Sulayman reportedly held a banquet for the deceased Marinid sultan Abu al-Hasan
   - Quran recitation and prayers for Abu al-Hasan and for the living Mansa Sulayman
   - Observable acts versus interior grief and prevalence
   - Control: CLM-023201

4. **Visiting, keeping company and supporting dependents—selected examples, unequal archives**
   - Visiting ill relatives; ruler's nighttime companionship; orphans/students
   - Status-conditioned selection; norms versus implementation
   - Control: CLM-023205, CLM-023206, CLM-023210, CLM-023222

5. **Grief in elite biography and political narrative**
   - Mahmoud's withdrawal/sighing and Amar's tears/despair
   - Hagiographic and court-political narrative forms
   - Control: CLM-023204, CLM-023211

6. **When a chronicle says observers thought someone was mad**
   - Public node remains blocked until Arabic term/manuscript/translation replay
   - Explain category and narrative frame; no retrospective diagnosis
   - Control: CLM-023207 HOLD

7. **Care, cure claims and evidence burdens**
   - Saint-associated material in epidemic narrative
   - Chronicle claim versus efficacy, denominator, consent and safety
   - Control: CLM-023208, CLM-023221

8. **Aisha al-Fulani: a name preserved through captivity and descendant genealogy**
   - Enslavement, gender, blindness and absent first-person voice
   - Requires trauma, disability and descendant/community review
   - Control: CLM-023209, CLM-023226

9. **Women titled al-Malika in the stones**
   - Dated epitaphs and locally situated material record
   - Separate observed title from plausible office-in-own-right interpretation
   - Control: CLM-023213, CLM-023214

10. **What looting, loss and colonial collection do to the story**
    - Gao-Saney clandestine pits; fragmented/lost stelae; colonial manuscript-copy routes; uncatalogued/private collections
    - Control: CLM-023212, CLM-023216, CLM-023217, CLM-023218

11. **Later oral performance is an archive, not a time machine**
    - Date performance, recorder, translator and web mediation
    - Cultural authority is source-specific and continuing
    - Control: CLM-023219, CLM-023220

12. **Four myths the evidence cannot sustain**
    - demonology-only
    - universal communal care
    - no internally produced precolonial history
    - timeless unitary “African spirituality”
    - Control: CLM-023221–224

## Proposed transitions

- **Material settlement → inscribed persons:** Gao-Saney's earlier occupation/material record provides context but does not identify the later epitaph subjects. Say explicitly that chronology and identity are not interchangeable (EVD-023118 → EVD-023113).
- **Stone → chronicle:** move from direct but formulaic local material/text to a later local narrative rich in human action but politically and hagiographically shaped (EVD-023113 → EVD-023115 → EVD-023104–111).
- **Chronicle → external traveler:** compare source positions, not “belief systems”: the local chronicle is later and genre-shaped; the traveler is nearer his own visit but external and court-bound (EVD-023112, EVD-023100).
- **Positive care → inequality:** place visits/support beside qadi access concerns, enslavement and restraint so neither romantic nor demonizing account dominates (EVD-023105–106 → EVD-023110 → EVD-023102–103).
- **Historical archive → later performance:** state the recording date before any narrative content, then explain why transmission/authority review is required (EVD-023121–122).
- **Packet → early modern:** hand forward the *Shifa al-Asqam* dossier's 1096 AH/1684 author/work association and its separately, broadly seventeenth-century non-autograph witness rather than expanding the cutoff (EVD-023123).

## What should not be outlined as established

- Any modern psychiatric diagnosis.
- “African psychology,” “the African mind,” or a West Africa-wide system.
- A first hospital, first therapist, first social worker or first mental-health treatment.
- Prevalence of grief practices, illness categories, disability or care.
- Clinical efficacy or safety of any preparation/material/ritual.
- Universal household/community access.
- Unbroken continuity between a medieval source and a living practice.
- Current object/manuscript custody or public reuse permission without direct 2026 confirmation.
