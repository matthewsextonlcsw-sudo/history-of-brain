# R04-IRAN — Lived experience and archive

**Purpose:** identify whose distress, care, labor, coping, and control can be responsibly seen—and
where empathetic narration must stop.

## Archive map: whose words survive?

| archive stream | surviving form | proximity to experience | institutional filter | safe use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Avestan liturgy and prescription | orally performed religious texts preserved through Sasanian and later written/redactional history, with medieval manuscripts and modern editions | May preserve old ritual language and normative classifications; not a personal account | Priestly performance, memorization, redaction, manuscript copying, Pahlavi glossing, community preservation, and modern translation (`EVD-007000–EVD-007007`) | Attribute every statement to text, stanza/fargard, edition, and transmission layer. Do not convert ritual language into ordinary belief or diagnosis. |
| Achaemenid royal inscription | monumental first-person royal narrative | Direct access to the king's public claim, not subjects' thoughts | Royal authorship, multilingual monument, legitimating genre, modern edition (`EVD-007008–EVD-007010`) | Say “Darius's inscription frames.” Do not infer consent, internalization, or population psychology. |
| Persepolis administrative tablet | ration/allocation entry | Close to an institutional transaction; distant from motive, negotiation, feeling, and outcome | Scribe/administrator classifies people, commodity, quantity, place, and date (`EVD-007011`) | Describe the allocation and unequal amounts. Do not call it care, reward, discrimination motive, or maternal testimony without more evidence. |
| Kartīr inscriptions | public elite priestly self-account | Close to one powerful actor's desired image; distant from suppressed communities' experiences | Monumental self-promotion, variant copies, damaged/contested words, modern editions (`EVD-007013–EVD-007015`) | Preserve both hostile/coercive claim and lexical uncertainty. Seek Jewish, Christian, Manichaean, Buddhist, and other counterarchives. |
| Aramaic incantation bowls | inscribed household/ritual objects naming clients or beneficiaries | Names can be unusually close to an individual care/protection request, but symptoms and voice may be absent | Scribe/practitioner, formula, archaeological loss/private collecting, museum catalog, disputed editions (`EVD-007016–EVD-007017`) | Name person, object, language, date/place, and cataloged purpose separately. Do not infer diagnosis, fear, belief, efficacy, or consent. |
| Gondēšāpur narrative history | later chronicles, specialist synthesis, limited archaeology | Very distant from individual treatment experience | Later institutional reputation, retrospective translation narratives, sparse excavation (`EVD-007018`) | Use only for institutional chronology/a source myth audit. No “patient at the first hospital” scene is available. |

No opened core source is a diary, letter, complaint, testimony, or interview authored by a person
seeking help. The absence is an archive condition, not evidence that sufferers lacked speech,
agency, family support, ambivalence, refusal, or their own explanations.

## Care, coping, and control visible—carefully

- **Spoken/sacred-word healing:** *Vendīdād* 7 ranks sacred-word healing with plant and knife
  practices (`EVD-007006`). This documents a textual hierarchy of healing modes, not mutually
  exclusive “religion versus medicine” systems or measured efficacy.
- **Practitioner qualification and unequal risk:** Vd 7.36–40 places confessional outsiders into a
  dangerous proof-of-competence prescription (`EVD-007005`). It is appropriate to name the
  devaluation and possible harm while explicitly separating prescription from documented events.
- **Material provision after birth:** PF 1224 documents grain allocation after birth, showing that
  a state work/administrative system classified reproductive events (`EVD-007011`). Whether women
  understood it as care, entitlement, surveillance, reward, or coercion is unavailable.
- **Household protection/healing:** holder catalogs place named individuals beside Aramaic written
  ritual objects (`EVD-007016–EVD-007017`). For Aphriduh, the healing purpose is the catalog's
  description because the cited Geller translation was not opened. The objects may index
  family/practitioner action, but commissioning, price, performance, and outcome are unavailable.
- **Afterlife and behavioral regulation:** Kartīr publicly links bodily and postmortem consequences
  with conduct and institutional loyalty (`EVD-007013`). That is evidence of moral governance and
  self-fashioning, not a clinical treatment model.
- **Religious suppression:** Kartīr's self-report makes coercive boundary-making visible even though
  the verb and scale are disputed (`EVD-007014–EVD-007015`). Avoid both sensationalism and erasure.

## Power, selection, and archive loss

1. **Priestly transmission:** ritual specialists and later manuscript communities preserved the
   Avesta, while redaction and copying also shaped it. “Late manuscript” must not become a reason to
   discount living Zoroastrian community knowledge, nor a license to project later commentary
   backward without argument.
2. **Imperial voice:** Behistun makes rebellion legible through the king's Truth/Lie opposition.
   Rebels, local communities, and punished people do not answer in the monument.
3. **Administrative visibility:** the Persepolis archive can count women, workers, commodities, and
   births while excluding pain, negotiation, refusal, caregiving, and the reasons behind policy.
4. **Monumental survival:** Kartīr could place his desired history in stone. The religions he named
   survive through different languages and archives, which this packet has not yet sampled evenly.
5. **Object collecting:** bowls without secure excavation can preserve names while losing household,
   neighborhood, ritual, and depositional context. Museum acquisition history must stay visible.
6. **Modern custody:** Fortification tablets and Avestan manuscripts became accessible through
   excavation, loans, private holdings, institutional permissions/refusals, digitization, and
   community collaboration (`EVD-007012`, `EVD-007019`). Research access is not neutral.
7. **Translation:** “mind,” “spirit,” “lie,” “demon,” “holy word,” “postpartum,” and “struck down”
   are analytical pressure points. Public close quotation requires source-language review.

## Missing or weakly represented voices

- Named sufferers explaining symptoms, hopes, fear, pain, disagreement, failure, recovery, or how
  they evaluated a healer.
- The women in PF 1224, their infants, partners, birth attendants, caregivers, and co-workers.
- People assigned the dangerous outsider role in *Vendīdād* prescription; dissenting Zoroastrians;
  practitioners described by competing authorities.
- Jewish, Christian, Manichaean, Buddhist, Hindu, and other communities in their own words in
  response to Kartīr's claims.
- Eastern Iranian, Central Asian, rural, pastoral, low-income, enslaved, disabled, and children's
  experiences across the packet's very large chronological/geographical span.
- Household caregivers, midwives, plant gatherers, ritual scribes, nurses/assistants, and people who
  sought no institutional or textualized care.
- Unsuccessful rituals/treatments, side effects, coercion, complaint, cost, exclusion, and return of
  symptoms.
- Archaeologically secure incantation-bowl contexts and community-accountable interpretations of
  their use.

## Empathetic public-language guardrails

- Start with what happened to a documented person or group, not exotic spectacle: “A tablet records
  unequal grain allocations after births,” not “Persia had a bizarre maternity program.”
- Name the narrator and institution: “the *Vendīdād* prescribes,” “Darius's inscription claims,”
  “Kartīr represents himself,” “the museum catalogs,” or “the tablet records.”
- Do not call people “superstitious,” “crazy,” “possessed patients,” or “primitive.” Sacred and
  bodily practices can coexist without fitting a modern religion/science split.
- When harm is in the source, do not euphemize it. State discriminatory testing, unequal allocation,
  and suppression while separating written prescription/self-report from verified implementation.
- Do not invent satisfaction or outrage. A postpartum worker may not have been “thrilled”; the
  archive gives no feeling at all. Empathy here means protecting the missing viewpoint.
- Treat Zoroastrian, Jewish, Christian, Manichaean, Buddhist, and other traditions as living or
  historically diverse communities, not props in a triumphal progress narrative.
- No retrospective psychiatric diagnosis from demon, vision, dream, sacred-word, or afterlife
  language (`CLM-007014`).

## Archive verdict for outline use

The packet can support an outline section on **transmission, normative healing, imperial truth,
administrative provision, priestly power, and named but mediated care**. It cannot support “what
Persian patients thought,” a single ancient-Iranian model of mind, or a progress story from magic to
medicine. PF 1224 is the strongest scene-level lived-experience anchor. The Aphriduh bowl is a
named-beneficiary, object/catalog-mediated care trace—not a patient voice—and must remain visibly
dependent on the unopened inscription edition.
