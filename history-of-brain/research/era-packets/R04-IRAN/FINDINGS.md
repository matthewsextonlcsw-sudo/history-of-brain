# R04-IRAN — Findings and evidence extracts

**Purpose:** internal research synthesis for outline and seam review; not article prose
**Boundary:** selected Iranian-language, imperial, and Sasanian-connected archives, approximately
1200 BCE–700 CE. Later Book Pahlavi literature and Abbasid institutions are handoffs, not direct
evidence for this period.

## Packet answer in one research proposition

The checked archive does not contain a single “ancient Persian psychology.” It contains differently
dated and differently transmitted materials: orally performed Avestan liturgies preserved in late
manuscripts; Achaemenid imperial inscriptions and administrative tablets; third-century Middle
Persian priestly inscriptions; and Aramaic ritual objects from Sasanian southern Iraq. These sources
show arguments about attention, decision, truth, speech, deed, bodily and sacred-word healing,
postpartum provision, afterlife, ritual protection, and coercive boundary-making. Almost all are
elite, prescriptive, administrative, or object-mediated. None licenses a modern diagnosis or a
general account of what distressed people in “Iran” believed or felt.

That proposition is **Strongly supported for the opened corpora**, not globally exhaustive. It also
requires three explicit negative findings: the extant manuscript is not the text's original date;
an imperial/prescriptive statement is not ordinary practice; and the opened evidence does not
establish a pre-700 Gondēšāpur teaching hospital.

## Chronological research transitions for the later outline

These are internal transitions, not public headings.

1. **Begin with the archive, not a civilization-wide mind (approximately 1200 BCE–700 CE).**
   Introduce unequal textual layers and regions before ideas. Separate Old/Young Avestan
   composition, Sasanian fixation, late manuscripts, modern editions, and ritual performance
   (`EVD-007000–EVD-007002`).
2. **Attention, choosing, thought, speech, and deed in one Old Avestan stanza.** Use Yasna 30.2–3
   as a close-reading scene and display the translation problem beside it
   (`EVD-007003–EVD-007004`). Do not label it the invention of free will or psychology.
3. **Healing, hierarchy, and confessional boundaries in a composite prescriptive text.** The
   *Vendīdād* offers a powerful section on word, plant, and knife, but also on practitioner testing
   and unequal status (`EVD-007005–EVD-007007`). Keep law/prescription distinct from practice.
4. **Imperial truth as governance, not an interiority survey.** Behistun shows a king attaching the
   Lie to revolt and legitimating punishment (`EVD-007008–EVD-007010`). This is a transition from
   ethical/religious vocabulary to imperial political use, not evidence of one Persian mindset.
5. **Provision through an administrative keyhole.** PF 1224 makes postpartum women visible only
   as recipients classified by origin, work, and infant sex (`EVD-007011`). It can anchor care,
   labor, and inequality without inventing patient satisfaction.
6. **Third-century priestly self-fashioning and pluralism under pressure.** Kartīr's inscriptions
   connect conduct, body, soul, and afterlife while claiming institutional expansion and attacks on
   other religions (`EVD-007013–EVD-007015`). Preserve the lexical dispute over violence.
7. **Sasanian-connected Mesopotamia as a multilingual border, not “Persia proper.”** Aramaic bowls
   name individual beneficiaries/clients amid religious and linguistic mixture
   (`EVD-007016–EVD-007017`). Geography and contested editions must remain visible.
8. **End with a two-sided myth audit and archive handoff.** Richter-Bernburg's chronology and
   Nayernouri's contrary argument agree that the explicit school/hospital record is eighth-century
   while disagreeing over what earlier circumstantial evidence permits (`EVD-007018`,
   `EVD-007020`). Modern “first teaching hospital” repetitions are a separate reception history
   (`EVD-007021`, `EVD-007024`). Later institutional history belongs to Islamicate packets;
   modern manuscript digitization belongs to archive history (`EVD-007019`).

## Competing interpretations to preserve

### `DIS-007000` — When and where do Avestan texts belong?

- Texts preserve old linguistic strata but survive through Sasanian and post-Sasanian editorial,
  oral, scribal, and community transmission.
- Precise dates, locations, and an Achaemenid codex remain uncertain; the thirteenth-century
  manuscript date is not the composition date.
- **Outline rule:** show composition layer, redaction, manuscript witness, translation, and present
  holder as separate fields.

### `DIS-007001` — Is Yasna 30 a doctrine of free will, two spirits, or states of mind?

- The UT edition supports a close reading of hearing, observing, alternatives/decision, and
  thought/speech/deed.
- Malandra documents materially divergent translations of `mainyū` and related terms.
- **Outline rule:** report the words/relations in one edition and the translation dispute; do not
  convert the stanza into a modern psychological doctrine or global “first.”

### `DIS-007002` — Does *Vendīdād* 7 document medicine in practice?

- It prescribes three healing modes, qualification testing, and fees.
- Its composite, enumerative, authoritative form and old translation prevent treating it as a
  clinical register. Prescribed deaths and discrimination are morally important but not outcome
  statistics.
- **Outline rule:** call it a prescriptive religious-legal text and describe harmful hierarchy with
  empathy without inventing implementation.

### `DIS-007003` — What did Kartīr mean by “struck down”?

- The inscription is direct evidence that Kartīr represented himself as suppressing named groups
  and destroying cult material.
- The key verb does not unambiguously mean killing, identifications vary, and the scale/acts are
  uncertain; later histories have exaggerated the wording.
- **Outline rule:** preserve coercion and uncertainty together. Do not minimize the hostile claim or
  turn lexical ambiguity into a massacre narrative.

### `DIS-007004` — Was Gondēšāpur the first teaching hospital?

- Richter-Bernburg reports an alleged c. 610 medical-philosophical disputation and the first
  specific hospital notice at 765. Nayernouri states that he found no Syriac mention of the 610
  event and acknowledges that the only explicit school-and-hospital reference is eighth-century.
- Nayernouri nevertheless argues for earlier institutions from circumstantial evidence: physicians
  sent to Nisibis, the Middle Persian word `bimārestān`, and inferred continuity. Those steps do
  not directly locate a pre-700 hospital at Gondēšāpur.
- Dols, Reinink, and the controlling Syriac/Arabic editions remain unopened, so neither a pre-700
  institution nor definitive absence is established.
- **Outline rule:** label the existence question Disputed, quarantine all priority language, and
  hand the institutional history to the Islamicate packet after upstream source access.

### `DIS-007005` — When did “free will,” “psychology,” and “psychiatry” enter the modern story?

- Jackson provides an accountable 1920 “doctrine of freedom of the will” frame, but not an ancient
  global-priority proof (`EVD-007022`).
- The 2012 title “Zoroastrian Priests: Ancient Persian Psychiatrists” and Mishra's 2019
  “healers of the soul”/demon-causation synthesis document modern clinical-category mapping
  (`EVD-007023–EVD-007024`).
- No verified source chain was found for the stronger exact formulations “Zoroaster invented first
  psychology,” “Persians invented psychiatry,” or “Persian medicine combined magic and science.”
- **Outline rule:** present these only as named, dated reception claims or Unknown discovery
  questions; never as ancient facts.

## Current-evidence boundary

This packet can support a **bounded outline architecture**. It cannot yet support verbatim public
quotation from the *Vendīdād* or Kartīr without a current edition check; a comprehensive history of
non-Zoroastrian Iranian traditions; a reconstructed patient viewpoint; incidence, efficacy, or
access claims; retrospective psychiatric diagnosis; or a definitive Gondēšāpur hospital verdict.
