## Claim-chain controls

- `CLM-007001–CLM-007003` draw on Avestan traditions with distinct texts but shared long
  transmission problems. A specialist article cannot turn a late witness into an autograph.
- `CLM-007004` has three records but one ancient Behistun chain. `EVD-007010` is interpretation,
  not a third inscription.
- `CLM-007005` has one direct tablet. The archive page provides context only; it is not independent
  confirmation of allocation motive or regularity.
- `CLM-007007–CLM-007008` and `CLM-007016` use one inscriptional complex in several copies and one specialist
  synthesis. Variant copies and competing translations do not create independent reports of
  events.
- `CLM-007009–CLM-007010` use distinct bowls, but they share a regional artifact corpus and museum
  mediation. They illustrate plurality; they do not supply prevalence.
- `CLM-007011` now includes opposed opened syntheses, but both still touch the unopened Dols and
  Syriac/Arabic chains. Disagreement is documented; independent primary replay is not.
- `CLM-007013–CLM-007014` are archive/method conclusions. Their strength concerns the packet's
  permissible inference, not an ancient historical proposition.
- `CLM-007017–CLM-007020` are reception-history and packet-audit claims. `repeats` relationships
  never count as ancient corroboration, and LX metadata cannot support body-level assertions.
