## Retrieval and verification note

Agent Reach was used for this research. Its Jina Reader route opened accessible full webpages and
documents. Exa was unavailable, so the project's built-in primary-source web search was used as the
documented discovery fallback. Search snippets, abstracts, generated answer text, and publisher
blurbs were leads only. Every ancient-history evidence record below rests on a full text, edition,
inscriptional translation, object catalog, institutional archive account, or complete specialist
synthesis that was actually opened. One LX record (`SRC-007020`) is retained only as a
bibliographic reception artifact: its title/authors/date/DOI are documented, but no body-level
assertion is permitted.

Ratings are relationship-specific: `L1–L5/LX` describes source level and `A–D` describes fit to
the proposition. A translated primary text may be L1-A for what that edition says while remaining
C or D for social prevalence, lived experience, or a modern clinical category.

## Shared citation-chain audit

- `SRC-007000` and `SRC-007002` are separate modern analyses, but both analyze the same surviving
  Avestan/manuscript tradition. They corroborate transmission method, not the original date or
  audience of a stanza.
- `SRC-007003` and `SRC-007001` are intentionally paired: the UT translation supplies exact
  parsing; Malandra demonstrates why another English rendering is not independent confirmation of
  a single meaning.
- `SRC-007004–SRC-007006` ultimately depend on the composite *Vendīdād*. Gignoux and Malandra add
  genre/text-history context; they do not create three ancient witnesses for enforcement.
- `SRC-007007–SRC-007009` are one Behistun inscriptional chain. The reference article interprets
  the political valence of the same occurrences.
- `SRC-007010` and `SRC-007011` concern the same Fortification Archive but perform different jobs:
  tablet edition versus archive composition/custody. They do not independently corroborate the
  postpartum allocation event.
- `SRC-007012` transmits Kartīr through modern editions and scholarly debate. Its presentation of
  competing readings must not be counted as multiple independent ancient reports.
- `SRC-007013` and `SRC-007014` are distinct objects, but both come from the southern-Mesopotamian
  incantation-bowl corpus and museum collection histories. Two bowls cannot establish prevalence.
- `SRC-007015` and `SRC-007017` are genuinely opposed modern arguments about Gondēšāpur, but both
  depend partly on Dols and on Syriac/Arabic materials not opened here. Nayernouri's full text adds
  a contrary interpretation, not independent replay of every upstream source.
- `SRC-007018` and `SRC-007021` repeat the modern Gondēšāpur priority chain; their agreement does
  not create ancient corroboration. `SRC-007020` and `SRC-007021` likewise document modern
  psychiatry/demonology mapping, not diagnostic evidence.
