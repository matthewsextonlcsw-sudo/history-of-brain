# R32 WHO Expert Committee on Mental Health, 1948–1955 — Lived Experience and Archive

## Governing result

This is an institutional-authority packet, not a patient-experience packet. The complete opened
corpus contains no direct affected-person, family, worker, nurse, social-worker, aide, legal-
profession, Indigenous, or traditional-healer testimony. It is therefore prohibited to write a
patient viewpoint, family reaction, worker experience, acceptance narrative, or composite voice
from this material.

## Mediation map

```text
WHA / WHO administration
        |
        +-- appoints/convenes and publishes
        v
rotating expert committee ----> collective TRS report ----> EN/FR/ES edition objects
        |                              |
        |                              +-- names/describes patients, families,
        |                                  workers and professions without their testimony
        v
unopened WHO/Ment working papers
  (authorship, debate, revision, working language unknown)
```

The report layer collapses individual contributions into collective prose. Translation and
regional publication add further mediation. The absence of a direct voice cannot be repaired by
turning a committee's description of a group into that group's testimony.

## Membership and geography audit

The four member lists contain 32 session-seats, not 32 necessarily unique people. Using printed
affiliations and an explicit present-day regional grouping for audit only:

| affiliation region | session-seats | limit |
|---|---:|---|
| Europe | 17 | not citizenship, population weight, or unified European view |
| Americas | 9 | Brazil, Chile, and USA affiliations are not one regional position |
| Asia | 4 | period place labels and colonial/geopolitical context must be preserved |
| Africa | 1 | one Sudan affiliation cannot represent a continent |
| Oceania | 1 | one Australia affiliation cannot represent a region |

The rosters clearly identify two member-seats as women by printed name or honorific: Dr Elsa B.
Nordlund and Mme J. Roudinesco, both at the second session. This is a cautious roster-based
observation, not self-identified gender data. Professional authority is overwhelmingly medical,
psychiatric, and health-administrative.

## Archive-production hazards

- **Collective authorship:** Published prose does not identify who drafted, opposed, revised, or
  approved individual passages.
- **Administrative self-report:** WHO annual reports describe the Organization's own activity.
  They are not independent evaluation.
- **Translation:** Official editions have distinct titles, imprints, pagination, and dates. No
  translator or working language was established.
- **Colonial authority:** The period corpus was produced while large populations remained under
  colonial rule. A small professional body could classify people without their participation.
- **Diagnostic authority:** Classification proposals are historical program objects, not proof
  of validity or current consensus.
- **Racialized prose:** TRS 73 contains colonial-development and racialized comparisons. These
  are objects of critical analysis only and cannot be repeated as population facts.
- **Legal authority:** TRS 98 addresses liberty and coercion through medical experts without an
  identified patient or legal-profession member voice.

## Dignity and privacy decision

The opened corpus is published institutional material and does not expose a case-level private
person dossier. Its main risks are representational: professional speech can erase affected
people, historical labels can be revived, and community language can be romanticized as
deinstitutionalization. Accordingly:

- no composite patient story or imagined reaction;
- no long quotation of stigmatizing/racialized language;
- no modern diagnosis applied to people described in the reports;
- no implication that lack of testimony means agreement, passivity, or absence of resistance;
- no use of a former-patient-club passage as a celebratory patient-empowerment anecdote;
- no illustration using institutional patient photographs or generic distressed-person imagery;
- no public asset or quotation until rights, survivor-informed, disability-history, colonial-
  medicine, legal-history, translation, and independent editorial review are complete.

## Reopening route for actual lived experience

Any patient, family, worker, nurse, or social-worker history must be a separate local packet with
its own complete primary objects, language and translation record, provenance, privacy/dignity
review, local authority, and outcome limits. It cannot be appended to R32 as an illustrative
anecdote or inferred from WHO recommendations.
