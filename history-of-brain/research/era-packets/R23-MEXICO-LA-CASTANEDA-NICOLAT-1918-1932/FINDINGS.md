## What the opened sources establish

### Deliberate affected-person publication

- Nicolat's book is not a private diary accidentally preserved by clinicians. Its title page
  describes three printings; the prefatory pages name a teacher who corrected his language; the
  final note asserts ownership, gives two order addresses and says Nicolat would sell copies while
  traveling. These material facts support **deliberate public authorship with editorial help**.
- Nicolat explicitly located the first version of his peace text in confinement and dated its
  origin to 1922. This establishes how he publicly framed the work's origin. It does not verify
  every accompanying claim about confinement, cognition, biography or institutional conduct.
- The book is polyphonic. It reproduces a 1913 speech attributed to Baltazar Izaguirre Rojo, an
  appeal attributed to an Orizaba mothers' association, and extended passages from other writers.
  “Nicolat's book” therefore does not mean every sentence is his testimony.

### Confinement, work and coping as self-report

- Nicolat publicly described a workshop, learned trade, financial handling for attendants,
  flowers and invitations, then removal to a bare cell, enforced idleness, pacing for sleep,
  Christian reflection, and intentionally constructed fantasies. These are direct evidence that
  he chose to report those experiences and strategies.
- The workshop passage does **not** establish that work was chosen freely, formally therapeutic,
  paid, safe or beneficial. The financial passage does not establish a lawful bank or agreed
  interest arrangement. The later institutional overview's references to `laborterapia` are a
  distinct 1920s reform narrative, not proof of Nicolat's conditions.
- His reported family longing, diagnostic wording, staff relationships, notebook transfer and
  other people's actions remain claims within a public autobiographical performance until the
  relevant case-file and independent records are opened.

### Medical, legal and family mediation

- Ríos's article makes its chain unusually visible: Nicolat's public/self writing, Oneto's 1924
  thesis, and AHSSA caja 97 expediente 25. Ríos says Oneto borrowed the file and interviewed
  Nicolat's wife. This is a source map, not independent confirmation of each embedded statement.
- Ríos argues that patient writing and clinical interviews are not pure transparent subjectivity;
  they are shaped by institutional questions, medical language, class and gender. That method is
  useful here, while the packet also refuses the opposite mistake of letting clinical notes erase
  deliberate self-authorship.
- Ríos reports competing assessments of Nicolat and Oneto's criminal-responsibility conclusion.
  Without the thesis and file, the packet can map the disagreement but cannot quote or adjudicate
  it.

### Racialized heredity and ancestry restraint

- Ríos reports that file-derived material described Nicolat's father as European, his mother as
  `indígena mexicana`, and several relatives through degeneration/heredity language. This is
  sensitive secondary reporting from an unopened institutional chain.
- The generic phrase does not identify a Nation, community, language or self-identification. It
  cannot authorize Indigenous cultural interpretation or a general claim about Indigenous mental
  health. The packet records the classification as an act within a racialized medical archive and
  places all ancestry narration on hold.

### Archive and data ceiling

- The UNAM project says its patient spreadsheet represents a 20-percent sample and has more than
  12,000 rows. The spreadsheet was intentionally not opened. No prevalence, diagnostic trend,
  re-entry, mortality or individual lookup may be derived here.
- The complete six-page UNAM digitized-thesis list contains 72 selected theses but not Oneto's
  title. This is a defined-corpus absence only, not evidence that the thesis is lost.
- The checked gob.mx archive-information route returned 404, but a current official Secretaría de
  Salud service page supplies an AHSSA consultation email, Donceles address, service description,
  hours and a link to the complete January 2023 policy. The service page states 09:00-14:00 while
  the policy states 09:00-15:00, so current hours require direct confirmation.
- The 2023 policy establishes general consultation and personal-data controls. Some confidential
  custodial material may require an exception route and a confidentiality agreement. Consultation
  is personal rather than group-based. The policy prohibits cellphone/tablet photography and
  permits only scheduled, paid, supervised photography with the researcher's camera; complete
  files or publications may not be reproduced.
- These general rules do not establish that caja 97 expediente 25 is consultable, reproducible,
  quotable, publishable or commercially usable. Item-specific confirmation and consequential
  folio replay remain required.
