## Direct public self-writing is still situated writing

Nicolat's book offers an unusually strong affected-person route because he published, promoted and
sold it outside the clinical-file container. That matters: clinicians did not alone select which
of these pages survived in the public sphere. But “direct” does not mean unmediated or factually
complete.

- Nicolat states that Benito Fentanes corrected his language.
- He incorporates other people's speeches, appeals and quotations.
- He wrote for a public peace campaign and a sales audience, years after the reported origin of
  the central text.
- He frames his capacity and confinement in relation to diagnostic, legal, moral and religious
  arguments.
- The scanned copy was selected, digitized and contextualized by a modern UNAM project.

These facts do not diminish his authorship. They specify the conditions under which it should be
read.

## Confinement and coping without spectacle

Potentially important first-person material includes:

1. removal from a workshop into a cell and the experience of noise/idleness;
2. walking to become tired enough to sleep;
3. Christian prayer/philosophy and directed thought;
4. intentional imagined roles within the institution;
5. writing with a supplied notebook;
6. longing for family and arguing for capacity;
7. peace advocacy as an intellectual project formed partly during confinement.

If this material is ever cleared for a public page, the reader should encounter Nicolat as a
writer making arguments—not as a colorful diagnosis, criminal legend, or “crazy genius.” Minimum
quotation, Spanish-language replay, a named translator for any English version, disability/
survivor review, and exact attribution are mandatory. No Reel or thumbnail may use his historical
labels, allegations, confinement, injury or death as a shock hook.

## Work and institutional life

The phrase “he ran a tailoring workshop” carries several questions the open sources cannot answer:

- Did Nicolat choose the work, negotiate it, or have a meaningful alternative?
- Was it paid? If so, by whom, at what rate, and under whose control?
- Did staff label it treatment, discipline, privilege, economic production, or ordinary work?
- Who supplied tools and materials and who received the product or revenue?
- Were the attendants' savings entrusted voluntarily and how was the reported return recorded?
- Did workers, attendants or other confined people describe the arrangement?
- What changed when he was removed to cell 8?

Nicolat's account supports a bounded individual claim that he reported this activity and used it
in an argument for capacity. It cannot support “occupational therapy helped patients,” “work was
humane,” “patients were exploited,” or any other treatment/efficacy/general labor conclusion.

## Family, privacy and descendant risk

The wife, children, mother, father, sister and other relatives enter the surviving open chain
through Nicolat's public narrative and Ríos's report of file/Oneto content. The packet does not
publish their names, intimate allegations, addresses or attributed statements.

- The wife's reported interview is not opened; it may have been elicited for a medico-legal thesis
  about her confined/accused husband.
- The public book's historical ordering addresses are evidence of distribution, not invitations
  to trace descendants or current occupants.
- Family health/alcohol descriptions were mobilized through degeneration theory. Repeating them
  can stigmatize relatives and falsely naturalize contested historical categories.
- A 1932 death does not remove dignity, privacy, family or commercial-indexing concerns.

Public use requires necessity review: if a family detail does not materially explain the evidence
chain, it should be omitted.

## Racialized heredity and the `indígena mexicana` category

The only opened route for the reported description of Nicolat's mother is Ríos's article, which
attributes family material to the case-file/autobiography chain. The phrase is generic and
institutionally mediated. It does not supply:

- her own identification;
- a Nation, pueblo, community, territory or language;
- who applied the category on the underlying folio;
- the purpose, date or conditions of recording;
- consent for cultural, genealogical or public reuse.

The category may eventually be relevant to how degeneration, heredity and postrevolutionary
nation-making operated in psychiatric writing. That would be a history of classification and
power, not a claim about Indigenous biology, psychology or tradition. Original-folio inspection,
Mexican specialist review, Indigenous/community/descendant guidance where identifiable, and harm
review are prerequisites. The currently anonymous generic category cannot be used to manufacture
an authority path.

## Translation protocol

All opened substantive sources are Spanish. The packet records no project translations for
public use.

1. Preserve original Spanish spelling and historical terms in the evidence layer.
2. Name a qualified translator for every public English rendering.
3. Keep `loco`, `anómalo`, `paranoia`, `paranoide`, `delirio`, `responsable`, `reo`, `peligrosos`,
   `locos orgullosos`, `reivindicación` and `laborterapia` in period/source context.
4. Do not silently translate them into modern diagnosis, disability identity, criminal capacity,
   danger, consent or therapy.
5. A second Spanish-language specialist must replay pivotal pages and the full Oneto/file sources
   before public wording.

## Images and material records

- The book scan is research evidence, not cleared commercial artwork. Even an apparently benign
  portrait or title page needs item-level rights, dignity and context review.
- No case-file photograph, handwriting, letter, diagnosis card, clinical image or dataset row was
  opened or cached.
- UNAM/INAH institutional photographs on the overview page were not downloaded. They cannot be
  used as if they depict Nicolat's workshop, conditions or experience.
- Any future image choice needs repository permission, exact provenance, caption, alt text,
  non-sensational purpose and separate harm review.
