## Claim-chain and dispute controls

- `CLM-010002` depends on Raphals's normalization/translation of the Guodian editorial reconstruction. It has several passage locators but one archaeological manuscript chain. Public quotation requires photographs/apparatus and Cook's full edition.
- `CLM-010005` is source-language documented but English-translation held. CText-generated English is not a second translation.
- `CLM-010008`, `CLM-010009`, and the ancient-text contextualization in `CLM-010016` share the received *Lingshu* tradition. Wikisource, Kanripo, Lee, and Unschuld are not independent ancient witnesses.
- `CLM-010012` has two modern translation checks but only one Zhangjiashan manuscript witness. The unverified strip-range claim has been removed; the 2007 base edition and variants remain a hold.
- `CLM-010011` supports a contested legal episode, not a general rule that Han law policed grief. The preliminary and final judgments point in different directions; both must remain.
- `CLM-010013` is deliberately held. Köhle's event summary is a useful specialist lead but is not enough for a pivotal “first appearance” claim.
- `CLM-010014` is a historiographic correction, not a claim that ritual and medical roles were indistinguishable. Brown contests the simple secular-break reading; further contrary scholarship should be added in review.
- `CLM-010016` treats the modern mapping as an object of reception history. It never flows backward as evidence for ancient diagnosis.
- `CLM-010017` comes through Wu's translation of the Shuihudi letters and the upstream Giele edition. It is direct epistolary language, but the emphatic death phrase is a rhetorical/semantic research question rather than clinical evidence.
