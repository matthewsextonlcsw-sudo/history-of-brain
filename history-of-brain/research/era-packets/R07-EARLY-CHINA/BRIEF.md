# R07-EARLY-CHINA — Heart-Mind, Emotion, Conduct, and Plural Healing

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `010000–010999`

## Research question

How did selected early Chinese philosophical, medical, ritual, legal, household, and material
sources describe *xin*, *qing*, perception, emotion, conduct, troubling states, body, care, and
social order—and how did these meanings change across texts and periods?

## Boundary

Named Warring States through early imperial contexts, approximately 500 BCE–600 CE. Choose a
bounded core of texts and manuscripts. Separate excavated witness date, received-text history,
commentary, and modern translation. Later “TCM” cannot be treated as one unchanged system.

## Required evidence

- critical/authoritative Chinese editions and translations with exact locators;
- excavated manuscript/object records where material to a claim;
- specialist history of philosophy, emotion, medicine, religion, and law;
- *xin*, *qing*, *dian*, *kuang*, wind, fire/phlegm, conduct, and ritual vocabulary without
  retrospective diagnosis;
- plurality across texts, schools, regions, practitioners, and households;
- gender, status, family, law, access, and non-elite archive limits; and
- citation-chain audits of “ancient Chinese psychiatry,” timeless TCM, and modern wellness
  continuity claims.

## Stop

Do not expand into Tang/Song or all East Asia. Create five standard outputs, typed relationships,
and local rights-safe cache; no public prose.
