- A chronology organized around named witnesses rather than a timeless “Chinese tradition.”

- Wuwei as a corpus-level contrast, not proof that religion disappeared.
- The widow case as contested official interpretation of grief/conduct, not her first-person voice or a settled general rule.
- Two Shuihudi soldiers' letters as direct evidence of material coping and family reliance, without clinicalizing an urgent death formula.
- Archive limits and the modern diagnostic mapping as reception.

## Explicit holds

- **HOLD:** English quotation or close paraphrase of *Xunzi* from CText. Its generated English is excluded.
- **HOLD:** any claim that Guodian represents all Warring States thought or that the graph reconstruction is uncontested.
- **HOLD:** translating *dian/kuang* as bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, psychosis, mania, or depression without a clearly modern reception frame and specialist semantic review.
- **HOLD:** “phlegm-fire was an early Chinese explanation of mental illness.” The current core does not establish it; the chronology source is only a specialist event summary.
- **HOLD:** “Han medicine became secular/rational” or “ritual healing was replaced.” Wuwei supports only a bounded corpus contrast.
- **HOLD:** prevalence, cure rates, efficacy, affordability, consent, coercion, satisfaction, or a separate psychiatric profession.
- **HOLD:** broad claims of gender equality/inequality from the single succession provision or widow case.
- **HOLD:** Shao Tuo retrospective diagnosis and invented patient feelings.
- **HOLD:** interpreting the Shuihudi soldier's urgent “I will die” formula as suicidality, intent, or diagnosis without genre/semantic evidence.

## Handoff boundaries

### Backward / neighboring ancient packet

- Earlier Shang/Zhou divination and ancestral causation should be handled in its own source-bound packet, not treated as a primitive preface to Warring States medicine.
- Compare heart/*xin*, *po/hun*, dream, possession, and ritual terms only through datable inscriptions/manuscripts and their specific contexts.
- Avoid priority claims such as “first concept of mind” across Egypt, Greece, India, Mesopotamia, and China; the source traditions are not commensurable by default.

### Forward to medieval and later China

- Stop this packet before a general Six Dynasties, Sui–Tang, Song, Jin–Yuan, Ming, or Qing story.
- Hand off Buddhist translation vocabularies, Daoist ritual/meditation institutions, demonological and medical interaction, hospitals/charity, and professional regulation to later packets.
- The phlegm/*tan* and phlegm-fire trajectory belongs chiefly in the later handoff and should include transmission and exchange, not an isolated national invention story.

### Central, South, and Inner Asian exchange

- Investigate Indic Buddhist, Persian/Islamic, and Inner Asian medical/affective vocabularies through named translations, routes, institutions, and datable texts.
- Do not infer exchange from superficial similarity. Require textual, material, linguistic, or institutional transmission evidence.

### Modern reception

- Separate twentieth-/twenty-first-century TCM, acupuncture, psychiatry, DSM translation, nationalist history, and global wellness marketing from ancient evidence.
- Trace popular claims upstream before repeating them. A modern clinical textbook is evidence of reception only.
