# R07-EARLY-CHINA — Source ledger

**Use:** internal research control; not public prose
**Packet state:** independent review repairs applied; specialist/publication review still required
**Bounded core:** selected Chu/Warring States manuscripts and received texts, followed by selected Qin–Han medical and legal witnesses, approximately 350 BCE–200 CE. Later phlegm discourse and modern diagnosis are reception/transition controls only.

## Retrieval and scope controls

- Research used Agent Reach's Jina reader and direct full-source access. Exa was unavailable, so the project's approved built-in primary-source search fallback was used to locate editions, institutional records, and author-hosted scholarship. Search snippets and abstracts were leads only; no retained substantive claim rests on them.
- “Early China” here is a packet label, not a timeless national mind or medical system. The evidence comes from different centuries, regions, genres, institutions, and textual layers.
- `received` means transmitted through later copying/editing; it is not interchangeable with a Warring States physical witness. `excavated` identifies an archaeological manuscript/object context, while still requiring attention to copying, selection, reconstruction, and publication.
- Primary texts support bounded claims about what a witness represents. They do not independently prove population prevalence, therapeutic efficacy, a patient's private experience, or an unbroken “TCM” tradition.

## Citation and translation rules for outline writers

1. Name the witness and layer: “the excavated Guodian *Xing zi ming chu* manuscript,” “the received *Xunzi*,” or “the received *Lingshu* chapter 22.”
2. Preserve `心 xin`, `情 qing`, `性 xing`, `神 shen`, `癲 dian`, and `狂 kuang` on first use when the semantic issue matters. English glosses are contextual, not diagnoses.
3. Attribute represented experience: “the Baoshan record describes Shao Tuo as…” and “officials represented the widow as…”. Neither is a transcript of interior experience.
4. Avoid “ancient Chinese believed,” “the Chinese discovered,” “traditional Chinese medicine always,” “mental illness,” “bipolar,” and “schizophrenia” unless the sentence is explicitly about modern reception.
5. Distinguish prescription, institutional representation, and actual practice. No source in this packet establishes prevalence, efficacy, satisfaction, consent, or equitable access.
