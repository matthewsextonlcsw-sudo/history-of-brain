## Route and influence handoff

This packet does not establish a single diffusion route. It offers four bounded exchange/transport problems for later synthesis:

1. Buddhist vocabulary/ideas in one Sun ethics passage, as argued by Steavu, require comparison with Indian/Buddhist textual loci rather than an “East influenced West” or “Buddhism invented compassion” slogan.
2. Song state editing of older medical corpora changed what later readers received; state recension is a transmission event, not proof of uniform practice.
3. Xu's Zhenzhou location at the Grand Canal/Yangzi junction and Southern Song print circulation are book/knowledge infrastructures. They do not prove that any specific case traveled along a named route.
4. Japanese reprints and present Kyoto custody preserve Chinese received texts, but modern custody is not evidence of medieval clinical movement.

Hand Buddhist routes back to R12 and East/Southeast Asian reception packets. Hand Song print/state medicine to later institutional and book-history packets. Do not extend R13 into universal China or Asia.

These are evidence-backed outline bridges, not public prose:

1. **From virtue to force:** place Sun's compassion norm beside the received ritual restraint procedure to show plural prescriptions within a layered corpus, without declaring contradiction across all medieval care.
