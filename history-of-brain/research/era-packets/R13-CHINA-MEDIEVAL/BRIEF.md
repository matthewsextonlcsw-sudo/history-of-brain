# R13-CHINA-MEDIEVAL — Emotion, Medicine, Religion, Family, and Governance

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `020000–020999`

## Research question

How did a bounded set of Chinese texts, manuscripts, medical cases, ritual/religious records,
family or legal sources, and material evidence from approximately 600 to 1400 describe emotion,
heart-mind, cognition, troubling states, body, care, cultivation, family obligation, and social
order after the early imperial corpus?

## Boundary

Choose no more than three named dynastic/regional/corpus settings. Medical, Buddhist, Daoist,
Confucian, legal, household, and local religious evidence remain distinct streams. Separate the
date of a work, edition, commentary, surviving witness, translation, and modern TCM reception.

## Required evidence

- accountable Chinese editions/translations, manuscripts, case records, legal/family documents,
  inscriptions, objects, or archaeology with exact locators;
- specialist history of medicine, emotion, philosophy, religion, family, law, gender,
  disability, book history, and local society;
- passage-specific treatment of *xin*, *qing*, *shen*, *zhi*, *dian*, *kuang*, phlegm and other
  selected terms without modern diagnostic substitution;
- change and disagreement across authors, genres, commentaries, regions, and periods;
- prescription versus delivered practice, and elite compilation versus local/household life;
- gender, age, class/status, family labor, poor and disabled people, cost, coercion, consent,
  access, and outcome;
- named translation or contact claims rather than similarity-as-influence; and
- audits of timeless TCM, ancient psychiatry, mind-body holism, first forensic psychiatry, and
  modern clinical continuity claims.

## Stop

Do not cover all Tang–Yuan China, project later Ming/Qing material backward, or use one medical
author as a civilization-wide system. Create five outputs and rights-safe ignored cache; no
public prose.
