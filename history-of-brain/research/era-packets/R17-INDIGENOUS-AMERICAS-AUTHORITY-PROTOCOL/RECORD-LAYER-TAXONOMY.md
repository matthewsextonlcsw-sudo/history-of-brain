# R17 — Record-layer taxonomy and evidence records

**Boundary:** this file classifies evidence layers and records governance evidence. It makes no
claim about any community's history, knowledge, practice, belief, health, or experience.

## Layer taxonomy for future packets

No layer outranks community authority. “Primary” describes proximity to a record/event; it does not
mean ethically cleared, unbiased, unmediated, or representative.

| layer | required provenance fields | may support, if authorized | cannot establish by itself | authority/reproduction gate | usual source rating ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LAYER-MATERIAL` — material/archaeological/object/site evidence | exact object/site; inventory; provenience; excavation/collection date and method; chain of custody; dating method; alterations; image/sampling history; human-remains/funerary/sacred/sensitive-site screen | bounded physical description, measured date, documented context, custody history | diagnosis, belief, emotion, ritual meaning, treatment, consent, voice, prevalence, or continuity | all affected present-day affiliations identified; authority for viewing, imaging, sampling, description, coordinates, public indexing; NAGPRA/other law where applicable | L1 for the object/context actually inspected; D fit for lived meaning without separate authorized evidence |
| `LAYER-ORAL-AUTHORIZED` — community-authorized oral knowledge/history | speaker/knowledge-holder; community/authority; recorder; language/variant; interpreter/translator; recording context; consent form/process; audience restrictions; review; custody; withdrawal | what a named speaker/community authorized the record to convey, within stated scope | universal community view, ancient continuity, prevalence, or meanings beyond the authorization | speaker plus collective/family authority as required; voice, transcription, translation, quotation, indexing, model use, and future use separately approved | L1 for authorized testimony/position; fit depends on claim and representativeness |
| `LAYER-LATER-RECORDED` — later-recorded tradition or recollection | event/tradition date distinguished from telling/recording/publication dates; speaker, recorder, sponsor; language/translation; genre; chain; selection/elicitation; authority | later memory, interpretation, transmission, or present community framing as authorized | automatic proof of an earlier event's details or unchanged continuity | same oral/community gates; no retroactive consent assumed from a public transcription | L1 for what was said in the later event; B–D for earlier event depending on corroboration |
| `LAYER-COLONIAL-MEDIATED` — colonial, mission, administrative, school, medical, legal, carceral, or ethnographic record | office/collector/recorder; purpose; coercive setting; original language; interpreter; selection; omissions; custody; privacy; affected person/people; consent/provenance | what the recorder/institution documented, imposed, categorized, requested, or preserved | transparent affected-person voice, consent, cultural truth, diagnosis, prevalence, or benevolent implementation | affected-community authority plus privacy/legal/repository gates; stigmatizing terms contextualized; names/details minimized | L1 for record wording/action; C/D for affected experience absent an independent authorized voice |
| `LAYER-INDIGENOUS-GOVERNED-MODERN` — modern Indigenous-governed interpretation/framework | governing organization; constituency/remit; authorship; consultation; version; jurisdiction; language; review/update status; local applicability | that body's current position/framework within its stated remit | automatic permission from every community, timeless tradition, or historical fact outside cited evidence | verify whether body can authorize this exact corpus/use; do not enlarge national/network scope | L1 for its own position/framework; D for local permission outside mandate |
| `LAYER-SPECIALIST-MODERN` — modern specialist scholarship | author expertise/relationship; methods; cited corpus; peer review; edition/translation; funding/conflict; dependence chain; access | bounded analysis, historiography, translation, archive map, disputes, and leads | community authority, consent, independent voice, or facts not supported by opened evidence | scholarship may inform but never confer authority; restricted upstream material remains restricted | L2 normally; fit A–D claim-relative |

### Separation rules

- Date the object/event, recording, transcription, translation, edition, digitization, custody, and
  present access separately.
- Record the source language and every interpreter/translator. Translation permission is not implied
  by reading permission.
- Record the recorder's purpose and position before treating words as an affected person's speech.
- A catalog, colonial record, or scholar can identify a potential affiliation but cannot decide it.
- Later-recorded tradition is not downgraded as “late”; its evidentiary job is explicitly bounded to
  the later telling unless an authorized, source-backed continuity argument exists.
- Community-governed modern interpretation is direct evidence of the issuing body's current
  position, not a license to project that position backward or across communities.

## Archive and missing-voice controls

- The holder's description is a custody voice, not the affected person's/community's voice.
- Colonial/mission/administrative/medical/school/legal records may contain an attributed voice, but
  the recorder, interpreter, coercive setting, editing, and filing purpose remain part of every use.
- A community government or representative may have collective authority while an individual,
  family, clan, gendered body, knowledge holder, or other group also has a necessary decision role.
- Formal community review must not be used to erase internal disagreement. Agreements must say
  how dissent or unresolved interpretation will be represented.
- When authorized oral/community evidence is unavailable, state the absence as an archive/project
  limitation. Do not fill it with colonial records, later scholarship, or generic “Indigenous” claims.
- Public repository silence about restrictions, consent, or cultural authority means unknown—not
  cleared.
