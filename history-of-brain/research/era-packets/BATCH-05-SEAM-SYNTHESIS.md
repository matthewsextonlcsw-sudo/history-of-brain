## Chronological, regional, and archival assembly decisions

### 1. Horn and Red Sea manuscripts preserve layered textual histories, not clinical records

`R14-HORN-RED-SEA-MANUSCRIPTS` separates composition, copying, colophon, cataloging,
translation, modern interpretation, and current custody. The *Maṣḥafa faws manfasāwi* evidence
supports a penitential or confessional manual using medical metaphor; it does not establish a
clinical handbook, delivered mental-health treatment, or patient outcome.

Catalog descriptions and manuscript records can document an object, contents, shelfmark, or
copying claim at their bounded level. They cannot silently establish authorial intent, practice,
popular belief, or a recipient's experience. Even a dated colophon may contain an internal
weekday/calendar conflict, which must remain visible rather than be harmonized away.

### 2. The Swahili coast packet contains three different archives, not one regional system

`R14-EAST-AFRICA-SWAHILI-COAST` separates Unguja Ukuu archaeology, later evidence used to
reconstruct parts of the Mombasa hinterland, and Pate/Lamu manuscript and collector histories.
An inscribed object does not identify its wearer, use, protective meaning, diagnosis, treatment,
or outcome. The opened corpus does not settle priority claims about the object or its setting.

The packet preserves the broad semantic and social range of *uganga* while exposing the
Bresnahan–Ray–Spear/Govi source chain and its later recording and translation layers. It does
not turn the term into psychiatry or medicine. Individual kayas and councils are living
authority questions, not merely historical sources. The traditional 1728 date attached to the
*Utendi wa Tambuka* is disputed within the opened transmission record, and the purported witness
is missing; later copies and collector editions cannot resolve that by themselves.

### 3. Southern African material and recorded narratives establish an evidence ceiling

`R14-SOUTHERN-AFRICA-MATERIAL-ORAL-PROTOCOL` separates Maloti-Drakensberg material evidence and
later recording, Tsodilo archaeology and living custodial relationships, and the colonial
Bleek–Lloyd archive. Rock art, excavation, place, motif, chronology, and later narrative may be
documented at different levels. None directly reveals a prehistoric diagnosis, mental state,
healing practice, patient experience, or uniform worldview.

Later oral, translated, collected, or scholarly interpretation must remain attributed to its
speaker, recorder, translator, collector, community, and date. Current community and custodian
authority, item-level rights, vulnerable locations, and sensitive narratives are public-use
gates. The packet's refusal to narrate a prehistoric “mental-health system” is a successful
source finding, not an empty result.

### 4. Anatomical print, melancholy writing, and mind–body argument are separate developments

`R16-PRINT-ANATOMY-MELANCHOLY-MECHANISM` separates Vesalius's page-specific anatomical work,
Burton's composite and repeatedly revised literary-medical project, and the Descartes–Elisabeth
correspondence and print chain. Visual page and edition control matters: printed foliation,
digital leaf labels, later editions, OCR, translation, and modern reception are not
interchangeable.

Vesalius does not become the discoverer of the brain or a simple founder of neuroscience.
Burton's “Democritus Junior” self-writing is persona, argument, satire, and compilation rather
than a transparent diagnosis or evidence that writing cured him. Elisabeth is an elite
correspondent and co-thinker whose letters discuss embodiment, health, sadness, obligation, and
the passions; she is not reducible to Descartes's patient or muse. Descartes's whole-person,
body, gland, passion, and mechanism claims must be read in their exact textual sequence rather
than collapsed into a slogan that he invented dualism or said the person was only a machine.

### 5. Forman–Napier records are mediated encounters, not a prevalence dataset or patient diary

`R15-EARLY-MODERN-ENGLISH-CASEBOOKS` uses a deliberately bounded 26-record sample representing
24 consultation events or subjects from two distinct practices. The entries can support exact
claims about who consulted, recorded questions, proxy routes, consent annotations, judgments,
treatments, payments, and later notations when the image and record actually show them.

They cannot support population prevalence, diagnostic equivalence, treatment effectiveness,
equal access, or a claim that silence means no treatment or outcome. A practitioner, associate,
scribe, querent, messenger, subject, editor, transcription, coded facet, and manuscript image
may all occupy different layers of one record. The packet preserves contradictions such as the
CASE18910 brother transcription versus inferred-female digital person link instead of choosing
the tidier story. Historical consent annotations are not modern informed consent, and an absent
annotation remains unknown.

### 6. Indigenous Americas research begins with authority, not accessible content

`R17-INDIGENOUS-AMERICAS-AUTHORITY-PROTOCOL` creates governance decisions only. It distinguishes
copyright, repository access, legal duties, research ethics, cultural authority, consent,
consultation, permitted use, public search indexing, commercial context, and withdrawal or
update expectations. These gates overlap but cannot substitute for one another.

The packet does not self-assign Traditional Knowledge or Biocultural Labels and does not treat
a repository, university, government, or general Indigenous framework as the authority for a
particular Nation, people, community, council, custodian, or knowledge holder. All thirteen
surveyed substantive lanes, including the scoped Nahua central-Mexico 1519–1606 proposal,
remain `AUTHORITY_UNRESOLVED` and held. Institutional accessibility is not authorization to
begin cultural interpretation.

## Cross-packet findings that survive the seam audit

### Access is not authority, and survival is not representativeness

A digitized manuscript, public catalog, open article, casebook image, colonial collection, or
archaeological report can be legally accessible while remaining culturally restricted,
ethically sensitive, unrepresentative, or unsuitable for public reuse. Archive survival reflects
power, collection, custody, editorial selection, preservation, and digitization.

### Source layers must remain visible

Object, site, speaker, recorder, manuscript, copy, colophon, transcription, translation,
facsimile, edition, image, OCR, catalog, scholarly interpretation, database relation, and modern
reception are different evidence surfaces. A downstream layer may accurately support its own
metadata or reception claim without independently corroborating the historical proposition.

### Historical words do not arrive as modern diagnoses

Medical metaphor, melancholy, passion, fear, distress, spirit, protection, *uganga*, and
practitioner-recorded questions must be interpreted within their language, genre, relationship,
and setting. They do not automatically equal psychiatric diagnosis, psychotherapy,
neuroscience, or wellness practice.

### A recorded encounter is not the whole experience

Letters, casebooks, hagiography, penitential instruction, later oral records, collector
archives, and institutional metadata preserve shaped voices. Even a named person may appear
through another person's question, a copy, an editor's relation, a translation, or a colonial
recording situation. Internal nodes must name the nearest surviving voice and every important
mediator.

### Negative audits prevent attractive myths

The batch does not establish a first African psychiatric text, a uniform African or Indigenous
psychology, a Vesalian discovery of the brain, Burton as the first psychiatry textbook, Descartes
as the inventor of dualism, or Forman and Napier as modern mental-health clinicians. These
negative findings belong in the outline because they prevent later search language from
overwriting the evidence.

## Publication blockers carried forward

### Language, edition, source, and chronology

- pivotal Gəʿəz, Arabic, Swahili/Ajami, Latin, French, early-modern English, and other
  source-language or critical-edition checks remain packet-specific requirements;
- manuscript, object, oral-recording, collector, print-edition, image, and database layers must
  be replayed before quotation or promotion;
- priority, continuity, diagnosis, institution, therapy, and influence claims remain held unless
  their upstream chains pass a defined comparison; and
- current clinical or scientific comparisons require a separate current-evidence review.

### Implementation, experience, privacy, and harm

- texts, prescriptions, consultations, institutional metadata, objects, and later narratives do
  not establish routine use, access, consent, safety, efficacy, outcome, or prevalence;
- recipients, patients, households, workers, poor and dependent people, disabled people, women,
  children, translators, scribes, and knowledge holders remain unevenly represented;
- self-harm, coercion, intimate allegations, sacred material, bodily images, private
  correspondence, and identifiable historical cases require specific dignity and harm review;
  and
- no historical remedy, ritual, or practice may be converted into present-day health advice.

### Community, custody, rights, and public discovery

- the named community, Nation, traditional council, Elders Council, custodian, archive, and
  repository roles remain distinct;
- open access or copyright permission alone does not authorize culturally sensitive,
  commercial, image, quotation, SEO/GEO, or AI-discovery use;
- current object, manuscript, site, community, and rights status must be rechecked near any
  proposed publication; and
- every Indigenous Americas substantive lane remains held until the relevant authority path and
  permitted actions are identified and recorded.

## Source-method lessons for the next batch

1. Freeze an archive or practice setting, not a continent or broad era.
2. Separate the historical proposition from the history of its recording, edition, database,
   and public reception.
3. Use one source per atomic evidence record and expose dependent citation chains.
4. Visually verify pivotal facsimile, manuscript, and casebook locators; OCR and metadata are not
   enough.
5. Model contradictions and ambiguous person links rather than silently resolving them.
6. Treat authority discovery as a prerequisite for research when cultural materials or claims
   require it.
7. Preserve a source ceiling as a finding; do not fill it with analogy, later ethnography,
   present-day diagnosis, or an imported civilizational story.
