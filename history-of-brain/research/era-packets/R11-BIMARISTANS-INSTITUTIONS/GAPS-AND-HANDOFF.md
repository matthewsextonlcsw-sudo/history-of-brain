# R11 Bīmāristāns and Institutions — Gaps and Handoff

**Disposition:** **PASS WITH GAPS after independent closure.** The packet now supports a bounded internal comparison with conduit-dependent claims held/attributed. It does not support public claims of a first hospital, first psychiatric hospital, universal humane care, medieval music wards, occupational therapy, pharmacy infrastructure, or institutional treatment success.

## What the outline may carry now

1. **Two dated material/documentary anchors with different gates:** Beksaç's L3 report supports al-Nūrī's 549/1154 date internally; Sabatier's 682/1283 repair translation remains attributed/HOLD. Monument authorities report Qalāwūn construction as 683–684 AH with discrepant CE conversions.
2. **Different archive profiles:** al-Nūrī offers direct critical-edition biography and case narrative; al-Manṣūrī offers monument records and modern scholarly guides to unopened endowment/appointment texts but weak direct evidence for everyday practice.
3. **Institutional care without a modern psychiatric category:** al-Nūrī has one bounded `māniyā` case; Ibn Jubayr specifically places treatment and chains in the **old** Damascus hospital, not the new one. Neither proves a psychiatric-only hospital or specialized psychiatric ward.
4. **Norm versus implementation:** Qalāwūn eligibility, exclusion, benefit, role, and division claims remain attributed to Qalawun VR/Kubo/Fernandes until upstream Arabic texts are opened. Later chronicles remain later evidence.
5. **Lived-experience asymmetry:** a mediated patient/family conflict and death survive at al-Nūrī; Manṣūrī recipient voices are effectively absent from the opened record.

## Mandatory publication gaps

These do not block internal-outline use, but they block publication of the affected claims.

### 1. Collate the Nūrī inscriptions upstream

- Open the diplomatic edition for RCEA no. 4820 and the 549/1154 foundation inscription.
- Capture Arabic, exact line numbers, dimensions/location, edition history, and any restoration uncertainty.
- Resolve whether “expected to recover” is inscription wording, translator expansion, or legal formula before quoting.

### 2. Open the Qalāwūn documentary corpus directly

- Waqf / *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* 1:358–59, 363, 366–67.
- Awqāf document no. 1010, including its several dated instruments (685–686 AH / 1286–87 CE).
- Appointment instruments in Ibn al-Furāt 8:22–25 / Ḥamāda pp. 324–327 and al-Qalqashandī 11:253–256.
- Capture Arabic wording for patient eligibility, exclusion, home provision, stay duration, discharge, roles, and teaching. Until then, modern syntheses remain transparent conduits.

### 3. Resolve the al-Maqrīzī and al-Nuwayrī chronology layers

- Open al-Maqrīzī, *Khiṭaṭ* 4:694, 696, and al-Nuwayrī, *Nihāyat al-Arab* 31:105–110.
- Date what each author could observe versus what they copied from earlier documents.
- Do not infer named wards from reconstructed plans or project summaries.

### 4. Finish Ibn Jubayr translation control

- **Completed internally:** printed p. 283 was checked against the public-domain page image; Arabic lines 8–14 and 14–16 are transcribed separately; syntax review distinguishes new-hospital administration from old-hospital treatment/chains.
- **Still required for publication:** use a named rights-cleared modern translation or independently reviewed project translation. Do not identify either hospital as al-Nūrī from this page alone.

### 5. Repair or retire music claims

- Manṣūrī: use the catalogued 1877 Prisse text volume, open p. 138, and trace every authority to a contemporaneous source. Treat Dols's 1887 as a bibliographic error unless a distinct edition is demonstrated.
- Nūrī: open Evliya Çelebi, *Seyahatname* 13:268, verify 1648 context and translation. Correct Dols locator is note 55.
- If the chains do not reach direct institution-specific evidence, remove both claims rather than repeating a famous story.

### 6. Add Arabic-language and Egyptian/Syrian specialist review

- This packet relied heavily on English-accessible scholarship and modern translated conduits.
- Seek Syrian architectural/epigraphic scholarship, Egyptian waqf/archival scholarship, and current Arabic critical editions.
- Ask reviewers to assess term translations, institutional names, date conversion, and whether exclusion formulas changed across deed recensions.

### 7. Recheck current sites, custody, conservation, and conflict status before publication

- **Cairo current representation:** as accessed 2026-08-18, the Egyptian Ministry states that the Qalāwūn bīmāristān now serves as an eye and dentistry hospital and lists monument hours/tickets. Recheck because this is time-sensitive.
- **Damascus Unknown:** TDV 2007 cannot establish current museum use. Sampled UNESCO 2024–26 records provide city-wide danger/conservation context but no institution-specific al-Nūrī use, custodian, access, condition, damage, conservation, or image-rights status.
- Obtain an institution-specific Syrian/DGAM authority before making a current-use claim. Do not use city-wide UNESCO status or medieval architecture to fill the gap.

## Non-mandatory but high-value research

- Trace Badr al-Dīn's al-Nūrī expansion date and material evidence beyond the biographical passage.
- Search for excavated objects, pharmacy vessels, instruments, water infrastructure, or repair records with secure institutional provenance.
- Seek legal disputes, payrolls, account books, petitions, funerary records, or estate documents that expose non-elite workers and recipients.
- Compare household care without making the institution the sole center of medical life.
- Establish what happened to people excluded from the Manṣūrī through Jewish, Christian, neighborhood, and household archives rather than assuming an institutional vacuum.

- Preferred names on first mention: **al-Bīmāristān al-Nūrī (Damascus)** and **al-Bīmāristān al-Manṣūrī, the Qalāwūn hospital (Cairo)**.
