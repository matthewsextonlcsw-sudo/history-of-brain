## Bounded corpus

This packet compares two named institutions in two connected regions:

1. **al-Bīmāristān al-Nūrī, Damascus** — a foundation inscription dated 549 AH/1154 CE, a repair inscription dated 682 AH/1283 CE, and twelfth- to thirteenth-century physician biographies and case narratives.
2. **al-Bīmāristān al-Manṣūrī (Qalāwūn hospital), Cairo** — building inscriptions dated 683–684 AH/1284–85 CE, waqf instruments dated 685–686 AH/1286–87 CE, two 684 AH/1285 appointment decrees, and later narrative descriptions.

Syria and Egypt are connected here through Ayyubid/Mamluk political and professional networks. The comparison does **not** presume one timeless “Islamic hospital” model. Baghdad, Jundishapur, Byzantine institutions, and later Ottoman institutions enter only to test priority or back-projection claims.

## Retained sources

### SRC-016000 — Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah, critical edition and translation, chapter 15

- **Citation:** Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah, *The Best Accounts of the Classes of Physicians*, ed. and trans. Emilie Savage-Smith, Simon Swain, and Geert Jan van Gelder (Leiden: Brill, 2020), chapter 15, [NCBI Bookshelf full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK621355/).
- **Form / language:** Arabic critical text with English translation and apparatus.
- **Level / fit:** **L1-A** for what the edited biographies and case narratives say; **L2-B/C** for editors' identifications and for reconstructing hospital practice from authorial anecdotes.
- **Pivotal locators:** `15.9`; `15.23.4.2`; `15.40.9`; `15.50.3`–`15.50.4`; `15.52.1`.
- **Limits:** physician-authored literary biography, not an administrative register or outcomes dataset. Patients and relatives appear through the author's framing. A single narrated response does not establish routine efficacy.
- **Rights/cache:** CC BY-NC 4.0; `ibn-abi-usaybia-ch15-2020.md`.

### SRC-016001 — Savage-Smith, “Medical Practice”

- **Citation:** Emilie Savage-Smith, “Medical Practice,” in *A Literary History of Medicine* (Brill, 2020), [NCBI Bookshelf full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK623276/).
- **Level / fit:** **L2-A/B** for synthesis and source criticism; **C** for quantitative or representative practice claims.
- **Pivotal locators:** section `1. Hospitals and Institutions`; discussion of chapter `15.50.4`, `15.23.4.2`, and Qalāwūn foundation documentation.
- **Limits:** explicitly notes that no patient counts, disease statistics, or success rates survive and that elite urban male practitioners dominate the biographical source. Its approximate Nūrī foundation date, 569/1174, conflicts with the 549/1154 building inscription and is not used.
- **Rights/cache:** CC BY-NC 4.0; `savage-smith-practice-2020.md`.

## Chain-independence rules

- SRC-016008 and SRC-016009 are modern independent analyses but **repeat** the same *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* deed chain for Qalāwūn waqf rules. They cannot be counted twice as independent medieval evidence.
- SRC-016001 and SRC-016012 often interpret the same Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah cases in SRC-016000; the critical edition is the evidentiary base.
- SRC-016002 and SRC-016003 both report Nūrī inscriptions. They provide accountability checks, not two independent foundation events.
- A later chronicler's report, a waqf norm, a surviving building, and a physician anecdote are different evidence types and must not be silently merged into one operational narrative.
