# R11 Bīmāristāns and Institutions — Findings

**Purpose:** internal evidence packet, not publication copy
**Cases:** al-Nūrī, Damascus; al-Manṣūrī/Qalāwūn, Cairo
**Rule:** building, endowment, foundation, ward, staffing, admission, mental-distress care, and outcomes remain separate propositions.

## Evidence stack A — al-Bīmāristān al-Nūrī, Damascus

### EVD-016020 — dated foundation inscription

- **Observation:** Beksaç reports that the east-iwan marble inscription names Nūr al-Dīn and dates the building to 549 AH/1154 CE.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016002, pp. 262–263.
- **Rating:** **L3-A/B**.
- **Inference limit:** this dates and attributes the building. It does not itself establish opening-day staffing, admissions, a mental ward, or treatment outcomes. SRC-016001's 569/1174 date is contradicted on this point.

### EVD-016021 — 1283 repair and endowment inscription

- **Observation:** Sabatier's modern page displays a French translation attributed to the lintel text catalogued as RCEA 4820 and dates it to Rabīʿ II 682/July 1283. The translation represents Nūr al-Dīn as having established a waqf for the hospital, describes poor and needy Muslim sick expected to recover, and records repair under Qalāwūn and a named supervisor.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016003, displayed nine-line French translation, identified there as RCEA 4820.
- **Rating:** **L3-B for the opened modern conduit only**. RCEA 4820 was not opened or rated as verified evidence.
- **Inference limit:** Arabic lines, reading/restoration history, and translation authority remain unverified. “Expected to recover” may be a legal formula or translator choice. The claim remains attributed/HOLD and cannot establish access or outcomes.

### EVD-016022 — supervisor, rounds, and teaching

- **Observation:** In a biography of Abū l-Majd ibn Abī l-Ḥakam, Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah says Nūr al-Dīn founded the Great Hospital, put Abū l-Majd in charge of medical teaching with salary/allowance, and describes hospital rounds with ward overseers/superintendents followed by consultation and roughly three hours of teaching or study with books kept in cupboards.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016000, `15.9`.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for the authored passage; **B/C** for actual routine.
- **Inference limit:** this supports a literary report of staffing, rounds, and teaching, not a complete organization chart or daily time-motion record. The passage does not report admissions or outcomes.

### EVD-016023 — a narrated `māniyā` case

- **Observation:** In a hospital ward described as serving bilious patients, the author says al-Dakhwār treated one person with `māniyā`, further glossed in the text as `al-junūn al-sabuʿī`, by administering ample opium in barley water; the author reports immediate improvement.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016000, `15.50.4`.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for the narrative; **C/D** for diagnosis, routine, or efficacy.
- **Inference limit:** retain the historical terms and translation problem. This is one physician-authored anecdote, not evidence for a specialized psychiatric ward, a modern diagnosis, safety, durable recovery, or treatment rate.

### EVD-016024 — a narrated adverse outcome and mediated distrust

- **Observation:** A story about a patient with dropsy describes a surgeon draining fluid while another physician watched the pulse. The patient's wife warned him not to remove the dressing, but he distrusted the physicians, wanted to return to work, removed it, and died in the author's account.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016000, `15.23.4.2`.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for the narrative; **C** for lived experience and causation.
- **Inference limit:** this is rare evidence of patient preference, work pressure, distrust, family negotiation, and death, but all are mediated through a physician's moralizing account that assigns blame. The clinical cause of death cannot be recovered.

### EVD-016025 — practitioner training and patient-directed food

- **Observation:** Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah presents himself as having trained at the Great Hospital under named physicians, witnessing diagnosis and treatment. He praises a teacher who ordered vegetarian dishes according to patients' desires and says chronically ill people recovered.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016000, `15.40.9` and `15.50.3`.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for self-representation; **C/D** for effectiveness and representativeness.
- **Inference limit:** teacher praise and claimed recovery are not independent outcome measures. Patient desire appears only through practitioner narration.

### EVD-016026 — later ward expansion and running water

- **Observation:** The biography says Badr al-Dīn purchased adjacent houses, incorporated them into the hospital, enlarged small rooms into patient wards, plastered them, and installed running water.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016000, `15.52.1`.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for the passage; **B/C** for completed building history.
- **Inference limit:** the passage does not give an exact construction date in this locator, nor prove how every space was used after alteration.

### EVD-016027 — Ibn Jubayr's “new” Damascus hospital

- **Observation:** Ibn Jubayr distinguishes a new and an old Damascus hospital. He calls the new one the more elaborate/larger and reports its daily allowance, administrators holding registers with patient names and expenses for medicines and foods, and physicians visiting daily to inspect patients and direct suitable medicines and foods.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016005, 1907 Arabic edition, printed p. 283, page-image lines 8–14; PDF scan page 138.
- **Original text checked against page image:** `وبها مارستانان قديم وحديث، والحديث أحفلهما وأكبرهما، وجرايته في اليوم نحو خمسة عشر دينارا، وله قومة بأيديهم الأزِمّة المحتوية على أسماء المرضى وعلى النفقات التي يحتاجون إليها في الأدوية والأغذية وغير ذلك، والأطباء يبكرون إليه في كل يوم ويتفقدون المرضى ويأمرون بإعداد ما يصلحهم من الأدوية والأغذية حسبما يليق بكل إنسان منهم`.
- **Translation status:** no named modern translation was opened. The observation is a conservative project paraphrase after syntax review of the printed Arabic on 2026-08-18; it is not approved public translation.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for Ibn Jubayr's report; **C** for routine and identification.
- **Inference limit:** the page does not name the new institution al-Nūrī in this passage. It is traveler narration, not an administrative register, recipient testimony, or outcomes evidence. Old-hospital restraint is separated in EVD-016044.

## Evidence stack B — al-Bīmāristān al-Manṣūrī/Qalāwūn, Cairo

### EVD-016028 — dated construction and surviving fabric

- **Observation:** Monument documentation identifies the Qalāwūn complex's hospital, mausoleum, and madrasa. A foundation lintel dates commencement to 683 AH/1284 CE and completion to 684 AH/1285 CE; much of the hospital fabric is now lost.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016006, `Date of Monument`, `Description`, `How Monument Was Dated`.
- **Rating:** **L3-A**.
- **Inference limit:** architecture establishes the built complex, not admissions, staffing, a mental ward, or outcomes. The Egyptian Ministry's 683–684 AH / 1283–84 rendering is a conversion difference to record rather than silently harmonize.

### EVD-016029 — waqf eligibility, duration, discharge, and exclusion norms

- **Observation:** The source-linked project and Kubo report that Qalāwūn's foundation instruments admitted Muslim patients without a fixed stay limit, directed discharge clothing or money and funeral support, and excluded Jews and Christians from patient and management roles. The dated documentary layer includes 12 Ṣafar 685/9 April 1286 and related instruments.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016008, `Hospital`, citing *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* 1:358–59, 363, 367; SRC-016009, pp. 118–119 and the same deed locators.
- **Rating:** **L2/L3-B for the opened Kubo and Qalawun VR conduits**. The cited deed was not opened and receives no verified packet rating.
- **Inference limit:** the evidence establishes what the modern authors report about a deed, not the deed's directly collated wording. Reported norms are not implementation. The two modern sources repeat one upstream chain; inclusion and exclusion must remain paired.

### EVD-016030 — home support as a waqf norm

- **Observation:** Kubo reports that the cited deed directs food, drink, and medicine for patients treated at home and that later al-Nuwayrī states a daily number of 200.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016009, p. 119, citing *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* 1:367 and al-Nuwayrī, *Nihāyat al-Arab* 31:108.
- **Rating:** **L2-B for Kubo's opened analysis**. Neither cited Arabic source was opened or assigned a verified L1 rating.
- **Inference limit:** this establishes an attributed scholarly report only. Keep reported deed norm and reported later implementation separate. The number is not an audit, roster, or outcome measure.

### EVD-016031 — departments and chronology seam

- **Observation:** Qalawun VR and Kubo report that the cited foundation-document chain names internal medicine, ophthalmology, and surgery, while later al-Nuwayrī adds orthopedics as a fourth division.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016008, `Hospital`, citing *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* 1:363, 367 and *Nihāyat al-Arab* 31:107; SRC-016009, pp. 118–119, note 16, citing *Nihāyat al-Arab* 31:105–110.
- **Rating:** **L2/L3-B for the opened modern conduits**. The cited deed and chronicle were not opened or rated as verified L1 evidence.
- **Inference limit:** the proposition must remain attributed. Do not back-project the later fourth division or infer a psychiatric/music ward.

### EVD-016032 — dated chief-physician and teaching appointments

- **Observation:** Fernandes identifies two chancery instruments dated 684 AH/1285 CE appointing a chief physician and a medical teacher/chair for the institution.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016010, pp. 111–112; underlying instruments in Ibn al-Furāt 8:22–25 / Ḥamāda pp. 324–327 and al-Qalqashandī 11:253–256.
- **Rating:** **L2-A/B for Fernandes's document identification**. The identified instruments were not opened or rated as verified evidence.
- **Inference limit:** the packet has not collated the Arabic texts. Fernandes's identification does not prove exact wording, attendance, curriculum, patient volume, or success.

### EVD-016033 — later chronicler's staffing and spatial report

- **Observation:** Qalawun VR's displayed English translation attributed to the fifteenth-century historian al-Maqrīzī reports paid male and female personnel, beds, patient division by condition and sex, rooms for ophthalmology, surgery, diarrhea, and women, and sex-separated mortuary work.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016008, `Hospital`, quoting/citing al-Maqrīzī, *Khiṭaṭ* 4:696.
- **Rating:** **L3-B for the opened university-project conduit**. The full Arabic passage was not opened or rated as verified L1 evidence.
- **Inference limit:** claim only what the project translation reports. It is later retrospective evidence, not foundation-year observation, and the displayed text does not establish a psychiatric ward.

### EVD-016034 — archive boundary and reconstruction dispute

- **Observation:** Fancy notes the survival of the Mansuri waqf deed and two 1285 decrees but says there is no surviving documentary evidence for day-to-day practice. He criticizes reconstructions in which hypothetical spaces harden into asserted specialist halls.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016011, pp. 138–139, 141–146, especially p. 143.
- **Rating:** **L2-B**.
- **Inference limit:** this is historiographical criticism, not proof that no practice occurred. It establishes a publication burden: label reconstructions and avoid treating plans or later narratives as direct observation.

## Cross-case priority and treatment audits

### EVD-016035 — category-dependent priority

- **Observation:** Horden defines a hospital as a distinct, permanent structure offering overnight accommodation and relief to poor and/or sick people, challenges simple Jundishapur and Umayyad origin stories, and places attested Islamic hospitals before the two selected foundations. Independently, Savage-Smith's official open synthesis identifies a possibly circa-800 Baghdad foundation, several named late-ninth-/early-tenth-century foundations, and the 981 ʿAḍudī hospital.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016013, pp. 369–371, 382–383, definition p. 371; SRC-016001, section `1. Hospitals and Institutions`, paragraphs on Abbasid hospitals in Baghdad.
- **Rating:** **L2-B**, with two independently authored syntheses and partly overlapping historiography.
- **Inference limit:** priority depends on definition and surviving evidence. This is sufficient to reject first-hospital claims for these twelfth- and thirteenth-century cases, not to announce one uncontested global first. Horden must be replayed through an authorized publisher/library full-text route before publication.

### EVD-016036 — medieval Mansuri music chain fails

- **Observation:** Dols's statement about music at the Manṣūrī in the medieval period rests, in note 54, on “Prise d'Avennes, *L'art Arabe*, Paris, 1887, p. 138.” Library of Congress, Qatar Digital Library, Bibliotheca Alexandrina, and national-library catalogues identify the four-volume work as published in 1877 (with parts issued 1869–1877), so Dols's 1887 is a bibliographic error or unresolved edition reference. The exact text-volume p. 138 and any authority it cites were not opened.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016012, pp. 8–9, note 54.
- **Rating:** **L2-C/D** for Dols's secondary assertion; SRC-016016 is an opened institutional catalogue chain and unopened-text lead.
- **Inference limit:** the claim remains HOLD. It cannot support a music ward, a foundation-era practice, frequency, patient selection, or therapeutic outcome.

### EVD-016037 — Nūrī music belongs to a later observation layer

- **Observation:** Dols's Nūrī concert statement depends on Evliya Çelebi's report of a 1648 visit.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016012, pp. 8–9, **note 55**, citing *Seyahatname* (Istanbul, 1971), vol. 13, p. 268; SRC-016017.
- **Rating:** **L2-C for Dols's report**; the cited Evliya edition is an unopened L5 lead, not verified L1 evidence.
- **Inference limit:** even if verified, a 1648 report cannot establish 1154 foundation practice, a medieval music ward, continuity, or outcome. It belongs in an early-modern handoff.

### EVD-016038 — “occupational therapy” chain absent

- **Observation:** The bounded audit in SRC-016015 found modern repetitions of “occupational therapy” but no opened institution-specific description for al-Nūrī or al-Manṣūrī naming an activity, actor, purpose, selection rule, or outcome. Carnevali and Masillo, pp. 100–101, use the label in a broad sentence without an institution-specific citation for that clause; two popular 2025–26 pages repeat analogies without upstream medieval locators.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016015, queries and exemplar log dated 2026-08-18; Carnevali and Masillo, *JISHIM* 6–7 (2007–08), pp. 100–101.
- **Rating:** bounded audit result; **LX-D for affirmative institution-specific use**.
- **Inference limit:** this is not proof that purposeful activity never occurred. It permits only the corpus-bounded negative and does not turn lead repetitions into evidence.

### EVD-016039 — humane-care generalization is not recipient evidence

- **Observation:** Dols both characterizes some learned treatment traditions as rational/humane and documents confinement, chains, and the difficulty of reconstructing actual care. Ibn Jubayr specifically reports treatment and chains in the old Damascus hospital. Neither source supplies recipient evaluation of “humaneness.”
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016012, pp. 1–3, 7–12; EVD-016044 / SRC-016005, printed p. 283, lines 14–16.
- **Rating:** **L2-B/C** and **L1-C**.
- **Inference limit:** “humane” is a modern/historian evaluative category. Specific provisions and restraints can be described; a universal moral verdict cannot.

### EVD-016040 — no institutional outcomes dataset

- **Observation:** Savage-Smith states that surviving sources do not provide patient counts, disease statistics, or success rates. The packet found anecdotes and prescriptive documents, not denominators, follow-up series, or recipient-reported outcomes.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016001, section `1. Hospitals and Institutions`; cross-check against SRC-016000 case form.
- **Rating:** **L2-A/B**.
- **Inference limit:** narrated recovery or death can be reported as narration, not generalized into institutional effectiveness.

### EVD-016041 — retired composite endowment/governance record

- **Status:** **REJECTED / RETIRED 2026-08-18.** The identifier remains reserved, but this
  record must not support a claim or relationship.

- **Former observation:** Al-Nūrī's 1283 inscription conduit and the Manṣūrī waqf/appointment
  conduits were grouped into one comparison. That composite duplicated `EVD-016021`,
  `EVD-016029`, and `EVD-016032` while merging distinct institutions and documentary layers.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016003, RCEA 4820; SRC-016010, pp. 111–112; SRC-016008, *Tadhkirat al-Nabīh* 1:358–59, 363, 366–67.
- **Rating:** **L2/L3-B for the opened conduits**; inscriptions/deeds named but unopened.
- **Retirement reason:** the narrower records preserve the usable observations and their source
  layers. Keeping this composite active would obscure institutional attribution and source
  dependence. Its lack of a typed relationship is now intentional, not a graph omission.

### EVD-016042 — pharmacy evidence gap

- **Observation:** The opened sources report medicines, prescriptions, syrups, and substances, but this packet did not recover a directly located deed, room record, inventory, payroll, object assemblage, or named worker that establishes an on-site pharmacy at either selected institution.
- **Locator / source:** negative audit across SRC-016000, SRC-016008–SRC-016010.
- **Rating:** affirmative pharmacy claim **HOLD / fit D**.
- **Inference limit:** drug administration does not by itself prove a dedicated pharmacy, its space, staffing, procurement, or dispensing system.

### EVD-016043 — current site and heritage-custody boundary

- **Observation:** As opened on 2026-08-18, the Egyptian Ministry page states that the Qalāwūn bīmāristān “now serves as an eye and dentistry hospital” and lists monument hours/tickets. Its adjacent unsourced sentence about medieval nervous/psychological treatment remains excluded. For Damascus, TDV's 2007 museum description is historical metadata, not a current-use source. UNESCO's 2024–26 Ancient City of Damascus documents show city-wide danger/conservation context but do not establish al-Nūrī's present use, custodian, access, condition, conflict/earthquake effects, or image permissions.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016007, monument page lines/paragraphs `Description`, `Opening Hours`, accessed 2026-08-18; SRC-016002, pp. 262–263 (2007 statement only); SRC-016019, Ancient City of Damascus documents index and 2024–26 records.
- **Rating:** **L3-A** for the Egyptian Ministry's current official representation; **L3-C** for UNESCO's city-wide context; **Unknown** for institution-specific current al-Nūrī status.
- **Inference limit:** current official representation is time-sensitive and not independently observed operation. Present use and medieval operation remain separate. Recheck both sites immediately before public use.

### EVD-016044 — Ibn Jubayr's “old” Damascus hospital, treatment, and chains

- **Observation:** After saying the other hospital followed the same general pattern but the new received greater attention, Ibn Jubayr identifies the old hospital west of the Great Mosque and specifically locates there confined people called mad, a form of treatment, and chains.
- **Locator / source:** SRC-016005, 1907 Arabic edition, printed p. 283, page-image lines 14–16; PDF scan page 138.
- **Original text checked against page image:** `والمارستان الآخر على هذا الرسم لكن الاحتفال في الجديد أكثر، وهذا القديم هو غربي الجامع المكرم، وللمجانين المعتقلين فيه أيضا ضرب من العلاج وهم في سلاسل موثقون، نعوذ بالله من المحنة وسوء القدر`.
- **Translation status:** no named modern translation was opened. The observation is a conservative project paraphrase after syntax review of the printed Arabic on 2026-08-18; it is not approved public translation.
- **Rating:** **L1-A** for Ibn Jubayr's report; **C** for routine, category, and recipient experience.
- **Inference limit:** this is specifically the old hospital, not the new hospital and not automatically al-Nūrī. The travel text provides no individual identity, admission reason, consent, duration, recipient speech, or outcome. The historical category must not be converted into a modern diagnosis.

## Findings synthesis

The defensible comparison is not “two psychiatric hospitals.” It is two general charitable-medical institutions documented through different surviving layers. Al-Nūrī has an unusually rich physician-biographical case corpus, including one bounded mental-distress treatment story and one adverse-outcome story with mediated patient/family voices. Al-Manṣūrī has stronger modern access to monument records and scholarly reports of waqf/appointment documents but much weaker direct evidence for daily practice or patient experience. Ibn Jubayr's new-hospital administrative description and old-hospital restraint report remain separate and neither is silently equated with al-Nūrī. The archive asymmetry must not be smoothed into parallel narratives.
