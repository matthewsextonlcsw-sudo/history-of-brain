# R35 lived experience, creator agency and archive audit

## Current voice verdict

**The opened corpus contains two direct later participant positions and no authenticated direct
creator testimony about retention, consent, title, interpretation, possession or circulation.**

- Mavignier is direct for what he chose to write in 1998 about his own earlier work. He is not a
  creator proxy, registrar, legal witness for every object, or contemporaneous administrator.
- Silveira is direct for what she told Leal in 1992. When she reports Fernando Diniz's words,
  the speech is third-party mediated.
- The current Fernando profile is institutional representation. Quotation marks do not replace a
  recording date, interviewer, transcript, accession, context, editorial chain or permission.
- Magaldi and Frayze-Pereira make creator agency and representation visible as research problems.
  Their analysis is not direct creator testimony.
- An artwork is a created object and may be evidence for an appropriately governed art-historical
  question. It is not consent to collection, a statement about title, or permission to reproduce
  or narrate medical history. R35 uses no artwork.

## Archive-power audit

- The surviving/opened story is weighted toward a founder-clinician, a prominent artist-monitor,
  current institutions and later scholars. People whose works formed the collection are most
  often represented through those actors.
- Current collection scale, heritage status and institutional longevity can visually overwhelm
  the smaller historical question of how any one object moved. R35 refuses that scale shortcut.
- Clinical and museum classification may cause the same object to be treated as treatment
  material, research document, artwork, collection object and heritage object. Those labels can
  change authority claims without recording the creator's own view.
- Current MII, municipal, SAMII and IPHAN interests may overlap but are not interchangeable.
  Repository custody is not necessarily ownership; heritage authority is not copyright;
  possession is not creator consent; research access is not publication permission.
- Later scholarly fieldwork reports present-day disputes over creators taking work, sale,
  authorship and representation. It shows that the problem is real; it does not prove that one
  practice operated continuously from 1946 to 1979.
- The personal archive reportedly donated after Silveira's death is a separate body from workshop
  objects. Its custody cannot be used as a shortcut for the collection's object-level chain.

## Required mediation fields after acquisition

For every creator statement, worker statement, administrative file and object-flow record:

- repository, collection, series, box/folder, item/accession and supplied order;
- complete page/folio/image sequence, covers, enclosures, annotations, blanks and missing leaves;
- document date, creation purpose, author, signatory, addressee, typist, copyist, translator,
  witness, certifier, later annotator and institutional chain;
- whether words are written, dictated, elicited, translated, summarized, reported, copied,
  attributed later, or supplied by an institution;
- Portuguese wording and any other language present, with qualified transcription/translation;
- clinical, administrative, museum, curatorial, legal or publicity context;
- object identifier, creator attribution basis, creation date/range, workshop source, material
  only when ethically necessary for internal provenance, and every linked ledger movement;
- offer, gift, deposit, custody, loan, accession, exhibition, return, refusal, withdrawal, sale,
  transfer and reproduction authorization as separate acts;
- whether the creator knew of, initiated, agreed to, contested, withdrew from, or was absent from
  each act—using `Unknown` unless a record directly establishes it;
- privacy, health-information, dignity, creator/heir, family, object-rights, custodial and public
  indexing decisions.

## Portuguese, legal and specialist gates

1. **Brazilian Portuguese textual review:** period institutional, museum, legal and psychiatric
   language; exact editions; quotation and translation control; no English-first synthesis.
2. **Archival/provenance and museum registration:** creator-by-creator and object-by-object chain;
   accession, deposit, loan, exhibition, return and transfer cannot be collapsed.
3. **Brazilian legal history and counsel:** applicable 1946–1973 Civil Code/copyright and
   administrative regime; Lei 5.988/1973 from 1974, including article 80's plastic-art default;
   current Lei 9.610/1998 including the materially different article 77 default; transitional law,
   alienation/object facts, contracts, succession, moral rights, privacy and
   limitations/exceptions. No legal conclusion from a statute alone.
4. **Creator/heir authority:** identify creators or heirs only through a separately approved,
   privacy-preserving protocol; seek views on attribution, quotation, description, reproduction,
   narration, cultural placement and benefit where feasible.
5. **Disability/survivor review:** reject incapacity-by-diagnosis, therapeutic-benefit-by-art,
   gratitude, rescue, genius-from-illness and founder-savior framings.
6. **Municipal/MII/SAMII/IPHAN authority:** obtain instruments defining current custody,
   administration, heritage protection, access, preservation, cataloguing and reuse—without
   treating one body as authority for every category.
7. **Worker and labor history:** include monitors, nurses, attendants, conservators, registrars,
   cataloguers, administrators, cleaners and 1979 technical staff; do not reduce work to one
   clinician or one artist.
8. **Human editorial and rights review:** mandatory before any public wording, quotation,
   description, metadata, timeline entry or asset proposal.

## Privacy, dignity and harm controls

- No artwork image or description, including a verbal workaround that makes a sensitive work
  searchable or identifiable.
- No creator diagnosis, symptom story, involuntary-status inference, recovery claim, treatment
  success claim, capacity conclusion, or statement that art reveals an inner diagnosis.
- No medical file, signature, handwriting, face, family detail, searchable object roster or
  creator-to-diagnosis graph.
- No unattributed or institutionally attributed creator quotation.
- No assumption that a deceased person has no privacy, dignity, family or heir interests.
- No claim that retention preserved work lawfully, dispossessed creators, constituted theft,
  prevented theft, or served creators' wishes until evidence can bear that exact burden.
- No sensational contrast between institutional harm and artistic brilliance.
- No use of creators as atmosphere around a founder biography.

## Missing voices that must remain visible

- every creator whose output entered, did not enter, left, returned to, or was refused by the
  collection;
- creators who declined, contested, requested, gifted, deposited, sold, withdrew or destroyed
  work, if records exist;
- family members, visitors and advocates involved in object movement or creator decisions;
- monitors and subordinate workshop workers beyond Mavignier;
- nurses, attendants, porters, clerks, cleaners, conservators, registrars, cataloguers,
  photographers and administrative staff;
- SAMII founders/members beyond the later founder narrative;
- FINEP reviewers, grantee staff, trainers and technical workers;
- people whose work or words were excluded, dispersed, lost, deaccessioned or never catalogued;
- creators and workers whose records survive only through institutional paraphrase.

No absence authorizes reconstruction of inner experience. If the frozen records are acquired and
still contain only institutional voices, that limit must remain the finding.
