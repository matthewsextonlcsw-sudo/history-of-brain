# R35 Brazil MII acquisition findings

## Verdict first

**STOP — the workshop-to-collection question is not substantively runnable from the opened
corpus.** Later participant recollections, current institutional timelines, present heritage
metadata, later scholarship, and statutes can map the questions and acquisition route. They do
not establish the contemporaneous acts, object flows, creator decisions, worker practices,
custody chain, title, consent, contracts, or 1979 program delivery needed to answer the packet's
question.

This is not a finding that relevant records never existed. It is a finding that the decisive
objects have not been completely and lawfully opened, ordered, page-anchored, language-checked,
rights-audited, and independently replayed.

## What the opened corpus can establish

1. Mavignier wrote a dated 1998 retrospective reporting his 1946–1951 workshop role, an exact
   opening date, and one gift/deposit episode. That directly establishes his later account, not
   the underlying event, a general policy, creator authorization, title, or legal deposit.
2. Silveira's interview was recorded in 1992 and published in 1994. It directly establishes her
   later statements. Her reported Fernando Diniz words remain third-party mediated because no
   original recording/transcript or provenance record was supplied.
3. Current MII and CCMS pages present a reported 1946–1979 institutional event spine. Their
   early exhibition positions are not atomic equivalents: CCMS reports São Paulo/Rio under
   1947/1950, while Magaldi reconstructs 1949/1949. Neither is contemporaneous control.
   Current IPHAN metadata separately distinguishes the webpage publication on 4 November 2021
   from the instrument's DOU publication on 29 October 2021. None is a missing founding,
   exhibition, custody, SAMII, FINEP, municipal-transfer or object-flow record.
4. Magaldi's collection genealogy makes creator agency, possession, interpretation, retention,
   and representation legitimate research questions. Its early chronology is not independent of
   the same retrospective/institutional lineages and does not replace administrative or object
   records.
5. The 1973 and 1998 statutes demonstrate that authorship, ownership of a physical original,
   patrimonial rights, moral rights, authorization, exhibition, and reproduction are legally
   distinguishable categories. The 1973 article 80 and 1998 article 77 plastic-art defaults also
   differ materially. Neither applies itself to an MII object; period facts, contracts,
   succession, provenance, transitional analysis and Brazilian legal review remain required.
6. The current English Pedrosa page reports a 31 March 1947 talk and publication on 13 and 21
   April, while a downstream 7 February candidate remains unresolved. It cannot control a date,
   quotation or edition claim because the Portuguese witnesses, translator, page concordance,
   and editorial apparatus were not opened.
