## Person/status/gender/access controls

### Angkorian formula

- `catvāro varṇā` is retained as Sanskrit and translated only with an explicit historical-category
  note. It must not become “everyone,” “all citizens,” “all classes equally,” or modern equity.
- Male and female roles are enumerated asymmetrically. The formula assigns two women with each of
  two physicians and names women in heating/grinding/rice work. It does not provide names, status,
  remuneration, voluntariness, mobility, authority, or first-person appraisal.
- Workers' protection from tax/other labor and the punishment exception are prescriptive legal-
  administrative language. Do not infer enforcement, sanctuary experience, leniency, coercion, or
  actual violence rates.
- No evidence identifies poverty, enslavement status, foreigners, disability category, age, family
  accompaniment, or consent of recipients.

### Tonle Snguot

- Material finds cannot be assigned to elite, commoner, military, foreign, patient, healer, or
  attendant persons without independent contextual evidence.
- The report's broad access categories are interpretation, not excavated identities. The authors
  explicitly state that payment is unknown.
- Statuary and ceremony are culturally sensitive. The current ritual is part of contemporary
  field/custodial relations, not a proxy for medieval patient belief.

### *Dharma Pātañjala*

- The dialogue's speakers are divine/literary figures, not observed practitioners.
- “The yogin” is a normative textual subject, not a demographic sample. No opened source establishes
  gender, caste/status, wealth, institutional admission, teaching relationship, or lay access.
- “Pleasure,” mind-stillness, and supernatural attainment must remain within the translated
  soteriological argument. Recasting them as subjective wellness outcomes invents experience.

## Custody, colonial collection, and community protocol

### Cambodia

- Tonle Snguot finds/samples are reported stored with APSARA Authority. The report's maps/images,
  statues, and ceremony are not cleared for Couch Commons reuse merely because the PDF is public.
- Before public visual use: obtain object/image permission from the stated rights holder and consult
  APSARA or the relevant Cambodian heritage authority about sacred imagery and ceremony.
- Name present-day Lao geography for Say Fong and Cambodian/Khmer political-cultural context without
  treating modern borders as medieval borders or erasing present custodial communities.

### Indonesia / Berlin

- Schoemann I 21 is presently in Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin. The 2024 institutional essay says its
  Merapi–Merbabu/Windu Sono origin is based on nineteenth-century reports; keep “reportedly.”
- Its route through Friederich and Schoemann to Berlin is a colonial-era collection history, not a
  neutral transmission footnote. Do not reproduce folios until SBB rights and relevant Indonesian
  cultural consultation are resolved.
- This packet did not identify a living community body authorized to speak for the manuscript.
  That gap forbids claims of community approval, continuity, or ownership resolution.

## Lived-experience questions that remain unanswered

1. What did a sick visitor expect, fear, choose, refuse, pay, or receive at an Angkorian foundation?
2. Were staff assignments fulfilled, coerced, remunerated, hereditary, rotating, or aspirational?
3. Did the four-`varṇa` rule change access in practice, and who remained unnamed by it?
4. What activities occurred in domestic/work areas around Tonle Snguot, and which finds—if any—can
   be securely connected to therapeutic encounters?
5. Who copied, taught, heard, or practiced DhP material, and how did they describe its effects?
6. How do Cambodian and Indonesian scholars, custodians, religious practitioners, and local
   communities want these objects/texts framed today?

## Empathy and language rules for later writing

- Say “people represented as sick in the inscription,” not “mental patients.”
- Attribute royal compassion language to the inscription; do not infer that recipients felt cared for.
- Do not romanticize hospitals as benevolent, call them brutal without evidence, or stage a modern
  religion-versus-science contest.
- Do not use “primitive,” “superstitious,” “exotic,” “mystical East,” or “timeless wisdom.”
- Do not diagnose historical actors or convert `manoroga`, `citta`, `samādhi`, `sukha`, or
  `ārogyaśālā` into modern clinical equivalents without a translation note and specialist approval.
- Preserve uncertainty as part of the story: royal intent, material traces, and human experience are
  different questions.

## Archive-limit statement

The opened core preserves powerful institutional and learned voices but no first-person patient or
practitioner account. `CLM-022014` is limited to these opened records. It does not assert that no
relevant inscriptions, vernacular traditions, later copies, oral histories, community knowledge, or
archaeological evidence survive elsewhere. Oral/ethnographic sources were not used to back-project
medieval experience because a responsible transmission chain was not established.
