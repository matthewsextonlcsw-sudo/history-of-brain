# R13-SOUTHEAST-ASIA-MEDIEVAL — Named Courts, Temples, Manuscripts, and Local Care Worlds

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `022000–022999`

## Research question

What can a small set of securely located Southeast Asian inscriptions, manuscripts, medical or
ritual texts, court/temple records, archaeological contexts, and later-recorded traditions from
approximately 600 to 1500 establish about mind, emotion, suffering, illness, healing, family,
ritual, and care?

## Boundary

Choose no more than three named sites, polities, communities, or corpus settings in no more than
two connected subregions. Khmer, Mon, Burmese, Thai, Malay, Javanese, Balinese, Cham, Pali,
Sanskrit, Chinese, Arabic, or other evidence enters only with named local and language context.
Later manuscripts and oral recordings must retain their own dates and cannot transparently fill
medieval gaps.

## Required evidence

- primary inscriptions, manuscripts, objects, archaeological reports, or accountable
  translations with exact locators;
- locally grounded specialist archaeology, history of medicine/religion, philology, gender,
  disability, household, court, and social history;
- local vocabulary and translation cautions without a single “Southeast Asian” worldview;
- court/temple norm versus village/household practice and material access;
- evidence for healers, monastics, ritual specialists, family caregivers, workers, women,
  children, poor or dependent people, and affected persons where it survives;
- named exchange/contact chains, including what changed locally and what is only parallel;
- colonial collection, war, excavation, custody, sacred knowledge, current community, and image
  protocol audit; and
- audits of Indianization-as-copying, Buddhist/Hindu therapy continuity, spirit-belief-only,
  and timeless traditional medicine claims.

## Stop

Do not write a regional survey, infer mental-health practice from iconography alone, or turn a
later ethnography into direct medieval testimony. Create five outputs and rights-safe ignored
cache; no public prose.
