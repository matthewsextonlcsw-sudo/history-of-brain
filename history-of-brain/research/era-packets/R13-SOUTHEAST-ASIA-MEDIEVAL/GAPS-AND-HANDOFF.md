## Known source limitations and holds

- K.209 is a royal prescription/eulogy; K.368 is a dependent parallel. Repetition is evidence of a
  formula/network program, not multiple independent observations of care.
- K.368 is damaged at the critical stanza XIII phrase; K.209 is clearer but still edition-mediated.
- Honda omits translation of stanzas 19b–41 and overextends provisions to poor relief.
- Tonle Snguot is preliminary and tiny relative to the site; broad health/access claims rate D.
- Schoemann I 21 is a codex unicus copied in 1467; composition date and original circulation are
  uncertain. The opened interpretive sources largely share one editor/author.
- Current ceremony and custody cannot be used as medieval ethnographic analogy.
- No efficacy, diagnosis, prevalence, payment, consent, coercion, or patient-satisfaction claim is
  supportable.
