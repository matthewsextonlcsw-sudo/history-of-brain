## Citation-chain holds

### HOLD-002000 — “Oldest epilepsy account”

The recoverable chain begins with two tablet witnesses as edited by SRC-002005. That paper
both translates the signs and applies modern epilepsy categories, then calls the account the
oldest then known. The priority clause depends on:

1. the date assigned to composition rather than only surviving copies;
2. the boundaries of “epilepsy” and “account”;
3. global survival and discovery, which cannot be demonstrated exhaustively;
4. the paper's retrospective diagnostic mappings; and
5. later critical editions not reviewed here.

**Handoff decision:** do not use “oldest” in the outline except inside a historiographical
myth/dispute node attributed to the 1990 authors.

### HOLD-002001 — “Oldest psychosis of epilepsy”

Only SRC-002009's abstract was accessible. It supplies modern categories but not enough
tablet/line/edition information to audit them. It is the same Reynolds/Kinnier Wilson
lineage as the epilepsy and depression papers.

**Handoff decision:** omit from the outline until the full article and upstream edition are
read. An abstract is a lead, not evidence.

### HOLD-002002 — “First clinical foundations of neurology and psychiatry”

SRC-002010's abstract aggregates the same authors' retrospective diagnoses and declares a
first. SRC-002000, SRC-002003, and SRC-002004 provide direct methodological reasons not
to turn those mappings into native categories.

**Handoff decision:** reject as a historical milestone. If retained for audience education,
frame it as a modern origin story and show the shared citation chain.

## Date, geography, and manuscript cautions

- Do not use “ancient Mesopotamia” as if it were one synchronic culture. Every public node
  should specify language, city/findspot if known, manuscript date, proposed composition
  date, genre, and edition.
- “Babylonian” can denote language, literary tradition, political era, or geographic origin.
  Select the intended meaning and show it.
- *Ludlul* is a multi-witness Standard Babylonian composition. Its text is reconstructed
  from manuscripts across several cities; no single manuscript equals the entire modern
  edition (SRC-002001).
- Sakikkû tablet 25/26 in SRC-002005 uses a Sultantepe witness and an unprovenanced British
  Museum witness. “Found in Babylon” would be false for the latter.
- The unusually rich early-seventh-century BCE Neo-Assyrian court record is not a default
  model for earlier/later or non-court practice (SRC-002007, pp. 85–86).
- The packet has no strong evidence for the earliest part of the authorized 3200–500 BCE
  boundary. An outline must show this as an evidence gap rather than filling it with generic
  “Sumerians believed” claims.
