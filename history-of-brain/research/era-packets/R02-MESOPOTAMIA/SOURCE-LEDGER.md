## Corpus limitations recorded at source level

- Most surviving diagnostic and therapeutic witnesses are learned, scribal, and heavily
  weighted toward the first millennium BCE; an ancient composition date is not the same as
  the date of a surviving copy (SRC-002003, pp. 140–142; SRC-002007, pp. 3–8).
- Provenance is sometimes archaeological, sometimes inferred, and sometimes absent. BM
  47753 in SRC-002005 is unprovenanced; do not silently convert “likely Babylonian” into a
  findspot.
- A quoted first person can belong to a ritual script or literary composition rather than a
  recoverable individual (SRC-002001; SRC-002002; SRC-002004, pp. 265–267).
- English words such as “depression,” “heartbreak,” “mind,” “physician,” and “exorcist” are
  interpretive translations, not stable one-to-one equivalents (SRC-002000, pp. 122–124,
  169–225; SRC-002007, pp. 3–4).
- Claims about preparations, rubbing, recitation, figurines, offerings, plants, beer, oil,
  and food describe what the surviving texts prescribe or represent. They do not establish
  archaeological frequency, delivery, compliance, acceptance, or efficacy. Material-culture
  claims remain GAP-002012.
- BAM 202, BAM 316, BAM 323, KAR 32, Suālu IV, CT 4 8a, and LKA 154/157 are currently
  mediated by translations reproduced in SRC-002000/003/004. They are adequate for
  attributed outline mapping, not L1 line-level quotation, until each critical edition,
  translator/editor, witness, and relevant variant is opened.
