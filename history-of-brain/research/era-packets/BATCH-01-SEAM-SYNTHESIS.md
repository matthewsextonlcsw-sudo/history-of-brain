## Chronological assembly decisions

### 1. Before writing is a methods opening, not an imagined origin story

`R01-METHOD` supports a public-facing evidence lesson built from a few bounded cases. The
sequence must remain:

1. physical observation;
2. survival or chronology inference;
3. functional inference;
4. possible assistance;
5. modeled care scenarios; and
6. an explicit stop before motive, compassion, stigma, psychiatric purpose, or subjective
   experience.

The timeline must not call any reviewed case the first surgery, first healthcare, first
compassion, or first social care. No direct voice survives in these cases.

### 2. Writing changes the archive, not the existence of mental life

Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Levantine, and Greek texts add genres, named terms, prescriptions,
institutional voices, literary speakers, translations, and textual histories. They do not mark
the beginning of distress, thought, emotion, coping, disability, or care. The transition from
`T00` to `T01` must say that surviving words change what can be studied.

### 3. “First written worlds” cannot be one synchronous civilization chapter

The internal outline should keep distinct regional lanes:

- Mesopotamian technical, ritual, literary, and learned textual networks;
- Egyptian medical, mortuary, literary, household, and administrative evidence;
- Nubian and Kushite material and heritage evidence on independent chronologies;
- selected Levantine genres, languages, manuscripts, and translation histories; and
- later-overlapping Greek-speaking medical, philosophical, sanctuary, household, and material
  evidence.

Their date ranges overlap. They should meet through named handoffs, not a universal “ancient
world believed” summary.

### 4. The Greek packet is not the climax of the earlier packets

`R05-GREEK` supports plurality: brain-centered, heart-centered, medical, philosophical,
regimen, sanctuary, and household evidence overlapped. It does not support a plot in which
Greek reason defeated earlier religion or invented modern neuroscience. The Greek lane begins
another set of contested traditions and exchanges; it does not resolve the prior lanes.

## Cross-packet findings that survive the seam audit

### Body, heart, breath, spirit, soul, and mind are not interchangeable tags

The packets repeatedly show that English “mind” can hide materially different words, genres,
social relations, and translation choices. The outline may compare questions—where agency,
emotion, memory, breath, conduct, suffering, or perception were located—but must not create a
single cross-cultural vocabulary table implying equivalence.

Every public comparison will need:

- original term and script/transliteration when useful;
- named passage, object, edition, translator, date, and genre;
- what the translation gains and loses;
- a statement that resemblance does not establish common meaning or transmission; and
- specialist language review before publication.

### Ritual, material treatment, regimen, household help, and learned medicine coexist

The evidence does not support a magic-to-medicine ladder. Texts may prescribe spoken, material,
dietary, ritual, observational, social, or bodily actions in the same corpus or context.
Prescriptions show what a text prescribes; they do not by themselves prove delivery, frequency,
choice, success, consent, or patient satisfaction.

### Surviving first-person language is not automatically patient testimony

The batch contains literary speakers, ritual petitioners, narrative characters, institutional
formulae, reported speech, object inscriptions, and a small amount of household communication.
Each has a different evidentiary job. `Ludlul`, lament poetry, Saul's narrative, 1QS, and healing
inscriptions cannot be introduced as transparent clinical interviews. Public pages must name
speaker, recorder, genre, translator, preserver, and mediation.

### First-and-origin stories repeatedly fail upstream replay

The reviewed chains do not support neutral milestones such as:

- first prehistoric surgery, healthcare, or compassion;
- first psychiatry or first psychiatric diagnosis in Mesopotamia;
- Egyptian discovery of neuroscience;
- a universal Egyptian belief that the brain was useless;
- David inventing music therapy or curing a modern diagnosis;
- Greek medicine defeating religion; or
- Hippocrates or Alcmaeon discovering modern neuroscience.

These claims may later appear only as explicitly dated reception or myth-audit material, with
the downstream claim and upstream failure both visible.

### An archive's power position is part of the fact

Material remains cannot speak. Technical corpora privilege learned and scribal work. Egyptian
and Levantine texts disproportionately preserve elite, religious, administrative, and literary
contexts. Sanctuary inscriptions are selected and institutionally preserved. Nubian and
Kushite evidence is shaped by excavation, collection, language decipherment, war, custody, and
unequal modern scholarship. The outline must carry these conditions into each node rather than
putting one generic “missing voices” disclaimer at the end.

## Exchanges that are not yet demonstrated

Batch 01 identifies possible comparison routes but does not establish a specific doctrine,
treatment, term, or theory moving among Mesopotamia, Egypt, Nubia/Kush, the Levant, and
Greek-speaking worlds. Similarity, geographic proximity, trade, conquest, or chronological
overlap is not enough.

Any future exchange claim must name:

1. what moved;
2. its source and receiving forms;
3. dates and places;
4. intermediary people, texts, objects, institutions, ports, courts, or routes;
5. language and translation changes;
6. evidence against independent development; and
7. scholarship that tests rather than assumes influence.

Until then, the outline uses “compare” and “contact question,” not “influenced,” “borrowed,”
“passed down,” or “originated.”

## Source-quality lessons from the pilot

1. **Two axes work.** `L1–L5/LX` plus `A–D` prevents institutional prestige from substituting
   for claim fit.
2. **Weak sources can have a legitimate narrow job.** A popular page can document that a myth
   was published; it cannot prove the ancient claim it repeats.
3. **Full-text access changes conclusions.** Lacunae, qualifications, page locations, and
   method limits disappeared in summaries but were visible in full editions and articles.
4. **A source count without independence is misleading.** The first-psychiatry and other origin
   claims often repeated one lineage.
5. **Rights checking changes the archive.** Unclear-rights cached material was deleted even
   when convenient; a stable link and metadata record remained.
6. **Reviewer replay is essential.** Independent checks found incorrect pages, missing
   translators, ambiguous kinship, overconfident labels, a stale digital revision number,
   incomplete manifests, and absent typed relationships.
7. **Unknown is productive.** Quarantines and holds define the next research job instead of
   becoming narrative filler.
