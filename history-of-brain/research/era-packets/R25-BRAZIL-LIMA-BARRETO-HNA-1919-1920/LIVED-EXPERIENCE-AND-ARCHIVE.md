# R25 Brazil: lived experience and archive audit

| layer | whose voice/power | direct for | cannot establish | dependence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| autograph *Diário do Hospício* | Lima Barreto writing in or close to confinement | what he wrote and revised; manuscript materiality | every event, diagnosis, motive, prevalence, another patient's experience | `BARRETO-DIARY-MS` |

## Direct self-writing remains situated writing

The diary opening is unusually direct for this project because the affected person is the
writer and the autograph object survives. That advantage does not erase situation:

- the writing was produced under or close to confinement;
- the paper, corrections, fragments and archive marks show work in process;
- access to paper, writing time, literacy, professional identity and later preservation made
  this voice more recoverable than many others;
- named staff or other confined people appear through Barreto's selection and language; and
- the surviving archive does not tell us how every other person experienced the institution.

The safe evidence unit is “Barreto wrote/reported…,” followed by a narrow locator and the limits
of that report. The packet must not demand institutional confirmation before acknowledging his
report. It also must not promote the report to a population or diagnosis claim.

## Fiction, narrator and autobiographical dependence

The novel is not a second patient witness. Its epigraph, city opening, narrator, scenes and
revisions show literary transformation. Birman connects it to diary material, which makes the
dependence explicit rather than speculative. Required separations:

1. author versus narrator;
2. remembered event versus invented/composite scene;
3. manuscript draft versus posthumous edition;
4. quotation in scholarship versus autograph wording; and
5. literary insight versus institutional fact.

No fictional character may be reidentified as a historical patient without independent,
necessary and ethically cleared evidence.

## Institutional power and the held counter-record

The observation page was produced within admission/classification systems that held power over
the person recorded. Birman's article reports a diagnostic heading, identification fields and a
photograph, but R25 deliberately does not reproduce those details. When acquired, the page must
be recorded as staff speech/action:

- identify recorder, form, administrative purpose and section when possible;
- record whether fields are staff observation, elicited response, copied data or later entry;
- preserve original period term without modern diagnosis;
- document the photograph's production, cropping, publication history and consent status;
- test whether the record is complete and whether neighboring pages affect interpretation; and
- keep image rights, medical privacy, dignity and historical analysis as separate decisions.

The held record cannot be made to “correct” the diary, and the diary cannot be made to nullify
the fact that staff classifications had institutional consequences.

## Family and descendant boundaries

FBN reports acquisition of the archive from relatives in 1947. This permits only a provenance
claim. It does not establish:

- a relative's view of the 1919 confinement;
- consent to quote medical or family details;
- motive for any admission or archive sale;
- a shared family interpretation of Barreto's writing; or
- descendant approval of facsimile, translation or commercial public use.

The diary's reports about relatives remain Barreto's reports. No relative becomes a proxy voice
without a directly opened letter, statement or other source in a separately approved scope.

## Other confined people and archive inequality

The manuscript includes descriptions of other people, but R25 is not a roster. Do not surface
names, alleged acts, diagnoses, race labels, bodies, speech or behavior merely because a famous
writer recorded them. Any proposed example requires necessity, minimization, source context,
reidentification risk and dignity review.

Barreto's education, authorship, public career and later archive preservation made his words
survive in an unusually visible form. That is historically important and also a selection
effect. It forbids claims that he was typical of Black, poor, police-referred or institutionalized
people. Less-documented voices remain a named gap, not a blank to fill from his prose.

## Portuguese and translation protocol

Before any public use:

1. A Brazilian-Portuguese specialist with historical-language competence must replay the exact
   manuscript image and identify uncertain letters, deletions, insertions and archive hands.
2. A diplomatic transcription must preserve spelling and punctuation when material, using
   brackets or uncertainty marks rather than silent modernization.
3. Any English translation must be made from the verified Portuguese, not from Spanish or an
   unidentified modern edition, and independently checked against the image.
4. Record the translator, checker, manuscript page, editorial interventions and unresolved
   alternatives.
5. Terms including `hospício`, `alienado`, `louco`, `alcoolismo`, `delírio`, `indigente`,
   `pardo`, `negro`, `degeneração`, `neurastenia`, section names and administrative categories
   require local historical context. No term maps automatically to a modern diagnosis, identity
   or treatment claim.
6. The French epigraph in the novel requires a separate French check if ever used.

No translation or long quotation is approved by this packet.

The repair pass removed three earlier modernized readings from the evidence records rather than
mislabeling them as diplomatic transcription. Their bounded page-image descriptions remain
internal orientation only; exact Portuguese or French wording stays held until the protocol
above is completed.

## Missing voices and facts

- the directly opened institutional recorder and full 1919 page;
- the exact newspaper interviewer, page and issue context;
- nurses, attendants and nonmedical workers in a source within this frozen corpus;
- relatives speaking in their own directly opened documents about this admission;
- other confined people's direct accounts under an approved privacy/dignity protocol;
- a checked posthumous-edition method and leaf-order history;
- a qualified Portuguese paleographic transcription; and
- item-specific FBN/IPUB reuse determinations.

These gaps narrow the packet. They do not authorize scope expansion into 1914, the 1920 revolt,
the full HNA, Fundo GIFI or a national psychiatry history.
