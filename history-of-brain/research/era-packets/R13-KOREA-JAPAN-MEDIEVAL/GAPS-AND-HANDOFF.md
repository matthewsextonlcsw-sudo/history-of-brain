7. **Transmission is not a national relay race.** The selected chapters quote continental works and are arranged in a Japanese court compilation; neither “indigenous invention” nor “passive copy” is sufficient (`CLM-021011`).

## Required myth and origin audits

### Timeless Kampo

- **Lead:** modern accounts often group court physicians' work under `kanpō`.
- **Upstream replay:** Poletto pp. 1–2 and p. 18 n.2 treats that label as a modern interpretive choice and uses `ijutsu`; the selected chapters themselves mix ritual/spirit, bodily, and drug/procedure material.
- **Disposition:** no “Kampo mental health treatment in 984.” A later terminology-history packet is required.

### “Imported copy”

- **Lead:** e-Museum says the work draws from many Chinese medical books; source titles in chs. 21–23 confirm continental dependence.
- **Contrary/qualifying layer:** Poletto calls the work continental material “reworked through Japanese eyes”; selection and arrangement are visible compilation acts.
- **Disposition:** use a two-part statement—continental textual dependence plus Japanese court compilation—without inferring local practice.

### “Buddhist therapy”

- **Lead:** Hyetong narrative, `新羅法師方` chant, and Kim's modern “therapy” language.
- **Upstream replay:** the Hyetong material is hagiographic/narrative; the chant is prescriptive and has no implementation record; broad diffusion is Kim's inference.
- **Disposition:** allow “Buddhist healing narrative” or “medicine-taking chant attributed to a Silla monk formula” after review; hold therapy, service, prevalence, efficacy, and continuity.

### Silla medical-book / national-priority claim

- **Lead:** Shin 1995, Korea Pharmaceutical Information Center, and *Minjok Medicine News* call `新羅法師方` an early Korean medical book and sometimes imply professional use.
- **Upstream replay:** the strongest opened object is the embedded title-like attribution in *Ishinpō* scroll 2. No standalone manuscript, colophon, author, full contents, or route was opened.
- **Disposition:** reception claim may be studied; historical book identity and priority remain Unknown.

### “First psychiatry/psychotherapy”

- **Lead:** none of the pivotal primary sources uses these modern professional categories; such claims arise through retrospective framing.
- **Upstream replay:** narrative genre, compilation layers, historical terms, missing implementation, and no global survey all fail the burden.
- **Disposition:** reject. The object-level museum phrase “earliest extant medical book in Japan” must never be rewritten as a mental-health first.
