## Executed boundary and method

- **Korean lane (one):** the `惠通降龍` (*Hyetong Subdues the Dragon*) narrative cluster in *Samguk yusa*, book 5, section 6. The retained units are Hyetong's claimed study in Tang, the Tang princess episode, and the return-to-Silla/royal-daughter episode. This is a late-thirteenth-century Korean Buddhist compilation, not a transparent seventh-century clinical record.
- **Japanese lane (one):** *Ishinpō* scroll 3, chapters 21–23 only: `治中風言語錯亂方第二十一`, `治中風癲病方第二十二`, and `治中風狂病方第二十三`. The consulted primary object is an 1854–1860 Ansei–Man'en woodblock scan, checked visually against a modern Wikisource transcription and contextualized against the twelfth-century Nakarai manuscript record.
- **Contact question (one):** what, exactly, does *Ishinpō* scroll 2's `服藥頌` attribution to `新羅法師方` establish? The answer is limited to textual survival of a named Silla-monk attribution in a Japanese compilation. Route, author, original book, use, prevalence, recipient, and efficacy remain unresolved.
- Excluded: the remainder of *Samguk yusa*; Korean secular medicine, shamanic archives, later Goryeo practice, and Joseon evidence; all other *Ishinpō* scrolls except the one contact passage; Japanese diaries, temple archives, and material practice outside the three selected chapters; modern clinical equivalence; and a general history of “Korean medicine,” “Japanese medicine,” or “Kampo.”
- Ratings follow the project scale: L1 direct/primary object, L2 specialist scholarship, L3 accountable institutional/reference conduit, L4 accountable secondary synthesis, L5 discovery lead, LX unusable. Fit is A direct, B strong indirect, C context only, D mismatch. Ratings are claim-job specific.
- Original-language status is explicit. The *Samguk yusa* portal displays Classical Chinese and a modern Korean translation; translator names were not shown on the individual records. The *Ishinpō* scan displays Classical Chinese/kanbun; no modern translation was consulted for chapters 21–23. Researcher glosses are provisional and not publication-cleared.
- Agent Reach diagnostics: Jina Reader available; Exa unavailable (`exa_search: off`). `agent-reach` exposes no search subcommand in this installation. Jina Reader, direct institutional files, publisher PDFs, and built-in primary/scholarly web search were used. Snippets and abstracts were leads only.
- Shared record fields: `opened_by=R13 packet researcher`; `opened_on=2026-08-18`; `accessed_on=2026-08-18`; `cultural_protocol=retain historical terms, disclose genre/status/coercion, avoid retrospective diagnosis, and do not turn prescription into practice or outcome`.

## Rejected or quarantined source jobs

- Search snippets, abstracts, encyclopedic snippets, general TCM/Kampo pages, and modern clinical articles were not used as proof.
- `SRC-021006` is quarantined from standalone use because the transcription is incomplete/corrupt in places.
- `SRC-021012`–`SRC-021013` are retained only to document modern reception and audit their upstream chain.
- Kim's inference that the chant was used by anyone taking medicine and Shin's inferences about a standalone Silla book, its seventh-/eighth-century date, national ownership, practice, superiority, or exchange are not retained as established facts.
- No claim in this packet uses “Buddhist therapy,” “Kampo,” “psychotherapy,” “psychiatry,” “psychosis,” “mania,” “epilepsy,” or another modern clinical category as a historical identity.
