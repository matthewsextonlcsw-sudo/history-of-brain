## Findings that may carry into internal outlining

- A heterogeneous, multi-millennial Maloti–Drakensberg material corpus exists, but dates and attributions are not interchangeable across images.
- Qing's recorded contribution is historically important precisely because the 1874 publication exposes military/employment context, unnamed translators, editorial rearrangement, uncertainty and later disagreement.
- A 2013–2015 published exchange disputes “trance,” “shamanism,” “healing” and altered-consciousness readings; current expert distribution is unknown, and no side authorizes a settled image-level mental-health claim.
- Tsodilo's archaeological sequence, broad painting chronology and current Hambukushu/San sacred-landscape relation are three different propositions.
- The Bleek–Lloyd archive preserves named contributor work but is a nineteenth-century colonial/carceral/translation archive, not precolonial testimony or a universal San worldview.
- Research permission, site entry, archaeological action, asset reproduction, quotation/translation, community knowledge, sacred detail, public indexing and commercial/GEO use require separate decisions.

## Findings that cannot carry

- Prehistoric diagnosis, therapy, patient experience, treatment efficacy, prevalence or “wellness practice.”
- A timeless “San psychology,” a single southern African spiritual system, or automatic continuity from ancient imagery to a present community.
- A claim that all rock art depicts trance/healing, or that none does.
- Public reproduction of any rock-art image, Bleek–Lloyd scan, community narrative, sacred detail, recording, map or vulnerable location.
