# R14 Southern Africa Material / Oral / Protocol — Lived Experience and Archive Audit

**Status:** protocol ceiling established; direct prehistoric lived experience remains unknown

## Bottom line

The opened material record has no patient voice. Paint, stratigraphy, artefacts and occupation dates cannot say whether a particular person felt fear, relief, grief, coercion, altered consciousness, illness or benefit. The later oral-text archives preserve named contributions, but they were created in colonial military, carceral, domestic, philological and translation relationships. Empathy here means making those relationships visible and refusing to write feelings into silence.

## Required population and power audit

### Colonial violence, confinement and dependency

- Orpen's opportunity to record Qing came through a military expedition and guide-recruitment relationship (EVD-027103). A relaxed campfire description does not cancel structural power or establish publication consent.
- The Bleek–Lloyd project began with imprisoned or recently released people and colonial permission to relocate a prisoner into Bleek's home (EVD-027119–120). Later residence, kin participation and contributor self-description as teacher show agency, but do not erase confinement, dependency or colonial control.
- Photographs and bodily measurements were made within nineteenth-century scientific conventions (EVD-027120). They are not neutral illustrations and are excluded from this packet.

### Gender

- Orpen names Qing but not Qing's wives or his brother's widow, and describes them through a diminutive, racialized and gendered gaze. Their role in his recruitment must not be romanticized or made decorative.
- Bleek's comparison imagines future recording from an old “Bushman or Bushwoman,” but named female knowledge, authority and translation work are not sufficiently recovered in this bounded chain.
- The Digital Bleek–Lloyd archive includes many creators and later materials; this packet did not run a complete gender audit of item-level authorship. No male-only or female-caregiver generalization is permitted.

### Age

- Orpen repeatedly characterizes Qing as young and uses that to question completeness; Bleek repeats the age comparison. Age is therefore part of how colonial editors ranked authority, not an objective measure of Qing's cultural competence.
- The four ǃkun contributors described as boys require an age, guardianship, displacement and consent audit before any item use.
- An unnamed Zhu elder, as reported by the authors, supplies a bounded shelter-use recollection. That recollection is separate from the reported Zhu tradition attributing white paintings to N/aekhwe. “Elder” does not automatically mean authority for every subject, site, asset or present-day use.

### Language and translation

- Orpen does not name his translators and admits variable understanding. Printed English cannot be treated as Qing's exact words (EVD-027104).
- Bleek's contributors differed somewhat in their explanations; this is evidence of plurality and mediation, not error by one side (EVD-027105).
- Bleek/Lloyd records were phonetic, translated at the time or later, sometimes with help from other ǀXam speakers (EVD-027120). Contributor assistance is intellectually significant and must be credited at item level.
- Terms such as `trance`, `shaman`, `healing`, `spirit`, `possession`, `sorcerer` and `medicine` are not interchangeable. Any future use must preserve the source-language term, collector gloss, later translation, speaker, date and dispute.

### Status, poverty and benefit

- Qing appears as a hunter/guide working through Nqasha; Bleek/Lloyd contributors lived in prison, post-prison and collector-household settings. Economic and institutional dependency may shape what could be said, refused or published.
- The San Code requires fairness, benefits and collective involvement through publication (EVD-027124). Public SEO/GEO extraction creates value and must be named in advance, not hidden as “education.”
- The packet located no benefit-sharing agreement for any proposed Couch Commons use.

### Disability, distress, altered consciousness and care

- No material feature in the opened corpus identifies mental illness, disability, emotion, treatment or recovery.
- The 2013–2015 published trance/healing exchange concerns interpretation of ethnography and imagery; it is not a clinical record and cannot establish diagnosis, efficacy, prevalence or patient experience (DIS-027400).
- No direct recipient of a purported healing action evaluates it in the opened packet.
- No claim that people were content, frightened, traumatized, helped, cured or harmed may be invented from silence.

## Consent and refusal audit

No modern project consent was sought because this packet performed rights-safe desk research only and did not reproduce controlled content. Before any new research or public use:

1. Define the exact action: quote, translate, summarize a narrative, reproduce an image/scan, name a location, index for search, monetize, or train/retrieve with a system.
2. Identify the current individual, community, custodian, heritage, site, archive and rights authorities for that action and jurisdiction.
3. Explain SEO/GEO visibility, commercial context, persistence, machine retrieval, correction and removal pathways.
4. Record whether refusal, restriction, attribution, benefit, review and withdrawal are available.
5. Treat silence, an open webpage, an expired plan target, a research permit or a copyright license as insufficient consent.

## Empathy controls for any later prose

1. Name people when the archive names them; do not turn Qing, ǁkabbo, ǀaǃkunta or other contributors into generic “informants.”
2. Explain coercive structures without reducing contributors to passivity.
3. Do not use racist historical language in navigation, SEO metadata or dramatic hooks; quote only when necessary to analyze the archive.
4. Do not stage sacred practices, paintings, imprisonment or bodily measurement as spectacle.
5. State “the archive does not tell us” rather than imagining feelings.
6. Present serious interpretive disagreement without ridicule and without equating spiritual language with pathology.
7. Keep living communities in the present tense and give them authority over present use; do not treat them as surviving illustrations of prehistory.
