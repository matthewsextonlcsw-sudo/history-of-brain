## Allowed internal interpretations

1. Material rock-art corpora exist at Maloti–Drakensberg and Tsodilo; selected material can support bounded chronological constraints.
2. Dates, creators and meanings differ within and between the corpora; a heritage property's long duration is not an image date.
3. Qing's 1873 contribution matters historically, but the published chain is colonial, multilingual, rearranged and uncertain.
4. The Bleek–Lloyd archive preserves named contributor work from 1870 onward and exposes colonial/carceral/translation power; it is not precolonial or universal testimony.
5. Current Hambukushu and San relationships to Tsodilo are institutionally documented and must remain separate from prehistoric authorship/continuity.
6. Trance/shamanism/healing interpretation is disputed and may be described only as attributed scholarship with its evidence chain and limits.
7. Authority is action-specific and plural.

## Held interpretations

- Any prehistoric or medieval diagnosis, psychology, mood, emotion, trauma, wellness, therapy, patient experience, care outcome, efficacy or prevalence.
- “Rock art was therapy,” “all images record trance healing,” “the San used altered states to treat mental illness,” or their categorical opposites.
- An unchanged ancient-to-present continuity, single San worldview, unitary African spirituality, or automatic ethnic authorship.
- Qing's printed English as exact verbatim speech or permission to reuse community narratives.
- Bleek–Lloyd materials as direct precolonial testimony, free-consent interviews or complete representation.
- Any public image, scan, recording, map, vulnerable location, sacred detail or community narrative.
