# R20 lived experience and archive audit

**Verdict:** lived-experience evidence is real but asymmetrical. Davis is an identified affected person publishing outside the institution; *The Opal* preserves selected institution-supported writing whose complete 1855 context is not yet available; workers and residents aggregated by the annual report do not speak in their own first person. No one source may substitute for the others.

## Davis evidence-form controls

- **Self-report:** admission, loss of control, her response to sewing, chosen sweeping, and felt relief. State as Davis's experience.
- **Direct observation:** acts she explicitly says she saw, including the p.81 violence allegations. State as Davis's direct-observation allegation, not as adjudicated fact.
- **Reported observation:** what another person is represented as seeing or doing. Preserve both reporter and reported actor.
- **Hearsay:** statements introduced as what she was told. Never remove that layer.
- **Allegation:** contested conduct or motive involving identifiable historical actors. Use minimal necessary detail and require legal/editorial review before public wording.
- **Argument:** Davis's conclusions about experiential understanding, institutional design, justice, and reform. Present as her position rather than outcome evidence.

Her book also contains anti-Irish and anti-Catholic prejudice and degrading historical labels. Those terms are not necessary to the retained claims. Do not quote them for color, normalize them, or allow criticism of institutional power to erase Davis's own prejudice.

## Opal mediation controls

*The Opal* is neither dismissed as “not really patient voice” nor promoted as transparent testimony.

- The archive describes patient editing, but the institution supplied the publication setting, presses, audience, and part of its public purpose.
- Contributors were anonymous or pseudonymous; the packet prohibits casebook linkage and retrospective diagnosis.
- Named specialists identify ward/class privilege, selection, and possible self-censorship.
- Satire, collective first person, praise, complaint, literary convention, and institutional promotion can coexist in one article.
- Google, the NYPL and UMN Hathi items, VCU, and DHM are separate access or transcription layers, but all are tied to the same `OPAL-1855-TEXT` origin and are not independent witnesses.
- The Google object exposed `PA27`, then `PA33`–`PA160`; `PA1`–`PA26` and `PA28`–`PA32` were unavailable, and no complete alternate witness was replayed. No 1855 *Opal* quotation or item claim is public-ready.

## Work, occupation, and agency

Every later use must specify which condition the source supports:

| condition | evidence in packet | prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| chosen activity | Davis says she chose sweeping/dusting and found it pleasant/useful | “occupational therapy worked” |
| prescribed occupation | Davis says Benedict directed her to sew | “treatment delivered” without naming contest/refusal |
| institutional production | report counts textile, tailoring, carpentry, and garden work and assigns value | “therapeutic activity” |
| alleged coercion | Davis says she was pressured to sew and threatened with prolonged confinement | institution-wide prevalence claim |
| directed mixed labor | report combines patients and attendants under gardener/shop direction | patient-only output, voluntary labor, or staff testimony |

Gender, pay, ward/class, staff/patient allocation, and consent are missing or uneven. Those missing fields are not neutral blanks.

## Outcome and efficacy controls

- “Recovered,” “improved,” “unimproved,” “discharged well,” and “none returned” are institutional categories or observations.
- The report supplies no independent assessor, comparable untreated group, validated measure, comprehensive follow-up, or causal design.
- Davis's statements that sympathy helped her or that chosen activity benefited her are self-reported individual experiences.
- The report's statement that garden employment facilitated recovery is an institutional assertion.
- *The Opal*'s existence, library revenue, and publication participation are not treatment outcomes.

No cure rate, durable-recovery conclusion, or efficacy claim is permitted.

## Privacy, identity, and dignity

- Historical publication does not eliminate dignity obligations.
- Do not identify anonymous *Opal* writers by joining the journal to casebooks, admission registers, diagnoses, or family records.
- Do not publish named third-party allegations from Davis unless the precise name is indispensable and separately approved; retained packet claims avoid unnecessary names other than public official N. D. Benedict.
- Do not reconstruct diagnoses for Davis, contributors, or unnamed residents.
- Use current respectful narrative language outside exact, necessary historical quotation. Explain historical titles rather than silently repeating slurs.
- Violence descriptions should be paraphrased minimally, preceded by reader context if ever published, and reviewed by a disability/survivor-informed editor.

## Missing voices that must stay visible

- residents who did not write, could not write, were not selected, or were excluded by ward/class/language;
- people subjected to force whose own words are not preserved here;
- attendants, domestic workers, mechanics, and gardeners in their own voices;
- family and community perspectives beyond Davis's positioned account;
- Black, Indigenous, immigrant, poor, non-English-speaking, and gender-diverse experiences that the bounded corpus does not responsibly recover.

Absence does not authorize invented interiority. A later article should name the archive's shape and then stop.

## Mandatory review sequence before any public use

1. Acquire and hash a complete lawful 1855 *Opal* witness.
2. Replay every proposed *Opal* locator in its issue and volume context.
3. Independent historian/source review.
4. Disability/survivor-informed review of voice, coercion, and violence.
5. Allegation and privacy review for Davis passages.
6. Rights review for every image, long quotation, and modern transcription.
7. Final wording review that keeps practice, purpose, delivery, experience, and outcome separate.

Until all seven steps are complete, the packet remains **STOP / NO PUBLIC PROSE**.
