## Cross-packet findings

### “First-person voice” names several different source conditions

An autograph diary, unfinished fiction, a complete published transcription of an unseen letter,
a composite self-published argument, an edited contribution, and a catalogue description of an
unopened file are not interchangeable. Authorship can preserve agency without independently
verifying every event. Editorial transmission can preserve words without authenticating the
unseen original. A title can identify an object without revealing anyone's voice.

### Family is an actor, source layer, and transmission network—not an inferred motive

Family appears as addressee, contact, archive custodian, biographical source, descendant
interview, alleged agent, and subject of a writer's claim. These positions do not establish
consent, abandonment, affection, hostility, financial motive, or later silence. Family-linked
scholarship must retain its source chain, and private family material requires consent and
rights review rather than automatic incorporation.

### Counterpublic is a relationship to authority, not a label applied to every personal text

Packard's publication may support an internally bounded node about publishing against legal and
institutional authority because the complete composite object and official comparators were
opened. Barreto's diary and unfinished fiction, and Wilbraham's mediated letter, require their
own genre, audience, circulation, and transmission analysis. Private or affected-person writing
does not automatically constitute a public movement, representative voice, or successful reform.

### Law, report, inquiry, catalogue, and archive policy perform different authority acts

A statute establishes legal design. A committee selects and frames evidence. A board report
states an official policy position. A catalogue identifies a described object. An access policy
sets a general route. None alone proves implementation, event truth, item completeness,
historical consent, institutional outcome, current reuse permission, or lived experience.

### Dependence must remain visible when sources appear to multiply

Barreto's diary and fiction share an authorial and transmission chain; Wilbraham's letter is
available through the same opened scholarly publication that reconstructs its context; the
Seacliff husband/brother conflict remains derivative of a restricted case; Packard's composite
object contains distinct contributors under an editorial frame; and catalogue records do not
corroborate the unopened files they describe. Model, context, and integrity records do not count
as independent confirmation.

### Archive access shapes whose experience can be written—but absence is not proof

Autograph survival, descendant access, publication, digitization, institutional retention,
viewer barriers, catalogue-only access, and community authority all shape the surviving record.
An unopened file or missing reply does not prove that a voice, visit, action, or record never
existed. Recording a lawful stop is more accurate than filling it with a later summary.

### Named cases are not representative samples

Lima Barreto, Harry Wilbraham, Elizabeth Packard, Case 3464, McGrath, and the people potentially
described in the Tanjong Rambutan files occupy different legal, racial, gendered, linguistic,
class, colonial, and archival positions. Their records cannot stand for an institution, nation,
diagnostic category, colonized population, family type, or all affected people.
