## Bounded design

This packet studies one linked archive in two settings: Simon Forman's London/Lambeth practice in the surviving 1596-1603 casebooks, and Richard Napier's Great Linford practice and regional catchment in the surviving 1597-1634 casebooks. The declared sample contains **26 digital records representing 24 consultation events and 24 named or unnamed subjects**. Ten records are assigned by the edition to Forman's practice and sixteen to Napier's. Three records preserve one Matthew Altham event; two records preserve separate George French consultations four days apart. These dependencies are not independent corroboration.

Selection was purposive, not random: proxy/self consultation, explicit consent/no-consent/unrecorded consent, recorded sex/age/status, repeat recording, treatment/payment/subsequent-event facets, historically recorded distress terms, and editorial/recorder mediation. Every selected record was opened in XML, in the public case display, and against its linked manuscript page image at the stated Bodleian shelfmark and folio/page. The replay establishes visible entry layout, writing and chart presence; it is not a new diplomatic transcription of the unedited text.

## Source-rating controls

- A single case record, its XML, display transcription, and image are one evidence chain. They must never be counted as independent corroboration.
- Project methodology pages describe the edition. They can qualify a case claim but cannot corroborate what happened historically.
- Kassell 2005 and the *Judgmentes* are partly dependent because the article reads the guide; their relationship is declared rather than counted twice.
- Carter 2021 is a specialist full-source comparator that complicates both purely cultural and purely biomedical readings, but it depends on the Casebooks archive and is not independent event corroboration.
- The CUDL image replay distinguishes visible physical evidence from editorial interpretation. It does not authorize reading faint untranscribed text as fact.
- No retained claim depends on a snippet, abstract, AI summary, or inaccessible full source.
