### Computed sample audit

- Practice assignment: 10 Forman records / 16 Napier records.
- Asking route: 16 proxy records, 9 self records, 1 mixed self-plus-proxy record.
- Explicit edition consent field: 4 yes, 7 no, 15 unrecorded. Unrecorded is not consent, refusal, knowledge or incapacity.
- Encoded subject sex among 24 distinct sample subjects: 14 female and 10 male. These are edition categories for a purposive sample, not a prevalence or access statistic.
- Edition content facets: 23 records include judgment information; 8 treatment information; 2 financial information; 3 subsequent-event information. Only CASE41111 and CASE48779 are marked transcribed in entirety (with the edition's chart/astrological-note exception); the other 24 are question-only.
- Astrological figures: the XML and page replay show/encode an active figure in 22 records, two active-plus-deleted figure records (CASE18216, CASE52192), and no figure in CASE46520 or CASE48779. This shows record construction, not that astrology caused help-seeking or that clients believed the same thing as practitioners.
- Event dependence: CASE8280, CASE18910 and CASE19524 are three records of one event. CASE18910 remains a proxy record although its brother transcription and female `#a2` querent pointer conflict; CASE2478 and CASE2513 are two consultations linked to PERSON2936. Record counts must not be promoted to independent people or independent events.

## Findings the packet can support

1. **Help-seeking could be socially distributed in these records.** The sample includes spouses, parents, mistresses, a daughter, a cousin and unidentified messengers acting as querents. That is a documented feature of these selected encounters, not an estimate for England.
2. **Consent notation is historically specific and uneven.** `cum consensu`, `sine consensu`, and English against-the-will wording appear, but most selected records have no explicit consent value. The edition warns that the notation may sometimes track presence, especially for children. It is not modern informed consent.
3. **The practitioner's pen combines voices.** A question may contain reported self-description, a proxy's accusation, a practitioner's compression or causal gloss, an astrological judgment, and later editorial categories. The records can preserve traces of clients' concerns without becoming direct quotations or diaries.
4. **Astrology and medicine are intertwined in the surviving page technology.** Charts, urine, judgments and treatment facets coexist in the sample; Forman's guide describes how a chart shaped inquiry. Nothing in the sample demonstrates a client's belief, a single causal reason for consulting, or a clean astrology-versus-medicine choice.
5. **Recorded words vary without mapping cleanly to modern diagnoses.** `troubled in mind`, `disquieted`, `melancholy`, `mopish`, `grief`, `despair`, `tempted`, `jealous`, `mad`, `lunatic`, `distracted`, `sottish`, spiritual attribution, and `the mother` occur in different grammatical and social positions. Some are subject-reported, some proxy-reported, some recorder labels, and some modern editorial facets.
6. **Treatment and outcome remain separate gates.** Eight selected XML records carry a treatment facet, but only two have financial facets and three subsequent-event facets. For 24 records, the relevant lower-page material is mostly untranscribed. A facet documents presence of a note, not delivery, use, safety, effect or recovery.
7. **The physical manuscript matters.** Page replay shows entries embedded among neighboring cases, calculations, deletions, charts, multiple columns and faint writing. The edited question is a useful bounded object; it is not the entire encounter or entire page.
8. **A record can multiply without an event multiplying.** The Altham chain varies wording, consent representation, and in CASE18910 the transcribed-versus-encoded querent identity across three copies/entries. It is an unusually useful demonstration of transmission and recorder mediation, not three corroborating witnesses.
9. **Status appears, access does not.** Servants, a child, married and unmarried women, a knight's daughter, and university men are named. Their presence proves these people were recorded; it cannot identify those excluded, unable or unwilling to consult.
