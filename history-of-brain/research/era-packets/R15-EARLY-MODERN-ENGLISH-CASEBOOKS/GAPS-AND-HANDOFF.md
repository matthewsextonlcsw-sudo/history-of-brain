## Exact unresolved gaps

### Archive and text

1. Twenty-four sample records expose only the edition's transcribed question. Their judgment, treatment, financial and subsequent-event text was not newly transcribed from the page. Facsimile inspection is adequate for layout/presence, not a diplomatic reading of faint script.
2. CASE41111 and CASE48779 are marked whole-entry with the edition's chart/astrological-note exception; this still does not reconstruct the spoken encounter.
3. Forman's pre-1596 and post-1603 casebooks and separate treatment/payment notebook are lost. The project reports a likely missing Napier volume from autumn 1613 to spring 1614. No absence claim may cross these gaps.
4. The cousin's letter behind CASE73181 is not separately preserved/opened in this packet. The record only says that the cousin wrote.
5. The precise path from Forman's original consultation to Napier's CASE13315 copy/new judgment cannot be reconstructed beyond the edition's staged metadata. Its English translation has not received an independent Latin specialist review.

### Lived experience and implementation

6. No selected subject-authored diary or letter was opened. Even “as she saith” remains practitioner-recorded reported speech.
7. Consent silence remains Unknown in fifteen records. Explicit historical consent wording does not establish modern informed consent or voluntariness.
8. Treatment facets do not establish prescription content, dispensing, use, dose, safety, adherence or efficacy. Financial facets do not establish amount or burden. Subsequent-event facets do not establish recovery.
9. No denominator supports prevalence, sex difference, class access, geographic reach, treatment rate or outcome rate. People who did not consult are absent by design.
10. CASE31759's recent childbirth and CASE44800's broader linked postpartum history may be placed beside specialist historiography, but Carter does not discuss Margery Turnam. Carter is linked only as corpus-level historiographic/method context and does not authorize a retrospective postpartum diagnosis or an individual causal claim.

### Scholarship and disagreement

11. Michael MacDonald's *Mystical Bedlam* (1981), Ronald Sawyer's dissertation and other antecedent quantitative work were identified through opened full scholarship but not opened in full for this packet. Carter notes uncertainty about whether MacDonald's “cases” count people or consultations and cautions against hard-data use. No MacDonald statistic should enter public prose from this packet.
12. Kassell and Carter offer different emphases—record/technology mediation, practitioner authority, gendered embodiment and a margin for corporeal experience—but they do not provide independent witnesses to individual events. A future historiographic section can present this tension only as attributed interpretation.
13. No patient/service-user-authored modern reflection on publishing identifiable historical distress records was located within scope. The packet therefore uses a conservative internal trauma/privacy protocol rather than claiming community authorization.

### Rights and public presentation

14. The 27 page images are private research copies. The Casebooks permissions route states CC BY-NC 3.0 for manuscript downloads; CUDL manifests state CC BY-NC 4.0 for downloadable images/metadata and separately reserve Bodleian zoom-image rights. These layers are not interchangeable. Because Couch Commons is commercial, public image reproduction is held pending separate clearance.
15. Casebooks XML/data are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Public commercial reuse of data beyond citation/linking requires a rights decision; this packet does not supply one.
16. Self-harm pages, treatment notes, degrading labels, pregnancy inquiries, accusations and no-consent records must not become thumbnails, hooks or spectacle. Public drafting needs a safety/sensitivity pass.

## Public holds

- **No retrospective diagnosis:** no depression, psychosis, anxiety disorder, PTSD, postpartum depression/psychosis, delusion, addiction or personality label.
- **No prevalence:** no “women were more likely,” “most clients,” “common mental illness,” class-access or national estimate from this sample/search.
- **No direct-voice claim:** do not call the records diaries, transcripts, verbatim testimony or unfiltered patient experience.
- **No efficacy/safety claim:** no implication that purge, sigil, physic, letter or other treatment was delivered, safe or effective.
- **No modern-consent equivalence:** use “recorded with/without consent” and preserve unrecorded as Unknown.
- **No belief causation:** no claim that astrology, religion, medicine or witchcraft belief caused consultation or was uniformly shared.
- **No sensational self-harm or witchcraft framing:** minimum detail, source attribution and empathy are mandatory.
- **No public manuscript images:** link to case page/shelfmark unless separate permission is documented.
- **No all-England or all-Europe narrative:** keep Forman and Napier settings named and bounded.

## Handoff to synthesis/outline

The packet can support the following transitions after review:

1. **From an individual “patient” to a consultation network:** subject, querent, spouse, parent, servant/master, messenger and practitioner may occupy different positions.
2. **From voice to record technology:** show how question, chart, judgment and later notes turn an encounter into a page, then into XML and facets.
3. **From consent notation to uncertainty:** contrast explicit yes, explicit no, knowledge without consent and silence, without imposing contemporary consent doctrine.
4. **From one event to three records:** use the Altham chain chiefly as a record-mediation lesson, with trauma-aware minimal detail.
5. **From comfort to physic without a false binary:** CASE41111 places a requested comforting letter and possible physic in one family-mediated request.
6. **From historical words to modern humility:** preserve mind, grief, melancholy, mopish and spiritual terms as situated language rather than diagnosis.
7. **From treatment note to outcome gap:** visible treatment/payment/event facets explain what the archive can flag and what it cannot prove.
8. **From diverse names to missing access:** servants, children, married women and scholars appear, followed immediately by the non-consulting majority the archive cannot show.
