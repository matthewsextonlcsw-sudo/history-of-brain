# R15-EARLY-MODERN-ENGLISH-CASEBOOKS — Consultation, Help-Seeking, and Recorder Mediation

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `030000–030999`

## Research question

What can the Simon Forman and Richard Napier casebooks, 1596–1634, establish about recorded
help-seeking, reported distress and emotion, household/community involvement, astrological and
medical consultation, remedies, gender/status, and the limits of outcome and patient voice?

## Boundary

The linked archive contains two explicitly different practice settings, not a sample of all
England or Europe:

1. Simon Forman's London/Lambeth practice in the surviving 1596–1603 casebooks.
2. Richard Napier's Great Linford practice and Buckinghamshire–Bedfordshire–Northamptonshire
   catchment, 1597–1634, with the likely autumn 1613–spring 1614 lacuna explicit.

Build a purposive, reproducible microcorpus of approximately 24–30 cases balanced across
patient/proxy querent, consent status, recorded gender/age/status, single/repeat encounter,
treatment/payment/later-event notation, and selected historically recorded distress language.
Replay every case against its manuscript image and folio, not only display transcription or
metadata. Forman's *Astrologicalle Judgmentes of phisick* may enter only as a practitioner-side
method/self-presentation comparator, not a second practice or proof of routine conduct.

## Required evidence and tests

- identify exact cases, dates, querents/patients, relationships, questions, symptoms/words,
  astrological figures, judgments, remedies, fees/status when recorded, and follow-up;
- distinguish what a client reportedly said, what another person asked on their behalf, what
  the practitioner wrote, what the editor normalized, and what a modern historian inferred;
- treat each case as a multi-voice practitioner-recorded encounter, never a patient diary;
- preserve early modern terms without converting them into depression, psychosis, anxiety,
  trauma, addiction, or another diagnosis;
- examine gender, age, occupation/status, household intermediaries, distance, repeat visits,
  refusal, consent, payment, coercion, advice, treatment delivery, and outcome only where the
  record permits;
- treat astrology, medicine, religion, household action, and commercial practice without a
  superstition-versus-science ladder;
- document transcription, editorial selection, encoding, search, missing/damaged pages,
  handwriting, manuscript custody, privacy, image rights, and digital-edition limits; and
- treat the fully transcribed opening question separately from untranscribed judgment,
  treatment, payment, and outcome text that may be represented only by metadata; modern topic
  facets such as “diseases of the mind” are editorial clusters, not historical diagnoses; and
- audit “patients spoke directly,” “the casebooks prove prevalence,” “astrology caused care,”
  “a prescribed remedy was taken/worked,” “women were passive,” and modern diagnosis claims.

## Exclusions

Burton, Vesalius, Descartes, and Elisabeth belong to R16. Exclude witchcraft trials, poor law,
madhouses, Bedlam/asylums, national prevalence, later moral treatment, continental astrology,
Forman outside 1596–1603, Napier's 1613–14 lacuna, post-1634 practice, celebrity/scandal lanes,
and an all-Europe practice narrative. Do not infer a patient's feelings, knowledge, consent,
treatment, or outcome from silence.

## Stop

Stop after the 24–30-case sample and edition/archive method needed to interpret it. No search
count becomes prevalence, treatment rate, sex difference, class access, or efficacy without a
defensible denominator and manuscript replay. Self-harm, coercion, intimate allegations, and
third-party accusations require trauma-aware review. Create five standard outputs and
rights-safe ignored cache; no public prose.
