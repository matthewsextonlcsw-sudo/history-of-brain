# R01-METHOD — Findings and Tested Inference Ladder

**Purpose:** calibrate what material evidence can and cannot establish before direct written testimony. This is research support, not article copy.

## Bounded case selection

Three cases were selected because they stress different links in the inference chain rather than because they represent world prehistory.

1. **Shuanghuaishu Tomb M487, Henan, late Yangshao period (site range 3500–2900 BC):** a cranial opening with no apparent healing. This tests intentional alteration, differential diagnosis, timing, tool inference, and the temptation to assign “medical” or “ritual” motive. [`SRC-001000`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12520-020-01151-w)
2. **Man Bac Burial 9 (`MB07H1M09`), northern Vietnam (cemetery range 1867–1524 BC):** severe skeletal changes compatible with juvenile-onset paralysis and survival into the third decade. This tests functional inference, necessary assistance, detailed care reconstruction, and overreach into personality. [`SRC-001002`](https://doi.org/10.1537/ase.081114), [`SRC-001003`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpp.2011.02.003)
3. **Shanidar 1, Iraq, approximately 50 ka:** multiple healed injuries/degenerative changes plus auditory exostoses, observed in the Iraq Museum. This tests cumulative impairment, ability assumptions, social-support inference, and the public conversion of survival into compassion. [`SRC-001005`](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684)

The cases are not commensurable as a prevalence sample. M487 is a cemetery case with uncertain timing of an intervention; M9 is a late prehistoric individual with unusually severe skeletal manifestations; Shanidar 1 is a Pleistocene hominin known through a historically layered excavation and analysis record.

## Reusable inference ladder

Each rung requires the lower rungs. Confidence does not automatically carry upward.

| rung | responsible question | evidence that can support it | normal ceiling | stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Provenance and context | What exactly is the specimen/object, where was it found, how was it recovered, dated, preserved, and curated? | excavation record, tomb/object ID, stratigraphy, dating, taphonomy, repository record | Documented | If context, holder, or dating is unclear, do not use a broad historical claim. |
| 1. Physical observation | What alteration is actually present? | measurements, morphology, microscopy, imaging, photographs, preservation map | Documented | Do not name disease, surgery, care, or motive yet. |
| 2. Formation and differential diagnosis | Which natural, pathological, traumatic, taphonomic, or human processes could produce it? | positive and negative diagnostic features; comparative cases; method | Strongly supported or Disputed | Keep alternatives alive when equifinality remains. |
| 3. Timing and duration | Did the alteration occur before, near, or after death; if before, what minimum survival interval is visible? | remodeling/healing, development/disuse changes, lesion sequence, dated context | Documented to Plausible | “No healing” cannot distinguish immediate death from postmortem action. Healing gives survival, not cause or motive. |
| 4. Functional range | What minimum and maximum effects on movement, sensation, feeding, communication, or other activity are supported? | anatomy, lesion severity, preserved activity markers, clinical comparison, environmental demands | Strongly supported to Plausible | State a range. Do not equate impairment with inability, dependency, or social disability. |
| 5. Assistance requirement | Would survival to the observed state have been very unlikely without another person's action or an accommodating group practice? | counterfactual alternatives, duration, minimum function, ecology, self-adaptation, preserved participation markers | Strongly supported only in exceptional cases; otherwise Plausible/Unknown | Assistance must be the best surviving explanation, not a synonym for impairment. |
| 6. Form and organization of care | What minimum assistance likely occurred, and what alternative arrangements could meet the same need? | bounded care tasks, lifeway context, transport/shelter/resources, multiple scenarios | Plausible | Never turn a modeled task list into observed daily routine. One caregiver, many caregivers, intermittent help, tools, and self-accommodation may be archaeologically indistinguishable. |
| 7. Social meaning, motive, emotion, and subjective experience | Was the person loved, pitied, stigmatized, respected, coerced, exploited, or considered disabled? What did they feel? | direct testimony, securely contextualized representation/text, unusually specific relational evidence | Normally Unknown in this packet | Bone survival alone cannot answer this rung. “Compassion,” “moral decency,” diagnosis of mood, quality of life, and identity stop here. |

### Short form for later packets

`context → observation → formation → timing → functional range → necessary assistance → care scenarios → meaning/experience`

Every public claim should declare the highest rung it actually reaches.

## Case calibration 1 — Shuanghuaishu M487

### What is physically documented

- Tomb `M487` is reported as a single primary burial in the central area inside the site's triple trench: west-oriented, extended, without grave goods, and poorly preserved apart from the relatively intact skull. The paper places the cemetery in the late Yangshao period, 3500–2900 BC; it does not report an individual direct date. `EVD-001000`; `CLM-001000`. [`SRC-001000`, Materials and methods, PDF pp. 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12520-020-01151-w)
- The adult male estimate is 35–50 years. The occipital perforation measures 12.51 by 10.07 mm; microscopy found sharp/distinct margins without scraping/cutting marks; CT found inner-table beveling and no apparent new bone. `EVD-001001`; `CLM-001001`. [`SRC-001000`, Results, Table 1, Figs. 3–4, PDF pp. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12520-020-01151-w)

### What the case authors favor, and where it stops

- The authors' differential considers trauma, pathological defects, taphonomic damage, and anatomical variants. The opening's regular form, absence of fracture/pathology, undisturbed context, experimental replication, and internal beveling lead them to favor deliberate drilling with a pointed/V-bladed stone tool. This is a **Plausible interpretation strongly favored by the case authors**, not direct observation of an operation or independent case-specific corroboration. `EVD-001002`; `CLM-001002`.
- No apparent healing is compatible with perimortem or postmortem alteration; the evidence does not resolve timing or motive. It does **not** show that the person was a living “patient,” survived, or received postoperative care. `EVD-001003`; `CLM-001003–001004`.

### Where the interpretation stops

- The paper offers two possibilities: attempted treatment of an occipital trauma that proved fatal, or postmortem ritual action. No cranial trauma is observed, and a single case among almost 300 excavated skeletons supplies no pattern for ritual. Both motives therefore remain **Plausible at most**, with the exact purpose **Unknown**.
- “Trepanation” should be defined operationally as a deliberate cranial opening. It must not silently mean “successful prehistoric brain surgery,” “medicine,” or “treatment.”
- The Smith College teaching page directly documents its own published wording (`L1-A` for that narrow reception-history claim). It provides no citations, dates, regions, or object record for prehistoric “doctors,” named indications, brain-tissue removal, or “brain surgery,” so the ancient-claims chain breaks and is `L5-D`. [`SRC-001011`](https://www.smith.edu/bigbad/historyscitech/museum/ancient_inventions/hsc06b.htm)

## Case calibration 2 — Man Bac M9

### What is physically documented

- `MB07H1M09` was one of three flexed burials among predominantly supine/extended adults and otherwise received similar mortuary treatment. The lower thorax, most ribs/lumbar vertebrae, and lower appendicular joints were missing after death. `EVD-001010`; `CLM-001007`. [`SRC-001002`, pp. 107–108, Figs. 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1537/ase.081114)
- Observed features include fusion from C1 through T3, very small/obliterated intervertebral foramina, torticollis/rotation, greatly reduced limb diameters, especially gracile lower limbs, weak muscle insertions, and some right upper-limb use/osteoarthritis. `EVD-001011`. [`SRC-001002`, pp. 108–111, Figs. 3–4, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1537/ase.081114)

### Diagnosis and function

- The authors consider immobilization-related ankylosis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis variants, fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, and Klippel–Feil syndrome. They favor KFS type III plus serious neurological impairment. Missing elements prevent a definitive diagnosis. `EVD-001012`; `CLM-001007`.
- The growth/disuse pattern supports onset before skeletal maturity, profound lower-limb immobility, and at least limited upper-limb function. “At minimum paraplegia, at maximum quadriplegia/quadriparesis” is safer than a settled modern diagnosis. `CLM-001008`.

### Assistance: strongest case, still bounded

- Survival into the third decade after juvenile onset, combined with the authors' reconstruction of lower-body immobility, restricted upper-limb function, torticollis, and environmental demands, makes some recurring assistance **Plausible and strongly favored by the case authors**, pending independent functional review. The practical counterfactual is stronger here than in Shanidar 1, but the 2009 and 2011 papers share the same specimen and overlapping authors and therefore do not constitute two-source corroboration. `EVD-001013`; `CLM-001008`.
- The later care paper constructs basic and advanced care scenarios—water and food provision, diet, transport, shelter, safe positioning, hygiene, skin inspection, massage, monitoring, dedicated carers, and continuous nursing—through modern clinical analogy. These are **models only**, not evidence that any named task, staffing arrangement, or routine occurred. `EVD-001014`; `CLM-001009`. [`SRC-001003`, pp. 36–40](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpp.2011.02.003)

### Overreach within a strong paper

- The care model later infers “an extraordinarily strong will to live,” “robust psychological adaptation,” self-esteem, and a personality capable of inspiring costly care. Those are not preserved in the skeleton. They may be narratively attractive but remain **Unknown** and must not enter the project as claims. `EVD-001015`; `CLM-001010`. [`SRC-001003`, p. 40](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpp.2011.02.003)
- Similar mortuary inclusion documents disposition of the body, not social acceptance throughout life, affectionate care, absence of abuse, or the person's own identity.

## Case calibration 3 — Shanidar 1

### What is physically documented

- The 2017 study is based on visual/low-magnification observation of the cranium in the Iraq Museum in 1976–78. No cranial radiography was available, and later reanalysis was not feasible. Both external auditory canals are preserved; the right is bridged and nearly blocked by Grade 3 exostoses, and the left is markedly narrowed. `EVD-001020–001021`; `CLM-001011`. [`SRC-001005`, Materials and methods, Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684)
- The supplementary review documents a fully healed left orbital fracture; multiple healed fractures in the right humerus with a rounded distal end and absence of the forearm/hand; asymmetry of shoulder elements; a healed clavicular infection; altered gait markers; and probable DISH. The arm evidence favors fracture/non-union followed by loss or amputation; it does not show a head injury caused right-side paralysis. `EVD-001022`; `CLM-001011`. [`SRC-001005`, S1 Text pp. 2–9](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=supplementary&id=info:doi/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684.s001)

### Functional inference, unbundled

- **Auditory anatomy and hearing:** bilateral Grade 3 auditory exostoses are documented. Effective right-ear deafness and at least partial left conductive hearing loss are **Plausible, strong clinical inferences**, but hearing was not measured. `EVD-001021–001023`; `CLM-001012`. [`SRC-001005`, Fig. 1 and discussion](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684)
- **Right arm:** healed distal-humeral fractures, non-union/atrophy, shoulder asymmetry, and absence of the forearm/hand are documented. The authors infer loss or amputation and major functional consequences, but actual one-arm task performance was not measured. `EVD-001022`; `CLM-001011`. [`SRC-001005`, S1 Text pp. 3–5](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=supplementary&id=info:doi/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684.s001)
- **Gait:** lower-limb asymmetry and remodeling are documented; altered gait is the authors' functional inference, not observed behavior. `EVD-001022`; `CLM-001011`. [`SRC-001005`, S1 Text pp. 7–8](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=supplementary&id=info:doi/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684.s001)
- **Orbit and vision:** a healed left orbital fracture is documented, but vision and coordination were not measured. This packet therefore makes no positive visual-impairment or coordination claim. `EVD-001022`; `CLM-001011`. [`SRC-001005`, S1 Text pp. 2–3](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=supplementary&id=info:doi/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684.s001)
- **Duration:** survival for more than two decades after the right-arm injury is the authors' skeletal-growth interpretation, not a directly dated life-history event. The cumulative picture matters, but no single alteration proves complete dependence. `CLM-001013`. [`SRC-001005`, S1 Text pp. 3–5](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=supplementary&id=info:doi/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684.s001)

### Assistance and contradiction

- Trinkaus and Villotte argue that cumulative impairments made long survival in a Pleistocene foraging setting extremely unlikely without social support. Dettwyler's critique warns that one-armed, deaf, or mobility-impaired people can adapt and contribute, and that inability/productivity should not be assumed. This packet therefore keeps **some social support Plausible but necessary care Disputed; total incapacitation and one-way dependency are not established**. `EVD-001024–001025`; `CLM-001013–001015`.
- A full 2025 disability-led reanalysis reportedly challenges incapacity and necessary-care assumptions, but only its abstract was accessible. Until `SRC-001006` is opened in full, it is a flagged contradiction, not verified evidence.

## Cross-case findings

1. **Survival is not one observation.** It may mean short survival after trauma, decades of adaptation, survival to skeletal manifestation, or simply that an alteration healed. Specify the interval and what dates it.
2. **Healing is bounded evidence.** It establishes biological response before death; it does not identify operator, indication, social response, emotion, treatment success, or quality of life. `SRC-001001` shows that even estimates of duration from cranial remodeling remain preliminary.
3. **Assistance is claim-relative.** M9 provides a substantially stronger counterfactual case for recurring assistance than Shanidar 1. M487 provides none.
4. **Care and compassion are different propositions.** Care is a behavior or arrangement that material evidence may sometimes imply. Compassion is an internal state/motive that this packet cannot observe. [`SRC-001007`, paras. 30–36](https://journals.openedition.org/alterjdr/10781)
5. **Impairment and disability are different analytical levels.** Skeletons can preserve some impairments. Disability includes environmental and social restriction, accommodation, identity, and relation; those are not recoverable from lesion alone. [`SRC-001004`, pp. 380–384](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.1330840402)
6. **The archive selects spectacular bone-visible cases.** Soft-tissue illness, sensory differences without bony markers, mental distress, fluctuating conditions, everyday interdependence, neglect without injury, and much caregiving disappear.
7. **Modern clinical literature is a comparator, not a time machine.** It can bound physiological consequences, but technology, nutrition, environment, individual variation, and cultural organization prevent exact reconstruction.
8. **Similar burial treatment is not a life-history transcript.** It may document inclusion in one mortuary act; it cannot by itself establish acceptance, status, affection, stigma, or the experience of care.

## Tested stop condition

The three cases are sufficient to test the ladder:

- M487 stops at deliberate modification/timing alternatives and cannot reach survival or care.
- M9 can reach plausible recurring assistance, strongly favored by the case authors but pending an independent functional check; it stops before precise routine, emotion, personality, or quality of life.
- Shanidar 1 reaches cumulative impairment and plausible social support but remains actively contested on incapacity and necessity.

The packet should not expand into a catalog of prehistoric healing. The next work is independent source/wording review, acquisition of `SRC-001006`, and application of this ladder by neighboring era packets.
