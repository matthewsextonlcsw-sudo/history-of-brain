## Archive-power audit

- The catalogue is organized through colonial and later archival administrative categories.
  Its historical labels identify the record but are not neutral clinical or identity language.
- Three files were created by the Kedah State Secretariat and one by the Selangor State
  Secretariat according to ANM. That provenance centers state administration before any
  person described in the titles.
- Naming is unequal: one transfer title names a woman while leaving two men unnamed. This is a
  catalogue fact, not evidence about why the difference occurred or how the underlying file is
  structured.
- The catalogue's `VERSI MELAYU-JAWI` marker is an acquisition signal. It does not establish
  who wrote Malay/Jawi, whether English and Malay texts correspond, or whether vernacular words
  came from an affected person.
- ANM's current public interface shows orderability but not file pages. The present silence is
  an access-layer limit, not evidence that testimony, documents or file leaves never existed.

## Mediation fields required after acquisition

Every page, enclosure and annotation must be logged with:

- physical or digital sequence, cover, folio/page number, missing or duplicated leaves;
- document form, date, issuing office, addressee and administrative purpose;
- author, hand, signature, typist, copyist, clerk, translator and certifier when identifiable;
- whether speech is first person, dictated, translated, summarized, reported, elicited,
  witnessed, certified, alleged or copied from another record;
- English, Malay, Jawi and any other language/script present, with qualified transcription and
  translation plus material variants;
- whether the person discussed knew of, participated in, agreed with, contested or was absent
  from the record—using `Unknown` unless the file directly establishes it;
- decision, implementation and outcome as separate fields; an application, recommendation,
  order and completed action must never be collapsed;
- redactions, stamps, marginalia, later annotations and archive-order changes;
- exact sensitivity, privacy, dignity, family/descendant and public-indexing decision.

## Language and specialist gates

1. **Malay/Jawi:** qualified reading of script, colonial administrative/legal usage, spelling,
   formulae, names and translation variants. Romanized catalogue text is not the control.
2. **Malaysian/postcolonial institutional history:** place the offices, law, hospital routes and
   archival practices without importing a progress story.
3. **Chinese and Malay community/language review:** required if opened content centers those
   communities; names and categories must not be treated as transparent ethnicity or language.
4. **Islamic/Malaysian family-law history:** required for the 1932 file before interpreting
   `fasakh`, jurisdiction, procedure, agency or outcome.
5. **Disability/survivor and privacy review:** required for every public-facing decision,
   including whether a historically named person should be named at all.
6. **Self-harm/trauma review:** the 1941 catalogue description is not a diagnosis, motive or
   dangerousness finding. No incident detail, quotation, image, name, headline or SEO hook.

## Privacy and dignity controls

- The brief prohibits public use of names, family conflict, alleged offenses, health
  information, self-harm and facsimiles. This dossier repeats accession-linked names only where
  necessary to identify the frozen source.
- Age of a record does not eliminate privacy, family or descendant interests.
- Do not create a searchable roster, diagnosis card, timeline entry, person profile or family
  reconstruction from these records.
- Do not search later genealogical, civil, medical or newspaper records to re-identify unnamed
  people or trace descendants without a separately authorized protocol.
- Do not publish signatures, handwriting, addresses, financial details, religious/legal family
  details or identifying medical material merely because ANM permits research access.
- Public indexing, quotation, facsimile reuse, archival permission, copyright and ethical use
  each require a separate affirmative decision.

## Missing voices that must remain visible

- every named or unnamed person described by the catalogue;
- relatives and caregivers other than a title-implied applicant;
- nurses, attendants, dressers, clerks, translators, messengers and subordinate workers;
- people who did not enter institutional or legal records;
- people whose words may have been translated, summarized or excluded;
- Malay-, Chinese-language-, Tamil-language- and other vernacular community accounts outside
  these administrative files;
- people whose names or records did not survive the catalogue system.

No absence above authorizes reconstruction of inner experience. If the four files are acquired
and still contain only official voices, the packet must say so and stop at that evidence ceiling.
