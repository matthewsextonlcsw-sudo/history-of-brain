## Frozen corpus and ceiling

This packet covers Dullunda Native Lunatic Asylum, Calcutta, from Act XXXVI of 1858 through the complete 1867, 1869, and 1872 Bengal asylum report items. It can establish what colonial law authorized and what the official archive recorded. It cannot by itself establish implementation of the Act, patient or worker experience, consent or coercion, family motive, therapeutic benefit, prevalence, or modern diagnosis.

The annual reports, their tables, visitor minutes, superintendent reports, and appended government resolutions form one colonial administrative chain. Repetition across years is not independent corroboration.

## Acquisition and replay method

Agent Reach 1.5.0 was current on 2026-08-19; Jina/direct web were available and Exa was unavailable. The official India Code viewer was used for the complete nine-page Act. Because ordinary direct download returned 404, a complete public legal-text access copy was captured, hashed and section/schedule-cross-checked to that official viewer; it is expressly not an official facsimile. The National Library of Scotland (NLS) parent collection was traversed leaf by leaf for the three annual-report items. Pivotal tabular and printed-page claims were replayed against page images. A similarly titled 1872 Internet Archive/Wikimedia PDF was downloaded during discovery but rejected after replay showed that its pagination/content did not match the NLS annual-report item; it supports no evidence or claim.

- **1867:** 136 NLS OCR leaves, `83377994–83378399`, including title, report, appendices, resolution, and terminal leaves.
- **1869:** 83 available OCR leaves in `83378647–83378905`; four blank opening leaves (`83378653`, `83378656`, `83378659`, `83378662`) returned no OCR object. Title, complete report, resolution, and terminal leaves were opened. This availability caveat does not affect the located claims.
- **1872:** 159 NLS OCR leaves, `83379523–83379997`, constituting the complete NLS item.
- **Image replay:** 27 pivotal NLS page images. Named-patient pages were used only for internal verification; no name is extracted into the packet.
