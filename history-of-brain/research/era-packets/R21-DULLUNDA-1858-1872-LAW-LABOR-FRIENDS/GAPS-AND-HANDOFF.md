## Myths and narrative traps to block

- **“The Act protected patients.”** It designed procedures; implementation and effects remain unknown.
- **“Work was humane occupational therapy.”** The reports document managerial work and value, not consent or benefit.
- **“Work was definitely forced labor in every recorded instance.”** Confinement and discipline are crucial context, but this corpus does not document the terms or experience of each task. Do not overclaim the legal or experiential conclusion.
- **“Families abandoned relatives to avoid costs.”** This is not family testimony; the official report itself speculates.
- **“Transfer to friends meant recovery.”** It is an administrative destination/category, not an outcome account.
- **“Women were less mentally ill.”** Admission shares are not prevalence; the reports call the figures doubtful.
- **“Cured” equals modern clinical recovery or treatment success.** It is a period administrative label with disclosed classification limits.
- **“Several annual reports prove the pattern independently.”** They are one colonial administrative chain.
- **“No direct quotation in these reports means patients and families were silent.”** The defined Dullunda sections yielded no affected-person statement, while the 1867 parent item contains out-of-scope Cuttack speech mediated by administrators. Neither finding establishes historical silence.
- **“Public domain means safe to publish.”** Copyright, privacy, dignity, harm, quotation, image use, commercial framing and indexing are separate gates.
