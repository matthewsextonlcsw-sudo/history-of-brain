# R21 Dullunda Law, Labor, and Friends — Research Brief

**Status:** Frozen candidate for Batch 06 internal research with a hard archive ceiling; no public prose

**Place and dates:** Dullunda Native Lunatic Asylum, Calcutta, Bengal Presidency; 1858–1872

**Question:** How did Dullunda's colonial archive connect legal admission, detention and release,
patient work and institutional revenue, mortality and transport, and relatives' or friends'
custody and maintenance obligations—and whose experience did that archive omit?

## Frozen corpus

Use Act XXXVI of 1858 in full and three exact administrative snapshots: the complete 1867, 1869,
and 1872 Bengal asylum report items, with Dullunda pages, visitor minutes, labor tables,
government resolutions, and expenditure records bound to their complete parent volumes. Other
Bengal institutions may appear only as explicitly denominated table context. Cache lawful full
items with hashes before substantive extraction; isolated page derivatives are not enough.

## Required controls

- Law is legal design, not implementation. Separate magistrate, police, civil-court,
  certification, visitor, friend/relative care, and maintenance-liability provisions.
- Official reports, resolutions, visitor minutes, and tables are one colonial administrative
  chain, even across years. Multiple years do not become independent viewpoints.
- Work, workshops, outside labor, production, revenue, and managerial appraisal do not establish
  consent, refusal, wages, injury, therapeutic benefit, or coercion.
- Named release decisions and individual institutional traces are not first-person patient voice.
  Do not publish names by default.
- Administrators' statements about relatives' motives, women's admissions, crowding, and custody
  applications remain attributed speculation, not family testimony or prevalence.
- Historic administrative categories, purported causes, religion, caste, occupation, cure, and
  “criminal” status require source-language and colonial-category control. Bengali-language and
  vernacular press/petition research remains a required publication gap.

## Exclusions and stop conditions

Exclude all-India/South-Asia generalization, Southeast Asia, post-1872 law or institutions,
modern diagnosis, efficacy, prevalence, causal caste/religion claims, and direct patient/family/
worker experience claims. If complete 1867 and 1869 parent items cannot be opened and hashed,
their claims remain `HOLD`. This packet may document mediated traces and archive silence; it may
not invent the missing voices or produce public prose.
