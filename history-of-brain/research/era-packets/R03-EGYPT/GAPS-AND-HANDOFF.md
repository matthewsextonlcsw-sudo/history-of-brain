# R03-EGYPT gaps and handoff

Status: internal stop report; no public prose.

## Packet stop assessment

The packet has enough evidence to propose a bounded outline segment on:

- Edwin Smith Case 6 as a trauma/anatomical observation with an explicit anti-generalization fence;
- Papyrus Ebers `jb`/`ḥꜣ.tj`, palpation, vessel language, and plural practitioner roles;
- Book of the Dead heart traditions and the Shabaka heart/tongue passage, with genre/date limits;
- the false certainty of the “discarded brain because useless” story, using Herodotus plus CT variation;
- grief/family harm and constrained social access through UC 16244, the *Dialogue*, and Heqanakht, with distinct voice types and no unqualified “isolation” inference for Heqanakht's wife;
- archive, collection, translation, regional, temporal, and human-remains cautions.

It does **not** have enough evidence for a comprehensive Egypt chapter across 3000 BCE–600 CE, a history of “mental illness treatment,” prevalence, or a single Egyptian model of self/mind.

## Cross-packet handoffs

### R03-NUBIA-KUSH — required, not optional

- BM EA498/Shabaka belongs to a 25th-Dynasty political and cultural context that cannot be narrated as merely another “Egyptian” episode. The Egyptian object record is relevant, but Nubian/Kushite histories, institutions, languages, and modern source authority need their own research.
- Research questions: How do Sudanese/Nubian scholars characterize Dynasty 25 intellectual/religious exchange? What evidence exists for healing, personhood, distress, care, and mortuary practice in Kerma, Napata, and Meroe? What changed through Egyptian occupation, Kushite rule, Ptolemaic/Roman frontiers, and later periods?
- Required source posture: prioritize Sudanese archaeological authorities, Nubian community/history projects, excavation reports with provenance, and scholarship that does not treat Nubia as an Egyptian periphery.
- Boundary: do not export `CLM-003007` or `CLM-003014` into a Nubia/Kush narrative without that packet’s review.

### Eastern Mediterranean / Greek packet

- Herodotus 2.86 is a Greek observer’s account of Egyptian embalming. It belongs simultaneously to Egyptian reception and Greek ethnographic writing.
- Handoff question: how did Greek authors select, translate, moralize, or systematize Egyptian practices, and how did later medical histories turn their descriptions into claims about Egyptian belief?
- Boundary: Herodotus is direct evidence for what Herodotus reports, not Egyptian first-person explanation.

### Ancient Near East / Levant packet

- Papyrus Ebers includes ingredients/geographic terms and traditions moving through wider networks; this packet did not research transmission.
- Handoff question: compare body-part, heart, breath, dream, affliction, and healing vocabularies without assuming borrowing from superficial similarity.

### Rome / Late Antiquity packet

- The Zesch sample extends to 395 CE and includes elite burials blending Egyptian and Greek artistic traditions. A late antique handoff should examine continuity, transformation, and plurality rather than treating “Roman Egypt” as the end of authentic Egyptian practice.
- Coptic and monastic care after ca. 300 CE remains a direct gap.

### Historiography / colonial collections packet

- Breasted’s “science” framing, Luxor-to-Leipzig acquisition of Ebers, BM EA498’s aristocratic donation, Amherst/Morgan collecting, ISAC purchases, and European mummy collections need a connected historiography.
- Handoff question: how have excavation, colonial law, collecting, translation, museum display, and popular mummy entertainment shaped what now counts as evidence about Egyptian minds?

## Claims withheld from the outline

- Any diagnosis of the *Dialogue* speaker, Ebers case descriptions, Heqanakht’s wife, or an unnamed trauma patient.
- Any claim that the heart was universally “the mind” or that the brain was universally worthless.
- Any claim that all Egyptian healers combined magic and medicine in the same way.
- Any population-level claim about how common distress, violence, cognitive impairment, or particular care practices were.
- Any claim that mummification method directly reveals belief about cognition.
- Any claim extending Egyptian evidence to Nubia/Kush or vice versa without the dedicated packet.
