## Claim records

### `CLM-003000` — Case 6 records direct observation of a severe head wound

- **Claim text:** In the copied trauma treatise now called the Edwin Smith Papyrus, Case 6 (recto II.17–III.1) describes a gaping skull wound, exposed corrugated internal tissue translated by Breasted as brain, pulsation, nasal bleeding, neck stiffness, a grave prognosis, and limited alleviatory care.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive; one textual case, Egypt, manuscript copied in the second millennium BCE; individual patient vignette; universality individual.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003000` from `SRC-003001`, OIP 3 pp. 164–171, L1-A; facsimile `SRC-003002`, L1-A.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in this edition; medium-high wording confidence pending modern translation check.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; medical terminology and trauma.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003000`.
- **Wording limits:** do not say “Egyptians discovered the brain,” “first neurologist,” “neuroscience,” or that the case localizes cognition.
- **Review:** evidence-linked; translation review pending.

### `CLM-003001` — Case 6 does not establish a general brain theory

- **Claim text:** Case 6 documents anatomical observation in a trauma genre but does not, by itself, establish a society-wide Egyptian theory that the brain produces thought, memory, emotion, or personhood.
- **Type / scope:** evidentiary-limit claim; Egypt, long-duration claims; unknown population.
- **Evidence:** absence within `SRC-003001` Case 6, pp. 164–171; contrast with passage-specific heart evidence `EVD-003002`, `EVD-003006`, `EVD-003007`.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported limitation; high.
- **Anachronism:** high if ignored.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003000`.
- **Wording limits:** do not substitute “Egyptians believed the heart was the brain”; multiple genres coexist.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003002` — Eb 854a joins palpation to multiple practitioner roles

- **Claim text:** Eb 854a, col. 99.1–5, describes palpation at several body locations and names a physician, Sekhmet priest, and magician as practitioners assessing the `ḥꜣ.tj` through vessels.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive; Papyrus Ebers compilation, New Kingdom manuscript; textual practitioner model, not prevalence.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003002`, `SRC-003005`, Eb 854a, L1-A.
- **Label / confidence:** documented; high for the current translation.
- **Current evidence note:** this vessel model is not modern circulation and the roles do not map cleanly to current professions.
- **Anachronism:** high; sacred/clinical language.
- **Wording limits:** do not frame as “science versus superstition,” collaborative licensure, or universal practice.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003003` — Ebers’s vessel books are not modern circulation

- **Claim text:** Eb 854–856 presents a heterogeneous network of vessels, air, fluids, organs, symptoms, and supernatural agents rather than a modern cardiovascular or nervous-system model.
- **Type / scope:** interpretive; same manuscript/sections.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003003`–`EVD-003004`, `SRC-003005`, cols. 99–103, L1-A/B.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported; high.
- **Anachronism:** high.
- **Wording limits:** do not claim discovery of circulation, pulse diagnosis as currently defined, or a unified physiological system without specialist qualification.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003004` — `jb` and `ḥꜣ.tj` are context-sensitive, disputed terms

- **Claim text:** The semantic and anatomical relationship of `jb` and `ḥꜣ.tj` varies by text and genre and remains disputed in scholarship; neither term should be assigned one universal modern equivalent.
- **Type / scope:** historiographic/translation; Pharaonic Egyptian texts.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003005`; `SRC-003006`, pp. 235–247, L2-B; `SRC-003005`, Eb 854–856, L1-B; dependence caution `SRC-003007`, L2-C.
- **Label / confidence:** documented disagreement; high.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003001`.
- **Anachronism:** high.
- **Wording limits:** prohibit “the `ib` was the mind” and “the `haty` was only the physical heart” as universal statements.
- **Review:** source-verified; requires Egyptian-language reviewer before public wording.

### `CLM-003005` — Ebers records embodied states without modern diagnoses

- **Claim text:** Eb 855u–z records descriptions involving anger, darkness/restriction, fatigue, racing, distraction, and forgetfulness in `jb`/body language.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive; Papyrus Ebers col. 102.5–15.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003004`, `SRC-003005`, L1-A.
- **Label / confidence:** documented; high for the bounded passage.
- **Current evidence note:** symptom resemblance cannot establish a modern diagnosis.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; clinical and identity risk.
- **Wording limits:** no “depression,” “dementia,” “anxiety,” “ADHD,” “psychosis,” or prevalence claim unless explicitly presented as a modern, disputed comparison.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003006` — Mortuary texts associate the heart with thought, memory, and testimony

- **Claim text:** In Book of the Dead heart spells and heart-scarab objects, the heart could be protected as a bearer of thought/memory and controlled as a possible witness in postmortem judgment.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive/interpretive; mortuary traditions attested across named objects, especially Dynasties 17–26; object-owning deceased.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003006`, `SRC-003008`, OIMP 39 pp. 183–190, L1-A/L2-B. Reserved `SRC-003022` is withdrawn as a duplicate and is not independent evidence.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in these mortuary sources; high.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; sacred/mortuary.
- **Wording limits:** not a universal daily psychology; do not call the heart a brain substitute.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003007` — The Shabaka heart-and-tongue passage is theological and date-contested

- **Claim text:** BM EA498 contains a cosmological passage in which sensation reports to the heart and the tongue enacts what the heart devises, but the text is religious, damaged, uniquely preserved, and its conceptual date is disputed.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive plus provenance limit; Memphis, object ca. 710 BCE.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003007`; `SRC-003009` EA498 object record, L1-A; `SRC-003010` transcript, L3-A/C.
- **Label / confidence:** documented passage; disputed dating.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003005`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; sacred.
- **Wording limits:** no “Egyptian cognitive science” or all-Egyptian generalization.
- **Review:** source-verified; specialist translation desirable.

### `CLM-003008` — Brain removal was variable, not universal

- **Claim text:** Available textual and CT evidence shows variation in embalming-related brain removal by method, period, location, age, and sample; universal removal is false.
- **Type / scope:** comparative; Herodotus’s 5th-c. BCE report plus a 21-child museum sample mostly 664 BCE–395 CE.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003008`, `SRC-003011` §§2.86.2–3, L1-A/B; `EVD-003009`, `SRC-003012`, Results subsection “Excerebration, evisceration and packing materials,” paragraph beginning “Excerebration was observed…,” plus S2 Table, L1-A.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported; high within stated scopes.
- **Current evidence note:** CT identifies material traces, not beliefs or motives.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003002`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; human remains.
- **Wording limits:** never “the Egyptians removed the brain” without period/sample qualifiers.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003009` — the packet audit found no source for the alleged disposal motive

- **Claim text:** Within this packet's named opened-source audit, no primary Egyptian source or checked direct study supplied the alleged rationale that brain tissue was discarded because it was considered cognitively useless; the ancient rationale remains unknown.
- **Type / scope:** evidence-absence/claim audit; sources opened in this packet.
- **Evidence:** `SRC-003011` lacks disposal/motive; `SRC-003012` measures retention/removal/filling but not motive; `SRC-003023` is an unsupported downstream public claim.
- **Label / confidence:** **documented packet-audit result** within the named search boundary; **unknown** for ancient motive. Future evidence can change the audit result.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003002`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; human remains and cultural stereotype.
- **Wording limits:** do not convert “not found” into proof that no one ever discarded tissue.
- **Review:** source-verified for opened corpus.

### `CLM-003010` — UC 16244 preserves a woman's family-protection appeal

- **Claim text:** The Hu letter to the dead, UC 16244, preserves a woman's first-person appeal about grief, wrongdoing against her daughter, pain, offerings, and a request for the deceased to intervene; UCL headlines her as a widow while transliteration `snt ... sn.s` and translation line 1 render “A sister speaks to her brother.”
- **Type / scope:** lived-experience/documentary; First Intermediate Period, Hu; one family voice.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003010`, `SRC-003014`, lines 1–6, L1-A/L3-B.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in the institutional translation; medium pending qualified resolution of the kin term and translation provenance.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; grief/family harm.
- **Wording limits:** not a clinical patient account; “first-person” does not mean unmediated or autograph. Do not collapse “widow” and “sister” into a dual identity or silently assign Gardiner/Sethe or Wente as the webpage translator.
- **Review:** source-verified for the webpage; English translator not named and Egyptian-language review required before public use.

### `CLM-003011` — The *Dialogue* stages distress, but its suicide reading is disputed

- **Claim text:** The *Dialogue of a Man and His Ba* stages social alienation, misery, death imagery, and internal disagreement; reading it as an explicit suicide declaration remains disputed.
- **Type / scope:** literary/interpretive; Middle Kingdom composition/manuscript witnesses; fictional or literary speaker.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003011`, `SRC-003017`, lines 11–19 and 103–155, L3-B; `EVD-003012`, `SRC-003018`, pp. 217–235, L2-B; object context `SRC-003016`/`SRC-003019`, L1-A.
- **Label / confidence:** documented literary themes; disputed suicide inference.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003003`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; self-harm.
- **Wording limits:** no diagnosis, “suicide note,” patient record, or autobiographical certainty.
- **Review:** source-verified; safety/editorial review required for public use.

### `CLM-003012` — Heqanakht’s letters document household mistreatment through a male authority

- **Claim text:** Heqanakht's Letters I–II document efforts to stop members of his household from mistreating/harassing his wife and from keeping a friend, hairdresser, or domestic from her, but the evidence reaches her through her husband's commands rather than her own testimony.
- **Type / scope:** lived-experience/household; Dynasty 12, Theban household; one named family network.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-003013`, `SRC-003021`, translations pp. 15–17 and analysis pp. 109–110, L1-A/L2-B; object 22.3.517, `SRC-003020`, L1-A.
- **Label / confidence:** documented mistreatment; exact form partly uncertain.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-003004`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium-high; family harm/gender/power.
- **Wording limits:** no sexual-assault claim; no claim that Heqanakht's defense supplies his wife's perspective. Use “isolation” only if explicitly labeled an inference from denied access, not Allen's translated term.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003013` — The surviving archive cannot support prevalence or a timeless “Egyptians believed” summary

- **Claim text:** The surviving evidence is selected by literacy, elite and mortuary production, material durability, excavation and antiquities-market history, museum collecting, translation, and modern research priorities; it cannot support population prevalence or a single stable Egyptian psychology across three millennia.
- **Type / scope:** archive/method claim; whole packet.
- **Evidence:** object/provenance records `SRC-003000`, `SRC-003004`, `SRC-003008`–`SRC-003009`, `SRC-003014`, `SRC-003016`, `SRC-003019`–`SRC-003021`; CT sample limits in `SRC-003012`, Materials, Table 1, and Discussion paragraphs beginning “Older textiles were reused…” and “This sample was not appropriate for a demographic evaluation…”.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported; high.
- **Anachronism:** high if omitted.
- **Wording limits:** default to “this text/object/sample”; avoid “the Egyptians believed” unless the sentence explicitly names evidence range and uncertainty.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-003014` — Egypt and Nubia/Kush require connected but distinct histories

- **Claim text:** Evidence assigned to Egypt in this packet cannot stand in for Nubian/Kushite communities, even where political rule, objects, people, or ideas crossed the Nile Valley.
- **Type / scope:** scope/relationship claim; Nile Valley.
- **Evidence:** packet boundary and BM EA498’s 25th-Dynasty context (`SRC-003009`) show why political/cultural entanglement matters, but this packet did not research Nubian/Kushite community evidence.
- **Label / confidence:** **required project scope guardrail / proposed cross-packet claim**; not established as “Strongly supported” by R03-EGYPT alone.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; colonial geography and identity.
- **Wording limits:** do not absorb Dynasty 25 into an undifferentiated “Egyptian” story; hand off to R03-NUBIA-KUSH research.
- **Review:** proposed; requires R03-NUBIA-KUSH evidence and cross-packet review before evidentiary promotion.
