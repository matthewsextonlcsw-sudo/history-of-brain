# R03-EGYPT — Heart, Brain, Body, Family, and Archive

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Owner:** One research session; this folder only

**Identifier range:** `SRC/EVD/CLM-003000–003999`

## Research question

What do surviving Egyptian medical, religious, literary, administrative, and household
sources support about heart, brain, body, emotion, suffering, care, and personhood—and how can
we prevent one trauma papyrus from becoming “Egyptian neuroscience”?

## Boundary

Egypt within its named periods and sites, approximately 3000 BCE–600 CE. Nubia and Kush belong
to `R03-NUBIA-KUSH`; contact is recorded as a handoff, not absorbed into Egypt.

## In scope

- Edwin Smith and other relevant medical papyri in authoritative editions;
- heart/*ib* and related terminology, with translation and genre distinctions;
- observation of head injury versus theories of mind or brain function;
- letters to the dead, petitions, literary laments, household and family evidence;
- healing, temple, household, specialist, and legal contexts where evidenced;
- changes across Egyptian periods rather than a timeless doctrine; and
- preservation, excavation, translation, class, literacy, gender, and colonial collection bias.

## Out of scope

- using Egypt as a proxy for Africa;
- Nubian or Kushite beliefs inferred from Egyptian texts;
- modern diagnosis of ancient writers or cases;
- the claim that Egyptians “discovered neuroscience”; and
- public prose.

## Required evidence targets

- papyrus/object/catalog records and reliable editions for core claims;
- Egyptological scholarship on terminology, genre, and historical context;
- more than one scholarly interpretation for contested heart/brain claims;
- direct or mediated non-elite/family evidence where it survives;
- provenance and rights for any papyrus image or museum object; and
- upstream checks for popular claims about the brain being discarded or ignored.
