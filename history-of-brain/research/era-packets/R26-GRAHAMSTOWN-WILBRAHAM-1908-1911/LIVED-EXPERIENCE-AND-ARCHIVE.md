## Source-layer and mediation chain

1. A historical person wrote a letter dated 23 May 1910.
2. The institution received, read, filed, and possibly withheld it; the particular reason remains uncertain.
3. The manuscript entered an institutional patient-letter file and was cross-referenced in a casebook.
4. Lindy Wilbraham located the source chain and published what she describes as the entire letter transcription in 2014.
5. R26 opened the complete 2014 article, not the manuscript or the other archival/private parent objects.
6. R26 describes the published transcription without reproducing its wording and treats event truth, diagnosis, causation, capacity, policy, and later family contact as separate questions.

No step may be collapsed. In particular, “the complete published transcription was opened” does not mean “the original letter was opened,” “the transcription was image-verified,” or “the reported events were independently corroborated.”

## Archive asymmetry and silence

- The article's unusually layered record trail is connected to a white settler, English-language, civil-service family and to descendant access to private materials. That makes the case valuable for studying record formation, not representative of the asylum population.
- Institutional and colonial-administrative systems produced more surviving description than affected people, families, or workers authored directly. Record density is not evidentiary neutrality.
- The absence of later correspondence or visits in the opened chain cannot establish abandonment, lack of care, or a spouse's motives. `DIS-038400` preserves this uncertainty.
- No worker-authored voice in the opened article means the packet cannot narrate worker experience or institutional conduct through inference.
- No Black or isiXhosa first-person trace in this case means R26 cannot fill that gap by analogy. A separately scoped and reviewed corpus is required.
- The annual report `G41–1909` is a held acquisition target, not a complete institutional context source.

| Risk | Required control before further use |

| Manuscript quotation or image | Confirm current custody, copyright/reproduction authority, privacy conditions, and exact object identity; replay every side before quoting |
| Racialized and colonial terminology | Preserve exact historical terms only when identification or analysis requires them; frame rather than normalize them; obtain South African historical/editorial review |
| Public narrative | Independent evidence review plus rights, privacy, descendant, regional/community, clinical-language, and anti-sensationalism review |

## Language and place-name controls

- The opened case material is mediated through English. No isiXhosa original or translation was opened, and none may be implied.
- Use `Grahamstown/Makhanda` and `Idutywa/Dutywa` at first reference. Retain an archival form only when needed to identify a record.
- Paired naming supplies temporal and geographic context; it does not resolve colonial power, community authority, or representativeness.
- Period diagnostic and administrative labels must be attributed and framed. They are not present-day clinical determinations.

## Public-use decision
