## Chronological and regional assembly decisions

### 1. South Asian texts and institutions need separate clocks

`R13-SOUTH-ASIA-MEDIEVAL` supports three bounded settings: chapter 20 of the received
*Mādhavanidāna*, the 1069 Tirumukkūḍal inscription, and the received transmission of poem 4 of
the *Tiruvācakam*. Composition, inscription, copying, edition, translation, and modern reception
dates must remain separate.

The Tirumukkūḍal inscription documents an institution and provisions but does not state mental
or behavioral indications. A modern psychiatric-hospital interpretation moves beyond what the
inscription says and remains quarantined. Devotional intensity in a Tamil poem is not a modern
diagnosis, patient chart, or proof of therapy.

### 2. “Medieval Chinese medicine” is not one stable book or practice

`R13-CHINA-MEDIEVAL` distinguishes Tang compilations known through later witnesses, Southern
Song recipe-book and transmission disputes, and selected household and death-investigation
texts. Later editions, commentaries, diplomatic transcriptions, simplified displays, and
modern translations are different evidence surfaces.

The packet can support passage-specific nodes about terminology, family norms, recipes, and
official investigation. It cannot establish timeless continuity, population-wide practice,
modern diagnoses, or one Chinese theory of mind and distress.

### 3. Korea and Japan require separate lanes before a contact story

`R13-KOREA-JAPAN-MEDIEVAL` deliberately preserves one bounded Korean lane, one bounded Japanese
lane, and a separate contact question. The contact passage `新羅法師方` is located on PDF page
59, right-hand leaf, in the consulted scan; that textual contact is not evidence that two
societies shared one system or that every associated practice traveled with it.

Later editions and modern disciplinary reception must not be mistaken for medieval use. The
packet does not authorize national “firsts,” diagnostic equivalence, Kampo continuity, or a
general Buddhist-therapy narrative.

### 4. Inscription, excavation, and manuscript are three different Southeast Asian archives

`R13-SOUTHEAST-ASIA-MEDIEVAL` separates the K.209/K.368 Angkorian hospital-formula chain, the
small Tonle Snguot excavation sample, and the Dharma Pātañjala known through a 1467 copy whose
composition date remains uncertain.

In K.209, *manoroga* belongs to royal eulogy; it is not a patient diagnosis or evidence of a
mental-health service. The four-varna formula is a norm, not proof of universal, free, or equal
access. A small excavated sample and material finds cannot establish building function,
treatment, or outcomes without additional evidence. The Dharma Pātañjala is a soteriological
text in a bounded edition chain, not evidence of psychotherapy.

### 5. West Africa and the Sahel cannot be reconstructed from one traveler

`R14-WEST-AFRICA-SAHEL` keeps Ibn Battuta's fourteenth-century Mali account, Gao/Gao-Saney
epigraphy and archaeology, later Timbuktu/Songhay chronicle transmission, and twentieth-century
Malio performance in distinct source positions. External observation, local Arabic writing,
inscription, radiocarbon modeling, later manuscript transmission, and recorded performance do
not answer the same question.

The packet corrects specific witness, commemoration, calendar, and dating errors while refusing
to use later performance as a verbatim medieval record. No opened 800–1600 local-language Ajami
item passed this packet's gate. That absence is a research gap, not evidence that local knowledge
or affected people's accounts did not exist.

### 6. Medieval Europe was not a march from possession to the asylum

`R15-EUROPE-MEDIEVAL` separates Canterbury and English royal-law records, Bingen/Rhineland
texts, and Salerno/Monte Cassino transmission. Household action, royal writ, theological or
medical writing, manuscript transmission, charitable foundation, and later institutional
reporting are different archives.

The evidence rejects possession-only and Church-versus-medicine binaries. It also rejects
Bethlem's 1247 foundation as proof of the “first psychiatric hospital.” The 1247 foundation,
the bounded pre-1350 evidence gap, and an attributed 1403 report are separate claims. The
surviving records do not establish consent, routine implementation, outcome, prevalence, or
ordinary recipient experience.

## Cross-packet findings that survive the seam audit

### A later witness is not the date of an idea

Across Sanskrit, Tamil, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Old Khmer, Old Javanese, Arabic, Latin, and
vernacular evidence, the date of composition may differ from inscription, copying, compilation,
translation, commentary, edition, cataloging, digitization, and modern reception. Every internal
node must expose the relevant clocks rather than choosing the oldest attractive date.

### An institution is a bundle of claims

A named place, foundation, donor formula, staff list, prescription, room, artifact, rule, or
later description can establish only its bounded proposition. It cannot alone prove admission
criteria, psychiatric specialization, delivered care, universal access, consent, effectiveness,
or patient experience. This applies equally to Tirumukkūḍal, Angkorian foundations, Bethlem,
shrines, households, courts, and charitable institutions.

### Elite and official archives cannot impersonate recipient voices

Royal eulogy, medical compilation, devotional poetry, law, forensic instruction, travel
writing, chronicle, foundation records, and learned treatises mostly preserve authors,
officials, patrons, scribes, translators, and later editors. Even when they describe distress or
care, they rarely preserve an unmediated affected person's account. Each outline node must name
the nearest surviving voice and the mediation that separates it from lived experience.

### Contact and influence require a named chain

A shared term, neighboring polity, religious label, similar practice, or later national
tradition does not establish transmission. Contact claims require a named witness, passage,
translation, person, route, object, or specialist textual comparison. Parallel resemblance must
remain a comparison, not become diffusion.

### Modern labels create false continuity

Words translated as mind, madness, illness, possession, hospital, therapy, psychology, or
psychiatry cannot be equated with current categories without passage-specific historical and
clinical work. Modern “first” and continuity claims belong in dated reception audits, not in the
historical voice of the outline.

### Archive, custody, and rights belong inside the history

What can be known depends on manuscript survival, excavation scope, colonial and national
collections, current custody, war and conservation, cataloging, digitization, translation,
copyright, sacred restrictions, and community authority. These are not production footnotes;
they are part of the evidence and its limits.

## Publication blockers carried forward

### Language, edition, chronology, and transmission

- pivotal Sanskrit, Tamil, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Old Khmer, Old Javanese, Arabic, Latin,
  and other source-language passages still require the packet-specific critical edition,
  translation, epigraphic, or specialist checks;
- later witnesses and derivative translations cannot be promoted to composition-date or
  original-wording certainty;
- several transmission, contact, priority, continuity, and institutional chains remain held;
  and
- public quotation remains blocked where edition, source-language comparison, rights, or
  sacred/community authority is incomplete.

### Implementation, access, and lived experience

- prescriptions, donor norms, laws, buildings, administrative acts, and literary narratives do
  not establish typical practice, access, consent, efficacy, or outcome;
- the packets do not provide representative patient, family, worker, poor, dependent, enslaved,
  disabled, rural, or otherwise marginalized archives;
- current clinical comparisons and self-harm, coercion, disability, caste/status, gender, and
  trauma-sensitive language require dedicated review; and
- no packet authorizes population prevalence or civilizational character claims.

### Region, community, custody, and rights

- African, Asian, and European regional specialists must review the relevant nodes before
  publication;
- Cambodian, Indonesian, Malian, and other source-community or custodial authority checks remain
  open where named in the packets;
- current object, manuscript, site, image, and cultural-protocol status must be rechecked near
  publication; and
- ignored research cache files are not a public asset library.

## Source-method lessons for the next batch

1. Define settings and corpora before searching; regional labels are not research questions.
2. Give composition, witness, translation, edition, excavation, custody, and reception their own
   records.
3. Keep one source per atomic evidence record and expose shared citation chains.
4. Treat “hospital,” “therapy,” “first,” diagnosis, continuity, and influence as high-risk claim
   classes requiring upstream audits.
5. Search for implementation and affected voices, then record a bounded absence when the archive
   does not supply them.
6. Build cultural protocol, custody, rights, and current-authority review into research rather
   than postponing it to image selection.
7. Preserve disputed readings and unknowns as useful results; do not fill gaps with a familiar
   Europe-to-world or text-to-practice story.

## Recommended next research boundary

Batch 05 should not jump to a universal early-modern narrative. The strongest next move is a
bounded Africa-and-Americas coverage batch that addresses major omissions without pretending to
complete either continent:

- one Horn of Africa/Red Sea packet with a fixed corpus and chronology;
- one East African/Swahili-coast packet with bounded sites and texts;
- separately scoped Central and Southern African material/oral-history packets only after
  protocol and source feasibility review;
- one Indigenous Americas protocol-and-archive foundation packet before interpreting sacred,
  community-controlled, archaeological, or later-recorded material; and
- one or more contact-zone packets for approximately 1492–1650, separated by region and archive
  rather than called “the Americas.”

North African seams already touched by earlier Egyptian and Islamicate packets must be mapped
before deciding whether a new North African packet closes a real gap or duplicates prior work.
The coordinator must freeze exact settings, languages, archives, protocol authorities, and ID
ranges before parallel research begins.
