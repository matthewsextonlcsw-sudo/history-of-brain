# R33 Aro Village Scheme lived experience and archive audit

## Voice map

The opened packet has four speaking positions: Lambo as clinician/organizer; UN editors
summarizing Leighton/Lambo; Western Region medical administrators; and a successor hospital
narrating its own history. Laws and repository pages add legal and custodial metadata. People
described by these sources are not thereby speakers in them.

## Patient position and selection

`SRC-045001` reports that patients were specially selected. The selection criteria, who could
refuse, who decided, whether certification applied, and what happened after refusal are not in
the opened corpus. The administrative diagnostic counts are not a village roster and must not
be exposed or converted into current diagnoses. No named case, patient image, clinical file,
signature, face, family graph or searchable roster is authorized.

## Family and host-household power

Relative presence may have supplied care and continuity; it may also have transferred labor,
time, expense or custody burdens. The sources do not let the project choose either story.
Likewise, a rented room proves neither generosity nor exploitation. `CLM-045005` and
`CLM-045006` keep these as bounded unknowns until direct or contract/account evidence opens.

The UN claim that villagers moved from misgivings to understanding is organizer-derived. It is
not a villager quotation, meeting minute, vote, complaint record or representative survey.
Empathy cannot be used to invent how hosts felt.

## Work, occupational activity and staffing

Institutional occupational therapy included varied activities. Nothing opened shows village
farm work, wages, work-for-rent, market output, compulsion, refusal, benefit or injury. The
proposed residential community for essential laborers and artisans inside hospital grounds is
a different administrative object from patient boarding. These lanes must remain separate.

BMJ p.1393 says much of the hospital labor force came from surrounding villages and that these
laborers were landlords of most patients living in the villages. This is an organizer account,
not a worker, landlord or patient statement. It does not expose identities, job classifications,
wages, duties, rent contracts, who received payment, whether group participation was required,
or how anyone understood the overlap. Employed labor, landlord/host status and patient
occupational activity remain separate analytic lanes.

BMJ's night-duty description, the UN staffing-availability qualification, and 1957 vacancies
must be read together. A listed position or assigned function is not proof of constant coverage
or a worker's own judgment. Nigerian nursing and labor specialists are required before any
worker-history carry-forward.

## Treatment, consent and harm

The sources record electrical treatment, insulin therapy, drugs, psychotherapy, occupational
therapy and other procedures. They do not record patient consent, family authorization,
capacity process, refusal, allocation rules, adverse events or comparative results. Historical
description does not imply current endorsement. No modern diagnosis, treatment advice or
efficacy comparison belongs in R33.

BMJ pp.1392-1394 also contain Lambo's positive assertions about day-hospital benefit,
advantages over hospitalization, recovery/readaptation, community tolerance and justification
for larger-scale use. These are organizer positions, not measured or independently confirmed
outcomes. The packet supplies no defined comparison group, outcome instrument, follow-up,
adverse-event accounting, participant preference evidence or independent community testimony.

## Language and cultural mediation

All opened substantive sources are English-language professional or administrative texts.
No direct Yoruba-language or other Nigerian-language testimony was opened, and the UN says
patients came from across Nigeria. Therefore:

- do not infer that all participants, relatives, hosts or staff were Yoruba speakers;
- do not use one translated English category as a timeless local category;
- preserve period institutional terms as attributed historical language, not project labels;
- require named Nigerian/Yoruba historical-language review for any local term;
- require original-language object, named translator and independent check for future testimony;
- do not route a Nigerian language through an unidentified English or colonial intermediary.

The UN phrase for religious leaders is an editorial category. Most are described as employed
by the hospital. That cannot establish autonomous or nationally typical healer integration.

## Law, custody and voluntariness

The opened official legal objects establish that Cap.121 was referenced in 1957 and that a
1959 amendment existed. They do not expose the complete operative law. Section 212 concerns
prisoners and cannot be projected onto selected village-boarded patients. Certification of
asylum inmates and relatives' reported role in later custody likewise do not resolve village
participants' legal status. Nigerian legal-historical review must follow acquisition of the
complete provisions and commencement/adaptation layers.

## Rights, privacy and dignity

- BMJ and UN text remain link-only; no long quotation or page image.
- BMJ p.1394 records government permission for Lambo to use Aro Hospital clinical material in
  his article. That institutional permission is not patient or family consent, privacy
  clearance, ethics approval, copyright permission or present-day reuse authority.
- The OGL administrative scan may be retained for research, but no clinical image, patient
  list or decontextualized diagnosis display is authorized.
- Any film requires exact object identity, full replay, shot/transcript inventory, image and
  voice rights, health-privacy review, dignity review, descendant/family/community authority
  assessment, and a necessity finding. Default is no patient-face use.
- A deceased or anonymized person still receives privacy and dignity review. No reidentification.
- Repository access, copyright expiry or licence never substitutes for consent and harm review.

## Colonial-transition and lexical-harm gate

The 1954-1960 evidence was produced under late British colonial rule in Western Nigeria; the
1960-1963 portion crosses Nigerian independence and early post-independence administration.
No public interpretation may flatten these changing institutions into a timeless national
system or treat organizer authority as politically neutral. BMJ and UN passages also contain
racialized and civilizational vocabulary and categorical descriptions of Nigerian people.
Such language may be described only when necessary, attributed to the source, minimized rather
than repeated, and reviewed by Nigerian historical and harm reviewers. It must never become
the project's own terminology, search copy, headline language or visual prompt.

## Archive silence and empathy rule

`EVD-045022` establishes only that direct affected-person and worker voices are absent from the
defined opened corpus. It cannot establish that no testimony survives elsewhere or tell us
whether any person felt supported, frightened, coerced, relieved, proud, burdened or harmed.
Future prose must state the silence plainly without filling it with a plausible scene.
