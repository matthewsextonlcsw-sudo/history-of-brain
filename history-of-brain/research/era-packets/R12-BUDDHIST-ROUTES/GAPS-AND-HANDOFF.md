## Contradictory scholarship / source-chain needs

- **Khotan colophon:** Brough, Falk, and Baums supply different alignments/readings. The 2022 physical and grammatical case is strongest in the opened corpus, but public wording must name it as a reconstruction.
- **Khotan manufacture:** presence near Khotan conflicts with any easy assumption of local production; Neelis explicitly preserves the possibility of carriage from Gandhāra.
- **Bower date:** Hoernle's late-fourth/early-fifth proposal was revised to first-half-sixth by scholars relying on Dani/Sander. Upstream works must be replayed before exact public dating.
- **Bower ownership/profession:** “Yaśomitra Manuscript” is a useful decolonizing/provenance proposal only if it does not become an unsupported biography of a monk-physician.
- **Diagnostic language:** Hoernle's English is evidence of 1909 translation practice, not neutral modern terminology. A specialist semantic history must precede any comparison to current categories.
- **Textual relationships:** Yao's examples show that an additional Sanskrit witness can agree with Tibetan, Chinese, both, or neither. “Tibetan translated the Sanskrit manuscript” is too singular.
- **Care history:** the Vinaya passage permits illness exceptions and assigns labor, but implementation could have differed by monastery, period, status, and recension.
- **Psychology/therapy framing:** Prasad et al. celebrate a psychotherapist analogy while Cohen critiques psychologization. Both are modern reception evidence; neither decides ancient identity by assertion.
