# R12-BUDDHIST-ROUTES — Mind, Medicine, Monastic Care, and Translation Across Routes

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `018000–018999`

## Research question

What can a small set of securely located texts, manuscripts, objects, monastic rules, medical
materials, and translation records establish about Buddhist concepts and practices concerning
mind, emotion, suffering, disturbing experience, medicine, and care as they moved across South
and Central Asian routes between approximately 100 BCE and 1200 CE?

## Boundary

This is a route packet, not a history of Buddhism or Asia. Choose no more than three documented
text/object/practice chains with identifiable source and receiving contexts. East Asian and
Southeast Asian reception may appear only at a specific handoff; their regional synthesis
belongs in later packets.

## Required evidence

- critical source/translation texts, manuscript or object records, colophons, catalogs, and
  archaeological context with exact locators;
- specialist Buddhist studies, history of medicine, manuscript, monastic, translation, and
  route scholarship;
- named languages, translators, sites, dates, patrons, practitioners, and communities;
- passage-level comparison of selected mind/emotion/illness/care terms without “Buddhist
  psychology,” mindfulness, therapy, or neuroscience continuity shortcuts;
- monastic rule versus implementation, doctrinal model versus medical practice, and source
  setting versus receiving use;
- patients, monastics, laypeople, donors, translators, caregivers, gender, status, and missing
  voices;
- custody, excavation, colonial collection, current community, sacred-text, and cultural
  protocol review; and
- audits of “Buddhism invented psychology/therapy/mindfulness” and simple eastward-diffusion
  claims.

## Stop

Do not compress South, Central, East, and Southeast Asia into one Buddhist network or infer
transmission from similarity alone. Create the five standard outputs and rights-safe ignored
cache; no public prose.
