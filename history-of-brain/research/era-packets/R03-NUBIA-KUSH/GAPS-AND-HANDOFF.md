## Handoff summary

Subject to the regional-review gate below, this packet is strong enough to seed conditional internal outline nodes on:

- a Tombos woman's healed fractures and the difference between survival, likely assistance, delivered care, and compassion;
- metastatic carcinoma in one Amara West individual, with colonial-site and diagnostic limits;
- the hard ceiling created by incomplete Meroitic translation;
- funerary/protective objects as evidence of practice but not transparent psychology;
- why the opened sources do not establish a Musawwarat hospital (not the untraced origin or reception history of that hypothesis);
- Nile/Red Sea exchange without automatic medical diffusion;
- colonial surveys, UNESCO salvage, division/export, and museum custody;
- named modern Nubian and Sudanese heritage experience.

It is **not** strong enough to seed public claims about an ancient Nubian psychiatric system, a Musawwarat hospital, therapeutic surgery at Nuri, lifelong compassionate care at Wadi Halfa, indigenous clinical vocabulary, or ancient patient feelings.

## Precise evidence gaps

### GAP-004000 — Ancient patient or care-recipient voice

- **Question:** Is there any securely translated Meroitic, Greek, Coptic, Egyptian, or other locally excavated text in the region that records an injured/ill person's own experience or a caregiver's direct account?
- **Current state:** None verified in this packet.
- **Why it matters:** Without one, ancient feelings, stigma, coping, and care relationships remain inaccessible.
- **Needed evidence:** Full edition, provenance, text/image, diplomatic transcription, translation, translator, lexical notes, and exact line locator; preferably multiple specialists or a bilingual anchor.
- **Handoff:** Meroitic epigraphy specialist plus papyrologist; search documentary rather than only funerary corpora.
- **Publication rule:** Do not invent first-person scenes while gap remains.

### GAP-004001 — Indigenous mental/emotional vocabulary

- **Question:** Are any Meroitic terms securely translated as mind, thought, memory, grief, fear, madness, healer, patient, medicine, or care?
- **Current state:** No such term verified. SRC-004002 says only a small vocabulary is secure and narrative/grammar remain limited.
- **Needed evidence:** Current Meroitic lexicon and corpus concordance; line-level examples; certainty grade per term; independent linguistic review; differentiation from Egyptian loanwords and modern glosses.
- **Handoff:** Meroitic historical linguist; consult 2019–2026 scholarship and current corpus projects.
- **Publication rule:** Use no indigenous clinical/emotion term until verified.

### GAP-004002 — Current state of Meroitic decipherment

- **Question:** What changed after Rilly's 2016 synthesis?
- **Current state:** Rilly 2019 was only partially opened and shares the same independence group. No independent 2020–2026 review was fully checked.
- **Needed evidence:** Full Rilly 2019 article; current lexicon/corpus release; at least one independent specialist review; exact changes to word count, grammar, and genre accessibility.
- **Handoff:** Language-research packet.
- **Publication rule:** If citing numbers such as “about 100 words,” date them explicitly to 2016.

### GAP-004003 — Tombos care inference

- **Question:** How strong is the inference that the Tombos woman received sustained assistance?
- **Current state:** Strong physical observation from one full article, with a recorded internal wording conflict: the abstract says “healed ... nonunion,” while the case description, Figures 1–5, Discussion, and Conclusion describe organized healed callus, varus malunion, and complete union. Functional/care reconstruction comes from that same article. The independent Index of Care framework structures but does not corroborate delivered care. Full methodological critique by Dettwyler was not opened.
- **Needed evidence:** Full Dettwyler 1991; related care-inference critiques; Tombos excavation report/context; functional assessment by clinician/bioarchaeologist; clarification of age estimate and injury timing; any later reassessment.
- **Handoff:** Bioarchaeologist and disability historian.
- **Publication rule:** “Likely needed assistance” may be used conditionally; “was compassionately cared for” may not.

### GAP-004004 — Tombos custody and descendant/community position

- **Question:** What do Tombos residents, relevant Sudanese authorities, and possible descendant communities say about study, export, data access, long-term Purdue curation, and return?
- **Current state:** Article records NCAM permission and community assistance but no descendant-consent or custody-preference account.
- **Needed evidence:** NCAM agreement/policy; Purdue curation and repatriation policy; locally governed consultation; community-authored or co-authored statement; current legal/security context.
- **Handoff:** Ethics/provenance researcher; contact only through appropriate institutional/community routes.
- **Publication rule:** State custody fact and consent gap; do not imply permission resolves ethics.

### GAP-004016 — Exchange versus medical transmission

- **Handoff:** Archaeobotany, residue analysis, history of medicine, Red Sea archaeology.
- **Publication rule:** Map contact without claiming therapeutic diffusion.

### GAP-004017 — Period and identity control

- **Needed evidence:** Project-approved chronology; site-level political/contextual notes; review by Sudanese/Nubian archaeologist; explicit treatment of mixed and changing identities.
- **Handoff:** Outline editor and regional specialist.
- **Publication rule:** Attach dates and political context to every case.
