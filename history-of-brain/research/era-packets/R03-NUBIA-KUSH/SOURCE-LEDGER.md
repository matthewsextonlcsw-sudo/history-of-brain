## Method and retrieval note

Sources were opened at the full object, excavation, article, catalogue, or project page whenever access allowed. Agent Reach's Jina reader was used for accessible web pages. Exa was unavailable in this environment, so discovery also used the built-in web search, followed by direct opening of the source. Search-result snippets and abstracts were treated as leads, never as claim evidence. Four openly licensed articles were cached in the ignored packet cache; restricted, ambiguously licensed, or culturally controlled material was not cached.

`Claim fit` uses A–D from the evidence model. `Level` uses L1–L5/LX. A source can be strong for a narrow institutional or first-person claim while remaining weak for broader historical inference.

- **Type / creator role:** Peer-reviewed bioarchaeology article; bioarchaeologists.
- **Publisher or holder / version:** Wiley/Hindawi; version of record, published 12 February 2024.
- **Language / translator:** English; none.
- **Identifier / stable URL:** DOI 10.1155/2024/8339694; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2024/8339694
- **Archive URL:** local lawful cache listed below.
- **Opened on / by:** 2026-08-18; packet researcher.
- **Exact locator:** pp. 2–4 case description and Figures 1–5; p. 9 Discussion; p. 10 Conclusion, Addendum, and Acknowledgments. The observation of union is anchored to the case description, imaging, Discussion, and Conclusion, not to the abstract.
- **Claim fit / summary:** A for observed healed fractures and burial context; B for inferred assistance and status. ### SRC-004002 — State of Meroitic decipherment

- **Level / status:** L2; full scholarly reference article opened.
- **Full citation:** Rilly, Claude. “Meroitic.” *UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology* (2016). https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3128r3sw.
- **Type / creator role:** Scholarly encyclopedia article; specialist historical linguist.
- **Publisher or holder / version:** UCLA eScholarship; 2016.
- **Language / translator:** English; none.
- **Identifier / stable URL:** eScholarship item 3128r3sw; https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3128r3sw
- **Archive URL:** none; link only because reuse status was not established for local caching.
- **Opened on / by:** 2026-08-18; packet researcher.
- **Exact locator:** printed pp. 2–3 for the corpus count, scripts, genres, approximate funerary formulae, largely inaccessible royal narratives, and the circa-100-word ceiling reported in 2016; printed p. 7 for the speculative state of verbal morphology; printed pp. 8–9 for the author's 2016 assessment of constraints and prospects.
- **Claim fit / summary:** A for what the specialist reported about the 2016 corpus and script; B for interpretations of text function. Rilly reported that more than 2,000 inscriptions were known and the scripts had been deciphered, while grammar and vocabulary remained incompletely understood. He described formulaic funerary translations as approximate, royal narratives as largely inaccessible, and about 100 words as understood with some certainty. All are 2016 state reports.
- **Limitations:** Specialist synthesis, not a bilingual corpus. Every corpus count, vocabulary count, and “state of decipherment” proposition is a report of the field in 2016, not a current 2026 census or consensus statement. Some genre/function labels are interpretations. The 2016 state must be paired with a complete post-2016 source and an independent specialist review before current-state wording. Linguistic family placement is a scholarly position, not a locally authored ancient self-description.
- **Independence group:** `RILLY-MEROITIC`.
- **Rights / cache / SHA-256:** link only; no local file; `n/a`.
- **Upstream checked / notes:** Full PDF read. Sixteen short texts described as descendants of Egyptian amuletic texts are only *presumably* protective; this must not be recast as evidence of mental-health treatment.
