# R04-LEVANT — Breath, Heart, Spirit, Distress, and Social Life

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Owner:** One research session; this folder only

**Identifier range:** `SRC/EVD/CLM-005000–005999`

## Research question

How did selected ancient Levantine texts and material contexts describe breath, heart, life,
spirit, agency, emotion, suffering, troubling behavior, care, and social obligation—and what is
lost when these terms are collapsed into the English word “mind”?

## Boundary

Named Levantine languages, corpora, sites, and communities, approximately 1200 BCE–600 CE.
The packet must choose a manageable core corpus during research and state its exclusions.
Iranian traditions are a later packet; Greek, Egyptian, and Mesopotamian links are handoffs.

## In scope

- Hebrew, Aramaic, Phoenician/Ugaritic, and other selected terms only where qualified editions
  and expertise are accessible;
- semantic fields for heart, breath, life, spirit, self, grief, fear, despair, altered conduct,
  and restoration;
- genre differences across law, poetry, narrative, wisdom, ritual, letters, and inscriptions;
- household, community, religious, and legal responses;
- translation history and the danger of later doctrinal meanings being read backward; and
- what elite textual archives omit.

## Out of scope

- “the biblical view of mental illness” as a single timeless position;
- demon-versus-medicine binaries;
- retrospective diagnosis of named figures;
- claims about population-wide belief from one text; and
- public prose.

## Required evidence targets

- critical editions or authoritative text databases with exact passage and version;
- specialist philological and historical scholarship;
- multiple translations when an argument depends on one word;
- material/archaeological context where it changes textual interpretation;
- scholarship on genre, redaction, dating, and community diversity; and
- citation-chain checks for popular claims about possession, melancholy, or “mental illness in
  the Bible.”
