# R04-LEVANT — Lived experience and archive audit

**Rule:** first-person grammar is not automatically eyewitness testimony, and a voice in a text
is not automatically a patient voice.

## Voice and mediation matrix

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gen 2:7; Deut 6:5; Proverbs; Ezekiel | narrator, law-address, sage, prophet/community | Canonical literary transmission; dating/redaction complex; medieval Masoretic witness and later translations | Culturally durable metaphors and normative language for life, heart, self, spirit, conduct | Individual hearers' interpretations; ordinary usage frequency; dissenting beliefs; outcomes |

## `EVD-005020` — Closest approach to lived language

Lachish 3 is the closest item in this packet to a **nonliterary administrative first-person
statement**, not an “everyday” voice. Zammit's own reading after physical examination places
“your servant has been sick at heart” inside an argument about a letter, rebuke, and ability to
read (`SRC-005021`, PDF pp. 7–10). Na'aman prints a similar dependent translation
(`SRC-005016`, pp. 64–65), but the upstream Dobbs-Allsopp page and a second independent
translation remain unopened (`SRC-005022`). The LOC item is evidence for a historic photograph,
not the object text (`SRC-005017`). The passage still does **not** make Hosha'yah a
“mental-health patient”: the idiom could convey anxiety, hurt, alarm, shame, or rhetorical
deference, and the opened evidence does not decide among those possibilities.

## `EVD-005021` — Care is relational but not necessarily therapeutic in the modern sense

- Saul's courtiers observe, advise, recruit a musician, and the narrative reports respite.
  This is a court network and political introduction as well as a care response.
- Psalm 42–43 may give a community a repeatable lament form, but text-to-performance inference
  is limited (`SRC-005007`, p. 610).
- Job's friends eventually become part of the suffering story, but their encounter lies outside
  the selected Job 3 core and was not researched here.
- 1QS offers discipline, moral classification, and eschatological purification. Those are
  community responses, but calling them treatment would import a modern service category.
- Kirta is outside the R04 core and belongs to an earlier Bronze Age handoff; no care claim is
  authorized from the incomplete lead.

## `EVD-005022` — Archive survival is structured by institutions

The core survives because scribes, royal/administrative systems, canonizing communities,
sectarian copyists, excavators, museums, religious publishers, and modern digital projects
preserved it. That survival pattern amplifies literate male elites, kings, officials, communal
leaders, and norm-producing texts. It does not justify assuming that their descriptions were
shared evenly by women, children, farmers, enslaved people, disabled people, foreigners, or
those expelled from communities.

## Absent or weakly represented people

- No identifiable patient-authored clinical narrative.
- No direct voice from a person subjected to restraint, exclusion, ritual correction, or
  punishment because of troubling behavior.
- Little household evidence beyond literary royal/family representation.
- Women's agency and gendered embodiment are methodologically important in `SRC-005006`, but
  the selected primary core does not recover a named woman's distress narrative.
- No safe account of children's experiences.
- No basis for estimating prevalence, access to care, efficacy, stigma, or mortality.
- No evidence that “demonological” and bodily explanations formed two mutually exclusive
  systems. The sources often join divine, bodily, moral, interpersonal, and practical action.

## Empathy constraints for later writing

1. Describe what a text does before explaining what a modern reader may recognize in it.
2. Do not turn pain-poetry into a diagnosis or a historical person into a case study.
3. Do not mock spirit language as primitive, and do not romanticize it as holistic wisdom.
4. For Saul, name the lack of his own voice and the political use of his distress.
5. For 1QS, include the experience of being classified or excluded as an institutional power
   question, while acknowledging that this packet lacks direct excluded-person testimony.
6. For all translations, retain the ancient term at first use and explain why the English word
   is only one choice.
