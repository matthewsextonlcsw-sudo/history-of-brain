# R04-LEVANT — Findings

**Status:** research synthesis, not public copy
**Core corpus:** selected Hebrew/Aramaic biblical passages; 1QS III.13–IV.26; Lachish
Ostracon 3 as a nonliterary administrative control. Kirta is quarantined as a pre-1200-BCE
handoff lead and is not part of the researched core.

## Evidence records

### `EVD-005000` — A person becomes a living *nepeš* in Genesis 2:7

- **Observation:** The Masoretic wording joins formed earth/dust, divine life-breath, and the
  result *nepeš ḥayyāh*. RJPS and NETS both render the result “living being,” not a separable,
  immortal soul (`SRC-005000`–`005002`; Gen 2:7).
- **Interpretive support:** Erbele-Küster reads the passage as embodied vitality and
  vulnerability and repeats the influential formula that humans do not *have* a *nepeš* but
  *are* one (`SRC-005006`, pp. 209–210).
- **Qualification:** Davis shows that *nepeš* is polysemous and genre-sensitive; even the
  monism-versus-dualism binary can force diverse cultural models into modern categories
  (`SRC-005007`, pp. 601–604). “Living being” in one passage cannot define every occurrence.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** for wording; **strongly supported** for an embodied reading;
  **disputed** if expanded into one timeless Israelite anthropology.

### `EVD-005001` — “Heart,” “soul,” and “mind” move across translation

- **Observation:** Hebrew Deut 6:5 has *levav* / *nepeš* / *me'od*; RJPS gives
  “heart / soul / might.” NETS's Greek-based English gives “mind / soul / power”
  (`SRC-005000`, `SRC-005001`, `SRC-005003`; Deut 6:5, NETS p. 152).
- **Meaning:** “Mind” is present in a legitimate translation history, but it is not a neutral
  one-word replacement for the Hebrew heart. The adjacent NETS verse 6 places commanded words
  “in your heart and in your soul,” demonstrating that even one version distributes functions
  across several body/self terms.
- **Assessment:** **Documented.** Any claim that ancient Hebrew simply had no concept of
  thinking, or that *levav* simply means the modern brain/mind, overstates the evidence.

### `EVD-005002` — Heart language combines bodily, emotional, cognitive, and moral work

- **Text set:** Prov 14:10 (heart knows its own bitterness), Prov 14:30 (calm heart/body),
  and Ezek 11:19; 18:31; 36:26–27 (new heart/new spirit, stone/flesh, changed conduct)
  (`SRC-005000`, `SRC-005001`). Lachish 3 is a separate administrative comparator, below.
- **Synthesis:** Erbele-Küster describes *leb/lebav* as physiological, emotive, and cognitive;
  Prov 14:10 locates self-reflection in embodied sensation and social relation
  (`SRC-005006`, pp. 209–211).
- **Nonliterary control:** Zammit's own transcription and translation after physical
  examination reads lines 6–7 as *lēb ‘abdekā dāweh* / “your servant has been sick at heart”
  in response to a superior's letter (`SRC-005021`, PDF pp. 7–10). Na'aman quotes a similar
  translation but depends on the unopened Dobbs-Allsopp edition (`SRC-005016`, pp. 64–65;
  restricted `SRC-005022`). This does not prove literal cardiac illness, a mental disorder, or
  ordinary speech across society.
- **Assessment:** **Strongly supported** for biblical heart-language variation; **plausible**
  for the exact Lachish idiom pending a second independent opened translation; **unknown** as
  to population prevalence.

### `EVD-005003` — Psalm 42–43 stages distress through thirst, tears, body, memory, and address

- **Observation:** The poem's *nepeš* thirsts, is poured out, is addressed as downcast or deeply
  grieved, and is directed toward hope (`SRC-005000`, `SRC-005001`, `SRC-005005`; Ps 42–43).
- **Translation comparison:** RJPS notes “soul” or “throat”; NETS gives “deeply grieved” and
  “throwing me into confusion.” These phrases should not be silently relabeled depression.
- **Counter-reading:** Davis argues that the same poem uses *nepeš* both metonymically and in
  apostrophe. The self-address is poetic and perhaps ritual action, not evidence for a separate
  inner substance; the lament may enact orientation toward God while expressing estrangement
  (`SRC-005007`, pp. 601–606, 610–615).
- **Boundary:** Davis explicitly notes that a ritual text is not the ritual itself (p. 610).
  The speaker's gender and historical identity are open; this is not a patient diary.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** for the poem; **plausible** for ancient ritual performance;
  **unknown** as individual biography.

### `EVD-005004` — Job 3 gives suffering a socially intelligible poetic voice

- **Observation:** Job curses his birth, asks why life is given to the bitter in spirit, speaks
  of groaning as food and roaring poured like water, and ends without repose, quiet, or rest
  (`SRC-005000`, `SRC-005001`; Job 3).
- **Interpretive boundary:** It is direct evidence for what the poem permits its character to
  say, including wishing not to have lived. It is not evidence that an identifiable historical
  patient met diagnostic criteria, nor does it authorize converting lament into a symptom list.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** literary expression; lived autobiographical status **unknown**.

### `EVD-005005` — Saul's relief by music is embedded in a succession story

- **Observation:** In 1 Sam/1 Reigns 16:14–23, a spirit of YHWH/the Lord departs from Saul; an
  evil/bad spirit from YHWH/the Lord terrifies or torments him; courtiers recommend a skilled
  lyre player; David's playing brings relief and the spirit departs (`SRC-005000`, `SRC-005001`,
  `SRC-005004`).
- **What is supported:** The narrative represents attendants noticing distress, proposing
  skilled music, and reporting temporary benefit. It also introduces David into Saul's service
  after divine favor has moved to David. The conclusion that the scene does literary and
  political work is a **plausible project inference from this sequence**, not a documented
  finding from the opened source text or the inaccessible Balfour article.
- **What is not supported:** “Music therapy cured Saul's depression/PTSD/epilepsy/psychosis.”
  The text supplies none of those diagnoses, no independent case history, no frequency or
  long-term outcome, and no patient-authored account.
- **Citation-chain replay:** Greenberg's dated public article calls David “The World's First
  Music Therapist,” states that David cured Saul's depression, and cites 1 Samuel 16 plus a
  short quotation from Robert Alter (`SRC-005023`). Replay to the Hebrew/RJPS/NETS passages
  (`SRC-005000`, `005001`, `005004`) reaches narrated torment, playing, relief, and departure
  of the spirit—not a modern diagnosis, cure, professional identity, priority claim, or trial.
  Alter's full commentary was not opened, so that downstream branch stops there. Separately,
  Williams and le Roux match narrative details to modern diagnoses and prefer depression/PTSD
  (`SRC-005012`); this is a specialist retrospective-diagnosis argument, not the upstream of
  Greenberg's public claim. Balfour remains inaccessible (`SRC-005013`), and Basher 2025 is an
  abstract-only current-field lead (`SRC-005024`); neither supplies evidence here.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** narrative relief; modern diagnosis **unsupported/disputed**.

### `EVD-005006` — Aramaic Daniel demonstrates why “spirit” and “mind” cannot be a word swap

- **Observation:** In Dan 7:15, the Aramaic text locates a disturbed *ruaḥ* “within” Daniel;
  RJPS renders “my spirit was disturbed within me and the vision of my mind alarmed me”
  (`SRC-005000`, `SRC-005001`).
- **Meaning:** A single modern sentence can use both “spirit” and “mind” to unpack an ancient
  expression. It is therefore unsafe to construct an ancient faculty chart from English nouns.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** translation example; larger Aramaic semantic history remains
  **unknown** in this packet.

### `EVD-005007` — Ezekiel's heart-and-spirit language is covenantal and behavioral

- **Observation:** Ezek 11:19; 18:31; 36:26–27 coordinate new heart, new spirit, removal of a
  stone heart, receipt of a flesh heart, divine spirit, and faithful conduct (`SRC-005000`,
  `SRC-005001`).
- **Meaning:** The imagery joins bodily material, agency, divine action, and communal ethics.
  Reading “new heart” as merely an emotional state or brain metaphor strips away its prophetic
  and covenantal setting.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** within these passages; redactional/diachronic claims are not
  established by this packet.

### `EVD-005008` — The Community Rule's Two Spirits is moral-cosmic and institutional

- **Observation:** 1QS III.13–IV.26 assigns two spirits, pairs truth/injustice and light/dark,
  describes affliction under the angel of darkness, lists humility, patience, compassion,
  insight and wisdom on one side and greed, falsehood, pride, cruelty, impatience, folly, lust,
  sorrow and bitter distress on the other, locates struggle in human hearts, promises healing,
  peace, long life and joy, and anticipates divine purification (`SRC-005008`, pp. 21–25).
- **Interpretive boundary:** This is a normative sectarian instruction joining cosmic powers,
  dispositions, conduct, consequences, and group identity. It is not a manual of psychiatric
  diagnosis and not representative of all Jews in the Second Temple period.
- **Textual-history correction:** 1QS is a Hasmonean-period parchment copy (`SRC-005009`).
  Metso documents distinct Serek editions; 4QSd lacks the opening four columns including the
  Treatise, while other evidence is fragmentary. Using 1QS as copy-text does not establish its
  priority (`SRC-005008`, intro pp. 1–7).
- **Assessment:** **Documented** for 1QS; historical primacy and community-wide uniformity
  **disputed/unknown**.

### `EVD-005009` — Kirta is a quarantined Bronze Age lead, not R04 evidence

- **Lead:** The Hanson teaching page displays only KTU 1.14 I.1–52 through an adapted,
  dependent translation (`SRC-005010`); the Göttingen page lists the tablet group but supplies
  no critical text (`SRC-005011`). No authoritative object/provenance record, complete current
  edition, or translation comparison was opened.
- **Scope:** The conventional fourteenth/thirteenth-century BCE dating advertised by the lead
  falls before this brief's approximately 1200 BCE boundary. R04 does not silently expand its
  time range.
- **Assessment:** **Unknown / quarantined.** `EVD-005009` is retained as a failed-evidence and
  handoff record only. No Kirta proposition is ready for the outline; send it to an earlier
  Bronze Age packet with the acquisition requirements intact.

### `EVD-005010` — The archive does not contain ancient mental-health patients as such

- **Direct testimony available:** Lachish 3 is a first-person official letter; one opened
  modern epigraphic examination translates a sick-at-heart response to rebuke and literacy
  status, but a second independent translation is still missing. It is not a patient complaint
  (`SRC-005021`; dependent `SRC-005016`).
- **Mediated voices:** Job and Psalm 42–43 use first-person literary/ritual speech; Saul is
  described by narrator and courtiers; 1QS prescribes sectarian norms. None can be called an
  interview, chart, or unmediated clinical testimony. Kirta is outside this core.
- **Assessment:** **Documented** archive characterization. Actual experiences of ordinary
  distressed, disabled, enslaved, poor, female, child, or socially excluded people are mostly
  **unknown** from this core corpus.

## Synthesis answer to the packet question

The selected evidence describes life, agency, cognition, emotion, and suffering through
overlapping terms and embodied scenes: breath in nostrils, living *nepeš*, knowing or sick
heart, disturbed *ruaḥ*, thirst, tears, groaning, song, divine presence/absence, moral
practice, office, and communal order. What is lost under the English word “mind” is
precisely this distribution across body, relationship, ritual, theology, ethics, and social
location. The corrective is not to replace “mind” with a different master word such as “soul,”
“heart,” or “spirit,” but to retain the source term, passage, genre, translation history, and
archive limits together.
