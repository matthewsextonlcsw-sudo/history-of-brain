# R05-ROMAN — findings and outline foundation

**State:** research support only; not final/public prose.

## Packet answer

The evidence supports an outline built around plural jurisdictions and lived settings, not a single Roman “discovery” story. A usable sequence moves from Republican philosophical self-care, through Latin and Greek medical classifications, household/status/legal power, Greek-imperial sanctuary and patient self-writing, and late-antique Greek-to-Latin adaptation. Every transition must state place, language, genre and status. `CLM-008000` is the governing claim.

## Proposed outline transitions and evidence anchors

### 1. From Greek philosophical schools into Roman elite self-work — not “the birth of therapy”

- **Anchor:** Cicero, *Tusculans* 3.1–13 (`EVD-008002`; `CLM-008001`).
- **Topic:** philosophy as “medicine” for the mind/soul; translation of Greek *pathē*; distinctions among *perturbatio*, *insania* and *furor*; disagreement about grief.
- **Transition:** Latin vocabulary is being made inside a Roman elite dialogue, not merely copied. This leads to Seneca's self-report but does not turn philosophy into a clinical profession.
- **Lived bridge:** Seneca Letter 78 (`EVD-008003`; `CLM-008002`) connects illness, desire for death, obligation to a parent, friends and study. It is a first-person but rhetorically curated sufferer account.

### 2. The Latin medical compilation: Celsus's mixed toolkit

- **Anchor:** *De Medicina* 3.18.1–21 (`EVD-008000`–`008001`; `CLM-008003`), with the later coercive measures specifically at 3.18.20–21.
- **Topic:** fever-linked delirium versus longer troubling states; conditional restraint; individualized light/dark trials; speech, music, reading, familiar work, sleep, drugs, threats and force.
- **Interpretive rule:** do not extract only the gentle or only the violent sentences. The coexistence is historically important.
- **Audit:** universal restraint is contradicted as a textual claim, while “humane psychiatry” is equally overextended (`DIS-008000`–`008001`). Actual practice remains Unknown.

### 3. Greek medicine inside the Roman Empire: categories remain mobile

- **Aretaeus:** *Chronic Diseases* 1.5–6 (`EVD-008004`–`008005`). Black-bile reasoning coexists with cases explicitly said not to involve black bile and with an unrequited-love interpretation. Context-specific and intermittent descriptions defeat one-to-one retrospective diagnosis (`CLM-008004`–`008005`).
- **Galen:** *De locis affectis* 3.10, Kühn VIII 180–191 (`EVD-008006`). The source-language witness connects some fear, sadness and cognition to multiple brain/body routes, not a single slogan. The packet's English summary is a wording-unverified working paraphrase, not a named translation. Name Greek language, Pergamum/Rome and the specific work (`CLM-008006`).
- **Transition:** these writers share an imperial world but not one classification. Galen is not the empire's spokesperson.

### 4. Law and household power: capacity, property, safety and status

- **Curators:** *Institutes* 1.23.3–4 and Digest 27.10 (`EVD-008009`–`008010`; `CLM-008009`). These organize who can manage affairs and who controls person/property/safety.
- **Risk case:** Digest 1.18.13.1/.14 (`EVD-008011`; `CLM-008010`) shows relatives, friends, home guards, chains and exceptional prison within a homicide/governor context. It is not a general care census.
- **Enslavement:** Digest 21.1 (`EVD-008012`; `CLM-008012`) records intermittent behavior and self-harm chiefly as disclosure, usefulness, price and liability. The archive gives owners' and jurists' categories, not the enslaved person's account.
- **Audit:** no empire-wide psychiatric asylum is supported by the consulted evidence (`CLM-008011`; `DIS-008003`). Household custody, curatorship, prison, sanctuary and hospital must remain separate.

### 5. Pergamum and a mediated patient self-account

- **Textual anchor:** Aelius Aristides, *Sacred Tales* I.1–28 (`EVD-008015`).
- **Material/context anchor:** Melfi's archaeology of the Roman-imperial Pergamene Asclepieion and Trapp/Steger's source analysis (`EVD-008016`–`008017`).
- **Topic:** bodily symptoms, sleep, dreams, bathing, fasting/vomiting, servants, attendants, friends, priests and doctors in one elite Greek-speaking network (`CLM-008013`).
- **Transition:** ritual and medicine interact; “religion versus science” is the wrong binary. The sanctuary is not thereby a psychiatric hospital and Aristides cannot represent typical visitors (`CLM-008014`).

### 6. Late-antique Latin adaptation and explicit disagreement

- **Anchor:** Caelius Aurelianus, *Tardarum passionum* 1.144–180 (`EVD-008007`–`008008`, `008020`).
- **Topic:** the Latin source-language witness addresses environment, attendant interaction, restraint, darkness, fasting, chains, bloodletting, beating and music theories. The packet's English summaries are wording-unverified working paraphrases pending a named critical translation.
- **Transmission point:** Caelius repeatedly marks Soranus/Greek schools, but the surviving Latin is an active argument/adaptation, not transparent access to a lost Greek original (`CLM-008007`, `008017`).
- **Audit:** named medical writers dispute methods (`CLM-008008`). Do not claim a linear progress from cruelty to kindness or infer professional jurisdiction from the restricted Polito lead.

### 7. Close with the archive, not a victory story

- **Anchor:** Laes pp. 39–52; Steger pp. 129–139; legal sale/curator material (`EVD-008012`–`008013`, `008016`, `008019`).
- **Topic:** the archive privileges elite men, property, legal controversy and prescriptive authors; it cannot give prevalence or ordinary outcomes (`CLM-008016`).
- **Audit:** hostile biography and category resemblance do not diagnose Caligula or other ancient people (`CLM-008015`; `DIS-008005`).

## Evidence-backed cautions

1. **Text is not practice.** Celsus and Caelius prescribe and polemicize; no outcome series validates use.
2. **Law is not diagnosis.** *Furiosus* and related categories perform legal work.
3. **A sanctuary is not an asylum.** Ritual healing space, household custody, prison, curatorship and hospital are analytically distinct.
4. **Greek-language is not non-Roman.** Aretaeus, Galen and Aristides belong to Roman-imperial settings, but must be named locally and linguistically.
5. **Transmission is not duplication.** Cicero and Caelius select and argue; translations/editions of one text do not add independent corroboration.
6. **Symptom resemblance is not diagnosis.** Ancient categories, genres, causal frames and life conditions differ.
7. **Absence is bounded.** The packet found no psychiatric asylum or population statistics; it does not prove that every lost locality and practice lacked all comparable arrangements.

## What physical/material evidence contributes

Melfi's archaeological reconstruction (`SRC-008011`, pp. 89–113) supplies a Roman-imperial sanctuary setting and rebuilding history for Pergamum. It contextualizes Aristides but does not prove his narrated procedures happened exactly as written, identify all sanctuary users, or make the site a custodial mental-health institution. No Roman household excavation or restraint object is used to infer care. That material gap is explicit rather than filled with textual imagination.
