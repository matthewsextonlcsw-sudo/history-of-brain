| claim_id | proposed research claim | status | safe boundary |

| `CLM-008000` | The surviving evidence does not disclose one “Roman mental-health system”: republican philosophy, Latin medical compilation, Greek imperial medicine, sanctuary self-writing, late-antique Latin adaptation, and Justinianic law speak from different places, dates, genres and statuses. | Strongly supported synthesis | Use only as an organizing guardrail; name the evidence behind every local claim. |
| `CLM-008001` | Cicero's “medicine of the mind/soul” is a Roman Republican philosophical and translation project shaped by disputes among Greek schools, not a clinical service description. | Documented | Preserve date, elite dialogue genre, and translated terms. |
| `CLM-008002` | Seneca Letter 78 preserves an elite first-person report in which illness, thoughts of death, obligation to his father, friends and study are connected. | Documented, high sensitivity | Do not diagnose Seneca or present Stoic rhetoric as suicide-care advice. |
| `CLM-008003` | Celsus 3.18 contains both individualized/social/environmental suggestions and coercive measures; it supports neither “universal restraint” nor a simple “humane Roman psychiatry” story. | Documented for text; Unknown for practice | Say “Celsus recommends,” never “Romans did.” |
| `CLM-008004` | Aretaeus's own discussion complicates humoral certainty: he includes black-bile explanations but also cases without black bile and a love-based differential interpretation. | Documented | Historical classification only; no modern diagnostic equivalence. |
| `CLM-008005` | Aretaeus's *mania* and *melancholia* descriptions cannot be mapped one-to-one onto bipolar disorder, depression, psychosis, or another modern diagnosis. | Strongly supported guardrail | Similar symptoms may be compared only in an explicitly modern-comparison layer. |
| `CLM-008006` | Galen locates some troubling cognition, fear and sadness in multiple brain/body routes, but his Greek-language, Pergamene/Roman writings are one influential node rather than “the Roman view.” | Documented source-language anchor; English wording unverified | Require a named current critical translation before public quotation or close English paraphrase. |
| `CLM-008007` | Caelius preserves a Latin, late-antique engagement with Greek Methodist/Soranic traditions that includes environmental care, attendant interaction, conditional restraint, and explicit criticism of beating and indiscriminate chaining. | Documented source-language anchor with transmission dispute; English wording unverified | Do not call Caelius merely a translator or make the prescriptions routine practice; require a named critical translation before public quotation or close English paraphrase. |
| `CLM-008008` | Named Roman medical texts contain explicit disagreement about coercion, diet, darkness, bloodletting and music. | Documented in named prescriptive texts; Caelius English wording unverified | Present named writers/passages and date layers; no civilizational average or unsupported professional-jurisdiction claim. |
| `CLM-008009` | The opened curator rules primarily organize legal capacity, property, person and safety; their labels are not medical diagnoses. | Documented | Name Justinianic compilation date and earlier layers. |
| `CLM-008010` | The matricide rescript and governor rule assume relatives, friends, household guards and exceptional prison/chains; they do not establish a general Roman psychiatric asylum. | Documented for law; negative clause is a Documented bounded packet audit and Unknown beyond consulted evidence | “No asylum in the consulted evidence” is safer than an absolute proof of absence everywhere. |
| `CLM-008011` | The consulted legal and specialist evidence does not support an empire-wide “Roman asylum”; a healing sanctuary, household custody, prison and modern hospital are different institutions. | Documented bounded packet audit; Unknown beyond consulted evidence | Keep search boundary visible and invite correction from new archaeological/legal evidence. |
| `CLM-008012` | Sale-law evidence exposes how enslaved people's distress, intermittent behavior and self-harm entered the archive through price, disclosure, usefulness and owner liability rather than their own testimony. | Documented | Center the enslaved person's missing voice; do not reproduce commodifying labels uncritically. |
| `CLM-008013` | Aristides's *Sacred Tales* is an unusually extensive patient self-account of symptoms, dreams, attendants, friends, priests and doctors in the Roman Greek east, but it is elite praise literature and cannot establish treatment efficacy or typical experience. | Documented self-account, strongly qualified | Use as a mediated individual voice, never a representative Roman patient. |
| `CLM-008014` | Pergamum evidence resists a “religion versus science” binary: sanctuary ritual, dreams, regimens, physicians and social networks could coexist, without making the sanctuary a psychiatric hospital. | Strongly supported | Attribute to named evidence and avoid claims about all sanctuary users. |
| `CLM-008015` | Retrospective diagnosis of Caligula or other named ancient people is not warranted from hostile literary anecdotes and category resemblance. | Documented specialist guardrail | Discuss diagnostic reception, not a diagnosis; this is one specialist audit, not independently corroborated clinical evidence. |
| `CLM-008016` | The archive cannot supply prevalence, population-level treatment outcomes, or ordinary experience; property-bearing men and elite authors are much more visible than women, children, enslaved, poor, rural and nonliterate people. | Strongly supported | Make the silence part of every outline node, not a closing disclaimer. |
| `CLM-008017` | Greek-to-Latin transmission was active selection, adaptation and dispute: Cicero translated philosophical vocabularies, Greek writers operated within Roman imperial settings, and Caelius reworked a Greek Methodist/Soranic inheritance in Latin. | Strongly supported | Name place, language, date and chain; avoid “Rome copied Greece.” |

## Dispute and audit records

| dispute_id | proposition tested | evidence for / against | packet resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DIS-008000` | “Romans universally restrained people with madness.” | Celsus and Caelius permit conditional restraint; Caelius rejects indiscriminate chains and prefers attendants when possible (`EVD-008000`, `008007`, `008008`). | Contradicted as a universal. Coercion was imaginable and prescribed, but conditions and criticism varied; actual prevalence is Unknown. |
| `DIS-008001` | “Roman care was humane.” | Celsus/Caelius include tailored environments and interaction alongside fasting, fear, binding and other coercion; Caelius attacks still harsher rivals. | Reject the moral victory label. Show mixed practices, internal disputes, and the patient's missing consent/outcome. |
| `DIS-008002` | “Roman writers were certain that humors caused mental illness.” | Aretaeus explicitly allows cases without black bile and a love differential; Galen offers multiple bodily routes; schools disagree (`EVD-008004`, `008006`, `008014`). | Contradicted as certainty/consensus. Humoral and bodily explanations were influential but plural and contested. |
| `DIS-008003` | “Rome had psychiatric asylums.” | Digest fragments assume household/friends/guards, exceptional prison and curators; Laes finds no mental hospitals/asylums; Pergamum was a healing sanctuary, not shown as custodial psychiatric institution. | Unsupported in the consulted evidence. Keep a bounded negative, not a timeless absolute. |
| `DIS-008004` | “Galen gives the Roman view.” | Galen is Greek-speaking and Pergamene/Roman; Celsus, Aretaeus, Caelius, law, philosophy and sanctuary narrative differ. | Contradicted. Use Galen as one influential node with named language/place/work. |
| `DIS-008005` | “Ancient descriptions diagnose Caligula/Seneca/Aristides or map neatly to modern disorders.” | Hostile biography, literary self-fashioning, broad categories and retrospective inference produce incompatible diagnoses (`EVD-008003`, `008005`, `008015`, `008019`). | Do not diagnose. Modern comparison requires a separate explicit method and cannot become historical identity. |
