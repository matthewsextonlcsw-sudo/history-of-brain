# R05-ROMAN — Households, Law, Medicine, Status, and Plural Care

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `008000–008999`

## Research question

How did Roman republican and imperial households, sufferers, physicians, philosophers,
temples, healers, courts, and administrators describe and respond to emotion, troubling
experience, cognition, brain/body relations, difference, and distress?

## Boundary

Named Roman and Roman-imperial contexts, approximately 300 BCE–600 CE. Greek-language works in
Roman settings enter only with their local and linguistic position named. Galen is a major
node, not a synonym for Roman medicine.

## Required evidence

- primary texts in critical editions/translations with exact locators;
- legal, epigraphic, archaeological, sanctuary, household, and medical evidence;
- reported patient speech and self-writing with mediation visible;
- status, citizenship, slavery, gender, cost, family authority, and institutional power;
- differences among medical writers, philosophical schools, popular/ritual practices, and law;
- transmission and reception of Greek traditions without a copy-and-paste story; and
- audits of “Roman asylum,” universal restraint, humoral certainty, and retrospective diagnosis.
