## Important research gaps

- **Provincial breadth:** no adequate deep packet coverage for Roman Africa outside Caelius's uncertain attribution, Roman Britain/Gaul/Iberia/Balkans, or most household papyri.
- **Women and children:** no sustained first-person account comparable to Aristides was opened.
- **Ordinary and poor people:** law and elite texts do not recover routine living arrangements, mutual aid, abandonment, street life or local healers reliably.
- **Cost/access:** sanctuary travel, physicians, attendants, drugs and household supervision lack robust price/account evidence.
- **Epigraphy/papyri:** the required evidence mix remains text-heavy; add inscriptions, petitions, contracts, letters and provincial administrative papyri.
- **Material care traces:** no restraint object or household feature should be identified from form alone; material claims need archaeological context and specialist interpretation.
- **Caelius chronology/agency:** date, African association and degree of independent authorship remain debated. The restricted Polito article (`SRC-008013`) was not used.
- **Asylum audit:** “Roman asylum” was tested against law, specialist synthesis and Pergamum archaeology, but no systematic empire-wide archaeological database search was completed.
- **Reception audit:** this packet tests propositions but did not assemble a dated corpus measuring how common each myth is online. Do not call them “popular” without a separate reception sample.

## Cross-packet handoffs

### Greek/Hellenistic packet

- Cicero's philosophical medicine, Aretaeus, Galen and Soranus/Caelius require Greek antecedents without a teleological “Greece invented, Rome copied” story.
- Handoff: trace *pathos*, *mania*, *melancholia*, *phrenitis*, brain/body localization and philosophical therapy in Greek sources before Roman adaptations.

### Roman Egypt / regional packets

- Greek and Latin papyri could supply petitions, household conflict, fees, dreams, work absence and less elite voices.
- Boundary: do not fold Roman Egypt into a generic Roman chapter; coordinate with R03-EGYPT and preserve Egyptian, Greek, Coptic, Jewish and Roman legal settings.

### Late antique Christian/Jewish packets

- Justinianic law and late Caelius approach the 600 CE stop, but monasteries, bishops, exorcism, charity, hospitals, Jewish communal care and ascetic experience belong in their dedicated packets.
- Boundary: this packet does not claim Christian institutions caused or replaced Roman household/medical arrangements.

### Disability, enslavement and law thematic packet

- Digest 21.1 should be re-read with scholarship on slavery, legal personhood, disability and self-harm rather than used as a medical casebook.
- Curatorship needs comparison across gender, citizenship, status and lucid intervals.

### Transmission/reception packet

- Track manuscript survival, Greek-to-Latin translation, Arabic/Syriac/Byzantine transmission and early-modern editions separately from ancient practice.
- Caelius's lost-Soranus chain and Kühn/Adams/Amman editions are ideal citation-chain examples.

## Claims withheld

- Any modern diagnosis of a named ancient person or medical case.
- Any prevalence, incidence, effectiveness, recovery-rate or “most Romans” claim.
- Any “first psychiatrist,” “first psychotherapy,” “first hospital/asylum,” or linear-progress claim.
- Any universal “humoral theory,” “universal restraint,” “Roman humane care,” or “religion versus science” claim.
- Any claim that Galen equals Roman medicine or that Greek-language writers are outside Roman history.
- Any claim that legal capacity categories are medical diagnoses.
- Any claim that an enslaved person's recorded sale-law behavior reveals their full experience or motives.
