## Evidence summary by setting

### Vesalius

- The strongest evidence is page-level and particular: named ventricular/olfactory claims and the reticular plexus are challenged in the 1555 Book VII.
- Revision is the story. The 1543 and 1555 witnesses plus Nutton's annotated-copy analysis resist both “unchanged Galenist” and “single revolutionary break” caricatures.
- The opened anatomical pages do not supply cadaver identity, consent, or lived voice. That is a bounded silence, not a reconstructed experience.

### Burton

- The persona says writing occupies/eases him and that he was affected by melancholy. Genre blocks modern diagnosis and outcome claims.
- The definition pages preserve plurality and locus dispute. The remedy pages preserve an uncomfortable mixture of companionship, professional help, surveillance, diversion, manipulation, gentleness, and coercive permission.
- All remedy language is prescription. Recipient uptake, consent, harms, and outcomes are Unknown in the opened mini-corpus.

### Descartes–Elisabeth

- Elisabeth is an active philosophical interlocutor. Her 1643 interaction question and 1645 request for a better passion definition are propositions and objections, not decorative inspiration.
- Her illness/distress letters are unusually valuable first-person material but are elite, private, copied, and later published against a history that includes her refusal of publication.
- *Passions* first joins the soul to the whole body, then gives the gland a principal/immediate functional role. The popular “soul lives in the pineal gland” sentence is too crude.
- *L’Homme* is controlled as a posthumous 1664 witness to a work that the BnF authority dates 1633; exact drafting and final-text status remain uncertain. Its machine model cannot stand for Descartes's full account of persons.
