## Bounded source design

The primary object is the complete 56-page institutional pamphlet *Official Documents on the
Case of Ann Pratt* (Kingston, 14 August 1860). The complete OCR-bearing Internet Archive
derivative was read from beginning to end. Pivotal passages were then replayed against the
complete Wellcome/University of Glasgow page-image PDF, including twelve risk-based visual
checks across Ann Pratt's elicited statement, former-patient examinations, worker examinations,
family mediation, hostile credibility material, historical classification, and the governor's
publication direction.

The packet concerns what this printed dossier records, selects, disputes, and circulates. It does
not adjudicate the allegations. Ann Pratt's separate pamphlet *Seven Months in the Kingston
Lunatic Asylum, and What I Saw There* and the 1861 commission testimony/report were not opened.
They remain `LX/HOLD`; specialist descriptions of them cannot substitute for their primary
folios.

## Route and full-source audit

- The Agent Reach command route was unavailable in this subagent environment because its
  `mcporter` dependency was not installed. Direct institutional, public-domain, parliamentary,
  repository, and scholarly full-source routes were used instead.
- The Wellcome PDF contains one rights/credit leaf plus sixty page images; the IA derivative
  contains sixty scanned pages and an OCR layer. The same Glasgow object underlies both routes,
  so they share one independence group and never count as corroboration.
- Fryar 2016, Fryar 2018, and Jones 2008 were opened as complete works. Their archival citations
  were used to map dependencies and holds, not to turn unseen primary records into evidence.
- Search snippets, abstracts, catalog blurbs, Wikipedia, popular retellings, and the Library and
  Archives Canada record for a different Kingston were not used as evidence.
- No packet author may approve publication. Public quotation, patient-name use, image use,
  Jamaican language/history review, disability/survivor review, descendant-risk review, rights,
  dignity, and harm review remain open gates.
