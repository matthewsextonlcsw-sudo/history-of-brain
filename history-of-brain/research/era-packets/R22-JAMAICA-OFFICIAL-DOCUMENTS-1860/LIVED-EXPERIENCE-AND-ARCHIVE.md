## Procedure is not consent

Pratt's refusal of an initial arrangement, request concerning who would be present, decision to
attend, and correction of the written statement show constrained procedural agency within an
official inquiry (`CLM-034002`). They do not establish consent to confinement, examination,
treatment, naming, printing, or metropolitan circulation. The dossier supplies no modern consent
process (`EVD-034019`). Later writing must not use “she agreed” without naming exactly what the
source says she agreed to and the power setting in which it occurred.

Steele's and Fare's answers likewise cannot be converted into voluntary testimonials. The question
format, discharge timing, marks, institutional setting, and selection for publication are part of
the evidence, not incidental formatting.

## Care, control, work, and violence

The opened object presents the institution simultaneously as a place of medical classification,
custody, bathing and restraint, food and labor, paid work, administrative investigation, and
contested violence. Those functions overlap. A later narrative must not force the evidence into a
single benevolent-treatment or pure-punishment story (`CLM-034023`).

The record contains allegations and denials involving sexual violence, assault, forced undressing,
injury, hunger, restraint, bodily exposure, and death. It also contains reported acts of care,
kindness, refusal, complaint, uncertainty, and limited observation. These statements can be set in
relation to one another, but this packet does not adjudicate them (`CLM-034022`). Graphic detail is
not required to establish that harm was alleged and disputed.

Workers are not one bloc. Donaldson, John Hall, and Caleb Hall held different positions and faced
different dependencies. Their statements combine care, complaint, denial, uncertainty,
gatekeeping, and employment risk. Later work must retain speaker, recorder, procedure, and
directness rather than relabeling all worker material as either resistance or institutional defense.

## Colonial power and the production of credibility

The administrative path is itself historical evidence: patient-associated speech moved through
parish officers, asylum staff, the inspector/director, legal and medical authorities, the governor,
printing, and intended metropolitan circulation. The institution answered criticism by selecting,
questioning, arranging, and publishing testimony about its own conduct. The dossier is therefore a
source about contested events and about the production of official credibility (`CLM-034000`,
`CLM-034001`).

Race, gender, class, sexuality, religion, parenting, illness, literacy, respectability, and official
status affected the archive's credibility practices (`CLM-034014`). That context must be named without inventing
identity data. In particular:

- preserve historical color labels only when the source makes them relevant;
- never infer race from occupation, spelling, printed Jamaican speech, a surname, or institutional
  role (`CLM-034015`);
- distinguish the speaker from the clerk, examiner, compiler, printer, and modern OCR;
- distinguish a direct observation from reported speech, retrospective interpretation, denial, or
  official classification;
- do not treat a government record as neutral or a patient's printed statement as transparent.

## Language and transcription

The dossier is in English, including printed Jamaican speech and historical racial and medical
terms. The printed form may already reflect clerical and editorial intervention; the OCR adds name,
date, pagination, punctuation, and character errors (`EVD-034020`). The following controls apply:

- use OCR for navigation only;
- verify every proposed quotation against the page image;
- retain the historical meaning and speaker position without mockery, eye-dialect performance, or
  silent “correction” presented as a quotation;
- attribute terms such as “mania,” “melancholia,” “lunatic,” and historical color labels to their
  source and period;
- do not map historical terms or described behavior onto a modern diagnosis (`CLM-034016`);
- obtain Jamaican language/history review before any public quotation or performance.

## Family is present only through mediation

One admission warrant states that no relatives or friends were known, while later records mention
a mother, grandmother, relative, and friends (`EVD-034015`, `EVD-034016`). This is a documentary
tension, not permission to decide that the family was absent, neglectful, deceptive, or steadily
supportive. The dossier contains no direct family-authored testimony. A family-centered account
requires additional primary research, including the held commission volumes, and cannot be
reconstructed from official paraphrase.

## Privacy, dignity, and harm controls

The historical object is public domain, but legal reuse does not settle ethical use. The dossier
names people in connection with institutionalization, alleged sexual and physical violence,
intimate bodily conditions, children and parenting, religion, illness, employment, bodily waste,
restraint, and death. Before public use:

- use the minimum detail necessary to explain the historical claim;
- do not use graphic or humiliating detail as a hook, thumbnail, headline, reenactment, or search
  teaser;
- do not treat a historical patient name as automatically necessary merely because it is legally
  reusable;
- assess possible descendant and community impact even when legal privacy has expired;
- separate allegations, denials, observations, reported speech, and later interpretation;
- require Jamaican historian/archivist, disability/survivor, dignity, descendant-risk, rights, and
  harm review;
- provide accessible transcripts and contextual warnings if any image or quotation is eventually
  approved.

## Public-story holds

This packet does not authorize public prose. Hold all of the following until the named evidence and
reviews exist:

- quotation, paraphrase, narrative sequence, or authorship claims from Pratt's unopened *Seven
  Months* pamphlet;
- any finding, testimony, witness count, vindication, recommendation, or outcome attributed to the
  1861 commission;
- claims that Pratt caused reform, that a new building improved care, or that the controversy had a
  defined imperial effect;
- a modern diagnosis for any person;
- prevalence, consent, voluntariness, recovery, efficacy, or representative-outcome claims;
- a single stable meaning for contested bathing, restraint, labor, or institutional “treatment”;
- a claim that one Kingston dossier represents Jamaica, the Caribbean, colonial institutions, or
  global mental-health history;
- public reproduction of patient-associated images, intimate passages, or Jamaican speech without
  the packet's language, rights, dignity, community, and harm gates.

## Minimum honest later frame

If independent review eventually clears this material, the smallest defensible frame is: an 1860
Jamaican government-directed dossier preserved competing, differently mediated accounts about one
Kingston institution and the treatment of named people; it also shows how colonial authorities
elicited, selected, classified, printed, and circulated those accounts. That frame must remain local,
contested, and explicit about the missing Pratt pamphlet, missing commission archive, and absent
direct family voice.
