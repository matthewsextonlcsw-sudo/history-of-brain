# R22 Jamaica Official Documents, 1860 — Research Brief

**Status:** Frozen candidate for Batch 06 internal research; no public prose

**Place and dates:** Female Lunatic Asylum within Kingston Public Hospital and Lunatic Asylum,
Jamaica; Dec. 9, 1859–Aug. 14, 1860. Hanover/Lucea appears only in Ann Pratt's committal/return
network.

**Question:** How did patients, workers, relatives, medical officers, parish authorities, and the
colonial government contest credible evidence about coercion, care, procedure, and institutional
authority in the official Ann Pratt counter-dossier?

## Frozen corpus

The complete 56-page *Official Documents on the Case of Ann Pratt* (Kingston, Aug. 14, 1860) is
the primary source object. Use complete opened scholarship by Christienna Fryar and Margaret
Jones to map the constructed archive and dependencies. Hansard may supply one bounded antecedent.
Ann Pratt's *Seven Months* and the 1861 commission records remain `LX/HOLD` unless their actual
primary folios are acquired and fully opened.

## Required controls

- Model patient, parish magistrate/police/churchwarden, worker, physician, inspector, governor,
  Colonial Office, print, OCR, and later historian as distinct source positions.
- Separate elicited first-person examination, reported patient experience, worker testimony,
  mediated family trace, official allegation, and institutional defense. Pratt's statement in the
  dossier is not automatically the text of her original pamphlet.
- Her refusal of a private examination, request for counsel/supporters, and correction of the
  read-back record show bounded procedural agency; they do not establish consent to confinement,
  treatment, or publication.
- Do not infer witness race from occupation or speech. Race, gender, class, post-emancipation
  labor, and sexual reputation are historical power relations and credibility practices, not
  neutral descriptors.
- The dossier contains sexual violence allegations, pregnancy, children, nudity, bodily waste,
  restraint, assault, death, and named people. Use minimum necessary detail, distinguish direct
  observation from hearsay, and do not adjudicate allegations.
- Jamaican historical, disability/lived-experience, dignity, descendant-risk, language, and
  item-level reproduction review are required before public use.

prevalence, diagnosis, efficacy, reform causation, sensational retelling, and “one scandal
represents the Caribbean.” Stop any dependent claim when its primary file has not been opened.
