# R19 St. Thomas enslaved letters — findings

**Status:** internal evidence extraction, not public prose
**Corpus:** six selected St. Thomas letters/petitions, 1738–1753
**Core ceiling:** evidence of recorded expression, petition, relationship, and religiously framed coping/repair; not diagnosis, prevalence, efficacy, or comprehensive healing history

## Finding summary

1. **Voice is real and layered.** Peter, Domingo Gesoe, and Cornelius are identified by the edition as writers of their own items; Lenathge's words survive in missionary Löhans's hand; the king petition names Domingo as writer for multiple petitioners; Damma/Marotta/Madlena survives through several African-language versions, a Creole rendering, copies, and modern translation. None is automatically diary, verbatim speech, or unmediated mind.
2. **The six documents record emotion and collective action.** They include heart language, sadness, crying, hope, prayer, shared meals, greeting, petition, emissaries, remembered flight, and reported violence. These are historically specific acts and phrases, not modern diagnoses or treatment outcomes.
3. **Religion cannot be filed under either comfort or control alone.** The writers appropriate religious language for relation, protest, solidarity, forgiveness and hope. Zinzendorf's comparator simultaneously commands obedience and explicitly justifies bodily slavery. The evidence supports tension, not a humanitarian-progress story.
4. **Damma's language layer is an active disagreement.** The 1996 edition calls the African language unidentified. A 2025 collaboration identifies it as Gbe and shows substantive differences from the Creole text, while acknowledging incomplete interpretation. Under the frozen brief, the packet retains `Unknown` for publication pending specialist adjudication and manuscript-chain replay.
5. **The archive remains selective and externally held.** The 1996 anthology chose a few items from roughly 150 letters. It was prepared from photocopies of microfilms, not the fragile originals. Herrnhut controls reproductions; DBNL controls the consulted digital edition route. Public use also needs USVI-rooted/descendant-community review.

## Evidence-ceiling decisions

- The terms translated as heart, sadness, anger, crying, happiness, hope, mercy, love and illness remain historically situated words inside letters/petitions. They are not mapped one-to-one onto depression, anxiety, trauma disorder, psychosis or any current diagnosis.
- `self-written` means the consulted edition or contemporary annotation attributes writer and author to the same person. It does not mean the packet independently verified handwriting, freedom from prompting, or untouched composition.
- `dictated/mission-written` preserves the recorder relation. It does not imply the enslaved speaker lacked agency, nor that the scribe recorded exact words.
- A petition's “on behalf of” formula establishes the petition's representational claim, not the actual number, assent, or unanimity of the constituency.
- The new Gbe identification is important evidence of a changed scholarly state. It is not adopted as settled public fact until a language specialist replays the exact manuscript versions and a reviewer reconciles the 1996 and 2025 archive identifiers and translations.
