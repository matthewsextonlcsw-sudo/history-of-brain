# R19 St. Thomas Enslaved Letters — Research Brief

**Status:** Frozen candidate for Batch 06 internal research; no public prose

**Place and dates:** St. Thomas, Danish West Indies (now USVI), 1738–1753

**Question:** How did named enslaved people write, dictate, translate, petition, organize, and
describe distress, emotion, bodily suffering, kinship, faith, and religious repair—and how did
missionary teaching, multilingual contact, scribes, copies, archives, and a modern diplomatic
edition mediate what survives?

## Frozen corpus

Use only six documents in Unity Archives Herrnhut record group `R.15.B.a`, accessed through the
complete diplomatic edition *Die Creol taal* (1996), pp. 49–86: Peter/Pitrus (1738), the 1739
congregation petition, Marotta/Madlena (1739), Lenathge (1740/1741), Domingo Gesoe (1752), and
Cornelius (1753). Record each archive locator, document/copy status, attributed author, physical
writer, dictator, translator, language, recipient, and modern editorial layer separately.

## Required controls

- Distinguish self-writing, dictation, translation, petition constituency, missionary marginal
  note, manuscript copy, and modern edition. No document is automatically an autograph, diary,
  verbatim speech, or unmediated experience.
- Preserve Virgin Islands Dutch Creole wording and exact English translation layers. Marotta's
  unidentified African language remains `Unknown`; “Poppo” does not establish a modern ethnicity
  or language.
- “Ill from sadness,” heart, fear, anger, crying, happiness, hope, prayer, and communal repair are
  historical language, not retrospective diagnoses or proof of treatment outcome.
- Moravian comfort and Black religious agency must appear beside Moravian obedience teaching and
  operation within slavery. This packet does not establish generalized African-Caribbean,
  herbal, medical, or Indigenous healing.
- Treat claimed petition constituencies as rhetoric unless independently verified. Do not infer
  unanimous consent.
- Public framing requires translation-sensitive review, USVI-rooted historical and descendant-
  community review, Herrnhut manuscript/reproduction clarification, and title-specific DBNL
  rights compliance.

## Exclusions and stop conditions

Exclude St. John's 1733 revolt, St. Croix, obeah, herbal practice, emancipation, later asylum
history, modern continuity, and all “first Creole letter” claims. Stop and mark a claim `HOLD` if
the full relevant edition passage or exact manuscript/edition chain cannot be replayed. No public
quotations, manuscript images, SEO case cards, or video scripts.
