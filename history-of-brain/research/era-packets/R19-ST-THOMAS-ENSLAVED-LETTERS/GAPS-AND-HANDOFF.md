# R19 St. Thomas enslaved letters — gaps and handoff

## Packet disposition

**READY FOR INDEPENDENT CROSS-REVIEW for bounded internal outline work; public drafting remains held.** The packet establishes a six-document correspondence and petition microhistory, not a general history of Atlantic slavery, African-Caribbean healing or Virgin Islands mental health.

## Frozen scope

- **Place:** St. Thomas, with document routes to Denmark, Herrnhut and Bethlehem explicitly named where supported.
- **Dates:** documents dated 1738–1753; the edition's larger described letter corpus spans 1737–1768 only as archive context.
- **Corpus:** Peter/Pitrus (1738); king petition (1739); Damma/Marotta/Madlena (1739); Lenathge/Milka (1740 [probably 1741]); Domingo Gesoe (1752); Cornelius (1753).
- **Comparator:** Zinzendorf's 1739 obedience/bodily-slavery letter solely to test a one-sided mission-rescue narrative.
- **Evidence jobs:** exact document metadata and wording layers; writing/dictation/copy/translation; petition and religious genres; distress/emotion/body language; resistance and relation; custody, naming, rights and community-review gates.

## What is complete

- Six item records replayed in the complete 1996 letters chapter, with archival signatures, edition list/definition numbers, page locators, dates, attributed authors, writers/scribes, recipients and translation layers.
- Edition method replayed through its preface, diplomatic symbols and description of the approximately 150-letter selection.
- Later full-source philology opened for writer/dictation practice, the Peter-heading correction, Lenathge's hand and archive-language context.

## Exact unresolved gaps

### Original objects and transmission

1. **Original manuscript images were not opened.** The packet cannot independently verify hands, marks, insertions, deletion, page order, signatures, copy state, paper or ink. Obtain Unity Archives-approved facsimiles for `R.15.B.a` items and replay each against the 1996 edition before public visual/textual claims.
2. **Locator reconciliation is pending.** The 2025 Damma project uses a current archive form such as `R.15.B.a.03.61_a-b`; the 1996 edition uses `R15Ba-3.61.r`. Unity Archives should reconcile exact current identifiers and object extents.
3. **Copy and composition paths remain incomplete.** The petition's negotiation, the Damma versions' sequence, the writer of the first version, the translator(s), and the relation between manuscript and 1742 print need object-level study.
4. **The corpus is selected.** The approximately 150 letters, unselected archival material, nonsurvival and uncatalogued material were not exhaustively searched. No frequency or archive-total claim may be made.

### Language, translation and naming

5. **Damma's language and translation are a hard public HOLD.** The 1996 unidentified-language state and 2025 Gbe identification/interpretation must be adjudicated by named Gbe and Creole specialists with the original object, not resolved by choosing the newest source automatically.
6. **The 2025 interpretation is not a finished diplomatic translation.** Its own team reports incomplete sentences, disagreement and interpretive choices. Public text must not silently replace the Creole layer with the Gbe layer or vice versa.
7. **English wording is edition-dependent.** Muysken's 1996 English versions are free translations. Any public quotation requires verification of exact source-language text, gloss, punctuation and translation, with rights clearance.
8. **Current naming is unsettled for publication.** “Carriols (Virgin Islands Dutch Creole)” is a documented candidate, but USVI-rooted and language-specialist review is required. Historical slur-containing labels are held except where necessary to identify a source.
9. **No ethnicity inference is cleared.** “Poppo,” “Aja,” Gbe or origin phrases cannot be turned into a modern ethnic identity without specialist historical analysis.

### Lived experience, status and outcome

10. **Directness varies.** Self-writing is edition-attributed for Peter, Domingo and Cornelius; Lenathge is mission-written; the collective and Damma chains are multiply mediated. Original hands, exact dictation and voluntary composition remain partly Unknown.
11. **Damma's legal/status biography needs replay.** A newer project describes a trajectory not independently established in this packet. Do not simplify her status at the document date until primary evidence is opened.
12. **Petition constituency and assent are Unknown.** The 650-plus and 250-women figures remain document claims without opened rosters, denominators or authorization records.
13. **No recipient response or practical outcome is established.** The packet cannot show whether petitions changed violence, whether letters received replies, or whether prayer/correspondence altered distress.
14. **No diagnosis or prevalence denominator exists.** Historical sadness, crying, heart/body and religious terms remain situated expressions rather than clinical cases.
15. **No generalized healing corpus exists here.** The six documents do not document herbal practice, obeah, Indigenous medicine, medical treatment, asylum care, therapeutic effectiveness or safety.

### Community, rights and harm

16. **No community or descendant approval was obtained.** Contact with USVI-rooted scholars, archivists, educators and descendant/community organizations is a public-review gate. Nonresponse must not be recorded as consent.
17. **No organization is presumed to be the sole authority.** Review should be plural and specific to naming, translation, historical context, reproduction and commercial use.
18. **Unity Archives reproduction permission remains pending.** Public manuscript images, digital exhibits, sharing or extensive quotation require archive review under its current rules.
19. **DBNL access is not commercial reuse clearance.** Link and limited citation rules are recorded; extended or commercial reuse requires a separate decision.
20. **Commercial/public use of scholarship differs by source.** Bøegh 2024 is CC BY 4.0; Gerbner 2025 is CC BY-NC 4.0 and contains separately governed archival images; other sources remain link-only absent a documented license.
21. **Trauma and dignity review remains required.** Violence, enslavement, intimate religious expression and named historical people must not be used as spectacle, clickbait or decorative imagery.

## Handoff to outline synthesis

After independent closure and before public drafting, the packet can support these bounded transitions:

1. **From “patient voice” to authored and mediated correspondence:** introduce named writers, scribes, petitioners, translators and editors rather than treating “enslaved voice” as one category.
2. **From emotion word to historical relationship:** place heart, sadness, crying, hope, forgiveness and bodily language inside letter, petition and devotional genres without diagnosis.
3. **From individual to collective claim:** contrast a named self-written letter with petitions that claim to speak for hundreds, then show why representation is not a headcount or consent record.
4. **From archive text to transmission chain:** expose the route from object through Moravian custody, microfilm, photocopy, edition, translation and digitization.
5. **From religious meaning to institutional contradiction:** retain Black uses of Moravian networks for relation and appeal beside Zinzendorf's obedience and bodily-slavery position.
6. **From a translated African text to epistemic humility:** use the Damma disagreement to show why language expertise, original objects and community context matter.
7. **From visibility to missing people:** end the section with nonwriters, non-Moravians, dissenters, children, disabled people, untranslated words and people whose records did not survive.

The synthesis must keep the named place, six-item corpus and document-level attribution visible. It should not absorb this packet into a generic “Caribbean beliefs” paragraph.
