## Declared corpus, places, dates, and languages

This is a bounded, multilingual comparison, not an account of “the Jewish view.” It uses:

1. Hebrew and Aramaic Judaean Desert manuscripts, especially 1QHa and 11Q11, from the
   second century BCE through the first century CE;
2. selected Greek works by Philo of Alexandria and Josephus, written in Roman-era
   Alexandria/Judaea/Rome;
3. selected rabbinic Hebrew and Jewish Babylonian Aramaic passages in the Mishnah
   (compiled in Roman Palestine around 200 CE) and Babylonian Talmud (redacted in
   Sasanian Mesopotamia in late antiquity);
4. named Jewish Babylonian Aramaic incantation bowls, prioritizing an excavated Nippur
   object and using one unprovenanced museum bowl only with an explicit provenance and
   mediation warning. The generic fifth–eighth-century corpus range crosses the packet's
   ca. 700 boundary and does not date either individual object; both are explicit
   boundary/handoff material unless object-specific dating is established; and
5. the Greek Theodotos inscription from Jerusalem only to test a hospital-origin claim.

Modern English translations are always named. Christian and New Testament material,
medieval Jewish medicine/philosophy, retrospective clinical diagnoses, and generalized
claims about all Jews are outside the packet. Exchange between texts, communities, and
regions is not assumed merely because dates or vocabulary overlap.

## Discovery, access, and capture note

Agent Reach reported Jina Reader available and Exa unavailable. Jina Reader and direct
publisher, repository, museum, and text-API routes were therefore used, supplemented by
the built-in primary-source web-search fallback. Exa's absence is a discovery limitation,
not an evidence substitute. Search-result snippets, abstracts, and inaccessible previews
were treated only as leads. Claims below rest on opened full texts or full object records;
lead-only sources are quarantined.

Files are cached in the git-ignored directory
`../../source-cache/R08-JEWISH-LATE-ANTIQUITY/`. A cache is for source continuity, not a
grant to redistribute. Restricted and copyrighted works must remain local; public work
should link to lawful host pages and quote only what permissions and fair-use constraints
allow.

### SRC-011000 — Schuller and Newsom, critical study edition of 1QHa

- **Citation:** Eileen M. Schuller and Carol A. Newsom, *The Hodayot (Thanksgiving
  Psalms): A Study Edition of 1QHa* (Atlanta: Society of Biblical Literature, 2012),
  [publisher-hosted PDF](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Hodyot_Schuller_Newsom.pdf).
- **Form / language:** Hebrew critical text, reconstruction, and English translation with
  introduction.
- **Level / fit:** **L1-A** for edited wording, columns, and lines; **L2-B** for the editors'
  literary/source-history interpretation.
- **Pivotal locators:** introduction pp. 9–13, especially the disputed relation of the
  first-person “I,” Teacher Hymns, and Community Hymns; 1QHa IV 31–38; VI 29; IX 34–35;
  X 5–9, 30; XI 8–13; XII 10–19.
- **Limit:** a literary/liturgical first person is not automatically a patient's
  autobiography. Reconstructions and translations remain editorial acts.
- **Rights / cache:** SBL copyright; local research cache only, no PDF redistribution.

### SRC-011003 — Philo, *On the Contemplative Life*

- **Citation:** Philo of Alexandria, *On the Contemplative Life*, trans. F. C. Conybeare,
  ed. David M. Tilden (Bloomington: Indiana University, 1922),
  [public-domain scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/66/Philo_Judeaus%2C_on_the_contemplative_life_%28IA_philojudeausonco00phil%29.pdf).
- **Form / language:** Greek philosophical-ethnographic treatise in an older English
  translation.
- **Level / fit:** **L1-A** for what Philo's text represents; **C** for using that
  representation as transparent social description.
- **Pivotal locators:** §§1–3 (name and “healing” of souls), §§21–39 (Lake Mareotis,
  dwellings, study), §§68–90 (men's and women's communal observance).
- **Limit:** “healing” is Philo's ethical/theological etymology and contrast with bodily
  medicine; it does not prove a clinic, psychotherapy, or all Alexandrian practice. The
  1922 editor's comparative commentary is outdated and not used as evidence.
- **Rights:** scan and translation are public domain; new critical translation still needed
  before public quotation.

### SRC-011004 — Josephus, *Jewish Antiquities* 8.45–49

- **Citation:** Flavius Josephus, *Antiquities of the Jews* 8.2.5 (§§45–49 in modern
  numbering), trans. William Whiston,
  [University of Chicago LacusCurtius transcription](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-8.html#S2.5).
- **Form / language:** Greek historiography in an older public-domain English translation.
- **Level / fit:** **L1-A** for Josephus's authored claim; **C** for whether the reported
  event occurred exactly as narrated or represented wider practice.
- **Pivotal locator:** book 8, chapter 2, §5: Eleazar, ring/root, Solomon's name and
  incantations, Vespasian's audience, and overturned water vessel.
- **Limit:** authorial eyewitness rhetoric is not independent verification; the ancient
  label must not be converted into a modern diagnosis.
