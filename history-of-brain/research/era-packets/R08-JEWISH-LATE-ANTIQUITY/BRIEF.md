# R08-JEWISH-LATE-ANTIQUITY — Plural Communities, Texts, Bodies, and Care

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `011000–011999`

## Research question

How did named Jewish communities and texts in late antiquity represent body, soul, emotion,
dreams, suffering, troubling conduct, healing, family/community obligations, law, ritual, and
care across Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, and other settings?

## Boundary

Approximately 200 BCE–700 CE, with named places, communities, languages, and corpora. Qumran,
rabbinic, diaspora, liturgical, legal, magical/amuletic, medical, and archaeological evidence
must remain distinct. This is not “the Jewish view.”

## Required evidence

- critical texts/translations and manuscript/object records with exact locators;
- specialist Second Temple, rabbinic, diaspora, social, medical, and material scholarship;
- household, communal, legal, charitable, ritual, and healer contexts;
- textual norm versus lived implementation;
- language/translation and manuscript/redaction histories;
- women, children, disabled, enslaved/dependent, poor, and non-elite archive limits; and
- audits of demonology-only, first-hospital, diagnosis, and simple Greek-versus-Jewish claims.

## Stop

Do not turn later medieval Jewish philosophy/medicine into late-antique evidence. Create the
standard outputs, typed relationships, and rights-safe cache; no public prose.
