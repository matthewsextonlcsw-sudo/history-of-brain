## Evidence records

### EVD-011000

- **Source:** SRC-011000.
- **Locator:** introduction pp. 9–13.
- **Observation:** the editors distinguish Teacher and Community Hymn groupings, describe
  the first-person voice as personal-seeming, and report disagreement about authorial unity
  and identification with the Teacher of Righteousness.
- **Fit / strength:** A for editorial statement; B for literary reconstruction.
- **Limits:** one edition team; not a biography.

### EVD-011001

- **Source:** SRC-011000.
- **Locator:** 1QHa IV 31–38; VI 29; IX 34–35; X 5–9 and 30; XI 8–13; XII 10–19.
- **Observation:** the edited hymns use first-person language of heart, spirit, soul,
  distress, affliction, support/comfort, social estrangement, and opponents' insults.
- **Fit / strength:** A for wording and locators.
- **Limits:** fragmentary sections and editorial reconstruction; emotional vocabulary is
  poetic/liturgical, not a clinical symptom inventory.

### EVD-011002

- **Source:** SRC-011001.
- **Locator:** IAA manuscript fields for 11Q11.
- **Observation:** official catalogue records a Hebrew, Herodian-script parchment
  poetical/liturgical manuscript from Qumran Cave 11 and cites DJD 23.
- **Fit / strength:** A.
- **Limits:** object metadata only.

### EVD-011003

- **Source:** SRC-011002.
- **Locator:** limited preview, partial cols. II, V, and VI; terminal access message.
- **Observation:** the cached artifact displays only a partial Hebrew transcription with
  brackets/reconstruction marks and states that content is not shown in the limited preview.
  It does not display an English translation.
- **Fit / strength:** A for this access-status observation and the visible Hebrew characters;
  **L5/LX-D** for any supplied English meaning, restoration-sensitive reading, or historical
  performance claim.
- **Limits:** no researcher translation or semantic claim is retained. Healing, a Raphael
  restoration, named spirits/demons, banishment, Davidic-charm wording, divine-name wording,
  and Psalm 91 sequencing remain HOLD until a complete authoritative edition/translation is
  opened and exact columns/lines are checked.

### EVD-011004

- **Source:** SRC-011003.
- **Locator:** *Contemplative Life* §§1–3, 21–39, 68–90.
- **Observation:** Philo calls the male/female group Therapeutae/Therapeutrides, offers
  healing/worship etymologies, contrasts bodily treatment with healing souls overcome by
  passions, griefs and fears, situates the group near Lake Mareotis, and depicts male and
  female communal observance.
- **Fit / strength:** A for authored representation; C for transparent social history.
- **Limits:** philosophical and rhetorical framing; older translation.

### EVD-011005

- **Source:** SRC-011004.
- **Locator:** *Antiquities* 8.2.5.
- **Observation:** Josephus says he saw Eleazar use a ring/root, Solomon's name and
  incantations, and an overturned vessel to demonstrate release of persons he labels
  demoniacal before Vespasian and others.
- **Fit / strength:** A for authorial report; C for event reconstruction.
- **Limits:** no independent witness opened; Whiston translation is old.

### EVD-011006

- **Source:** SRC-011005.
- **Locator:** Mishnah Hagigah 1:1.
- **Observation:** the legal list exempts `shoteh` alongside a minor, sensory/mobility and
  illness/age statuses, gender categories, and unfree status from pilgrimage appearance.
- **Fit / strength:** A for consulted text.
- **Limits:** normative classification and bundled statuses, not diagnosis or implementation.

### EVD-011007

- **Source:** SRC-011006.
- **Locator:** Bavli Hagigah 3b–4a.
- **Observation:** Rav Huna and Rabbi Yoḥanan disagree whether one or all of three actions
  establish `shoteh`; the passage tests contextual alternatives and then adds destruction
  of what one is given.
- **Fit / strength:** A for redacted disagreement; B for legal interpretation.
- **Limits:** attribution/redaction history not independently established here.

### EVD-011008

- **Source:** SRC-011007.
- **Locator:** Nedarim 39b–40a, sick-visiting rules and sayings.
- **Observation:** the passage urges repeated sick visits and crossing status hierarchy and
  supplies aphorisms about sharing suffering and the moral stakes of visiting.
- **Fit / strength:** A for text; C for implementation/efficacy.
- **Limits:** homiletic quantities and conditions are not medical measurements.

### EVD-011009

- **Source:** SRC-011007.
- **Locator:** Nedarim 40a, Rabbi Akiva/neglected student and Rava/prayer units.
- **Observation:** a didactic story represents Rabbi Akiva directing cleaning and watering
  of the floor before a student's improvement; nearby text discusses disclosure and prayer.
- **Fit / strength:** A for narrative content; C for historical event and causal efficacy.
- **Limits:** redacted exemplum, not patient chart.

### EVD-011010

- **Source:** SRC-011008.
- **Locator:** Mishnah Peah 8:7–9.
- **Observation:** the text specifies provisions, communal dish/fund staffing, asset
  thresholds, and moralized consequences for feigning disability or need.
- **Fit / strength:** A for norm/rhetoric; C for implementation.
- **Limits:** no recipient voice or delivery record.

### EVD-011011

- **Source:** SRC-011009.
- **Locator:** Berakhot 55b–57b.
- **Observation:** the redacted unit includes a dream-betterment rite before three people,
  prayer, numerous competing interpretations, twenty-four Jerusalem interpreters, and the
  paid interpreter Bar Haddaya narrative.
- **Fit / strength:** A for contents; C for historical prevalence.
- **Limits:** contradiction cannot be flattened into one theory.

### EVD-011012

- **Source:** SRC-011010.
- **Locator:** Shabbat 67a.
- **Observation:** material and verbal fever/boil/wound/demon procedures appear alongside
  disputes over “ways of the Amorite,” an “element of healing,” and publicizing pain for
  prayer.
- **Fit / strength:** A for text; C for practice/efficacy.
- **Limits:** edition includes explanatory gloss; no performance record.

### EVD-011013

- **Source:** SRC-011011.
- **Locator:** Gittin 67b.
- **Observation:** the text discusses temporary `kordeyakos`, attributes a demon and
  remedy, tests communicative competence for a legal act, and adds fever-remedy narratives.
- **Fit / strength:** A for text; D for retrospective diagnosis.
- **Limits:** multiple legal/narrative layers; no modern equivalence.

### EVD-011014

- **Sources:** SRC-011013, SRC-011014.
- **Locator:** Montgomery introduction pp. 13–15; Penn B2963 object fields.
- **Observation:** bowl B2963/CBS 2963 was excavated at Nippur in the University of
  Pennsylvania's 1889 expedition; ceramic with inked Aramaic text.
- **Fit / strength:** A for object/provenance.
- **Limits:** early excavation recording and colonial collection history.

### EVD-011015

- **Sources:** SRC-011013, SRC-011014.
- **Locator:** Montgomery bowl no. 3, pp. 127–129; Penn description.
- **Observation:** the inscription names Ardoi bar Hormizduch, his wife Ahath daughter of
  Parkoi, and children in a household protection formula against destructive beings/angel
  of death, ending with Zechariah 3:2.
- **Fit / strength:** A/B for edition and function.
- **Limits:** Montgomery's philology is old; names appear through a formulaic scribal
  inscription, not direct testimony. Commissioner, payer, scribe identity or professional
  status, placement, use, and beneficiary response are unknown.

### EVD-011016

- **Sources:** SRC-011014, SRC-011015.
- **Locator:** Penn field `Date Made ca. 200 BCE`; Brodie pp. 9–10.
- **Observation:** Penn's current date conflicts with the specialist fifth–eighth-century
  range for Aramaic incantation bowls and with the wider corpus context.
- **Fit / strength:** A for recorded contradiction; B for corpus dating.
- **Limits:** the generic corpus range can extend beyond the packet's ca. 700 CE boundary and
  does not redetermine B2963's individual date. Treat B2963 as chronology-disputed
  boundary/handoff evidence; quarantine Penn's field pending curator/current specialist
  resolution and do not assign the object to either 200 BCE or pre-700 from these sources.

### EVD-011017

- **Source:** SRC-011015.
- **Locator:** pp. 9–10 and 10–14.
- **Observation:** few bowls have documented archaeological contexts; hundreds appeared
  without provenance after 1991 and are widely linked to illicit excavation/market trade.
- **Fit / strength:** B.
- **Limits:** modern ethics/provenance evidence, not ancient use.

### EVD-011018

- **Source:** SRC-011016.
- **Locator:** pp. 131–133, especially translated lines 1–5.
- **Observation:** Fein publishes a reading of a Jewish Babylonian Aramaic bowl placed in a
  broad fifth–eighth-century corpus, Lisbon Inv. 10895, naming Mihranahid daughter of
  Ahat/Kuṭus and requesting heavenly healing, “wine of life” in her body, children, and
  Rachel-linked aid.
- **Fit / strength:** B for published reading; C for biography.
- **Limits:** depends on Bhayro 2017, not opened; provenance/acquisition history and the
  individual object's date are unresolved. The generic range may extend beyond ca. 700 CE;
  this is boundary/handoff evidence, not a securely pre-700 object.

### EVD-011019

- **Source:** SRC-011016.
- **Locator:** pp. 137–138 and methodological framing.
- **Observation:** the author explicitly says the exact situation and inner thoughts cannot
  be known and then uses critical fabulation/methodology of imagination to construct agency.
- **Fit / strength:** A for the author's stated method; D for constructed biography as fact.
- **Limits:** interpretation must not be converted into lived testimony.

### EVD-011020

- **Source:** SRC-011017.
- **Locator:** pp. 552–556.
- **Observation:** Gross rejects a simple binary between elite rabbis and “superstitious
  masses,” arguing that rabbinic narrative participates in a wider late-antique Babylonian
  ritual discourse.
- **Fit / strength:** A/B for attributed argument.
- **Limits:** one scholarly synthesis; no universal community identity claim.

### EVD-011021

- **Source:** SRC-011017.
- **Locator:** pp. 572–574.
- **Observation:** Gross compares Zechariah 3:2 forms in bowls and Bavli narratives and
  argues that redactors selected among apotropaic options in a shared environment.
- **Fit / strength:** B.
- **Limits:** parallel and selection do not establish one-way transmission or author identity.

### EVD-011022

- **Source:** SRC-011019.
- **Locator:** inscription record and translation.
- **Observation:** the Greek inscription records a Jerusalem synagogue built/restored for
  law reading/instruction together with hostel, guest rooms, and baths for foreigners.
- **Fit / strength:** A/B.
- **Limits:** no physician, disease, treatment, or hospital term.

### EVD-011023

- **Source:** SRC-011018.
- **Locator:** pp. 373–375.
- **Observation:** Horden reviews the Theodotos claim, finds no reason to suppose doctors
  attended the hostel, and reports little/no corroborating early evidence for synagogue
  hospices before later guesthouse references.
- **Fit / strength:** A/B for comparative audit.
- **Limits:** bounded survey and definition argument; absence is not nonexistence proof.

### EVD-011024

- **Sources:** SRC-011020, SRC-011021.
- **Locator:** *Jewish Encyclopedia*, “Hospital”; Jerome Letter 77 §6.
- **Observation:** the old tertiary entry points toward Fabiola, while Jerome's source
  praises the Roman Christian Fabiola for founding a “hospital” and personally caring for
  sick and poor people; it does not document a Jewish institution.
- **Fit / strength:** A for what the fully opened Fremantle English translation represents;
  C for institutional reconstruction; D for the tertiary priority inference.
- **Limits:** the working full-text URL was replayed and the cache now preserves a verified
  §6 locator capture. The prior colon-form cache was a 404 and was not evidence. Jerome's
  text is eulogistic, the Christian comparison belongs in the neighboring packet, and this
  English witness does not establish the Latin term sometimes supplied in secondary work.

### EVD-011025

- **Sources:** SRC-011000, SRC-011004, SRC-011007, SRC-011013, SRC-011016.
- **Locator:** genre/method passages cited above and packet-wide voice audit.
- **Observation:** every apparent person/experience in the selected ancient evidence reaches
  us through literary composition, authorial report, legal redaction, or formulaic scribal
  artifact; no unmediated patient account was found.
- **Fit / strength:** A within the bounded corpus.
- **Limits:** does not imply experiences did not occur or that all testimony is equally distant.

### EVD-011026

- **Sources:** SRC-011000–SRC-011019.
- **Locator:** each source's place/language/date metadata and declared-corpus audit.
- **Observation:** sources span distinct Greek, Hebrew, rabbinic Hebrew, and Aramaic genres
  and communities across Judaea/Jerusalem, Alexandria/Rome, Roman Palestine, and Sasanian
  Mesopotamia; only the Babylonian text/object comparison supplies a focused interaction
  argument.
- **Fit / strength:** A for corpus geography; B for bounded interaction.
- **Limits:** a mapped corpus is not itself a transmission history.

### EVD-011027

- **Source:** SRC-011024.
- **Locator:** pp. lxxiii–lxxvi and cii–ciii.
- **Observation:** Bakhos and Schwartz describe the Mishnah as a rabbinic compendium
  redacted circa 200 CE, the Talmuds as the result of a complex later compilation, and the
  Bavli as difficult to date/contextualize amid limited evidence for life in Sasanian
  Mesopotamia.
- **Fit / strength:** B.
- **Limits:** modern synthesis, not a critical edition; corpus dates do not date every unit.
