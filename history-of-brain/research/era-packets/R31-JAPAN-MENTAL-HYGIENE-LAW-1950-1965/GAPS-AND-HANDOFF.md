## Answer to the bounded question

The corpus documents a mixed legal-administrative system rather than a demonstrated shift from
hospitals to autonomous community care. The 1950 law authorized consultation and visiting
guidance while assigning prefectural hospital provision, protector duties, multiple
non-patient-consent admission routes, restrictions, discharge retrieval, and a temporary home
restraint route. Government reporting documents severe capacity pressure in 1956 and rapid
hospital/bed growth through the early 1960s. The government nevertheless described surveys,
consultation, centres, and visits as community care. The 1965 amendments expanded centre/PHC
functions and created outpatient cost support at the packet endpoint, while also expanding
police notification and emergency-admission powers.

The opened material therefore supports a law/program and administrative-scale account. It does
not show that non-hospital services were consistently staffed or used, that they reduced
institutionalization, or that patients and families experienced them as support rather than
surveillance or transferred burden. The bounded Kanagawa study shows family-applicant,
financial, official-applicant, and location variables within one 1959 Article 23 route; it does
not supply lived experience or a national sample. Family organizing is visible only through a
later mediated reconstruction and unopened original materials.
