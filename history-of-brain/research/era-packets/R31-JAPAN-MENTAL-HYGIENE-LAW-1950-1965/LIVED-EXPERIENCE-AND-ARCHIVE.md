## Lived-experience ceiling

R31 can say what national law authorized, what national administrators instructed or reported,
what one later study counted in a defined Kanagawa administrative subset, and how one later
scholar reconstructed family organizing from mediated materials. It cannot say how a patient
experienced admission, restriction, discharge, a home visit, family decisions, outpatient
support, or an opened ward. It cannot say how a family understood or felt its legal obligation,
why a person filed an Article 23 application, or how a public-health-centre worker exercised
discretion.

The absence of direct patient and frontline-worker material is structurally important. The
opened system speaks most clearly through statutes, ministries, tables, catalogues, and later
scholars. That source imbalance must remain visible in any future synthesis; it cannot be fixed
by writing imagined first-person transitions or by treating an administrative variable as a
human explanation.

## Translation protocol

- Preserve `精神衛生` as period *seishin eisei* / “mental hygiene” when discussing the
  statute or administrative system. Do not silently replace it with a current diagnostic,
  disability, or service category.
- Preserve `保護義務者` as *hogogimusha* plus a cautious working gloss (“legally designated
  protector”) until a Japanese legal historian approves a context-specific rendering.
- Do not translate `同意入院` or Article 33 as patient-consensual or voluntary admission. The
  opened provision centers protector consent and absence of the person's consent.
- Treat “community care” in the 1962 English report as the Japanese government's English
  self-description. It is not an authoritative translation of the statute, a WHO finding, or a
  synonym for deinstitutionalization.
- Translate consultation, visiting guidance, restraint, emergency admission, occupational
  titles, and confidentiality terms only at claim level, from the exact provision and period.
- A bilingual legal-historical reviewer must align Japanese text, researcher gloss, and any
  contemporaneous English usage before public wording. Translation disagreements must become
  records, not be silently harmonized.

## Privacy, dignity, and harm rules

- Medical-record access and public use are separate decisions. Repository permission never
  authorizes unnecessary extraction or publication.
- Use Kanagawa evidence only at the aggregate level already published by Goto and Ando. Never
  join fields to reconstruct cases or search for identities.
- Family letters and newsletters require issue/item authorship, editorial mediation, consent,
  copyright, pseudonym, relational privacy, and survivor/descendant-risk review.
- Do not reproduce government eugenic slurs, normalize hereditary claims, or call an aggregate
  operation count psychiatric. No sensational framing, victim-count inference, or diagnosis.
- Do not convert historical legal or administrative categories into current diagnoses.
- No patient, family, nurse, social-worker, or local-government quotation is approved.
- No table image, page image, facsimile, portrait, chart recreation, or long quotation is
  approved. A future derived chart would require commensurability, rights, and claim review.
- Public availability, public-domain status, or a website licence does not erase privacy,
  dignity, moral rights, family authority, or the packet's public hold.

## Archive silence statement

The defined corpus lacks direct patient-authored voice and opened frontline-worker testimony.
It has mediated family traces but no opened original family newsletter or letter. This silence
is limited to R31's frozen corpus and 2026-08-19 acquisition state. It does not establish that
such testimony never existed, does not survive, or cannot be found.
