# R31 Japan — Promise and Pressure under the Mental Hygiene Law, 1950–1965

**Status:** Internal research GO with gaps; public prose prohibited

**Place and dates:** Japan's national Mental Hygiene Law administration, with prefectural and
public-health-center implementation and a bounded 1959 Kanagawa lens; May 1, 1950–Oct. 1, 1965.

**Question:** Did statutory prevention, consultation, and visiting-guidance provisions shift
care away from families and hospitals, or did admission rules, financing, and protector duties
continue to channel care through families and rapidly expanding institutions?

## Frozen corpus

Use the 1950 law and implementation directive; 1956 and 1965 welfare white papers; the 1962
Japanese government report hosted by WHO; the 1965 amendment, directive, and legislative
history; the complete Kanagawa administrative-record study; and the complete specialist study
of 1964–65 family organizing. Original family newsletters and the 1959 nursing article remain
acquisition gates. Do not access underlying case records without permission and a separate
privacy protocol.

## Controls and stops

Separate law, administrative design, government self-description, aggregate implementation,
one prefectural subset, family publications, and lived experience. “Consent admission” cannot
mean patient consent. “Guidance” cannot silently become supportive community care. Bed, hospital,
consultation, and utilization counts do not establish quality or outcomes. Require Japanese
legal/historical-language review and original-family-source rights review. Preserve the direct
patient-voice gap. No case narrative, national generalization from Kanagawa, deinstitutional-
ization claim, eugenics conflation, public quotation, table/image reproduction, or modern
diagnostic translation.
