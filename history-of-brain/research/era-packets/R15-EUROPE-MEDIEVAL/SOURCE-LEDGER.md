## Bounded research design

The packet uses three named settings rather than treating “medieval Europe” as a unit:

1. **Canterbury and the English royal-law record**, c.1170–c.1325: two Benedict of Peterborough miracle narratives and the `Prerogativa Regis` property/custody provisions.
2. **Bingen/Rhineland**, 1141–1151 as narrated in Hildegard's *Scivias* declaration: a first-person visionary account plus the later migraine-diagnosis reception chain.
3. **Salerno/Monte Cassino**, late eleventh century, through a 1536 Basel printed witness: Constantine the African's Latin *De melancholia* and its Arabic source/transmission chain.

The small corpus can show what particular texts recorded and how later scholars have read them. It cannot establish European prevalence, ordinary household practice, or treatment outcomes.
