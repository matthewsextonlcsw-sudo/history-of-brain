# R15-EUROPE-MEDIEVAL findings

**Purpose:** research support for later outline reconciliation; not public prose.
**Boundary:** three settings, c.800–1350; no early-modern narrative except explicitly marked myth/reception audits.

## High-value findings by setting

### 1. Canterbury and English royal law

- Benedict's Matilda record (Book IV.37) is simultaneously evidence of restraint, violence, sacred explanation, reported grief/trauma, linguistic distance, and monastic mediation. The Latin says she was brought from the Cologne region, was described as `plena daemonio`, tore her garment, struck an intervenor, endangered a child, and was bound (`alligata`) for four or five hours at the shrine. After the narrated cure, Benedict reports questioning her through language that was barely intelligible to the monks. This is not transparent patient testimony and not a verified clinical outcome.
- Benedict's Hermer record (Book IV.19) describes a seven-year-old whose gait, movement, head rotation, appearance, and speech led the recorder to `mente captus`. His father consulted several physicians; one demanded a fee the father could not pay, after which poverty redirected him to a relic remedy. This is strong evidence against a simple “medicine or religion” binary and against universal family abandonment, but it is one selected miracle narrative, not prevalence evidence.
- Trenery argues, for her defined Benedict-and-William corpus, that vocabulary and attributed contexts vary and that possession is one frame among bodily, mental, traumatic, punitive, poisonous, and other explanations. This is a documented attribution to one dissertation route, not independent proof of a broad medieval culture or a prevalence estimate.
- The `Prerogativa Regis` provisions distinguish `fatuorum naturalium` from someone who previously had `memoriam & intellectum` and later became `non compos mentis`, including `lucida intervalla`. The provisions regulate land, profits, heirs, household sustenance, and possible recovery. They are normative property/custody text, not evidence that these provisions were implemented benevolently or universally.
- Buhrer argues that the text's date/status is uncertain and that its archive privileges landed people. The NLS record independently confirms one early-fifteenth-century witness (Adv.MS.28.7.5) with `Prerogativa Regis` at fols.179r–184r, but no manuscript image was opened. A 15 November 1285 Close Roll calendar supplies one concrete administrative order concerning examination and conditional custody; it does not prove execution, consent, care quality, or outcome.

### 2. Bingen/Rhineland

- The opened online Latin `Scivias` transcription displays Hildegard describing visions received not in dreams, sleep, or `phrenesis`, but `vigilans et circumspecta in pura mente`. Because the page does not name its edition/manuscript basis, this is L3 evidence for displayed wording, not an opened critical edition. It supports Hildegard's textual self-description, not a modern diagnosis.
- Hildegard also says she kept the visions largely silent, delayed writing because of doubt and `diversity of men's words`, and began writing amid illness and social/religious validation. Gender and institutional authority matter to how the account became recordable.
- Foxhall reports that Charles Singer formulated the migraine attribution in 1913 and published it in 1917, and traces its later reuse. Both propositions remain explicitly attributed to Foxhall because Singer and the downstream nodes were not independently opened.

### 3. Salerno/Monte Cassino textual network

- The replayed opening of Constantine's `De melancholia`, printed pp.280–281, distinguishes hypochondriac/stomach and brain-centered species, names fear and sadness, defines sadness through loss of a loved thing, and defines fear through expectation of harm. Other faculties, regimen, humor, and therapy material was not retained as a claim without page-specific evidence.
- Ben Yahia compared Constantine's Latin with Ishaq ibn Imran's Arabic `Kitab al-Malikhuliya` and argued for the Arabic origin. Pormann's later synthesis qualifies the relation: Constantine follows Ishaq fairly faithfully through part of Book II and then diverges, including therapeutic material not found in Ishaq. Therefore use “translation/adaptation” or “largely derived from,” not “independent invention” and not “word-for-word translation.”
- The opened primary object is a 1536 printed witness to a late-eleventh-century Latin work. The edition gap is about 450 years. It establishes the witness's wording, not exact original wording, circulation scale, bedside practice, or patient reception.

## Contradictions and qualifications retained

1. **Ben Yahia versus later transmission refinement:** Ben Yahia's strong “translation” conclusion is qualified by Pormann's report of Garbers: substantial dependence followed by divergence. Approved research wording is “Latin translation/adaptation largely derived from Ishaq, with divergence in part of Book II.”
2. **Bethlem foundation versus specialization:** a 1247 institution date does not equal a 1247 psychiatric function. Later evidence must not be back-projected.
3. **First-person versus transparent voice:** Hildegard's authored declaration is more direct than the Benedict cases, but it is still literary, theological, transmitted, and institutionally validated. “Direct” does not mean unmediated in every respect.
4. **Legal protection versus coercive power:** household sustenance and preserved residue appear in the law's wording, while the same text places people/property under royal custody and extracts profits from one category. Normative protections do not prove humane implementation.

## Immediate outline transitions this packet can support

1. **From categories to situations:** open with the same person/event being read through movement, pain, fear, grief, spirit, reason, property, and sacred meaning.
2. **Whose words survive?** contrast Hildegard's first-person declaration with Matilda's questioned/translated report and Hermer's child-status mediation.
3. **Care was a route, not a sector:** use Hermer's father to show sequential movement among paid physicians, poverty constraints, family action, and a shrine—without claiming efficacy.
4. **Translation changes ownership:** trace Ishaq → Constantine → later Latin witness and show why “Western invention” erases Arabic authorship and textual adaptation.
5. **Law sees property:** the Prerogativa provisions are best used to explain jurisdiction and archive production, followed immediately by the missing landless majority.
6. **A date is not a function:** Bethlem's 1247 foundation belongs in an institutional-myth sidebar, not as proof of a psychiatric first.
