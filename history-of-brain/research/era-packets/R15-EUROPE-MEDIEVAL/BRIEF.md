# R15-EUROPE-MEDIEVAL — Family Care, Law, Charity, Religion, Medicine, and Coercion

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `024000–024999`

## Research question

What can a bounded set of European household, legal, medical, religious, charitable,
institutional, literary, and material sources from approximately 800 to 1350 establish about
emotion, cognition, troubling conduct, disability, illness, family/community care, support,
confinement, and coercion?

## Boundary

Choose no more than three named local/regional settings and a small primary corpus. Latin,
Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, and vernacular materials enter only with named language/community
contexts. Medicine, canon/civil law, household accounts, monasteries, shrines, hospitals,
miracle narratives, and literature remain separate archives. Stop before the early-modern
print/anatomy/melancholy packet.

## Required evidence

- critical primary texts/translations, legal/administrative records, letters, accounts,
  archaeology, objects, or institutional documents with exact locators;
- specialist medical, legal, disability, emotion, family, gender, labor, religious, literary,
  institutional, and social history;
- historical terms and translation disputes without “madness” as a universal category or
  modern diagnosis;
- law/ideal/prescription versus implementation, and elite record versus household/non-elite
  experience;
- family labor, charity, medicine, ritual, pilgrimage/shrine, confinement, punishment, support,
  and community obligation without a superstition-to-science ladder;
- women, children, poor people, disabled people, servants, enslaved/dependent people, workers,
  affected persons, cost, coercion, consent, refusal, and outcome;
- Jewish, Christian, Muslim, medical, local, and philosophical coexistence/contact only where
  named evidence supports it; and
- audits of “medieval people thought mental illness was possession,” Great Confinement before
  asylums, first psychiatric hospital, universal family abandonment, and Church-versus-medicine
  claims.

## Stop

Do not cover all medieval Europe, make England/France the continent, or project early-modern
witchcraft/asylum narratives backward. Create five outputs and rights-safe ignored cache; no
public prose.
