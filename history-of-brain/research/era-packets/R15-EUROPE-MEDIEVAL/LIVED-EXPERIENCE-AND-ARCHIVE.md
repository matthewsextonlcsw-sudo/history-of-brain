# R15-EUROPE-MEDIEVAL lived experience and archive audit

**Rule:** a record of someone is not automatically their voice. A cure formula is not automatically their outcome. A provision for maintenance is not automatically implemented care.

## Person-by-person dignity and archive notes

### Matilda

**Documented:** Benedict records restraint, striking behavior, risk to a child, bodily/mind suffering language, a dream/vision, questioning, an account involving bereavement and infant death, and a cure/penitence closure.

**Not documented:** whether she consented to travel, restraint, shrine exposure, questioning, or onward pilgrimage; who brought her; how translators handled her words; what support she had; how she understood every event outside Benedict's selected report; whether the narrated cure endured; whether any secular or medical care was attempted.

**Power conditions:**

- adult woman before male monastic recorders;
- pilgrim from another language region;
- questioned after restraint and a sacred cure event;
- her account is made to serve Becket's cult and penitential closure;
- the record's term `muliercula` carries status/gender inflection that should not be reproduced as a neutral English label.

**Publication safeguards:**

- Do not use the infant death as a sensational hook.
- Do not diagnose her or state possession as fact.
- Do not infer satisfaction from `sana et laeta`; name it as Benedict's closing representation.
- Use only the minimum detail necessary to discuss voice, restraint, grief, and recorder power.
- A public writer should consult a Latin specialist before quoting a new translation.

### Hermer

**Documented:** a recorder's description of a seven-year-old's gait, movement, head rotation, appearance, limited speech, father's physician-seeking, an unaffordable fee, poverty, relic water, and narrated recovery.

**Not documented:** Hermer's own understanding, assent, fear, discomfort, relationship with his father, physician examinations or proposed treatment, household resources, sustained outcome, or whether the described signs were continuous.

**Power conditions:**

- child represented by father, physicians, witnesses, and clerical recorder;
- economic constraint directly affects the route of care;
- cure narrative rewards a move from paid medicine to sacred remedy;
- the father's activity is visible, while maternal and other household roles are absent.

**Publication safeguards:**

- Say the father “sought” routes of help; do not celebrate a treatment result as proven.
- Use the case to show cost and sequential care, not to shame physicians or romanticize poverty.
- Do not infer abandonment merely because only the father is named.

### Hildegard

**Documented:** an authored description of visions, wakefulness/attention/`pura mente`, bodily illness, long silence, reluctance, the effect of men's words, collaborators, and writing.

**Not documented by the selected declaration:** a modern neurological mechanism, a stable medical diagnosis, every collaborator's editorial contribution, an autograph wording, or how other women in the region experienced vision or illness.

**Power conditions:**

- elite religious woman with unusual but constrained authority;
- self-presentation uses humility and divine command within an institution that needed to validate revelation;
- later male scholars and clinicians repeatedly recategorized the account through modern science;
- her first-person status is stronger than the miracle cases but does not remove genre and transmission.

**Publication safeguards:**

- Center her terms before later diagnoses.
- If migraine reception is discussed, make Singer and the citation chain the subject—not Hildegard as a diagnostic specimen.
- Do not oppose “religious” to “real” experience.

## Required dimension audit

### Family and household

| evidence | what can be said | what remains Unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Hermer's father | Actively consults several physicians and obtains shrine remedy after a cost barrier | Quality of relationship, other carers, burden, consent, outcome |
| Matilda's brother/lover/infant in her reported account | A kin/relationship event appears in Benedict's causal sequence | Who accompanied her, current household, voluntary travel, legal facts, durable support |
| Trenery corpus | Some records name parents, friends, and companions | Frequency of support, neglect, conflict, abandonment, gendered labor |
| `Prerogativa Regis` `familia` | Normative text says person and household should be sustained from land profits in one category | Who counted as household, implementation, distribution, abuse, recipient preference |
| Andrew le Merk order | Calendar says proposed custodian should administer necessities to Andrew, wife, and household if the justices found the stated condition | Whether examination, custody, support, or household arrangement occurred; wife/household voices and consent |

**Verdict:** the “families abandoned everyone” myth is untenable as a universal claim. A positive prevalence claim about family care is also unsupported.

### Disability and historical categories

- `mente captus`, `amentia`, `insania`, `furor`, `plena daemonio`, `non compos mentis`, `fatuorum naturalium`, `phrenesis`, `melancholia`, `timor`, and `tristitia` are not interchangeable.
- The same English word “madness” can hide bodily, legal, sacred, moral, and cognitive distinctions.
- `Prerogativa Regis` categories perform a property-jurisdiction task; they must not be back-translated into modern intellectual-disability or psychiatric diagnoses.
- Movement, speech difference, pain, visionary experience, grief, and legal incapacity should not be automatically psychiatricized.

### Status and poverty

- Hermer's case directly records an unaffordable medical fee and poverty changing the care route.
- The royal-law archive overrepresents landholders because land/profit/inheritance create the jurisdiction and record.
- Constantine's learned text shows literate medical theory, not access among poor or rural people.
- Hildegard's account is produced from an elite monastic position that most medieval women did not share.
- Bethlem public-history language about “poor” clients cannot be safely back-projected into c.1247–1350 patient experience without primary records.

### Gender

- Matilda's gender and foreign-language status shape the recorder relationship; her story is preserved by male clerics.
- Hildegard authors her own account but repeatedly frames fear, humility, illness, and male social judgment within authorization processes.
- Buhrer's legal-history analysis warns that women appear in property archives under particular inheritance and control pressures; this packet does not open an individual female inquisition before 1350.
- The 1285 order names Andrew's wife only through a maintenance formula; her name, wishes, labor, property interest, and later circumstances are not recorded in the opened entry.
- Hermer's father is visible while women carers are not; absence is not proof they were uninvolved.
- No gender-prevalence claim is supported.

### Labor, servitude, and dependency

- No enslaved person is identifiable in the selected core corpus. No conclusion about enslaved people's mental lives, disability, care, coercion, or access is permitted.
- The legal term `familia` should be rendered cautiously as household, not automatically as a modern nuclear family; servants and other dependents may be included, but the opened sections do not enumerate roles. A legal-Latin specialist must review any public translation.
- Hermer's record makes physicians' paid labor visible only through the father's search and one demanded fee; it does not name the practitioners, their training, work conditions, or proposed treatment.
- Monks, translators, scribes, printers, witnesses, and carers perform the labor that makes these archives possible, while that labor is rarely described from the worker's perspective.
- No selected record permits a prevalence claim about servants, dependent workers, or unpaid household care labor.

### Coercion and restraint

- Matilda is explicitly `alligata` for about four or five hours. The record provides no consent process and presents restraint inside a cure narrative.
- Trenery's table includes other bound/chained cases, but only selected underlying cases were replayed; do not quantify.
- Royal custody over persons/property is coercive legal power even where the text also states maintenance protections.
- The Andrew order makes custody of body and lands conditional on examination, but neither the examination result nor the experience of custody survives in the opened entry; do not soften the power relation or invent harm.
- The Constantine text is prescriptive; whether recipients accepted or refused treatment is absent.
- Bethlem restraint/regime details on the heritage page belong mostly to later periods and must not be imported into this packet's c.1350 boundary.

### Access and care routes

- The only directly recorded care-access sequence is Hermer: father → several physicians → one possible but unaffordable physician → poverty → shrine/relic route.
- This sequence shows that routes could be plural and constrained. It does not prove that physicians and shrines were normally combined or that one was more effective.
- Constantine's translation setting demonstrates availability of learned Arabic-derived medicine to some Latin readers, not general access to care.
- The normative law and the 1285 Andrew order demonstrate Crown interest in examination, property, custody, and household maintenance. The order is an administrative action, not proof of a clinical service or of implementation.
- The Bethlem 1247 foundation cannot be assumed to provide psychiatric access within the packet period.

## Archive-production audit

### Miracle records

**Why created:** to record and authenticate saintly miracles, organize cult memory, and demonstrate Becket's efficacy.

**Selection effects:** successful cure closure, unusual behavior, pilgrimage, witnesses, relics, and the shrine are more recordable than chronic ambiguity, refusal, failed cure, ordinary household coping, or post-visit deterioration.

**What the archive may systematically miss:** people who never traveled, could not afford travel, refused sacred framing, were managed without a dramatic event, did not recover, or lacked intermediaries who brought them to clerical notice.

### Visionary theological text

**Why created:** to transmit revelation and theological interpretation under an authorizing command.

**Selection effects:** the declaration explains credibility, humility, wakefulness, and orthodoxy. It is not a symptom diary or clinical interview.

**What it may miss:** ordinary intervals, collaborators' exact drafting work, experiences not useful to validation, and how non-elite visionaries were treated.

### Learned medical text

**Why created/transmitted:** theoretical explanation, classification, regimen, and therapy; also cross-lingual scholarly transmission.

**Selection effects:** learned authorities and textual categories dominate; everyday negotiation, local practice, treatment failure, cost, disability identity, and recipient speech are mostly absent.

**What it may miss:** women healers, vernacular knowledge, household adaptations, refusal, and the gap between prescription and practice.

### Royal property law

**Why created:** define and administer Crown rights, land, profits, heirs, and wardship/custody.

**Selection effects:** landed wealth produces visibility. Categories are shaped by legal capacity and inheritance risk.

**What it may miss:** landless people, informal support, domestic violence, recipient preferences, medical treatment, and outcomes unless they affect jurisdiction/property.

**Action-record distinction:** the 1285 Close Roll calendar adds one concrete order beyond doctrine. It still stops before the examination result, executed custody, material support, recipient or family response, and durable outcome. One order cannot establish routine practice.

### Hospital heritage/archive catalogue

**Why created:** current public history and current holdings description.

**Selection effects:** retrospective institutional identity can pull later psychiatric function backward toward a foundation date; catalogued surviving series reflect later administration.

**What it cannot prove:** that psychiatric specialization existed in 1247, that no earlier records survive elsewhere, or that Bethlem was a priority “first.”

## Empathy and public-language gates

Before any case becomes public prose, a writer/reviewer must confirm:

1. The person is introduced before the spectacle-producing detail.
2. The recorder, genre, translation, and power relation are visible.
3. Historical terms are translated carefully and not converted into a modern diagnosis.
4. Restraint, poverty, legal custody, and medical access are described plainly without moral softening.
5. Claimed cures and benefits are separated from observed outcomes and recipient reports.
6. Missing consent is labeled Unknown, not silently assumed.
7. One case is never made typical without population evidence.
8. Sacred interpretations are neither mocked nor treated as clinical proof.
9. Violence involving children is included only when necessary and never as clickbait.
10. The archive's missing poor, landless, women, workers, and non-success stories are named.
