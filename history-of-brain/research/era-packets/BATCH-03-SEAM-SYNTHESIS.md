## Chronological assembly decisions

### 1. Translation is work, not a civilizational relay race

`R09-TRANSLATION-NETWORKS` supports two bounded textual chains: selected material from Galen's
*Commentary on Hippocrates' Epidemics VI* and Aristotle's *De anima*. Those chains expose named
languages, versions, editors, translators, patrons, manuscripts, and consequential textual
differences.

They do not support “the House of Wisdom translated Greek science,” “Arabs preserved Greek
knowledge for the West,” or a single-genius preservation story as neutral historical summaries.
The internal outline must separate:

1. a source work;
2. a particular Greek witness or edition;
3. a Syriac version or reported Syriac intermediary;
4. an Arabic version or recension;
5. manuscript survival and modern editorial reconstruction;
6. patronage and institutional claims; and
7. later European or modern reception.

### 2. Soul, intellect, ethics, medicine, and patient experience are different archives

`R10-SOUL-INTELLECT-ETHICS` is an archive of argument, translation, and ethical instruction.
It can support bounded nodes on intellect, faculty sequences, imagination, bodily localization,
prophecy, and habituation in selected works. It is not evidence of a regional consensus,
ordinary practice, or patient experience.

`R10-MEDICINE-AFFECT-DISTRESS` uses different genres: medical and ethical prescriptions,
reported interventions, later biographies, and literary anecdotes. These packets may meet at
named passages and authors, but philosophical vocabulary cannot silently prove clinical care,
and a clinical anecdote cannot prove a theory's population-wide use.

### 3. A historical medical category is not a current diagnosis

The reviewed evidence does not authorize converting *mālīkhūliyā*, love-sickness, mania,
disturbance, fear, anger, or another translated category into depression, bipolar disorder,
psychosis, OCD, psychosomatic illness, or a modern case formulation. An apparent resemblance
may become a clearly labeled comparison question only after the historical term, source layer,
genre, and current clinical evidence are separately established.

The famous Ibn Sīnā love-diagnosis story is a reception chain with chronology and literary
problems, not a transparent case record. The al-Balkhī/Badri chain documents a modern CBT
framing; it does not establish that a medieval author invented CBT.

### 4. Institutions must be reconstructed claim by claim

`R11-BIMARISTANS-INSTITUTIONS` supports two named institutional evidence stacks: al-Nūrī in
Damascus and al-Manṣūrī/Qalāwūn in Cairo. A building, foundation inscription, endowment norm,
appointment, travel description, later chronicle, narrated case, and current heritage page each
answer different questions.

The outline may not infer a staffed mental-health ward from a room label, psychiatric
specialization from a troubling-state anecdote, everyday implementation from a waqf ideal, or
outcome from the existence of an institution. Claims about the first hospital, first psychiatric
hospital, universal humane care, occupational therapy, medieval music wards, and generalized
effectiveness remain rejected or held.

### 5. Most care did not wait for a hospital archive

`R11-HOUSEHOLDS-LAW-SHRINES` preserves a bounded counterweight through Fustat Geniza letters,
selected Hanafi legal doctrine, and Coptic documents connected to Apa Phoibammon. These sources
show relational burden, material support, liability categories, donation/service arrangements,
and potential dependence outside the named bimaristans.

They do not establish universal family tolerance, actual implementation of a legal rule, the
reason a reply does not survive, excluded shrine access, or one stable boundary among religion,
medicine, law, and household care.

### 6. A textual genealogy is not automatically a travel itinerary

`R12-BUDDHIST-ROUTES` supports three bounded chains: CKM 77 near Khotan, the Bower/Yaśomitra
bundle acquired at Kucha, and selected Sanskrit–Chinese–Tibetan *Bhaiṣajyavastu* witnesses.
Manuscript language, textual relationship, reported find location, purchase location, proposed
date, present custody, and translated passage must remain separate.

Similarity or textual affiliation does not by itself establish the physical route traveled by
a manuscript, person, treatment, or idea. The packet does not authorize “Buddhism invented
psychology, therapy, mindfulness, or neuroscience,” or a simple eastward-diffusion story.

## Cross-packet findings that survive the seam audit

### Translators, compilers, editors, and modern interpreters change the evidence surface

Translation is not a transparent pipe. Word choice, omission, expansion, recension, reordering,
commentary, manuscript loss, editorial reconstruction, and modern extraction can change what a
reader thinks a source says about mind, soul, brain, emotion, or care.

Every outline node that crosses languages must name the consulted version and state whether the
project opened the source language, a historical translation, a modern translation, a
specialist paraphrase, or a derivative teaching extract.

### Priority labels repeatedly collapse different achievements

“First psychologist,” “first cognitive scientist,” “first psychotherapy,” “first CBT,” “first
music therapy,” “first hospital,” and “first psychiatric hospital” merge authorship, analogy,
institutional foundation, treatment description, professional identity, effectiveness, and
modern disciplinary recognition. The reviewed citation chains did not establish those bundled
claims.

Priority language may appear only in a dated myth/reception audit that identifies who made the
modern claim, what narrower historical evidence exists, and which part remains unknown.

### Prescription, narration, and implementation must remain separate

Across medical books, ethical works, legal texts, waqf norms, monastic rules, institutional
histories, and care passages, a source can establish what was prescribed, narrated, praised,
forbidden, funded, or translated. It does not automatically establish frequency, access,
consent, delivery, outcome, or typical experience.

This distinction is a permanent outline field, not a one-time disclaimer.

### Care and coercion can occupy the same record

The batch contains represented food, rounds, treatment, conversation, bodily intervention,
household support, legal dependency, institutional chains, shrine service, and divided care
labor. It also exposes restraint, status hierarchy, coercive intervention, dependence, missing
consent, and adverse outcome. “Humane for its time” is not an acceptable substitute for asking
whose agency and outcome the record preserves.

### Direct voice remains rare and uneven

Translation records foreground elite intellectual labor. Philosophical texts preserve argument,
not patients. Medical cases and biographies mediate sufferers through authors and retellers.
Institutional sources privilege founders, administrators, travelers, jurists, and chroniclers.
Geniza letters and Coptic documents bring social relationships closer, but they still require
speaker, recorder, translation, survival, and missing-reply controls.

The outline must describe the best surviving voice type in each node and cannot relabel a
recipient, legal subject, literary character, or named beneficiary as a patient memoir.

### Archive and rights conditions are historical facts

The packet's knowledge depends on acquisition, excavation claims, colonial collecting,
manuscript dispersal, current custody, war and conservation conditions, digitization,
translation rights, unstable links, and restricted modern scholarship. Those conditions belong
beside the claim they constrain.

## Exchanges that are demonstrated—and those that are not

Batch 03 improves the project's exchange standard. It demonstrates selected textual
relationships only where named witnesses, versions, translation reports, or specialist textual
comparison support them. It does not prove that every associated doctrine, treatment, or
institution traveled along the same path.

The internal outline may now distinguish:

- **documented textual relationship** — a bounded source/version chain;
- **reported intermediary** — a historical or scholarly attribution requiring qualification;
- **textual genealogy** — witness relationships without a known physical route;
- **contact or custody point** — where an object was acquired, recorded, or held;
- **parallel comparison** — resemblance without demonstrated transmission; and
- **modern bridge story** — a reception claim requiring its own dated evidence.

No broader Greece-to-Baghdad-to-Europe or India-to-Central-Asia-to-China master relay has passed
the evidence gate.

### Institutions, law, and practice

  and

  claims remain held or rejected.

### Lived experience, harm, and access

- no packet supports treating its mediated cases as unfiltered patient-authored clinical
  testimony;
- consent, refusal, treatment result, cost, discharge, exclusion, restraint, adverse outcome,
  family burden, worker experience, and long-term dependence remain uneven;
- women, children, poor people, enslaved/dependent people, disabled people, rural communities,
  workers, and non-elites remain underrepresented; and
- self-harm, coercion, disability, possession, legal incapacity, and adverse treatment require

### Region, community, custody, and rights

- six packets do not constitute complete Islamicate, Buddhist, Asian, African, European, or
  medieval coverage;
- manuscript acquisition and current custody require provenance and community review;
- sacred text, heritage, human subject, image, and excerpt use may require protocol beyond
  copyright; and
- regional specialists and relevant language/community reviewers remain publication gates.

## Source-quality lessons from Batch 03

1. **A full text can contradict a famous anecdote.** The Ibn Sīnā/Qābūs chronology changed once
   the opened source's contrary statement was carried into the graph.
2. **One omitted sentence can change the ethical picture.** The Isḥāq record required an
   overlooked bodily intervention while outcome and consent stayed unknown.
3. **A building claim is not a treatment claim.** Institutional evidence had to be decomposed
   into foundation, funding, staffing, ward, admission, care, and outcome propositions.
4. **Negative search memos are low-level evidence.** A heuristic search cannot prove that a
   reply, implementation record, practice, or competing source never existed.
5. **Textual dependence is not physical movement.** The Buddhist route graph improved only after
   witness genealogy, find report, acquisition, custody, and travel were separated.
6. **Canonical fields prevent narrative drift.** Per-record schemas exposed grouped ratings,
   hidden source layers, invalid universality values, unused evidence, and malformed
   relationships.
7. **Independent replay earned its cost.** It corrected locators, author/editor roles, source
   types, priority wording, timestamps, chronology, rights, and claim direction across every
   packet.
