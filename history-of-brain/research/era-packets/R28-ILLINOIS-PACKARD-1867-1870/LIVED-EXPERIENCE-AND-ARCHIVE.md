# R28 Illinois Packard counterpublic — lived experience and archive audit

## Voice map

| Layer | What survives | Directness | Power and mediation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elizabeth Packard | Self-authored narrative, prefaces, notes, campaign claims | Direct for her words and publication choices | Also publisher-editor who selects and frames others |
| Sophia Olsen | Separately titled first-person narrative | Direct for authored wording | Packard's preceding note supplies a motive/explanation Olsen withheld |
| Minard, Shedd, Yates, Lake | Separately headed testimony | Attributed/partly reported | Collected and published by Packard; upstream elicitation/manuscripts not replayed |
| Other patients | Named and unnamed reports inside narratives and committee report | Often hearsay or represented speech | High risk of spectacle; not available for diagnosis or SEO case cards |
| Families | Husband/relative traces reported by Packard, contributors, or officials | Mediated | No bounded family-authored production/motive account opened |
| Workers | Committee summaries and reported observations | Elicited institutional evidence | Rank, employment dependency, observation, and committee selection matter |
| Legislative committee | Mandate, procedure, credibility decisions, recommendations | Direct institutional voice | Could compel, classify, select, and recommend; not equal power with witnesses |
| Legislature | Statute | Direct legal authority | Law on paper does not reveal implementation or lived effect |
| Board of Public Charities | Later policy/institutional synthesis | Direct official position | State oversight framing, not patient voice or neutral outcome study |

## Lived-experience ceiling

The packet can recover what named women chose or were represented as saying in a public reform
volume. It cannot turn every event claim into fact, every contributor into an independent
witness, or Packard's editorial additions into Olsen's or a family's interior experience. The
most historically important fact is not merely that women “spoke,” but that their words moved
through authorship, collection, print, testimony, legal competency, state selection, and later
public memory.

## Harm and dignity rules

- Attribute historical clinical, moral, religious, marital, and credibility labels.
- Do not settle modern diagnosis or capacity for Packard, contributors, relatives, patients, or
  workers.
- Minimize names and intimate allegations involving people not necessary to the source-form
  argument. No roster or event-by-event abuse gallery.
- Do not use reported suicide, violence, bodily treatment, family conflict, or institutional
  suffering as a hook.
- Public-domain law does not erase privacy, dignity, survivor, or descendant-risk review.
- No facsimile, signature, portrait, long quotation, or searchable case card before human
  editorial and rights/harm review.

## Missing voices

No opened source supplies an unmediated family account of why a contributor wrote, how a family
received the publication, or how the law was experienced. The printed committee report does not
contain its entire several-hundred-page testimony journal. People who did not publish, testify,
or survive official selection are not represented. The corpus cannot answer prevalence,
implementation, treatment effect, or the experience of institutionalized people generally.
