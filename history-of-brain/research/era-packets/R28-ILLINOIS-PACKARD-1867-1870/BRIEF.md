# R28 Illinois — Packard Counterpublic, 1867–1870

**Status:** GO for bounded internal research; no public prose

**Place and dates:** Illinois, 1867–1870. Earlier confinement enters only where the frozen public
records explicitly require it.

**Question:** How did Elizabeth Packard assemble her own writing, Sophia Olsen's narrative,
fellow-prisoner testimony, an official investigation, and legislation into a rights
counterpublic—and how did Packard's editorial authority shape the other women's voices?

## Frozen corpus

- Complete 1868 *The Prisoner's Hidden Life, or Insane Asylums Unveiled*, including separately
  paginated Sophia Olsen narrative, testimony by named coadjutors, and Packard's editorial notes.
- Complete Illinois 1867 session laws, especially the Personal Liberty Act, printed pp. 139–140.
- Complete official *Report of the Investigating Committee on the Hospital for the Insane*,
  dated Dec. 1, 1867 and printed 1869.
- Complete 1869–70 Board of Public Charities report only where it directly addresses the law or
  institution.
- Open specialist/legal scholarship for source mapping, dependence, reception, and context.

## Required controls and stops

Separate Packard's direct public authorship, Olsen's separately authored narrative within
Packard's editorial frame, collected testimony, state investigation, legislation, and later
interpretation. Do not count voices published in the same volume as independent merely because
they have different names. Law establishes design, not implementation; an investigation is a
state evidence apparatus, not neutral truth.

Test rather than repeat claims that Packard alone caused reform, the statute immediately
protected everyone, coadjutors are unmediated, the committee resolved truth, or Packard was the
first survivor activist. Do not adjudicate diagnoses or intimate allegations, generalize to all
institutionalized women, or turn named people into sensational case cards. Require disability/
survivor, legal-history, privacy/dignity, family/descendant-risk, and human editorial review.
No images or long quotations before object-specific rights and harm review.
