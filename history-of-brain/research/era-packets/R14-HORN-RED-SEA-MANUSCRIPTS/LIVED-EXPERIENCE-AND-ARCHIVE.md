## Voice ladder

1. **No direct care-recipient testimony was recovered.** No source in the defined packet is
   a first-person account elicited from an ill or distressed person about a care encounter.
2. **A named first-person prayer is still textual and mediated.** It creates a human trace
   without becoming an interview or outcome report.
3. **Community report is closer than institutional rubric but not transparent.** The
   *gädl* preserves selected community memory through a male clerical compiler and a
   disputed modern translation.
4. **Prescriptive roles are not experiences.** A priest, confessor, penitent, patient, or
   owner named by a text is not automatically a documented historical encounter.
5. **Catalog records are custody evidence.** They can identify folios, dates, scripts,
   holdings, owners, and scribes. They rarely show why a text mattered to a historical user.

## Gender, rank, labor, and access

### Gender

- Wälättä P̣eṭros and women in her community make the *gädl* the packet's strongest
  woman-centered archive, but elite/saintly/monastic selection and male compilation remain.
- Śāhla Rufāʾēl is named through a manuscript prayer, not through a biography or direct
  testimony.
- The anointing and confessional texts assign formal authority to male clerical roles as
  represented by the consulted scholarship. Do not extrapolate all household or informal
  caregiving from those prescriptions.

### Rank and coercion

- Wion's reading places *Maṣḥafa bāḥry* inside royal and ecclesiastical normalization.
  The same archive that describes care also describes authority to label, punish, exile,
  imprison, and deprive land rights.
- No accused, punished, converted, ill, or dissenting person's words were recovered from
  the upstream enforcement chain.
- A care/control binary would still be too simple: sacrament, political consolidation,
  pastoral intention, inter-church relations, and coercive power can coexist in one archive.

### Labor and custody

- Named labor appears most clearly through Gälawdewos as compiler, Muḥammad b. Idrīs
  b. Yūsuf as scribe, modern translators/catalogers, and institutional digitization teams.
- Credit cataloged custody/repository information precisely: Addis Ababa University IES is
  recorded for IES 00782 and IES00272; Marṭula Māryām Monastery is the cataloged repository
  for EMDA 00028; Masāqo Śellāsē Church is the cataloged repository for EMML 2845. Digital
  hosts are not owners of every original.
- Current status is explicitly unknown for EMDA 00028 and EMML 2845. Cataloged repository
  must not become a confirmed current holder, and “digitized” must not imply secure local
  custody, public ownership, or permission to reproduce.

### Access

- Formal ritual texts do not state who actually received rites, what they cost, whether a
  person could refuse, or what alternatives households used.
- No caste-equivalent, class, disability, poverty, age-distribution, rural/urban, or
  population-access analysis is possible from this sample.
- Registered or order-a-copy access affects what the present researcher could verify;
  restricted visibility must remain a gap, not be filled from catalog English.

## Empathy rules for later writers

- Lead with the represented person and source situation, not with an exoticized rite.
- Say “the text prescribes,” “the prayer asks,” “the community biography represents,” or
  “the catalog records” before adding interpretation.
- Do not narrate terror, relief, pain, shame, hope, consent, or coercion unless a source
  directly records it and the genre/translation are identified.
- Do not use medieval patient/client language for these settings. Name “represented sick
  person,” “penitent,” “saint,” “owner,” or “community member” when those roles are actually
  evidenced.
- Treat miracle and protective claims respectfully as historical devotional claims. Neither
  ridicule nor endorse efficacy.
- Avoid “magic versus medicine.” Where historical actors or modern catalogers use magic,
  sorcery, medicine, or spiritual language, attribute the category and its power context.

## Community and cultural protocol

### Ethiopian Orthodox materials

Before public quotation, translation, ritual description, or image use, obtain review from:

- an independent Gəʿəz philologist;
- an Ethiopian Orthodox scholar or designated community reviewer;
- the holding institution/partner where image or manuscript-specific permission is needed;
- affected-community reviewers for Wälättä P̣eṭros, especially on contested passages.

### Ethiopian Muslim / Harar materials

IES00272 remains conditional. Before public body prose beyond catalog metadata, obtain:

- Arabic manuscript specialist review;
- Harari/Ethiopian Muslim community or heritage review appropriate to custody and origin;
- acquisition/custody clarification;
- source-level confirmation that the selected passage is appropriate to reproduce or explain.

### Translation dispute

Community review is not a veto substitute for philology, and philology is not a substitute
for cultural and ethical accountability. The packet requires both. Do not ask one reviewer
to stand in for an entire church, language, region, or diaspora.

## Missing voices inventory

- people who received or declined anointing;
- people targeted by royal religious discipline;
- penitents and confessors describing actual encounters;
- ordinary women outside saintly/noble/monastic selection;
- children, elders, disabled people, poor people, servants, enslaved people, and rural
  households;
- Ethiopian Orthodox community custodians speaking about present ethical use;
- Harari and other Ethiopian Muslim knowledge holders describing manuscript custody and
  appropriate public framing;
- Eritrean, Somali, Afar, Djiboutian, Oromo, Yemeni, and wider Red Sea voices excluded by
  this packet's bounded evidence.

## Archive-silence statement for the master outline

> In this defined manuscript sample, institutions and texts speak more clearly than care
> recipients. One prayer names an owner and one sacred biography preserves community
> memory, but neither supplies an unmediated clinical account. The missing testimony is an
> archive condition, not permission to imagine what people felt.

This wording is an internal proposal only and still requires editorial approval.
