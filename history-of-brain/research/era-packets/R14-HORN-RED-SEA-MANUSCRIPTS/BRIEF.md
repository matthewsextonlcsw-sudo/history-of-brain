# R14-HORN-RED-SEA-MANUSCRIPTS — Ethiopian and Harari Texts of Illness, Care, and Protection

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `025000–025999`

## Research question

What can selected Gəʿəz and Arabic manuscript chains from Ethiopian highland and Harari
settings, approximately 1400–1800, establish about represented bodily and spiritual illness,
ritual care, community responses to suffering, manuscript ownership, and textual exchange?

## Frozen settings

1. Ethiopian Orthodox anointing texts: *Maṣḥafa bāḥrey* and *Maṣḥafa qandil*, with named
   witnesses and the 1441/42, 1561, and later-transmission disputes kept separate.
2. *Gädlä Wälättä P̣eṭros*, written in 1672–73, as community-mediated hagiobiography rather
   than autobiography or clinical record.
3. Arabic-to-Gəʿəz *Maṣḥafa faws manfasāwi* transmission, including the later EMML 2845 witness.
4. IES 00272 as a conditional Harar manuscript lane limited to manuscript circulation,
   ownership, and exact opened content; no Harari practice claim from catalog metadata.

## Required evidence and tests

- complete manuscript records, editions, full specialist studies, exact folios/pages,
  colophons, languages, translators, copyists, owners, and custody;
- composition, translation, copying, co-transmission, catalog, and modern-edition dates kept
  distinct;
- prescription, narrated event, miracle, hagiographic representation, and implementation kept
  distinct;
- represented grief, fear, illness, bodily strain, ascetic practice, companionship, leadership,
  priestly care, household ownership, and communal support without diagnosis;
- Coptic-Arabic-to-Gəʿəz translation differences and the handoff to earlier Egyptian and
  Islamicate packets;
- exact audit of royal authorship, 1441/42 conversion, the 1561/Gälawdewos chronology problem,
  Walatta Petros interpretation disputes, and whether IES 00272 actually contains protective or
  healing content; and
- sacred-content, named-owner, image, community, monastery/church, Ethiopian Orthodox, Harari,
  and Ethiopian Muslim authority review.

## Exclusions

Do not represent the whole Horn or Red Sea. Eritrean, Somali, Afar, Djiboutian, Oromo, Yemeni,
and wider Red Sea histories remain gaps. Do not make “magic” the opposite of medicine, infer
practice from manuscript survival, turn hagiographic voice into patient testimony, or absorb
earlier Coptic-Arabic doctrine owned by adjacent packets.

## Stop

Stop after the four settings and approximately 10–12 core sources. Downgrade or remove every
pivotal proposition lacking full text, exact locator, translation accountability, and defensible
provenance. Create five standard outputs and rights-safe ignored cache; no public prose.
