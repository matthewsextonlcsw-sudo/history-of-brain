## Pseudonym, privacy, and descendant-risk controls

- Use only “Annie.” Do not search names, admission indexes, newspapers, genealogical databases, shipping lists, family trees, Scottish asylum records, electoral rolls, or death records to reverse-identify her.
- Do not expose relatives’ names, institutional locations, health descriptions, disability labels, alleged hereditary information, or migration routes merely because secondary scholarship prints them.
- Do not create a searchable clinical roster or family graph.
- Do not infer capacity, consent, coercion, truthfulness, intent, or diagnosis from either a letter or a clinician note.
- Historical age does not eliminate dignity, whānau, or descendant risks.

## Mana whenua and Māori data sovereignty

Seacliff sits on an East Otago coast that a community-governed source identifies as home to Kāti Huirapa ki Puketeraki. This must be named as a relationship and authority context, not decorative geography.

If lawful replay reveals Māori identity, whakapapa, te reo Māori, mātauranga, collective knowledge, iwi or hapū affiliation, or culturally sensitive references:

1. stop extraction immediately;
2. preserve only the minimum access/provenance note needed to explain the stop;

## Treatment, delivery, and outcome controls

The packet contains no image-replayed evidence for treatment, delivery, experience, or outcome. In particular:

- a clinician’s reported diagnosis would be a historical classification, not a current diagnosis;
- a discharge entry would establish administrative status, not recovery, cure, safety, consent, or efficacy;
- a relative’s action would not establish motive or benevolence;
- a letter would establish what Annie wrote in that context, not every event’s independent truth or every resident’s experience;
- silence in the casebook would not establish absence of resistance, care, harm, relationship, or recovery.
