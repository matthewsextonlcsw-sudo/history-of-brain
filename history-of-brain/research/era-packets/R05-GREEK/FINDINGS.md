# R05-GREEK — Findings and evidence extracts

**Purpose:** internal research synthesis for outline/seam review. These are not article sentences.
**Boundary:** selected Greek-speaking Mediterranean contexts, approximately 800 BCE–300 CE;
Roman-imperial synthesis and Galen are handoffs.

## Packet answer in one research proposition

The checked evidence supports **plural, competitive, and overlapping accounts** rather than a
single Greek discovery of mind or mental health. Homeric psychological vocabulary, later
philosophical soul models, competing bodily theories, Hippocratic case writing, a held regimen
lead, and
Asclepian sanctuary narratives performed different jobs. A late-fifth-century medical author
could naturalize a “disease called sacred” and attack some ritual specialists while retaining a
concept of the divine; sanctuary healing expanded during the same broad period. Surviving
“patient” material is largely speech selected, rewritten, or observed by medical and sanctuary
institutions.

That proposition is **Strongly supported** for the selected corpora, not a claim about every
Greek-speaking place, person, or century. Core evidence: Jouanna, pp. 99–110
([full text](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/19/66/80/37/2019668037/2019668037.pdf));
IG IV²,1 121 ([Greek text](https://epigraphy.packhum.org/text/28551)); Barrenechea, pp. 255–273
([full article](https://api.drum.lib.umd.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/685b1a96-5148-4544-869f-94bcc643056b/content));
and the Hippocratic *Epidemics* I/III ([full translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Hippocrates/epidemics.1.i.html)).

## Chronological research transitions for the later outline

These are candidate transitions, not public page titles.

1. **Epic language before a unitary “mind” (roughly eighth–seventh centuries BCE).** Track
   *psychē*, *thymos*, *noos*, *phrenes*, heart terms, breath, and embodied agency as poetic
   vocabularies rather than premature neuroanatomy. Use `EVD-006009–006010`; show the Snell/Gill/
   Cairns dispute rather than a “discovery of the self.”

2. **Archaic/classical arguments over where perception and understanding happen (sixth–fourth
   centuries BCE).** Alcmaeon is available only through later testimony (`EVD-006007–006008`);
   the *Sacred Disease* brain model (`EVD-006005`) and Aristotle's cardiocentrism
   (`EVD-006006`) demonstrate live disagreement. Avoid a relay-race history in which one correct
   brain theory replaces error.

3. **Natural causes as professional and theological argument (late fifth–early fourth centuries
   BCE).** `EVD-006000–006004` supports a tight reading of one treatise: naturalized causation,
   polemic against particular rivals, retained divinity, and competing corpus models. The correct
   transition is not religion → science.

4. **Observation and a held regimen lead (fifth–fourth centuries BCE).** The case-writing archive
   and a normative patient/physician sentence are usable (`EVD-006021–EVD-006023`). Regimen is
   only a research lead (`EVD-006024`) until a named human translation is opened; do not carry its
   English prescription list into the outline.

5. **Philosophical *mania* is not a medical diagnosis (fourth century BCE).** Plato's beneficial
   divine *mania* (`EVD-006011`) belongs to a philosophical/religious discourse. It can
   complicate translations of “madness,” but cannot be mapped onto DSM categories or sanctuary
   patient policy.

6. **Sanctuary expansion alongside medical writing (late fifth–fourth centuries BCE onward).**
   The Epidaurian collection and Athenian votive (`EVD-006012–006019`) show institutional
   narrative, dream incubation, offerings, persuasion, and material devotion. Nissen and
   Barrenechea support interaction and mediation, not replacement.

7. **Hellenistic and Roman handoff.** The packet can flag continued sanctuary/professional
   interaction and material dedications, but imperial authors, Rufus, Soranus, Galen, Aelius
   Aristides, and later philosophical therapies must move to `R05-ROMAN`/later packets. The Melos
   leg (`EVD-006020`) is a continuity marker only.

## Competing interpretations to preserve

### `DIS-006000` — What is *On the Sacred Disease* doing?

- **Position A: naturalizing/rational medical explanation.** The author explicitly supplies
  bodily and environmental causes and rejects named divine causation (`EVD-006000`,
  `EVD-006002–006005`). Jouanna emphasizes a natural/divine universal order.
- **Position B: competitive rhetorical intervention.** The hostile ethnography of rival healers
  and Laskaris's protreptic reading treat the work as a bid for authority, students, and clientele
  (`EVD-006001`, `EVD-006026`).
- **Area of agreement:** the text rejects some ritual specialists' causal/treatment claims and
  advances its own bodily model. It does not reject all divinity or sanctuary piety.
- **Project wording limit:** never “Hippocrates separated medicine from religion.”

### `DIS-006001` — Is Alcmaeon “the first brain theorist”?

- The transmitted Theophrastean report connects senses and brain (`EVD-006007`).
- Dating, exact doctrine, dissection, and wording are reconstructed from later testimonia
  (`EVD-006008`).
- Egyptian and other regional evidence must be compared before any global priority language;
  this packet did not conduct a global predecessor search.
- **Project status:** `Disputed` for a narrowly Greek priority; `Unknown` for global priority.

### `DIS-006002` — Did Homeric people lack a unified self?

- Older “psychic organs” interpretations treated multiple terms as fragmentation.
- Cairns argues their metonymy/personification can represent an agent's integrated embodied
  cognition and affect (`EVD-006009`).
- **Project status:** dispute concerns literary/semantic interpretation, not a diagnosable
  population psychology.

### `DIS-006003` — How much patient experience survives in the iamata?

- Nissen distinguishes more personal isolated dedications from official collections and sees
  reported worshipper experience behind some records (`EVD-006016`).
- Barrenechea independently argues for selection, adaptation, and institutional narrative shaping
  (`EVD-006017`, `EVD-006027`). His source-layer list is an attributed report dependent on
  LiDonnici (`EVD-006015`), not separate corroboration.
- **Project status:** names, origins, conditions, offerings, and represented dream dialogue are
  documented on stone; unmediated speech, belief, cure outcome, and emotional response remain
  unknown.

## Current-evidence boundary

No ancient physiological account here is endorsed as current brain science. The packet does not
diagnose described people with epilepsy, psychosis, depression, anxiety, delirium, or postpartum
conditions. If a later public page compares an ancient description with current evidence, that
requires a separate recent neurological/psychiatric review and wording review; this packet only
establishes historical description and transmission.
