## Disputes that must survive outline compression

- `DIS-006000`: *On the Sacred Disease* as naturalizing medicine versus a simultaneously
  rhetorical, theological, and professional bid for authority. The interpretations can coexist;
  do not collapse the treatise into a secular-revolution slogan.
- `DIS-006001`: Alcmaeon as an early brain-centered thinker versus claims that he “discovered” the
  brain creates mind. The first is a bounded later attribution; the second exceeds the archive.
- `DIS-006002`: Homeric psychic-organ language as evidence of a fragmented/undeveloped self versus
  a flexible embodied vocabulary in which agency remains intact. Do not stage semantic difference
  as primitive deficiency.
- `DIS-006003`: sanctuary cures as preserving visitor-originated experiences versus sanctuary
  personnel actively selecting and shaping them. Possible visitor origins and documented
  institutional form must remain distinct; the surviving inscription is not an unedited patient
  transcript, and LiDonnici's source-layer reconstruction remains unopened here.

## Handoff seams

### Earlier and neighboring histories

- **Egypt, Nubia, Levant, and Mesopotamia:** compare body/heart/brain language, seizure or altered-
  state categories, divine/natural causation, ritual expertise, and therapeutic texts before any
  “Greek first.” Similarity alone is not proof of direct borrowing.
- **South Asia, Persia, and exchange networks:** compare regimen, humoral/systemic reasoning,
  dream/divination practices, and later translation routes only through demonstrable contact and
  source chronology.
- **Southern Italy and western Mediterranean:** Alcmaeon's Croton context should not be flattened
  into Athens or an undifferentiated “Greek world.” Add local mobility and colonial power context.
- **Caria/Asia Minor:** Nissen's regional evidence shows that Greek-language medicine and healing
  cults operated in multilingual, politically changing settings. Coordinate rather than treat the
  region as culturally homogeneous.

### Roman-imperial continuation

- Transfer `SRC-006008` / `EVD-006020` (Tyche's leg dedication, 100–200 CE) as a concrete
  continuity object.
- Roman packet should examine Galen, Rufus of Ephesus, Soranus, Aretaeus, and Aelius Aristides with
  their own source/genre controls—not as a simple culmination of classical Greece.
- Track expansion and adaptation of Asklepieia, medical professions, anatomical debate, dream
  writing, imperial mobility, slavery, gender, and translation.
- Galen's later Hippocrates is also part of the “Hippocrates” reception chain and should not be
  back-projected onto composite classical texts.

### Modern reception and SEO/GEO origin claims

- Route “Father of Medicine,” “birth of rational medicine,” “first neuroscientist,” “Greeks
  discovered the brain,” and “epilepsy stopped being sacred” through `CLM-006006`, `CLM-006016`,
  and the shared-chain audit before any public wording.
- A high-ranking popular page is evidence of present-day reception only. It is not independent
  evidence for antiquity.
- If a concise answer box is later required, pair the common claim with the bounded correction and
  a direct primary-text locator plus specialist source.
