# R08-CHRISTIAN-BYZANTINE gaps and handoff

Status: internal research handoff; not public prose.

## Executive disposition

This packet supports a defensible outline segment for a **300–600 CE, named-source core**, not the full 200–800 CE Christian/Byzantine brief. Its strongest anchors are Evagrius on acedia, Athanasius plus Krueger on hagiographic representation, Augustine on grief, Pachomian rules on care/control, Gregory plus Caner/Horden on Basilias classification, and Justinianic law on charitable administration.

It does **not** yet support a regional synthesis across the eastern Mediterranean, an account of medical continuity, a patient-centered institutional history, a possession/insanity treatment synthesis, or any hospital/psychiatry priority claim.

## Exact exclusions in this pass

- **Time:** 200–300 and 600–800 CE except bibliographic orientation.
- **Languages/traditions:** Syriac, Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopic, and most Coptic; no independent Greek or Latin translation work.
- **Regions:** Syria/Mesopotamia, Armenia/Caucasus, Ethiopia/Aksum, most of Palestine, Balkans, Italy, and western Latin Europe; Augustine is a single named North African comparator.
- **Communities:** Jewish, pagan, and other non-Christian communities were not researched here; coexistence/replacement cannot be inferred.
- **Medical streams:** physicians' texts, materia medica, medical schools, temple or shrine healing continuities, and household remedies were outside the runnable core.
- **Institutional streams:** no excavation report, patient register, staff roster, fiscal account, or verified recipient petition was retained.
- **Later networks:** Islamicate hospitals and translation networks remain excluded by the parent brief.

## Mandatory gaps before public prose

- **Problem:** Full text of Crislip 2005 (`SRC-012012`) and Daly 2007 (`SRC-012013`) was not accessible. Primary Evagrian text alone cannot adjudicate modern depression analogies.
- **Required repair:** Obtain lawful full text; add at least one recent specialist history of emotion/medicine; compare Greek terminology and Evagrian corpus beyond *Praktikos*; record competing positions and translation dependence.

### G2 — Possession, unusual experience, and retrospective diagnosis

- **Problem:** Horden 1993 (`SRC-012014`) remained abstract-only, leaving the packet with primary hagiography and genre theory but no full specialist synthesis of responses.
- **Required repair:** Open the full article and at least one independent specialist study; add legal, medical, shrine, household, and non-hagiographic evidence; distinguish possession, madness-language, seizure, vision, and ritual conflict.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012005` supports representation only; `CLM-012013` records a packet-audit limit.

### G3 — Basil, medicine, and Christian/medical continuity

- **Problem:** Basil *Long Rules* Q55 was not available as an authorized full text. A previously consulted excerpted handout citing Wagner pp. 330–337 now fails TLS hostname verification; the stable replacement is only a WorldCat bibliographic record for the Wagner volume (`SRC-012011`).
- **Required repair:** Open an authorized full edition/translation and Greek critical text; verify locator and wording; compare medical writers and actual practitioners; include non-Christian continuities.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012012` remains unknown. No replacement, rejection, or seamless-integration narrative.

### G4 — Basilias operations and archaeology

- **Problem:** Gregory's oration is rhetorical, Caner is revisionist scholarship, and Horden supplies methodological caution. No excavation or administrative documentation was retained.
- **Required repair:** Open Basil Ep. 94 and 150 in critical translation and Greek; Sozomen HE 6.34.9; Gregory of Nyssa witnesses; archaeological/topographic publications for Caesarea; financing, staff, clientele, and duration evidence.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012007`–`CLM-012008` are usable only as rhetoric plus documented dispute. “First hospital” and “first psychiatric hospital” are prohibited.

### G5 — Pachomian textual transmission and implementation

- **Problem:** The usable English is a web adaptation of a modern translation against Jerome's Latin; the underlying rule passed Egyptian → Greek → Latin → modern English. No implementation archive was paired.
- **Required repair:** Compare Boon 1932 and Veilleux 1981; verify Rules 40–47 in critical editions; search documentary papyri and archaeology for infirmary provisioning and visitor practice.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012010` is a rule-specific prescription, not practice.

### G6 — Justinianic numbering, terminology, and implementation

- **Problem:** Blume numbers the constitution C. 1.3.48 (49), while Scott presents the parallel text as §37 and prints an anomalous date. English institutional labels risk modern assumptions.
- **Required repair:** Cite Blume as base with its 23 Aug. 531 date; compare the Latin critical text and a current legal historian; locate petitions, disputes, accounts, or later enforcement evidence.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012009` is usable as normative law only.

### G7 — Lived experience and institutional labor

- **Problem:** Recipient, worker, poor, enslaved, disabled, child, and non-elite female voices are largely absent.
- **Required repair:** Run a dedicated papyrological, epigraphic, and legal-document search; consult Coptic/Syriac corpora; distinguish direct, dictated, reported, formulaic, and literary voice.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012011` must accompany any care narrative. No patient-satisfaction inference.

### G8 — Regional and religious breadth

- **Problem:** The actual evidence is heavily Greek/Latin, elite, male, monastic, and episcopal.
- **Required repair:** Create separate bounded follow-on packets rather than appending token examples: Syriac/Mesopotamian; Coptic Egyptian; Armenian/Georgian; Ethiopic/Aksumite; Palestinian; Jewish late-antique; pagan/medical continuity; 600–800 transition.
- **Current disposition:** `CLM-012014` blocks empire-wide or takeover claims.

## Claims to reject or quarantine

- **Reject:** “Christians invented hospitals.” The terms, continuities, functions, and priority criteria are not established by this packet.
- **Reject:** “Basil founded the first psychiatric hospital.” No checked source supports psychiatric specialization.
- **Reject:** “Acedia was depression.” The category and modern comparison remain disputed and incompletely researched.
- **Reject:** “Early Christians treated mental illness as demon possession.” Hagiographic representation cannot establish totality, prevalence, or modern diagnostic equivalence.
- **Reject:** “Monasteries cared compassionately for every sick member.” Rules prescribe both support and control; implementation and recipient appraisal are absent.
- **Quarantine:** “Christianity replaced Greek medicine.” The necessary medical and comparative evidence was not researched.
- **Quarantine:** “Byzantine hospitals were medicalized.” Definition, chronology, and institutional variation require a separate research stream.

## Cross-packet handoffs
