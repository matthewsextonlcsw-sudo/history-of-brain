# R08-CHRISTIAN-BYZANTINE — Inner Lives, Ascetic Practice, Charity, and Institutions

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `012000–012999`

## Research question

How did named late-antique Christian and Byzantine communities, writers, ascetics, households,
healers, charities, laws, and institutions describe and respond to emotion, suffering, unusual
experience, bodily states, sin, illness, care, and social obligation?

## Boundary

Approximately 200–800 CE in named eastern Mediterranean, North African, Syriac, and Byzantine
contexts. Split further if language or archive breadth becomes unmanageable. Augustine,
Evagrius, monastic rules, saints' lives, hospitals/charity, law, and medical continuities are
different evidence streams.

## Required evidence

- primary texts/critical translations with exact locators and genre;
- institutional, legal, archaeological, medical, household, and charitable evidence;
- *acedia*, temptation, grief, vision, possession, illness, and discipline without modern
  diagnosis;
- stated ideals versus implemented care/control;
- debates over hospitals and the danger of “first psychiatric hospital” claims;
- patient/sufferer, family, worker, poor, enslaved, disabled, and gendered experience where
  evidence permits; and
- Jewish, pagan, medical, philosophical, and regional coexistence without a Christian takeover
  simplification.

## Stop

Do not cover medieval Europe or the Islamicate hospital/translation networks. Create the five
standard outputs, typed relationships, and rights-safe cache; no public prose.
