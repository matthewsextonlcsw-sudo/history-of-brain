# R08-CHRISTIAN-BYZANTINE claim map

Status: proposed, evidence-linked internal claims; none is approved public prose.

“Documented” means documented in the stated text, law, or opened-source audit—not a universal fact about Christians or Byzantium.

## Required claim-record normalization

Fields shared by `CLM-012000`–`CLM-012015`: `created_by` = R08 packet researcher; `created_on` = 2026-08-18; `review_status` = evidence-linked/source-verified within the consulted editions, never wording-verified or approved for publication. Each record states claim text/type, time/geographic/population scope (sometimes combined on one scope line), universality, evidence label/confidence, anachronism and sensitivity, wording limits, and review needs. `current_evidence_note` is embedded in the claim's limits where a modern comparison is at issue; it is not applicable to claims that only describe an ancient source or packet audit. No record is withdrawn.

## Claim records

### `CLM-012000` — The opened corpus contains multiple Christian evidence streams

- **Claim text:** The narrowed 300–600 CE corpus contains distinct ascetic, hagiographic, autobiographical, monastic-regulatory, charitable-rhetorical, and legal ways of representing distress and care; it cannot be collapsed into one “Christian psychology.”
- **Type / scope:** archive and synthesis claim; named opened corpus only; universality = corpus.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012013`, audit of `SRC-012000`–`SRC-012010`.
- **Label / confidence:** documented packet-audit result; high within the corpus.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high if ignored.
- **Wording limits:** do not generalize to all Christians, all eastern Romans, or 200–800 CE.
- **Review:** evidence-linked; regional expansion pending.

### `CLM-012001` — Evagrius classifies and responds to acedia in an ascetic setting

- **Claim text:** In *Praktikos*, Evagrius places acedia among eight tempting thoughts/demons, describes its pressure on a monk's time, attention, social perception, memory, and desire to leave the cell, and prescribes perseverance within an ascetic program.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive; late-fourth-century ascetic manual; a modeled monk; universality = text-specific.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012000`–`EVD-012002`, `SRC-012000`, chs. 6, 12, 28–29.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in this translation; medium-high pending critical translation review.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-012000`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; mental-health analogy.
- **Wording limits:** retain *acedia*; no depression, ADHD, burnout, or diagnostic-prevalence claim.
- **Review:** source-verified; Greek/critical-edition review needed before quotation.

### `CLM-012002` — Acedia/depression equivalence is not established

- **Claim text:** The opened evidence does not establish that Evagrian acedia is equivalent to modern depression; surface similarities cannot bridge differences in category, setting, cause, audience, and remedy.
- **Type / scope:** evidence-limit claim; packet audit.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012001`–`EVD-012002`; full specialist texts unavailable at `SRC-012012`–`SRC-012013`.
- **Label / confidence:** unknown equivalence; high confidence that public equivalence must be withheld.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-012000`.
- **Anachronism:** very high.
- **Wording limits:** “has sometimes been compared with” remains publication-blocked until full dispute scholarship is reviewed.
- **Review:** mandatory specialist follow-up.

### `CLM-012003` — Augustine narrates a layered grief-and-coping sequence

- **Claim text:** In *Confessions* IX.12, Augustine retrospectively describes suppressing tears, communal ritual and company, recurrent inward grief and grief about grieving, an ineffective bath, mitigation after sleep, recollection, and private weeping that brought rest.
- **Type / scope:** lived-experience/literary; one elite male author, late-fourth-century Latin North African comparator; universality = individual narrative.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012007`, `SRC-012003`, IX.12 §§29–33.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in the historical translation; high for the sequence, lower for psychological interpretation.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium-high; grief and retrospective self-presentation.
- **Wording limits:** no stages-of-grief theory, diagnosis, treatment-effect claim, or eastern-population generalization.
- **Review:** source-verified; Latin/critical-edition check before quotation.

### `CLM-012004` — Monica's words are mediated testimony

- **Claim text:** *Confessions* IX.11 preserves Augustine's report of Monica's last illness and words, including burial and altar-remembrance instructions; it is not Monica's direct transcript or unmediated patient testimony.
- **Type / scope:** mediated family/lived-experience evidence; one reported woman.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012006`, `SRC-012003`, IX.11 §§27–28.
- **Label / confidence:** documented as Augustine's report; high.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; illness, death, gendered archive.
- **Wording limits:** write “Augustine reports that Monica said,” never “Monica's testimony proves.”
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-012005` — Antony's demons and cures are hagiographic representations

- **Claim text:** *Life of Antony* represents demonic attack, possession, exorcism, and healing within a sacred narrative explicitly designed to model ascetic discipline; those episodes are not clinical diagnoses or outcome records.
- **Type / scope:** literary and genre claim; one fourth-century vita.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012003`, `EVD-012005`; `SRC-012001` prologue and chs. 38, 63–64, 83–84; `SRC-012002` pp. 43–47.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported genre reading; high.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-012002`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** very high; possession and disability.
- **Wording limits:** no psychosis, epilepsy, dissociation, mass belief, or efficacy inference.
- **Review:** source-verified; possession scholarship gap remains.

### `CLM-012006` — Antony's vita also represents human care networks

- **Claim text:** Alongside demonological explanation, *Life of Antony* depicts an acquaintance bringing bread and carrying an incapacitated Antony to a church where kin and villagers gather, and later depicts two long-term attendants in old age.
- **Type / scope:** descriptive literary; named episodes in one vita.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012004`, `SRC-012001`, chs. 8–9 and 91.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in the vita; high for representation, low for prevalence.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** medium; illness and dependency.
- **Wording limits:** no claim of standard emergency, community, residential, or elder care.
- **Review:** source-verified.

### `CLM-012007` — Gregory's Basilias passage records praise and represented stigma

- **Claim text:** Gregory of Nazianzus's Oration 43.63–64 praises Basil's “new city,” depicts people with leprosy as socially expelled, and presents Basil's embodied care as exemplary charity.
- **Type / scope:** rhetorical/ideological; Cappadocia; one eulogy.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012008`, `SRC-012004`, §§63–64.
- **Label / confidence:** documented in the oration; high for rhetoric, low for operations.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; leprosy, stigma, sacred praise.
- **Wording limits:** do not reproduce dehumanizing imagery without context; no institutional quality or patient-satisfaction claim.
- **Review:** source-verified; Greek/critical-edition review needed before quotation.

### `CLM-012008` — “First hospital” is not a secure Basilias claim

- **Claim text:** The classification and clientele of Basil's charitable complex are disputed: Caner argues for a leprosarium-focused interpretation, while Horden shows why names, doctors, and modern hospital definitions cannot be mapped mechanically. The checked evidence does not justify “first hospital” or “first psychiatric hospital.”
- **Type / scope:** historiographic and myth-audit claim; fourth-century Cappadocia and modern scholarship.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012008`–`EVD-012009`; `SRC-012005`, `SRC-012006`.
- **Label / confidence:** documented dispute; high that the superlatives are unsupported by this corpus.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-012001`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** very high; institutional lineage.
- **Wording limits:** use “charitable complex/facility”; state classification dispute.
- **Review:** source-verified; Basil letters and archaeology remain follow-up.

### `CLM-012009` — Justinianic law recognizes and governs charitable categories

- **Claim text:** C. 1.3.48 (49).3–8 directs an indefinite inheritance for “the poor” toward a city's hospital and ill residents, assigns selection and distribution authority to superintendents, bishops, and clergy, and bars administrators' personal profit.
- **Type / scope:** normative legal; sixth-century imperial compilation; universality = rule, not implementation.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012010`; `SRC-012007`, C. 1.3.48 (49).3–8, Blume PDF pp. 31–32, dated 23 Aug. 531; parallel `SRC-012008`, Scott §37.3–8.
- **Label / confidence:** documented law; high for Blume locator, medium for translated institutional terms.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; law versus practice.
- **Wording limits:** no claim that every city had a hospital or that allocations reached recipients; do not use Scott's numbering without the Blume parallel.
- **Review:** source-verified; legal historian/source-language check desirable.

### `CLM-012010` — Pachomian rules combine accommodation with institutional control

- **Claim text:** Rules 40–47 in the Pachomian tradition prescribe ministers, differentiated provisions, and abundant food for ill monks while restricting self-service, access to spaces and supplies, removal of food, and visits even by kin.
- **Type / scope:** normative monastic rule; Egyptian cenobitic tradition; universality = rule-specific.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012011`–`EVD-012012`, `SRC-012009`, Preface 5 and Rules 40–47; transmission context `SRC-012010`.
- **Label / confidence:** documented prescription; medium-high pending critical-translation comparison.
- **Disagreement:** `DIS-012003`.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; institutional authority and dependency.
- **Wording limits:** no compliance, benevolence, neglect, coercion, or satisfaction claim beyond the text's explicit provisions.
- **Review:** source-verified; Boon/Veilleux comparison required before quotation.

### `CLM-012011` — Recipient voices are largely absent or mediated

- **Claim text:** In the retained corpus, authoritative male authors and institutions dominate; ill, poor, disabled, and allegedly possessed people mainly appear through someone else's narration, while Augustine is the clearest first-person distress narrator and Monica's words remain reported by him.
- **Type / scope:** archive/voice audit; named opened corpus.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012006`–`EVD-012008`, `EVD-012010`–`EVD-012013`.
- **Label / confidence:** documented packet-audit result; high within the corpus.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** very high if absence is concealed.
- **Wording limits:** never invent reaction, consent, fear, gratitude, or dissatisfaction; state mediation at first use.
- **Review:** source-verified within corpus.

### `CLM-012012` — The medical-replacement question remains open in this packet

- **Claim text:** This packet does not establish that Christian care replaced, rejected, or seamlessly absorbed Greco-Roman learned medicine; a key frequently cited Basil passage was not available in a usable full translation, and the medical continuity stream was outside the narrowed executable core.
- **Type / scope:** evidence-limit and handoff claim.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012014`, `SRC-012011` restricted; packet boundary.
- **Label / confidence:** unknown; high confidence that a takeover/rejection synthesis must be withheld.
- **Anachronism:** high.
- **Wording limits:** no “faith versus medicine” binary and no positive coexistence generalization from the present evidence.
- **Review:** requires full Basil Q55, medical texts, practitioners, and non-Christian comparison.

### `CLM-012013` — No checked source establishes a specialized psychiatric hospital

- **Claim text:** No retained source in the 300–600 CE corpus establishes an institution specialized in the modern category of psychiatric care; hagiographic possession episodes, charitable buildings, monastic infirmaries, and legal hospitals are different evidence streams.
- **Type / scope:** packet claim audit; named opened corpus.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012005`, `EVD-012008`–`EVD-012012`; restricted specialist lead `SRC-012014` does not alter the result.
- **Label / confidence:** documented packet-audit result; ancient institutional reality beyond the corpus remains unknown.
- **Anachronism:** very high.
- **Wording limits:** do not convert “not established here” into “none ever existed.”
- **Review:** possession/insanity scholarship and broader legal archive required.

### `CLM-012014` — The packet cannot support empire-wide or transreligious generalization

- **Claim text:** Because major languages, regions, religious communities, centuries, archaeological streams, and non-elite archives remain unresearched, the packet cannot support claims about all eastern Christians, the Byzantine Empire as a whole, or a Christian replacement of Jewish, pagan, and medical traditions.
- **Type / scope:** scope and archive guardrail.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012013` plus the documented packet boundary in `SOURCE-LEDGER.md`.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported methodological limit; high.
- **Anachronism / sensitivity:** high; colonial and confessional flattening.
- **Wording limits:** every synthesis must name time, region, genre, and community.
- **Review:** regional packets required.

### `CLM-012015` — Prescriptive genres cannot be treated as transparent practice

- **Claim text:** Hagiography and monastic rules in this corpus actively form ideals and discipline; their prescriptions and exemplary narratives must be separated from evidence of routine implementation.
- **Type / scope:** genre/method claim; `SRC-012000`–`SRC-012002`, `SRC-012009`.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-012002`–`EVD-012003`, `EVD-012011`–`EVD-012012`; Krueger `SRC-012002` pp. 43–47.
- **Label / confidence:** strongly supported; high.
- **Anachronism:** high if ignored.
- **Wording limits:** use “prescribes,” “praises,” or “narrates,” not automatically “people did.”
- **Review:** source-verified.
