# R09-TRANSLATION-NETWORKS — Greek, Syriac, and Arabic Translation Labor

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `013000–013999`

## Research question

What can named texts, manuscripts, translators, patrons, schools, libraries, and documentary
records establish about Greek–Syriac–Arabic translation between approximately 500 and 1000 CE,
especially where concepts concerning soul, intellect, imagination, emotion, brain, medicine,
and troubling states changed in transit?

## Boundary

Select a small, named corpus and no more than three translation chains. The packet may include
earlier Greek source layers and later manuscript witnesses only to reconstruct those chains.
It must distinguish Greek-to-Syriac, Syriac-to-Arabic, and direct Greek-to-Arabic work; named
Baghdad settings are nodes, not a synonym for all translation.

## Required evidence

- opened source and receiving texts in critical editions or authoritative translations;
- manuscript, colophon, bibliographic, prosopographic, or documentary evidence for translators,
  patrons, languages, and dates;
- specialist Syriac, Greek, Arabic, book-history, and translation-history scholarship;
- exact comparison of selected terms or passages, including uncertainty and textual variants;
- labor, confession/community, patronage, payment, institution, and political context;
- audits of “House of Wisdom translated Greek science,” “West preserved by Arabs,” frictionless
  relay, and single-genius origin stories; and
- explicit gaps where a translation relationship is inferred rather than demonstrated.

## Stop

Do not summarize all the Graeco-Arabic translation movement, write biographies of every major
translator, or use later philosophical reception to prove an early translation. Hand doctrines,
clinical systems, and institutions to their own packets. Create the five standard outputs and
rights-safe ignored cache; no public prose.
