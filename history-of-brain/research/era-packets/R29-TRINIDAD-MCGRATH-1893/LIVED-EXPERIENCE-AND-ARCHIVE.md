## The central archive fact

This packet has an exact archival target but no primary content. A catalogue can point to a
possible affected-person-associated record; it cannot reveal the record's exact voice, production
conditions, material boundaries, institutional reception, or truth. Banton's guide explains an
archival route but does not change that ceiling.

The responsible result is therefore a visible absence: **no lived experience is extracted in
R29**. This is not a claim that experience, family, workers, resistance, care or communication
were absent historically.

## Power and mediation audit

If the file is acquired, each document must receive separate fields for:

- physical layer and folio-side boundary;
- writer, signatory, copyist, printer, editor, recipient and archive selector;
- self-writing, dictation, reported speech, official paraphrase, quotation, enclosure or minute;
- conditions of production, including institutional dependency and any evidence of prompting,
  censorship, assistance, payment, circulation or withheld access;
- colonial administrative power: who could classify, forward, confine, route, answer, preserve or
  discard another person's words;
- independence: facsimile, transcription, catalogue description, Banton summary and future retelling
  are one target-object chain, not corroboration; and
- what each layer cannot prove.

The project must not flatten this into “both sides.” Any future official assessment and affected-
person-associated writing occupy different positions of authority, exposure, preservation and
consequence.

## Language gate

- Catalogue-level language is English; actual layer-level language, handwriting, print state,
  spelling and embedded terms remain unverified.
- Transcribe only from images, preserving spelling, punctuation, deletions, insertions, damaged
  text and uncertainty. OCR may assist navigation only after image replay.
- A second reader must check handwriting and pivotal quotations.
- Separate the person's words from editorial, clerical, medical and archival language.
- Do not infer mental state from prose style, capitalization, accusations, religion, political
  criticism, repetition, handwriting or print form.
- Historical clinical/capacity terms may be retained minimally and attributed when indispensable;
  they must never be silently mapped to a modern diagnosis.
- A Trinidad historian/archivist and disability/survivor-informed reader must review any future
  interpretation or public wording.

## Privacy, dignity, descendant and harm gates

The file is dated 1893, but age and open status do not remove ethical obligations.

- Treat names, health/capacity descriptions, addresses, finances, alleged misconduct, family ties,
  religion, occupation, signatures and accusations as potentially sensitive until replayed.
- Do not build a descendant tree, contact relatives, publish addresses or profile family members
  without a separate approved purpose and protocol.
- Do not publish a scan, signature, medical portrait, intimate detail, sensational quotation or
  searchable personal record by default.
- A Trinidad-based regional review is required before public use. Descendant/community contact is
  not authorized merely because the record is open or named.
- The associated pamphlet's provocative catalogued title must not become an SEO hook, thumbnail,
  joke, diagnosis cue or substitute for reading the complete object.
- Rights, repository permission, privacy, dignity, disability/survivor review, descendant/community
  authority, commercial use, quotation and image use are separate gates.

## Representativeness ceiling

Even after complete replay, the object would remain one selected colonial correspondence file.
Without a separately approved comparative design it cannot establish:

- Trinidad, Caribbean or British imperial prevalence or typical practice;
- patient, family, worker, hospital or asylum experience in general;
- treatment efficacy, benefit, harm, voluntariness, recovery or diagnostic validity;
- the frequency with which affected people's writing reached colonial or metropolitan officials;
  or
- that survival under a governor's despatch represents ordinary archival preservation.
