# R10-SOUL-INTELLECT-ETHICS — Soul, Intellect, Imagination, Ethics, and Revelation

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `014000–014999`

## Research question

How did a bounded group of Arabic-language philosophers, theologians, physicians, and ethical
writers between approximately 800 and 1250 describe soul, intellect, imagination, perception,
emotion, character, moral cultivation, revelation, and bodily faculties—and where did they
materially disagree?

## Boundary

Choose no more than four named authors or tightly linked corpora and exact passages. Name each
author's geography, language, intellectual community, genre, and transmission setting. Persian,
Central Asian, Andalusi, North African, Iraqi, Syrian, Egyptian, Jewish, Christian, and Muslim
participants may enter through the selected Arabic-language evidence but cannot be collapsed
into one “Islamic view of mind.”

## Required evidence

- Arabic source editions and accountable translations with exact locators;
- specialist history of philosophy, theology, ethics, medicine, and science;
- passage-specific treatment of *nafs*, *rūḥ*, *ʿaql*, imagination, internal senses, emotion,
  habit, and ethical cultivation without English one-to-one equivalence;
- disagreement among authors and within later reception;
- relationships among philosophical argument, medical model, ethical practice, and revelation;
- manuscript, translation, and commentary chronology;
- audits of “first psychologist,” “first cognitive scientist,” “invented psychotherapy,” and
  modern-diagnosis continuity claims; and
- archive and lived-experience limits: argument is not patient testimony or typical practice.

## Stop

Do not cover all Arabic or Persian thought, later Sufi histories, clinical regimens, or hospital
practice. Create the five standard outputs and rights-safe ignored cache; no public prose.
