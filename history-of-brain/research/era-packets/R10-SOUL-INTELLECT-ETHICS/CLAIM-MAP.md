### `CLM-014000` — bounded comparison, not a unitary tradition

- **Claim text:** Four selected Arabic-writing authors from different places, centuries, genres, and intellectual roles cannot establish a unitary “Islamic psychology” or a consensus across Muslim societies.
- **Type / scope:** `comparative`; ninth–twelfth centuries; al-Kindī's Baghdad/Iraq career context, al-Fārābī's probable Farab/Farayb birth and Baghdad/Syrian career, Ibn Sīnā's Afshana/Bukhara and later court networks, and al-Ghazālī's Tus/Nishapur/Baghdad/travel contexts; the four authors only, excluding unsearched communities; universality individual/comparative sample. Birthplace, career, ethnicity, and modern borders are not collapsed.
- **Label / current evidence / risk:** Documented as a sampling limit; current-evidence note not applicable; high anachronism/identity risk because modern religious and disciplinary labels can erase difference.
- **Sensitivity / disagreement / limits:** Identity and sacred; none; use named authors/works/places, never “the Islamic mind,” “Muslims believed,” or “medieval people believed.”
- **Review:** source-verified.
