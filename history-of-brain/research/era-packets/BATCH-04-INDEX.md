## Why the planned regional sections split this way

The master plan's `R13–R15` rows are planning sections, not researcher-sized assignments.
Batch 04 separates South Asia, China, a deliberately comparative but nonrepresentative
Korea/Japan packet, Southeast Asia, a bounded West African/Sahel packet, and a bounded medieval
European packet.

This batch does not represent all of Africa, Asia, or Europe. North, East, Central, and Southern
African lanes; additional Central Asian, Himalayan, island, and regional Asian archives; and
later early-modern developments remain explicit future packets. Absence from Batch 04 is not
evidence of historical absence.

and culturally controlled full text remains link-only.

## Shared research rules

The rules in `BATCH-01-INDEX.md`, `../../../sources/EVIDENCE-MODEL.md`, and
`../../../sources/SOURCE-RATING-AND-CAPTURE-PROTOCOL.md` remain active.

- Use `L1–L5/LX` source level and `A–D` claim fit for every consequential source–claim job.
- Open complete sources and record exact passage, page, manuscript, object, inscription,
  archival unit, or archaeological context.
- Abstracts, snippets, search summaries, catalogs, and later retellings are leads unless their
  narrow metadata or reception role is the actual claim.
- Trace priority, diagnosis, continuity, influence, institution, and treatment claims upstream;
  record shared citation chains as dependent.
- Separate composition, copying, translation, compilation, commentary, inscription, excavation,
  collection, oral recording, edition, and modern reception dates.
- Name the source language, consulted language, translator/editor, region, community, polity,
  genre, and archive position.
- Do not turn selected materials into “Indian psychology,” “Chinese medicine,” “Japanese
  wellness,” “African spirituality,” “medieval European madness,” or another unitary tradition.
- Historical terms do not equal modern diagnoses or English “mind,” “emotion,” “therapy,”
  “hospital,” “possession,” “disability,” or “patient” without a passage-specific argument.
- Prescriptions, laws, ritual manuals, biographies, miracle accounts, donor records, buildings,
  and ideals do not prove implementation, access, consent, outcome, or typical practice.
- Seek affected people, families, caregivers, workers, women, children, poor people,
  enslaved/dependent people, disabled people, migrants, rural communities, and non-elites.
  State when their words are missing or mediated.
- Later-recorded oral tradition must be dated to its recording and protocol context. It cannot
  silently become a verbatim medieval witness.
- Indigenous/local knowledge, sacred or restricted material, human remains, current community
  relationships, and heritage objects require protocol and authority review beyond copyright.
- No public prose, SEO copy, quotation clearance, implementation, publication, or live claims.
