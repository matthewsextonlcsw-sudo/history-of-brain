# R06-SOUTH-ASIA — Selves, Suffering, Medicine, and Cultivation

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `009000–009999`

## Research question

How did selected South Asian textual, medical, philosophical, Buddhist, Jain, legal, household,
and material traditions describe selves, mind, perception, emotion, suffering, altered conduct,
care, and cultivation without being flattened into one “Indian psychology”?

## Boundary

Named languages, regions, schools, texts, sites, and communities from approximately 1000 BCE
to 700 CE. Choose a bounded core. Separate textual composition, compilation, redaction,
manuscript, and translation dates. Later Ayurveda, yoga, Buddhism, or modern wellness claims
cannot be projected backward.

## Required evidence

- critical editions or authoritative translations with exact passages;
- specialist Indological, history-of-medicine, Buddhist, Jain, and philosophical scholarship;
- careful treatment of *manas*, *citta*, *ātman*, *anātman*, *unmāda*, suffering, emotion,
  conduct, and cultivation without one-to-one modern equivalence;
- practice, household, practitioner, patronage, law, status, gender/caste, and access evidence;
- internal disagreement and regional/language plurality;
- colonial translation and modern appropriation/reception chains; and
- direct, literary, normative, mediated, and absent lived-experience distinctions.

## Stop

Do not cover all South Asian history or claim unbroken modern continuity. Create standard
outputs, typed relationships, and rights-safe cache; no public prose.
