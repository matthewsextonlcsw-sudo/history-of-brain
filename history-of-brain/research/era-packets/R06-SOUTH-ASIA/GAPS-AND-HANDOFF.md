# R06-SOUTH-ASIA — Gaps and handoff

**Packet status:** complete for a bounded internal outline handoff; not publication-ready prose

## Named core actually researched

**Five textual laboratories of mind, suffering, disturbance, and cultivation in northern and peninsular South Asia, placed only within the brief's broad first-millennium-BCE to first-millennium-CE horizon:**

1. Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.3.3–11 and Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.5–7.
2. SN 22.59, SN 36.6, MN 10, and *Therīgāthā* 5.10, with oral-transmission and attribution controls.
3. Jain *manas* in selected *Tattvārthasūtra* passages and named commentarial analysis.
4. *Carakasaṃhitā* *Nidānasthāna* 7, *Cikitsāsthāna* 9, and access/training passages in *Vimānasthāna* 8.
5. Selected *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* passages and the reception of *rasāyana*.

This is deliberately not “all of ancient India.” The core is strongest on textual categories and transmission; weakest on everyday care, patients, vernaculars, material culture, geography, and social distribution.
The packet did not verify a shared `500 BCE–500 CE` range. Each corpus needs its own composition/redaction chronology before it is placed precisely in the master outline.

- **Vimānasthāna access passage:** replace or verify the anonymous Siva.sh lead with a named critical edition/translation and inspect variants/commentary. `SRC-009016`/`EVD-009015` are L5 lead-only and `CLM-009014` is excluded from supported outline nodes.
- **Modern clinical safety boundary:** any public page discussing current use, safety, or effectiveness of Ayurvedic, yogic, or meditation interventions needs a separately commissioned current clinical evidence review. This historical packet supplies none.

### P1 — high-value research before a definitive outline

- Add a specialist chronology of early/middle Upaniṣads that distinguishes composition layers from manuscript witnesses.
- Add a specialist history of Jain *Tattvārthasūtra* composition, redaction, Digambara/Śvetāmbara reception, and manuscript history. Soni's article is strong for *manas* but not a complete transmission history.
- Add an independent Therīgāthā specialist study beside Sujato for dating, attribution, gender, and manuscripts; inspect K. R. Norman's edition lawfully.
- Compare selected Pāli discourses with at least one Chinese Āgama or Gāndhārī parallel where available. Do not infer earliest wording from a single Pāli edition.
- Add a social history of Ayurvedic practitioners, medical education, patronage, household care, caste, women, and disability. Textual codes do not establish practice.
- Find legal/epigraphic/material sources for institutions, healers, patients, or patronage rather than asking medical/philosophical texts to answer every social question.
- Add scholarship from South Asian researchers and, where relevant, practitioners/readers who can identify colonial translation habits without substituting community authority for textual evidence.

## Source and method limitations

- Research date is August 18, 2026. Web availability, repository rights, and digital editions may change.
- Agent Reach's Exa route was unavailable. Discovery used built-in web search; full pages were replayed through Jina or opened directly. This affects discovery breadth, not the rule that snippets/abstracts are non-evidence.
- English-language scholarship is overrepresented. The primary languages sampled are Sanskrit and Pāli; no Prakrit, Tamil, Chinese, Tibetan, Sinhala, or regional-language competence is claimed.
- The packet's former `500 BCE–500 CE` core range was not independently established. Do not use it as a verified lower/upper boundary; add corpus-specific specialist chronology first.
- The packet opened complete relevant chapters/articles, but not every cited upstream monograph. Where a modern paper asserted continuity, the claim was traced to the opened classical text when possible and otherwise quarantined.
- Source independence is claim-specific. Repeated translations or scholarship built on the same classical text do not count as multiple ancient witnesses.
- No search-volume or public-interest conclusions belong to this research packet.
