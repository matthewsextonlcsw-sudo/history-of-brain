## Chronological assembly decisions

### 1. This is an overlap map, not one classical age

The researched boundaries overlap from approximately 1200 BCE to 700 CE, but the surviving
evidence does not move in one synchronized sequence. Iranian-language corpora, Roman imperial
settings, South Asian traditions, early Chinese manuscripts, Jewish late-antique archives, and
Christian eastern Roman materials require their own local chronologies.

The internal outline must show overlapping lanes and dated handoffs. It must not use “the
ancient world believed,” “classical medicine taught,” or “religion replaced medicine” as
shortcuts.

### 2. Composition, witness, commentary, translation, and modern reception are different dates

Several core corpora survive through much later manuscripts, received-text histories, edited
collections, or modern translations. A date attached to an idea cannot silently become the
date of every surviving wording.

Every outline node must distinguish, where applicable:

1. the proposed setting or composition range;
2. compilation and redaction;
3. the date and provenance of the surviving witness;
4. commentary and transmission;
5. the edition and translation actually consulted; and
6. the date when a modern continuity, priority, or diagnostic claim appeared.

### 3. Iran is not a timeless “Persian psychology” lane

`R04-IRAN` supports a bounded investigation of named Iranian polities, languages, texts,
inscriptions, objects, and later reception. It does not support projecting later Pahlavi
manuscripts transparently backward or treating imperial, Zoroastrian, medical, legal, and
material evidence as one worldview.

The packet also blocks a simple Gondeshapur origin story. Current internal use must show the
modern argument and contrary scholarship, while leaving the pre-700 institutional proposition
disputed and upstream Syriac, legal, and historical chains open.

### 4. Roman evidence is plural and status-bound

`R05-ROMAN` does not support “Roman psychiatry” as one system. Medical writers, philosophical
practices, households, sanctuaries, courts, law, enslavement, citizenship, gender, and imperial
administration expose different kinds of conduct and care. Greek-language writers in Roman
settings remain Greek-language writers in named Roman contexts; Galen is a major witness, not
a synonym for Roman medicine.

Roman legal classifications are not diagnoses, and a prescribed restraint or regimen is not
evidence of universal practice, consent, or outcome.

### 5. South Asian and early Chinese materials require internally plural lanes

`R06-SOUTH-ASIA` cannot become a single “Indian psychology,” Ayurveda, Buddhism, yoga, or
wellness-continuity chapter. `R07-EARLY-CHINA` cannot become timeless TCM or one Chinese theory
of mind. Each packet supports passage-specific comparisons among texts, communities,
practices, manuscript layers, and social positions.

The outline may explain terms such as *manas*, *citta*, *ātman*, *anātman*, *unmāda*, *xin*,
*qing*, *dian*, and *kuang*. It may not turn them into one-to-one translations of “mind,”
“self,” “emotion,” “psychosis,” “mania,” or another current category.

### 6. Late antiquity is not a religious takeover chapter

Jewish, Christian, philosophical, medical, household, ritual, legal, and charitable evidence
coexisted. `R08-JEWISH-LATE-ANTIQUITY` and `R08-CHRISTIAN-BYZANTINE` must remain distinct,
internally plural lanes. Neither represents a single religious psychology.

The Christian/Byzantine packet successfully narrowed itself to a 300–600 CE Greek eastern
Roman core with Augustine as a named Latin North African comparator. Syriac, Coptic, later
Byzantine, and the rest of the original 200–800 span remain future work, not implied coverage.

## Cross-packet findings that survive the seam audit

### Historical terms are not modern diagnoses in costume

Words about heart-mind, conduct, suffering, spirit, possession, disturbance, cultivation, or
legal competence have meanings within particular texts and institutions. Resemblance to a
modern term may help a reader formulate a question, but it does not establish diagnosis,
etiology, treatment equivalence, or continuity.

This rule applies to the packet's major search-risk terms, including *unmāda*, *xin*, *qing*,
*dian*, *kuang*, *acedia*, and *shoteh*. Public pages will need original-language expertise,
edition-stable locators, translation comparison, and a visible “not equivalent to” boundary.

### Norms, prescriptions, and laws do not prove implementation

Medical recipes, monastic rules, legal categories, philosophical exercises, household law,
charitable ideals, and institutional claims document what a source prescribed, argued,
authorized, or praised. They do not alone prove:

- how often anyone followed the rule;
- who could access or refuse care;
- what workers or recipients experienced;
- whether an intervention helped;
- whether coercion occurred; or
- whether the source represented typical practice.

The outline must treat practice and implementation as separate research questions.

### First-person language remains mediated evidence

Hymnic, ascetic, philosophical, legal, narrative, magical, and medical first-person language
is not automatically a patient diary. Hodayot speakers, attributed case speech, household
documents, inscriptions, and bowl texts have different relationships to lived experience.

Every public use must identify who speaks, who records, the genre, the surviving witness, the
translator, and what cannot be recovered about agency or emotion.

### “First hospital” and charity origin stories repeatedly break under review

The Batch 02 chains do not support a clean ancient invention story for the hospital, psychiatric
hospital, or organized mental-health care. Theodotos documents guest accommodation rather than
a staffed Jewish hospital. The Fabiola chain supports a bounded later Christian charitable
institutional proposition, not a Jewish hospital or psychiatric specialty. Gondeshapur remains
a disputed historical and reception problem. Basil's Question 55 and broad pro-hospital
scholarship remain incomplete.

These cases belong in a myth-audit method node, not a “who invented the hospital?” ranking.

### Care, control, cultivation, ritual, and social order often coexist

The sources do not sort cleanly into humane care versus oppressive control, science versus
religion, or individual therapy versus social discipline. A source may join bodily treatment,
ritual practice, ethical cultivation, family authority, legal status, charity, restraint, and
community obligation.

Public writing must identify whose goal is visible: sufferer, household, healer, ruler,
monastic superior, jurist, editor, or later historian.

### Archive conditions are part of every claim

The evidence is shaped by survival, excavation, manuscript transmission, modern collection,
cataloguing, translation, colonial scholarship, digital access, restricted editions, and
copyright. Household and non-elite experience is especially uneven. The packet archive does
not permit one generic disclaimer to cover women, children, poor people, enslaved or dependent
people, disabled people, workers, family caregivers, or recipients of charity.

Their visibility and absence must be audited within each node.

## Exchanges that are not yet demonstrated

Batch 02 identifies contact zones and later transmission problems, but it does not establish a
simple intellectual relay among Greece, Rome, Iran, South Asia, China, Jewish communities, and
Christian communities. Empire, trade, bilingualism, proximity, or similarity does not by
itself demonstrate that a term, treatment, institution, or theory moved.

The next exchange-focused packet must document:

1. the source form and receiving form;
2. named languages, texts, translators, communities, and institutions;
3. dates and locations for each stage;
4. what changed in translation or use;
5. which sources are independent;
6. evidence for and against transmission; and
7. the people and labor erased by a “great thinkers passed ideas west” story.

Until that work is complete, the master outline may place contacts and comparison questions,
but may not narrate a continuous chain of influence.

## Publication blockers carried forward

- current critical editions and specialist language review remain incomplete for several
  pivotal corpora;
- exact composition, redaction, and witness chronologies remain disputed or unresolved;
- some core works remain restricted, preview-only, abstract-only, or mediated through older

  and
- the Christian/Byzantine packet does not cover its original full 200–800 CE or regional brief.

### Lived experience and social coverage

- literary, legal, ritual, ascetic, magical, and medical voices must not be relabeled as direct
  patient testimony;
- access, cost, refusal, treatment outcome, coercion, caregiver labor, and institutional daily
  practice remain thin;
- women, children, enslaved/dependent people, disabled people, poor people, workers, rural
  communities, and non-elites remain unevenly represented; and
- bowl, inscription, household, and archaeological materials need provenance, commissioning,
  agency, and community-context review before emotional stories are inferred.

### Specialist and ethical review

- Iranian philology, Roman law, Sanskrit/Pali/Prakrit and South Asian medical history, early
  Chinese language/manuscripts, Jewish late-antique/rabbinic studies, Greek/Latin patristics,
  disability history, and relevant regional review remain packet-specific gates;
- self-harm and suicide-related Roman material requires a safety, empathy, and dignity review;
- enslavement, disability, possession, and legal-incapacity language needs dedicated review;
  and
- community and heritage review remains necessary where modern identities, excavated objects,
  or culturally sensitive materials are involved.

### Institutions and transmission

- Gondeshapur needs upstream Syriac, legal, and institutional sources plus specialist review;
- hospital and charity claims need independent institutional, archaeological, legal, and labor
  evidence;
- 11Q11, related Qumran materials, *shoteh*, B2963, bowl provenance, and Theodotos retain exact
  edition or provenance gaps; and
- no broad cross-regional influence narrative has passed an exchange-focused evidence packet.

## Source-quality lessons from Batch 02

1. **Full-text replay changed claims.** It exposed truncated previews, false cache content,
   incorrect page claims, and limits hidden by abstracts or summaries.
2. **A translation is a source layer.** Named translators, edition dates, and direct-versus-
   mediated access changed source ratings and quotation permissions.
3. **Source prestige remains claim-relative.** Museum records can strongly establish an object
   fact while being unable to establish a person's emotion, commissioning role, or ancient
   diagnosis.
4. **Same-witness translations are not independent corroboration.** The relationship graph now
   records shared witnesses, laws, studies, and reception lineages.
5. **Rights review is factual review.** A 404 capture, unstable file, or ambiguous license can
   corrupt the evidence trail as surely as a wrong citation.
6. **Negative audits are valuable research.** Quarantining 11Q11 content, a Roman asylum story,
   Gondeshapur priority, or a hospital claim prevents a seductive false milestone from steering
   the outline.
7. **Independent review caught consequential errors.** Author identity, source-layer wording,
   legal sequence, chronology, locators, labels, cache descriptions, and relationship semantics
   all changed before synthesis.
