# R10-MEDICINE-AFFECT-DISTRESS — Medical Categories, Affect, Regimen, and Troubling States

**State:** Authorized; research only

**Identifier range:** `015000–015999`

## Research question

How did a bounded group of Arabic- and Persian-language medical texts and case traditions from
approximately 800 to 1400 describe melancholy, mania, love-sickness, fear, anger, sleep, memory,
brain/body relations, regimen, drugs, conversation, music, and other responses to distress?

## Boundary

Select no more than four named works or case chains and their relevant passages. Separate author,
compiler, manuscript, edition, translation, and later retelling. A work's language, region,
genre, and relationship to Greek, Syriac, Persian, Indian, or other materials must be named.

## Required evidence

- opened critical texts or accountable translations with exact passages;
- specialist histories of medicine, pharmacy, humoral theory, emotion, and case writing;
- term-specific analysis of *mālīkhūliyā* and other selected categories without retrospective
  depression, bipolar, psychosis, OCD, or psychosomatic diagnosis;
- distinctions among prescription, reported case, literary anecdote, later biography, and
  actual treatment or outcome;
- drug, diet, environment, conversation, music, restraint, family, and practitioner evidence
  where the chosen texts support it;
- harm, coercion, consent, status, gender, cost, access, and missing-patient-voice audit;
- reception-chain audits for “first psychotherapy,” “music therapy,” “cognitive therapy,” and
  famous diagnostic anecdotes; and
- explicit dependence links where modern sources repeat the same medieval text or story.

## Stop

Do not turn the selected corpus into universal “Islamic medicine,” use modern clinical labels as
translations, or infer hospital implementation from a medical book. Create the five standard
outputs and rights-safe ignored cache; no public prose.
