### `CLM-015000` — The selected corpus contains different evidence genres

- **Claim:** The four selected chains preserve ethical counsel, medical prescription/classification, authorial observation, later biography, and Persian *adab* anecdote; these genres cannot be treated as equivalent records of clinical practice.
- **Scope:** opened corpus only; c. 800–1400 source traditions through named translations.
- **Evidence:** `EVD-015023`; source-specific records `EVD-015000`–`EVD-015021`.
- **Evidence label:** **Documented**.
- **Wording limit:** name the work, genre, and reporting verb at every public use.

- **Claim:** In *Spiritual Medicine* VIII, XI, and XII, al-Rāzī prescribes recollection, anticipation of consequences, bounded concern, diversion, and reasoning in response to anger, worry, and grief.

## Disputes and priority holds

### `DIS-015000` — Al-Balkhī and CBT

- **Position A:** Badri/IIIT and recent narrative reviews describe al-Balkhī as a CBT/cognitive-therapy pioneer and translate passages into modern psychological vocabulary (`EVD-015005`, `EVD-015009`–`EVD-015010`).
- **Position B:** The primary extract documents textual techniques but no modern construct, institutional lineage, controlled outcome, or comparative priority; translator insertions and coercive passages complicate continuity (`EVD-015004`–`EVD-015008`).
- **Packet disposition:** modern reception is reportable; identity/priority is held.

### `DIS-015001` — Ibn Sīnā's famous diagnosis

- **Position A:** Niẓāmī and later medical-history retellings present a successful diagnostic/treatment episode (`EVD-015016`, `EVD-015020`).
- **Position B:** Browne's note says Ibn Sīnā's own chronology places his Jurjān arrival after Qābūs's deposition, while Álvarez Millán classifies the story as later *adab*, identifies analogues and changing names, and finds it insufficient as clinical documentation (`EVD-015018`, `EVD-015026`).
- **Packet disposition:** report as story/reception only; event and efficacy held.

### `DIS-015002` — First psychotherapy

- **Position A:** modern reviews call al-Rāzī or al-Balkhī early/first practitioners and align advice with psychotherapy (`EVD-015009`–`EVD-015010`).
- **Position B:** the primary texts are genre-specific counsel/prescription without a modern profession, comparable global survey, or continuous lineage (`EVD-015003`–`EVD-015004`, `EVD-015023`–`EVD-015024`).
- **Packet disposition:** superlative rejected for publication; narrower text claims retained.

### `DIS-015003` — First music therapy

- **Position A:** a translated al-Balkhī passage recommends music/songs (`EVD-015007`), broader historical texts connect music and lovesickness (`EVD-015019`), and a 2017 article explicitly calls al-Kindī earliest/first (`EVD-015027`).
- **Position B:** the records differ between prescription and preference, lack administered practice/outcome, and do not survey earlier or parallel traditions; the replayed Shiloah chapter traces music/healing to earlier biblical/Pythagorean precedents and does not contain the 2017 child/priority wording (`EVD-015014`, `EVD-015022`, `EVD-015024`, `EVD-015028`).
- **Packet disposition:** passage reportable after translation review; priority and modern professional identity held.
