## Executed boundary and method

- The packet is limited to four named work/case chains: (1) Abū Bakr al-Rāzī's *al-Ṭibb al-rūḥānī*; (2) Abū Zayd al-Balkhī's *Maṣāliḥ al-abdān wa-l-anfus*, second part; (3) Isḥāq ibn ʿImrān's *Maqāla fī l-mālīkhūliyā*, first book; and (4) the Ibn Sīnā love-diagnosis and al-Majūsī porter anecdotes in Niẓāmī ʿArūḍī's *Chahār Maqāla*. Later biography and specialist scholarship are parts of those source chains, not a fifth primary corpus.
- This is not a survey of “Islamic medicine.” It is a source-critical sample spanning Arabic ethical counsel and medical writing, one Persian *adab* collection, and modern Arabic-to-English, Arabic-to-French, and Persian-to-English conduits. The locations represented include Rayy/Baghdad intellectual networks, Balkh/Baghdad attribution, Qayrawān, and Persian courtly literary memory. Andalusia, South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, Ottoman contexts, Jewish and Christian medical writers in Arabic, hospitals, pharmacy, women healers, vernacular traditions, and most manuscript transmission are excluded and remain handoffs.
- Ratings follow the project model: L1 direct/authoritative evidence; L2 specialist scholarship; L3 authoritative reference or accountable conduit; L4 accountable secondary synthesis; L5 discovery lead; LX rejected/unusable. Claim fit is A direct, B strong indirect, C context, D mismatch. These source ratings are separate from claim evidence labels.
- No Arabic or Persian original was opened. The consulted translation/edition object is rated for the wording and claim job actually checked; the unconsulted medieval original receives no phantom L1 score.
- Agent Reach diagnostics found Jina web access available and Exa unavailable. The installed command did not expose an Exa search workflow. Jina/direct institutional pages and PDFs were used where reachable; built-in primary-source web search was the discovery fallback. Snippets and abstracts nominated sources only and do not support retained facts.
- Every retained passage was opened in full context. Prescription, first-person authorial observation, anonymous clinical example, later biography, literary anecdote, practice, and outcome are kept distinct. Modern diagnostic equivalence is not inferred.
- Shared record fields unless a row overrides them: `opened_by` = R10 packet researcher; `opened_on` and `accessed_on` = 2026-08-18; `archive_url` = none unless a lawful cache is named; `cultural_protocol` = preserve historical terms where necessary, contextualize stigma and violence, do not diagnose historical people, and do not invent consent, access, or outcome.
