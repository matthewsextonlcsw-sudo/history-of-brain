## What can safely enter an outline

1. **Ethical regimen is not automatically clinical practice.** Al-Rāzī's anger/worry/grief chapters provide structured counsel within an ethical-philosophical work (`CLM-015001`).
2. **Textual techniques and modern labels must be separated.** Al-Balkhī's translated excerpt distinguishes internal thought and external advice and includes music and habituation prescriptions, while Badri supplies the explicit CBT/pioneer frame (`CLM-015002`–`CLM-015005`).
3. **Historical categories have their own body models, and treatment claims need seams.** Isḥāq's first book presents a humoral/faculty taxonomy, defers a formal treatment discussion, yet reports a lead-cap intervention whose consent, result, safety, duration, routine use, and efficacy are Unknown (`CLM-015006`–`CLM-015008`, `CLM-015020`–`CLM-015021`).
4. **A memorable case may be literature, not a chart.** Niẓāmī's Ibn Sīnā love story and porter story are *adab* anecdotes; the first mutates in later retelling and the second exposes coercive status power (`CLM-015010`–`CLM-015012`).
5. **The archive belongs mostly to authorities.** Patient, family, beloved, child, and laborer perspectives are absent or mediated (`CLM-015013`).
6. **Priority and modern-diagnosis claims remain held.** “First psychotherapy/CBT/music therapy,” efficacy, and retrospective diagnosis do not pass. The packet now records exact sampled upstream breaks instead of implying the chains resolve (`CLM-015014`–`CLM-015018`, `CLM-015023`–`CLM-015025`).

## Upstream myth and reception audits

### “First psychotherapy”

- **Sampled downstream nodes:** `SRC-015005`, `SRC-015015`, popular search results.
- **Upstream result:** the al-Rāzī route in Mitha explicitly stops at unopened Sleim Ammar, *Médecins et médecine de l’Islam* (1984) (`EVD-015030`). Al-Balkhī routes braid Badri with Haque, Awaad, Deuraseh/Talib, and other modern literature. No sampled chain resolves into a comparative priority study.
- **Disposition:** no retained priority claim. Safe alternative: named texts offer structured ethical/medical counsel.

### “First CBT” or “first cognitive psychologist”

- **Identified sampled upstream node:** Badri/IIIT's 2013 title, foreword, complete translation, exact numbered annotations, and chapter framing (`SRC-015003`–`SRC-015004`; `EVD-015009`, `EVD-015029`). This is not asserted as the origin of every modern comparison.
- **Dependent repeaters:** `SRC-015005`, `SRC-015006`, `SRC-015015`, and popular pages (`DEP-015003-Balkhi-modern-repeaters`).
- **Contrary/qualifying evidence:** parenthetical insertions, modern glosses, missing Arabic collation, no modern institutional lineage, and coercive habituation text (`EVD-015005`–`EVD-015008`).
- **Disposition:** reception claim allowed; historical identity/priority held.

### “First music therapy”

- **Primary anchors:** al-Balkhī prescription (`EVD-015007`); Isḥāq preference/sign description (`EVD-015014`); Avicennian context in specialist history (`EVD-015019`).
- **Accountable downstream exemplar:** Sufie and Sidik (2017), p. 197, calls al-Kindī earliest/first and cites Shiloah 1995 for an attempted cure of a paralysed child (`EVD-015027`).
- **Upstream replay:** the relevant Shiloah chapter segment, pp. 48–53, traces therapeutic music to earlier biblical/Pythagorean precedents and does not reproduce the attributed priority/child wording in the replayed segment (`EVD-015028`). Origin remains Unknown.
- **Upstream result:** prescription, preference, contextual synthesis, and modern priority wording have been kept separate. None documents a delivered treatment with consent or measured outcome.
- **Disposition:** no priority or practice claim. Keep the evidence types separate.

### Ibn Sīnā's famous love diagnosis

- **Primary story node:** Niẓāmī, Anecdote XXXVI (`EVD-015016`).
- **Editorial/textual lead and contradiction:** Browne's note supplies both an unopened *Qānūn* lead (`EVD-015017`) and a separate direct chronology contradiction placing arrival after Qābūs's deposition (`EVD-015026`).
- **Independent source criticism:** Álvarez Millán documents *adab* genre, analogues, changed names, and the lack of stronger clinical documentation (`EVD-015018`).
- **Downstream mutation:** 2014 vignette changes the patient from male royal relative to sultan's wife (`EVD-015020`; `DEP-015009-Avicenna-retellings`).
- **Disposition:** story and reception history allowed; event, diagnosis, treatment, and cure as fact are held.
