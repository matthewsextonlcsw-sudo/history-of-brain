The promise

Not a parade of famous Western doctors. A connected public reference that asks what people believed, what happened to them, who held power, how ideas traveled, what historians dispute, and what evidence can honestly support.

The present is not the finish line. Historical explanation and current evidence remain visibly separate.

The collection

  • Start here: global story, fair terminology, methods, archive, then versus now.
  • Time: material lives before writing through networked minds.
  • Questions: mind, distress, care, treatment, emotion, disciplines, trauma, addiction, social work, wellness.
  • Experience: who spoke, who was described, who cared, who organized, who is missing.
  • Places: region-led doors built only when the evidence is ready.
  • Connections: named routes and mechanisms; resemblance alone does not prove influence.
  • Evidence: sources, labels, translation, “firsts,” diagnosis, treatment evidence, ethics, corrections.
  • Discovery: useful answer blocks and internal links without thin keyword pages.

Twelve human questions

These are the authority guides—the pages most likely to satisfy broad search intent without turning history into keyword confetti.

Q01

Where did mind live?

Brain, heart, breath, soul, body, land, kin, and false equivalence.

Q02

What counted as illness?

Period terms, distress, difference, disability, danger, gift, and diagnosis.

Q03

Who cared?

Household, community, religious, medical, Indigenous, peer, and mutual aid.

Q04

How did treatment change?

Purpose, reception, evidence, relief, risk, coercion, and consent.

Q05

Do emotions have history?

Words, moods, temperament, regulation, relationship, and judgment.

Q06

How did psychologies form?

Philosophy, lab, school, clinic, industry, empire, adaptation, critique.

Q07

How did psychiatry gain power?

Law, institution, diagnosis, medicine, courts, resistance, reform.

Q08

What changed with brain science?

Injury, anatomy, neuron, chemistry, imaging, genetics, prediction.

Q09

How did trauma become an idea?

War, violence, memory, testimony, culture, diagnosis, collective response.

Q10

How did addiction change?

Vice, crime, illness, commerce, mutual aid, medicine, public health.

Q11

How did social care become systems?

Poor relief, mutual aid, settlement, social work, welfare, rights.

Q12

Is wellness ancient or a business?

Coping, good-life ideals, mindfulness, resilience, recovery, markets.

Experience is a structure

“Patient voice” is not a decorative quotation. Every page says who spoke, who recorded it, who translated it, who kept it, and what remains unknown.

V01 · Speaking for themselves

Letters, petitions, diaries, memoirs, testimony, oral history, publications, art, blogs, and recorded speech.

V02 · Words inside another record

Healers, clerks, judges, relatives, missionaries, and translators control what survives.

V03 · Families and chosen families

Care, conflict, cost, commitment, discharge, advocacy, burden, love, and refusal.

V04 · The workers history forgets

Nurses, attendants, social workers, peer workers, interpreters, domestic workers, and ritual specialists.

V05 · Writing back and organizing

Survivor, disability, Mad, neurodivergent, Indigenous, queer, feminist, antiracist, family, and labor movements.

V06 · When the archive goes quiet

Record destruction, language, privacy, protocol, unequal preservation, incomplete searches, and honest unknowns.

Anatomy of a strong page

Direct answerAnswer the real question in 40–80 honest words.
The catchExpose the misconception, category problem, or evidence limit.
Time & placeSay where and when the account applies—and where it does not.
Words thenOriginal terms, language, translation, and semantic change.
ExplanationsWhat different people thought was happening, in context.
ConsequencesCare, coping, relief, status, rights, labor, coercion, and harm.
Voice or silenceDirect experience where ethical; otherwise name the exact gap.
MovementNamed routes, translators, migrants, institutions, media, and power.
DisputeSerious interpretations, evidence, and fair wording.
Then versus nowCurrent evidence in a visibly separate section; never medical advice.
How we knowClaims, sources, objects, records, translation, limits.
Where nextTime, theme, place, experience, and evidence links.
CitationsUseful body links plus complete end references.

Balance audit

What the architecture prevents

  • No Western chronology with “elsewhere” sidebars.
  • No ancient Egypt standing in for Africa or India for Asia.
  • No demon-to-science progress ladder.
  • No modern-equals-humane assumption.
  • No founder-only discipline histories.
  • No patient quotation used as garnish.
  • No weird-treatment spectacle.
  • No thin search-doorway pages.

What still blocks “complete”

  • Local-language and locally authored work in weak cells.
  • Nation- and community-led Indigenous review.
  • More direct experience outside Europe and North America.
  • Global nursing, social work, counseling, family, peer, OT, and harm-reduction histories.
  • Country-specific political abuse, coercion, eugenics, and reform.
  • Quantitative search-demand validation.
  • Claim-level sourcing for every selected page.
