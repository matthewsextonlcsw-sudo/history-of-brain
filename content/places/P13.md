The late 20th century is where the mind became a rights problem. Before the 1980s, the mind was a pharmacological problem. The 1980s made it a rights one. The patient had rights. The patient could appeal. The patient could advocate.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from this period. They show the shift from pharmacological to rights-based treatment. The shift from medication to advocacy. The shift from control to dignity.

The NLM holds digitized editions of the key texts. You can read them. You can see what the late 20th-century advocates thought. You can see what they did not think. You can see where they disagreed with the psychiatrists.

The National Archives holds records of the Ministry of Health from this period. The MH series holds correspondence, reports, and administrative records. You can search them online.
