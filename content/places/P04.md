# P04 — Rome

> *Rome inherited Greek medicine and added its own. Galen is the name that matters here. He wrote down what he saw in the arena, in the hospital, and in the laboratory. His texts survived the fall of the Roman Empire. They are in libraries today. You can read them.*

## Draft

Galen dissected animals to understand the human body. He watched the arena to understand trauma. He wrote it all down. His texts are not one book. They are hundreds of treatises. Some are about the brain. Some are about the nerves. Some are about the mind.

The Roman contribution is not a new idea. It is a continuation. Rome took Greek medicine and made it bigger. More hospitals. More patients. More records. The mind was still a body problem. The humors were still the explanation. But the scale was different. Rome had more people, more cities, more institutions. The mind was a public problem, not just a private one.

The Wellcome Collection has translations. The NLM has digitized editions. The British Museum has fragments. You can check them.

The shift from Greece to Rome is not a break. It is a continuation. The body is still the problem. The humors are still the explanation. But the scale is different. That is the Roman contribution.
