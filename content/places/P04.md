## Draft

India is where the mind became a spiritual and medical problem at the same time. The Ayurvedic tradition treated mental illness as a body problem. The religious tradition treated it as a soul problem. The two traditions ran in parallel. They did not always agree.

The Ayurvedic texts describe mental illness in terms of the body. The doshas (the three bodily humors) are out of balance. The treatment is herbal. The treatment is dietary. The treatment is lifestyle. The mind is a function of the body.

The religious texts describe mental illness in terms of the soul. The soul is in distress. The treatment is prayer. The treatment is ritual. The treatment is meditation. The mind is a spiritual entity.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from both traditions. The NLM holds digitized editions with English translations. You can read the texts. You can see what the Ayurvedic doctors thought. You can see what the religious leaders thought. You can see where they disagreed.

The British Museum holds manuscripts and documents from this period. They show the medical practice. They show the religious practice. They show the gap between the two.

The Ayurvedic texts are the body system. The religious texts are the soul system. The two systems ran in parallel. They did not always agree. The Ayurvedic doctor treated the body. The religious leader treated the soul. The patient received both. The record shows both. The gap shows what each system did not want to say.

The Ayurvedic texts describe mental illness in terms of the body. The doshas are out of balance. The treatment is herbal, dietary, and lifestyle. The religious texts describe mental illness in terms of the soul. The soul is in distress. The treatment is prayer, ritual, and meditation. The two systems ran in parallel. The patient received both.

The Indian contribution is not a story of progress. It is a story of two systems running in parallel. The body system. The soul system. The records show both. The gaps show what each system did not want to say.
