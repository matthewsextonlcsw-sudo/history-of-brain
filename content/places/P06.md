## Draft

Rome is where the mind became a legal and medical problem. The Roman legal system dealt with mental illness as a property problem. If you were "insane," your property was managed by a guardian. The legal system did not care about your mind. It cared about your estate.

The Roman medical system dealt with mental illness as a body problem. The Galenic tradition (named after Galen, the Roman physician) described the mind as a function of the body. The humors were the explanation. The treatment was herbal. The treatment was dietary. The treatment was lifestyle.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from this tradition. The NLM holds digitized editions with English translations. You can read the texts. You can see what the Roman doctors thought. You can see what the Roman lawyers thought. You can see where they disagreed.

The British Museum holds papyri and documents from this period. They show the medical practice. They show the legal practice. They show the gap between the two.

The Roman contribution is not a story of progress. It is a story of two systems running in parallel. The legal system. The medical system. The records show both. The gaps show what each system did not want to say.
