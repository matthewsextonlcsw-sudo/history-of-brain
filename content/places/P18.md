## Draft

The Americas are where the mind became a colonial and medical problem at the same time. The 19th and 20th centuries are the parts that matter here. The colonial system dealt with mental illness as a property problem. The medical system dealt with it as a body problem.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from this period. They show the shift from colonial to medical treatment. The shift from property to patient. The shift from exclusion to inclusion.

The NLM holds digitized editions of the key texts. You can read them. You can see what the colonial administrators thought. You can see what the medical doctors thought. You can see where they disagreed with each other.

The National Archives holds records of the Ministry of Health from this period. The MH series holds correspondence, reports, and administrative records. You can search them online.

The American contribution is two systems running in parallel. The colonial system dealt with mental illness as a property problem. The medical system dealt with it as a body problem. The two systems did not always agree. The record shows both. The gaps show what each system did not want to say. The colonial administrators did not want to say that the property was not the person. The medical doctors did not want to say that the body was not the mind. The record shows both.
