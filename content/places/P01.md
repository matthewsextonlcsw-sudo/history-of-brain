# P01 — Mesopotamia

> *Mesopotamia is the first place where people wrote down what they thought about the mind. Not in a temple. Not in a law code. In a diagnostic tablet. A doctor held a clay tablet, looked at a patient, and wrote down what he saw. That tablet is in a museum today. You can see it. You can read what the doctor wrote. That is the point of this page: the record exists, and you can check it.*

## Draft

The first written record of a doctor thinking about a patient's mind is a clay tablet from Mesopotamia. Not a poem. Not a prayer. A diagnostic tablet. The doctor held the tablet, looked at the patient, and wrote down what he saw. The tablet says: "If a man's head is heavy, and he sees dark, and his ears are blocked, then..." That is a medical observation. It is not a guess. It is a record.

The tablet is in a museum today. You can see it. You can read what the doctor wrote. The Electronic Babylonian Library has a digital copy. The British Museum has the physical tablet. The Wellcome Collection has a translation. You can check all three. That is the point of this page: the record exists, and you can check it.

The doctor did not say "the gods are angry." He said "the head is heavy." That is a shift. It is not a jump from superstition to science. It is a shift from one kind of explanation to another. The tablet does not say which one is right. It says what the doctor saw, and what he did about it.

You can walk through the collection. You can look at the sources. You can disagree with the interpretation. That is the point.
