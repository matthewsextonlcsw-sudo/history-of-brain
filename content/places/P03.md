## Draft

Greece is where the mind became a philosophical problem. Before the Greeks, the mind was a body problem. The humors were the explanation. The body was the cause. The Greeks asked a different question: where does the mind live?

The Hippocratic tradition is the key here. The Hippocratic Corpus is a collection of medical texts attributed to Hippocrates and his students. The texts describe the mind as a function of the body. The brain is the seat of sensation. The humors still matter. But the brain matters too.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from this tradition. The NLM holds digitized editions with English translations. You can read the texts. You can see what the Greeks thought. You can see what they did not think.

The Greek contribution is not a story of progress. It is a story of a shift. The shift from body to mind. The shift from humors to brain. The shift from religion to philosophy. The records show the shift. The gaps show what the Greeks did not want to say.

The British Museum holds papyri and documents from this period. They show the medical practice. They show the philosophical debate. They show the gap between what the doctors said and what the patients felt.
