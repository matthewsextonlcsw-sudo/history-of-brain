> *A lived-experience page is a window. The window shows what the record says about a specific experience. The window does not show what the record does not say. That is the point of this page: the lived-experience page is a window, and the window is limited.*

## Draft

A lived-experience page is a window. The window shows what the record says about a specific experience. The window does not show what the record does not say.

The hardest rule on a lived-experience page is the voice rule. The page must label whose voice it is. There are four labels: direct, near-direct, reported, and institutional. A direct voice is the person's own words. A reported voice is someone else's account of the person. An institutional voice is a doctor's, a clerk's, or a judge's words. A modern reconstruction is a later writer's attempt to imagine the person.

The National Archives guide says most patient files have been destroyed. That means the direct voice is often gone. What survives is the institutional voice: the admission register, the quarterly return, the correspondence with the poor law union. A lived-experience page tells you which voice you are reading. It does not let you mistake the doctor's words for the person's words.

Here is a concrete example. Take a page about the asylum. The record gives you the name, the age, the date of admission, and the date of discharge. That is the institutional voice. It does not give you what the person felt in the room. A good page says so plainly. It names the gap.

Here is the difference between reported and institutional. A reported voice is a relative's or a neighbor's account of the person. An institutional voice is a doctor's or a clerk's record. The reported voice is closer to the person, but it is still not the person's own words. The institutional voice is further from the person, but it is more reliable as a record. A good page tells you which one you are reading.

That is how you read a lived-experience page: check the voice label, and notice where the person's own words are missing.
