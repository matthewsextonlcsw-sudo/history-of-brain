## Draft

This is not one person. Not one time. Not one place. Not one record. It is a set of variations, each with its own decision, its own promise, its own violence. The question "Who decided a person was 'mad'?" looks simple. It is not.

In Egypt, the [backbone's B1.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows family petitions and heart-centered vocabularies. The decision was the family's. The promise was restoration. The violence was the family's authority — the commitment, the discharge, the retrieval.

In the classical world, the [backbone's B2.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks who had a choice of healer. The decision was not one person's. It was a set of variations — status, gender, enslavement, household authority, cost, pilgrimage. The "medical marketplace" is not a free market. It is a market with power built into every transaction.

In medieval and early modern Europe, the [backbone's B4.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) places the decision in specific jurisdictions: Bethlem, family custody, charity, courts, medicine, religion, and alleged possession. The decision was not one person's. It was a set of variations — the court, the family, the physician, the priest, the magistrate.

In the asylum century, the [backbone's B5.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks who was believed, and who was not. The [R22 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R22-JAMAICA-OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTS-1860/FINDINGS.md) shows the archive's structural bias: no family-authored text survives directly. Every family trace is mediated. The decision was not one person's. It was the archive's.

In the institutional explosion, the [backbone's B7.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the institution and the law: the law is the framework. The decision was not one person's. It was the law's. The [R31 Japan packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows this in legal form: the 1950 law assigned duties to a legally designated protector, including financial and discharge-retrieval obligations. The decision was the law's.

The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction.

The [evidence model's voice rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) is blunt: protector, elder, clinician, archive, or institutional speech is not family or patient consent. Do not infer burden, support, abandonment, motive, or consensus. The decision is not one person's. It is a set of variations, each with its own decision, its own promise, its own violence.
