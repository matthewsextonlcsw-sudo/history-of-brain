## Draft

An evidence page is a tool. The tool shows how to check a citation. It does not show what the record does not say.

The References section at the bottom of every page is the citation ladder. Each entry has a name, a URL, and a tag. The tag tells you what kind of source it is: Primary, Scholarly, Institutional, Reference, or Web. A Primary source is the original record. A Scholarly source is a peer-reviewed book or article. An Institutional source is a museum, archive, or government guide.

Here is how you check a citation. Take a fact from the Draft. Find the matching entry in the References. Open the URL. Read the passage. Does the passage actually say what the fact claims? If yes, the citation holds. If no, the fact is shaky.

The National Archives guide is an Institutional source. It says lunatic asylums were first established in Britain in the mid-19th century. You can open the guide and read that sentence. It is there. The citation holds.

But a citation is not a proof. It is a pointer. The pointer takes you to the source. You still have to read the source. That is the job of the evidence page: to show you how to follow the pointer and check the source.

Here is what to do when a citation fails. If the source does not say what the fact claims, the fact is shaky. You can fix it two ways. You can find a better source that does say it. Or you can change the fact to match what the source actually says. You never keep a fact that the source does not support. That is the rule: the source comes first, the fact second.

That is how you check a citation: find the entry, open the URL, read the passage, and decide if the fact holds.
