## Draft

This is not one thing. Not one person. Not one time. Not one place. Not one record. It is a set of variations, each with its own recovery, its own promise, its own violence. The question "What counted as recovery?" looks simple. It is not.

In Egypt, the [backbone's B1.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows family petitions and heart-centered vocabularies. Recovery was the family's restoration. The promise was the person's standing in the community. The violence was the family's authority — the commitment, the discharge, the retrieval.

In South Asia, the [backbone's B2.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows layered selves, *manas*, suffering, and transformation. Recovery was transformation. The promise was the person's standing in the community. The violence was the transformation itself — the ritual, the confinement, the public performance.

In the asylum century, the [backbone's B5.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows moral treatment: Pinel, Tuke, and their successors. Recovery was cure. The promise was the person's return to the community. The violence was the asylum itself — the architecture, the regimen, the confinement.

In the pharmacological turn, the [backbone's B6.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows chloral hydrate to chlorpromazine. Recovery was control. The promise was the person's return to the community. The violence was the drug itself — the sedation, the confinement, the public performance.

In the organizing movements, the [backbone's B8.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows patients, survivors, disabled people, and families organizing: rights, refusal, peer support, independent living, recovery, mad liberation, family advocacy, and internal disagreements. Recovery was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

In the right to leave, the [backbone's B8.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the right to leave the hospital—and the right to real support. Recovery was support. The promise was the person's return to the community. The violence was the gap between closure and care.

In the present, the [backbone's B10.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows neurodiversity, mad pride, recovery, and disability justice: separate and overlapping movements, each with its own arguments, its own internal disagreements, and its own institutional effects. Recovery was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

The [R31 Japan packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows the family's role in legal form: the 1950 law assigned duties to a legally designated protector, including financial and discharge-retrieval obligations. The [R33 Aro packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows the worker's role in care: organizer/admin descriptions include "described nursing and medical roles" and "treatment and occupational activity."

The [1990 Caracas Declaration](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241580520) names the rights to mental health care and to protection from abusive treatment as central. Recovery was not one thing. It was a set of variations, each with its own recovery, its own promise, its own violence.
