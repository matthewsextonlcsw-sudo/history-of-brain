## Draft

This is not one thing. Not one person. Not one time. Not one place. Not one record. It is a set of variations, each with its own community, its own promise, its own violence. The question "What was the community for?" looks simple. It is not.

In Egypt, the [backbone's B1.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows family petitions and heart-centered vocabularies. The community was the family. The promise was the person's standing in the community. The violence was the family's authority — the commitment, the discharge, the retrieval.

In the classical world, the [backbone's B2.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks who had a choice of healer. The community was not one thing. It was a set of variations — status, gender, enslavement, household authority, cost, pilgrimage. The "medical marketplace" is not a free market. It is a market with power built into every transaction.

In medieval and early modern Europe, the [backbone's B4.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) places the community in specific jurisdictions: Bethlem, family custody, charity, courts, medicine, religion, and alleged possession. The community was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the court, the family, the physician, the priest, the magistrate.

In the asylum century, the [backbone's B5.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) names the asylum as architecture, promise, workplace, and instrument of rule. The community was the asylum. The promise was care. The violence was the asylum itself — the architecture, the regimen, the confinement.

In the institutional explosion, the [backbone's B7.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the institution and the community: the institution is not isolated. It is embedded in the community, and the community is embedded in the institution. The community was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the institution's promise, the institution's violence.

In the organizing movements, the [backbone's B8.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows patients, survivors, disabled people, and families organizing: rights, refusal, peer support, independent living, recovery, mad liberation, family advocacy, and internal disagreements. The community was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

In the right to leave, the [backbone's B8.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the right to leave the hospital—and the right to real support. The community was the support. The promise was the person's return to the community. The violence was the gap between closure and care.

The [R31 Japan packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows the family's role in legal form: the 1950 law assigned duties to a legally designated protector, including financial and discharge-retrieval obligations. The [R33 Aro packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows the community's role in care: organizer/admin descriptions include "accompanying relatives" and "selected boarding in neighboring villages."

The [1990 Caracas Declaration](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241580520) names the rights to mental health care and to protection from abusive treatment as central. The community was not one thing. It was a set of variations, each with its own community, its own promise, its own violence.
