## Draft

This is not one thing. Not one person. Not one time. Not one place. Not one record. It is a set of variations, each with its own law, its own promise, its own violence. The question "What was the law for?" looks simple. It is not.

In the classical world, the [backbone's B2.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks who had a choice of healer. The law was not one thing. It was a set of variations — status, gender, enslavement, household authority, cost, pilgrimage. The "medical marketplace" is not a free market. It is a market with power built into every transaction.

In medieval and early modern Europe, the [backbone's B4.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) places the law in specific jurisdictions: Bethlem, family custody, charity, courts, medicine, religion, and alleged possession. The law was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the court, the family, the physician, the priest, the magistrate.

In the asylum century, the [backbone's B5.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks who was believed, and who was not. The [R22 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R22-JAMAICA-OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTS-1860/FINDINGS.md) shows the archive's structural bias: no family-authored text survives directly. Every family trace is mediated. The law was not one thing. It was the archive's.

In the institutional explosion, the [backbone's B7.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the institution and the law: the law is the framework. The law was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the institution's promise, the institution's violence.

In the right to leave, the [backbone's B8.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the right to leave the hospital—and the right to real support: Brazil's anti-asylum movement, the [1990 Caracas Declaration](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241580520), Italy's influence, Ghanaian user organizing, and the gap between closure and care. The law was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

The [R31 Japan packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows the law in legal form: the 1950 law assigned duties to a legally designated protector, including financial and discharge-retrieval obligations. The [Walker account](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780195395181.013.0004) shows the law as a legal rather than medical category: a person was insane if they lacked capacity to manage their affairs. The boundaries shifted over time. The organizing principle was not symptoms. It was legal capacity.

The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction.

The law was not one thing. It was a set of variations, each with its own law, its own promise, its own violence.
