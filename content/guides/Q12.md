> *A lived-experience page is a window. The window shows what the record says about a specific experience. The window does not show what the record does not say. That is the point of this page: the lived-experience page is a window, and the window is limited.*

## Draft

A lived-experience page is a window. The window shows what the record says about a specific experience. The window does not show what the record does not say.

The hardest part of a lived-experience page is the gap. The gap is where the person's own words should be, but are not. The National Archives guide says most patient files have been destroyed. That means the direct voice is often gone. What survives is the institutional voice: the register, the return, the letter.

Here is how you check the gap. Take a claim from the Draft. Find the voice. Check the source. Is the voice direct, reported, or institutional? If the source is an admission register, the voice is institutional. The register gives you the name, the age, the date. It does not give you the feeling. A good page says so.

The gap is not a flaw. It is a fact. The record is what it is. A lived-experience page names the gap and does not fill it with invention. It does not guess what the person felt. It does not imagine the person's inner life. It says: the record does not show this.

Here is how to handle the gap honestly. The gap is not a failure. It is a fact. The record is what it is. A lived-experience page names the gap and does not fill it with invention. It does not guess what the person felt. It does not imagine the person's inner life. It says: the record does not show this. That is the honest answer. And the honest answer is enough. It is better than a smooth guess.

One more check: the honest answer. The gap is not a failure. It is a fact. The record is what it is. A lived-experience page names the gap and does not fill it with invention. The honest answer is enough. It is better than a smooth guess.

That is how you check the gap on a lived-experience page: find the voice, check the source, and notice where the person's own words are missing.
