# Q07 — How to Follow a Citation

> *A citation is not a decoration. A citation is the foundation. A citation tells you where the fact comes from. A citation tells you how to check the work. A citation tells you how to disagree with the work and still trust the source.*

## Draft

You are reading a collection. The collection is built on a set of rules. The rules are not a decoration. The rules are the foundation. Without the rules, the collection is a guess. With the rules, the collection is a claim with a trail. You can follow the trail. You can check the work. You can disagree with the work and still trust the source.

The rules say: "Never cite a source that has not been opened and checked." The rules say: "Record the exact page, folio, timestamp, table, figure, object number, or passage supporting a claim whenever the source permits it." The rules say: "A link is access, not proof. Record what was checked and when." The rules say: "No factual or interpretive claim becomes publishable because it sounds plausible."

The rules are not a list. The rules are a foundation. The foundation is the collection. The collection is the mind. The mind is the record. The record is the fact. The fact is the label. The label is the trail. The trail is the point.

You can walk through the collection. You can look at the sources. You can disagree with the interpretation. That is the point.
