## Draft

The voice is real. It is never raw. Every letter, petition, memoir, and testimony that survives has passed through a chain of hands — a dictator, a translator, an editor, a selector, a printer, a filer. The honest work is to trace that chain, not to pretend it was not there.

The [backbone's lived-experience rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt and we keep it prominent: **no direct voice survives. Say so.** The missing are predictable — non-literate people, children, enslaved people, rural households, and most women reach us through elite or institutional records, if at all. When the archive is silent, name the silence. Do not fill it with our categories.

But some voices do survive, and they are worth indexing with their mediation notes intact.

**Ann Pratt, Jamaica, 1859–1860.** Her narrative exists in the record as a printed summary ordered at the governor's direction and a five-page first-person report — but nearly everything else passed through the solicitor and supporters who gathered it. Her elicited statement of July 1860 was taken down by Trench, read back to her, corrected, signed, and closed with a refusal to alter its substance: dictation, verification, and constraint in a single documented moment. The voice is hers. The chain is documented. The [R22 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R22-JAMAICA-OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTS-1860/FINDINGS.md) preserves both.

**Six enslaved people, St. Thomas, 1738–1753.** Peter's 1738 letter is self-written as Martin's German note attests. The 1739 king petition was composed by Domingo Gesoe writing for a constituency of 650-plus. Damma's letter survives in three language layers — an African-language original, a Creole rendering, an English translation — which a 2025 collaboration has identified as Gbe, overturning the 1996 edition's "unidentified" label. Cornelius's 1753 letter, with its devotional blood-and-wound language, reaches us through the Herrnhut/Danish transcription chain. Even the best letter preserves a fraction of a speech community: Van Rossem reports that most 1741 correspondents dictated, and Bøgh cautions that the 1753 Seidel sample cannot represent the whole enslaved population. The [R19 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R19-ST-THOMAS-ENSLAVED-LETTERS/FINDINGS.md) preserves the transmission matrix.

**Wilbraham, Grahamstown, 1908–1911.** A 2014 descendant-scholar article publishes a complete letter transcription and reconstructs clinical, administrative, employment, pension, family, and retrospective-memory layers. The node is direct only for what that opened publication reports and transcribes. The manuscript, casebook, ledgers, estate file, register, family objects, and annual report remain unopened. The [R26 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R26-GRAHAMSTOWN-WILBRAHAM-1908-1911/FINDINGS.md) preserves the boundary.

**Alberto Nicolat, Mexico, 1918–1932.** His deliberately published book preserves affected-person authorship and public argument. It does not verify every biographical, family, legal, clinical, or institutional event. The Oneto thesis and consequential case-file folios remain held. The [R23 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R23-MEXICO-LA-CASTANEDA-NICOLAT-1918-1932/FINDINGS.md) preserves the distinction between authorship and verification.

**Lima Barreto, Rio de Janeiro, 1919–1920.** Two FBN autograph objects preserve different genres in one dependence chain: a situated asylum notebook and an unfinished literary reworking. Track authored self-report, revision, posthumous editing, family-to-archive provenance, digitization, and later scholarship as separate layers. The institutional observation page and January 1920 newspaper interview remain unopened. Diary and fiction do not independently corroborate one another. The [R25 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R25-BRAZIL-LIMA-BARRETO-HNA-1919-1920/FINDINGS.md) preserves the dependence chain.

The pattern is consistent: the voice is real, but it is always mediated. The chain of hands is part of the evidence, not a flaw in it. The [evidence ladder](H03.md) applies to voice the same way it applies to bone: the object supports a claim about what was said, not about what a person felt. And the [whose-voice-survives page](H04.md) is the method for tracing that chain: who speaks, who translates, who selects, who edits, who prints, who files — the archive is all of that, and the patient story is the last link, not the first.
