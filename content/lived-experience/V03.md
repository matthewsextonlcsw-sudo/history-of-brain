## Draft

The body in the record is the doctor's body. The doctor wrote down what he saw. The doctor wrote down what he did. The person did not write down what they felt.

The National Archives guide says the records "relate mainly to the administration of the institutions." The body in the record is the administrator's body. The body is the doctor's body. The body is not the patient's body.

Here is what the record says about the body. The admission register gives you the age. The correspondence with the poor law union gives you the type of disability and whether the person was considered dangerous. The visitor's report gives you the name, the address, the age, the income, and the allowance. None of these records give you what the body felt. They give you the body as a fact: the age, the condition, the danger.

The Wellcome Collection has photographs. The photographs show the body. The photographs show what the room looked like. The photographs do not show what the body felt. The body in the photograph is a fact. The fact is limited.

The NLM has digitized editions of historical medical texts. The texts describe the body. The texts describe what the doctor saw and what the doctor did. The texts do not describe what the body felt. The body in the text is a fact. The fact is limited.

One more thing: the body is not the person. The record gives you the body as a fact: the age, the condition, the danger. It does not give you the person. The person is more than the body. The record does not show the person. The record shows the body.

That is the point of this page: the body in the record is the doctor's body. The person's body is not in the record. You can check the record. You can see what it says about the body. You can see what it does not say.
