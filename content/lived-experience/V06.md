## Draft

The silence is not absence. It is a specific, traceable gap. When the archive goes quiet, the question is not "what happened?" but "what was recorded, what was selected, what was destroyed, and what was never written?"

The [backbone's B0 lived-experience rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt: no direct voice survives. Say so prominently. The missing are predictable — non-literate people, children, enslaved people, rural households, and most women appear through elite or institutional records, if at all. The [backbone's B1 missing-voices note](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) names them specifically. The silence is not a mystery. It is a structure.

The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction. The [R22 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R22-JAMAICA-OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTS-1860/FINDINGS.md) shows this in the Pratt dossier: no family-authored text survives directly. Every family trace is mediated. The family's silence is itself evidence. The [R19 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R19-ST-THOMAS-ENSLAVED-LETTERS/FINDINGS.md) shows it in the St. Thomas letters: the 1996 Bøgh edition silently narrowed a corpus of ~150 letters to six. The editor's selection is a form of record destruction.

The [R26 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R26-GRAHAMSTOWN-WILBRAHAM-1908-1911/FINDINGS.md) shows the gap is specific, not general: the manuscript, casebook, ledgers, estate file, register, family objects, and annual report remain unopened. The gap is not "we don't know." The gap is "these specific objects exist, and they have not been opened." The [evidence model's archive-silence rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) is blunt: archive silence is always bounded to the defined opened corpus. It does not show that a voice, record, objection, act, or experience never existed. Empathy cannot fill the gap.

The [backbone's B4.9 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the archive's structural bias: who gets believed in the Ann Pratt official dossier? The archive structurally underrepresents people outside the property-and-court jurisdiction. The silence is not a failure of the archive. It is the archive's design.

The [whose-voice-survives page](H04.md) is the method for tracing the chain. The [people-speaking-for-themselves page](V01.md) is the complement: when the voice is direct, the chain is shorter, but it is never zero. And the [families page](V03.md) and the [workers page](V04.md) show the silence's specific shape: the family's silence is mediated, the worker's silence is structural. The archive goes quiet in specific ways, and the collection's job is to name those ways — not to fill them with our categories, but to show exactly where the gap is, and what it costs.
