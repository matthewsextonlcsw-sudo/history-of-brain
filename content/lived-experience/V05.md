The community in the record is the doctor's community. The doctor wrote down what he saw. The doctor wrote down what he did. The person did not write down what they felt.

The National Archives guide says the records "relate mainly to the administration of the institutions." The community in the record is the administrator's community. The community is the doctor's community. The community is not the patient's community.

Here is what the record says about the community. Before the asylum, pauper lunatics were dealt with locally under poor law, vagrancy law, or criminal law. They ended up in workhouses, houses of correction, or prisons. The community's response was the poor law union, the house of correction, the prison. The record gives you the community's institution, not the community's feeling.

The County Asylums Act of 1808 encouraged justices of the peace to build county lunatic asylums. The community's response changed from the workhouse to the asylum. The 1845 Acts made the asylum compulsory. The community was now required to build and staff the institution. The record gives you the law, not the community's motive.

The Wellcome Collection has documents. The documents describe the community. The documents describe the institution and the law. The documents do not describe what the community felt. The community in the document is a fact. The fact is limited.

One more thing: the community's institution is not the community's feeling. The record gives you the law and the institution. It does not give you the community's motive. The community may have been afraid, or angry, or indifferent. The record does not show that. The record shows the institution.
