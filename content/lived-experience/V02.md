# V02 — The Voice in the Record

> *The voice in the record is the doctor's voice, not the person's voice. The doctor wrote down what he saw. The doctor wrote down what he did. The person did not write down what they felt. That is the point of this page: the voice in the record is the doctor's voice.*

## Draft

The voice in the record is the doctor's voice. The doctor wrote down what he saw. The doctor wrote down what he did. The person did not write down what they felt.

The National Archives guide says the records "relate mainly to the administration of the institutions." The voice is the administrator's voice. The voice is the doctor's voice. The voice is not the patient's voice.

The Wellcome Collection has documents. The documents are the doctor's words. The documents are not the patient's words. The patient's voice is not in the record. The patient's voice is in the gap.

The NLM has digitized editions of historical medical texts. The texts are the doctor's words. The texts are not the patient's words. The patient's voice is not in the text. The patient's voice is in the silence.

That is the point of this page. The voice in the record is the doctor's voice. The person's voice is not in the record. The limitation is the point. You can check the record. You can see what it says. You can see what it does not say.
