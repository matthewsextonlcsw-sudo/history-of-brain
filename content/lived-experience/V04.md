## Draft

The mind in the record is the doctor's mind. The doctor wrote down what he saw. The doctor wrote down what he did. The person did not write down what they felt.

The National Archives guide says the records "relate mainly to the administration of the institutions." The mind in the record is the administrator's mind. The mind is the doctor's mind. The mind is not the patient's mind.

Here is what the record says about the mind. The record uses the words of the time. The guide says "lunatic" was used to describe a person who was "sometimes of good and sound memory and understanding and sometimes not." The word "idiot" described "natural fools from birth." Those are the doctor's words. They are labels, not the person's own account of their mind.

The Chancery records are a different kind of mind record. They were about property, not treatment. The Lord Chancellor determined whether a person was of sound mind for the purposes of administering their estate. The inquisition removed the person's power of independent legal action. The mind in the Chancery record is a legal category, not a medical one.

The Wellcome Collection has documents. The documents describe the mind. The documents describe what the doctor saw and what the doctor did. The documents do not describe what the mind felt. The mind in the document is a fact. The fact is limited.

One more thing: the label is not the person. The doctor's label is a category. It is not the person's own account of their mind. The person may have disagreed with the label. The record does not show that disagreement. The record shows the label.

That is the point of this page: the mind in the record is the doctor's mind. The person's mind is not in the record. You can check the record. You can see what it says about the mind. You can see what it does not say.
