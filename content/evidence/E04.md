# E04 — "First" Claim Checker

> *The word "first" is the most dangerous word in history. Not "first" as in "the first person to do X." "First" as in "the earliest surviving record of X." "First" as in "the earliest known X." "First" as in "the earliest named X." These are not the same thing. The checker separates them.*

## Draft

You are reading a claim. The claim says "the first psychiatric hospital was built in X." But "first" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. First what? First by whose definition? First in what place? First in what time?

The "first" claim checker separates the different kinds of "first."

**Earliest surviving.** The oldest record we have. Not the oldest thing that happened. The oldest thing we can prove happened. Example: "The earliest surviving record of a trepanation is from this site, dated to approximately 6500 BCE." That is the earliest surviving. It does not mean it was the first trepanation. It means it is the oldest one we can prove.

**Earliest known.** The oldest thing we know about. This is broader than "earliest surviving." It includes things we know about from indirect evidence. Example: "The earliest known use of a specific herb for mental distress is in this text, dated to approximately 1000 BCE." That is the earliest known. We know about it because the text says so. But the text might not be the first use. It might be the first written record of the use.

**Earliest named.** The oldest thing that has a name. Not the oldest thing that happened. The oldest thing that someone put a label on. Example: "The earliest named category for a specific mental state is in this text, dated to approximately 500 BCE." That is the earliest named. The category existed before the name. The name is the first time someone wrote it down.

**Definition-dependent.** The "first" changes depending on how you define the thing. Example: "The first psychiatric hospital" depends on what you mean by "psychiatric hospital." If you mean "a building where people with mental illness are kept," the answer is different from "a building where people with mental illness are treated." The definition changes the answer.

**Actual origin.** The thing that actually started it. Not the first record. Not the first name. The actual origin. Example: "The actual origin of a specific treatment practice is unknown. The earliest surviving record is from this date, but the practice likely existed before that." That is the actual origin. We do not know it. We know the earliest record. The actual origin is earlier.

The checker separates these five kinds of "first." It does not collapse them into one. It does not say "the first" and mean all five at once. It says: here is the earliest surviving. Here is the earliest known. Here is the earliest named. Here is the definition-dependent. Here is the actual origin. Each one is a different claim. Each one has a different burden of proof.

The burden of proof for a "first" claim is high. The evidence model says: "Broad predecessor and translation search; use cautious wording when incomplete." That means: you have to search broadly for earlier examples. You have to check translations. You have to use cautious wording if the search is incomplete. You cannot say "the first" unless you have done the search and found nothing earlier.

The sources in this collection come from trusted places. The [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection) holds cuneiform tablets from Mesopotamia, including some of the earliest known medical texts. The [National Library of Medicine](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/ma10/ma10_hmd_reprint_papyrus.html) holds the Edwin Smith Papyrus, one of the oldest surgical texts in the world. The [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/) holds medical history materials, including early medical texts and their translations.

These are not blogs. These are institutions that have been checking and preserving these sources for centuries. The "first" claim checker tells you which institution holds which source, and what the source actually says.

You do not have to trust the "first." You have to trust the checker. The checker shows you the different kinds of "first." You can disagree with the claim. You can look at the source yourself. That is the point.

The "first" claim checker is not a decoration. It is the foundation. Without it, the claim is a guess. With it, the claim is a claim with a trail. You can follow the trail. You can check the work. You can disagree with the "first" and still trust the source.

That is the "first" claim checker. Not a list. A checker. You can run it on any claim.
