## Draft

You are reading a page. The page has people behind it. Not just the writer. The historian. The translator. The community reviewer. The lived-experience reviewer. The archivist. The clinician.

The contributors and reviewers page credits them by actual role. Not by title. Not by institution. By role.

Here is how it works. Every page in this collection has a list of people. The list is not a bibliography. The list is a credit. The credit tells you: this person did this. This person checked this. This person reviewed this. This person approved this.

The roles are specific. The historian is the person who researched the historical sources. The translator is the person who translated the text from the original language. The community reviewer is the person from the community who reviewed the page for accuracy and sensitivity. The lived-experience reviewer is the person who has lived experience of the topic and reviewed the page for accuracy and sensitivity. The archivist is the person who checked the archival sources. The clinician is the person who checked the clinical claims.

The credit is not a decoration. It is a fact. The credit tells you who did the work. The credit tells you who checked the work. The credit tells you who approved the work.

The credit is public. It is not a secret. It is not hidden. It is public. You can see the credit. You can see who did the work. You can see who checked the work. You can see who approved the work.

The credit is not a list of names. The credit is a list of roles. The role tells you what the person did. The name tells you who the person is. The two are separate. The role is the fact. The name is the person.

The sources in this collection come from trusted places. The [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/) holds medical history materials. The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) holds mental health patient files.

These are not blogs. These are institutions that have been checking and preserving these sources for centuries. The contributors and reviewers page tells you which institution holds which source, and who checked it.

You do not have to trust the page. You have to trust the credit. The credit shows you who did the work. You can disagree with the work. You can look at the source yourself. That is the point.

The contributors and reviewers page is not a decoration. It is the foundation. Without it, the page is a guess. With it, the page is a claim with a trail. You can follow the trail. You can check the work. You can disagree with the work and still trust the source.

That is the contributors and reviewers page. Not a list. A credit. You can walk through it and see who did the work.
