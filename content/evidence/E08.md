# E08 — Corrections and Version History

> *Every page in this collection has a version history. Not a secret history. A public history. When a claim is corrected, the correction is dated. The old claim is preserved. The reason is recorded.*

## Draft

You are reading a page. The page has a claim. The claim was written by a person. The person made a mistake. The mistake was found. The claim was corrected.

The corrections and version history shows you the correction. Not just the new claim. The old claim. The date. The reason.

Here is how it works. Every claim in this collection has an identifier. The identifier is immutable. It does not change. Even when the claim is corrected, the identifier stays. The identifier is the claim's name. The claim can change. The name does not.

When a claim is corrected, the correction is dated. The date is not "recently." The date is a specific date. The date tells you when the correction happened. The date tells you who made the correction. The date tells you why the correction was made.

The old claim is preserved. It is not deleted. It is not hidden. It is preserved. The old claim is there so you can see what the claim was before the correction. The old claim is there so you can see why the correction was made. The old claim is there so you can disagree with the correction.

The reason is recorded. The reason is not a guess. The reason is a fact. The reason tells you why the claim was corrected. The reason tells you what evidence was found. The reason tells you what the new claim is.

The version history is public. It is not a secret. It is not hidden. It is public. You can see the version history. You can see the corrections. You can see the dates. You can see the reasons.

The version history is not a decoration. It is the foundation. Without it, the correction is a guess. With it, the correction is a claim with a trail. You can follow the trail. You can check the work. You can disagree with the correction and still trust the source.

The sources in this collection come from trusted places. The [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/) holds medical history materials. The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) holds mental health patient files.

These are not blogs. These are institutions that have been checking and preserving these sources for centuries. The corrections and version history tells you which institution holds which source, and what the corrections are.

You do not have to trust the correction. You have to trust the history. The history shows you the correction. You can disagree with the correction. You can look at the source yourself. That is the point.

That is the corrections and version history. Not a list. A history. You can walk through it and see the corrections.
