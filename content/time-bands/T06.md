## Draft

This is not one story. It is a set of national and colonial variations, each with its own drug, its own promise, its own violence. The dates overlap because the variations overlap. There is no single "pharmacological turn." There are many, in many places, in many jurisdictions.

The [backbone's B6.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces the pharmacological turn: from chloral hydrate to chlorpromazine. The turn is not a single event. It is a set of variations, each with its own drug, its own promise, its own violence. The [backbone's B6.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt: the body is the site of control. The drug is not neutral. It is a mechanism.

The [backbone's B6.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) names the drug as promise and as control. The promise is care. The control is the mechanism. The [backbone's B6.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) centers the patient's own words about the drug: letters, poems, petitions, and testimony. The [backbone's B6.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces the drug in colonial and postcolonial contexts: the drug travels, and it changes as it travels.

The [backbone's B6.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the drug and the family: the family is the enforcement mechanism. The [R31 Japan packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows this in legal form: the 1950 law assigned duties to a legally designated protector, including financial and discharge-retrieval obligations. The family — or its legal proxy — was the enforcement mechanism. The [backbone's B6.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the drug and the worker: the worker is the administration mechanism. The [R33 Aro packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) shows the worker's role in care: organizer/admin descriptions include "described nursing and medical roles" and "treatment and occupational activity." The workers were the care infrastructure.

The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction.

The transition is not a clean break. The pharmacological turn does not end. It changes. The next band picks up where these variations leave off — not where they end, because they do not end.
