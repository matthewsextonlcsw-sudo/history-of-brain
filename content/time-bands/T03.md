## Draft

This is not a dark ages. It is a translation world — multilingual networks, locally changing practices, and knowledge that moved and changed as it moved. The dates overlap because the networks overlap. There is no single "medieval" moment. There are many, in many places, in many languages.

The [backbone's B3.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) opens with late-antique inner lives: Augustine, ascetic disciplines, *acedia*, natural and demonic explanations, charity, and the disputed history of hospitals. The inner life is not a private matter. It is a site of spiritual discipline, social obligation, and institutional care. The [backbone's B3.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces the translation network: Greek → Syriac → Arabic was a web, not a relay race. Translators such as Hunayn, patrons, families, physicians, editors, and libraries changed texts as they moved. The [NCBI account](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622612/) shows the network in action: Greek medicine moved into Arabic through a web of translators, each one changing the text as they went.

The [backbone's B3.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) maps the sciences of the soul in Arabic and Persian: perception, imagination, memory, sleep, emotion, body, ethics, and revelation. These are not a timeless "Islamic psychology." They are specific writers, specific debates, specific contexts. The [backbone's B3.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks the question the popular story skips: what did a bimaristan actually do? The [Dols account](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0025727300046287) is blunt: a general hospital, not a psychiatric one. The ruling is explicit: reject unsupported "first psychiatric hospital" claims.

The [backbone's B3.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) treats Jewish scholars as producers and readers, not merely intermediaries. Jewish thought inside Arabic, Hebrew, and Latin worlds is not a footnote. It is a main thread. The [backbone's B3.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the Dunhuang and Central Asian crossroads: manuscripts in many languages can show contact and translation, not population-wide belief. The [backbone's B3.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) tracks Buddhist mind and medicine on land and sea routes: translators, monasteries, courts, ports, and changing terminology. The [backbone's B3.8 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) builds named African lanes: North, West, East, Central, and Southern. Timbuktu manuscripts are one anchor, not a proxy for a continent.

The lived-experience route is the same as always: separate direct self-expression, reported speech, institutional description, and modern reconstruction. The transition is not a clean break. Knowledge networks expand while local households, shrines, healers, and courts continue to decide what care and control look like.
