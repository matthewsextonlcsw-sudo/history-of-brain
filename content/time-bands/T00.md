## Draft

You are reading about a person who lived ten thousand years ago. The person has no words. No letters. No diary. No recording. The person left a body. The body is in a museum. The body has a hole in the skull.

The hole is a fact. The story is an inference.

Here is the problem. The hole tells you that someone cut into the skull. The hole tells you that the person survived. The hole does not tell you why the cut was made. The hole does not tell you who made the cut. The hole does not tell you what the person felt. The hole does not tell you what the community thought.

The limit of inference is the rule. You can say: "This skull has a healed trepanation. The person survived the procedure." You cannot say: "The community cared for this person." You cannot say: "The person consented to the procedure." You cannot say: "The person was sick." You cannot say: "The person was possessed." You cannot say: "The person was a warrior."

The hole is a fact. The story is an inference. The inference is where the violence lives.

The [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection) holds prehistoric skulls with healed trepanations. The [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/collections/) holds prehistoric human remains, including skulls with evidence of trepanation. These are not blogs. These are institutions that have been checking and preserving these sources for centuries.

The evidence model says: "No direct voice survives. Say so prominently." The voice rule says: "protector, elder, clinician, archive, or institutional speech is not family or patient consent; do not infer burden, support, abandonment, motive, or consensus."

The claim type `individual_experience` says: "Direct testimony when available; otherwise label reported or inferred." There is no direct testimony. There is no diary. There is no letter. There is no recording. There is a hole in a skull. The claim is inferred. The inference is labeled.

The claim type `archival_silence` says: "Named archive/corpus and search boundary; never convert silence into inner experience." The silence is not absence of experience. The silence is the result of a specific, traceable gap in the record. The gap is named. The boundary is named. The silence is not converted into inner experience.

The anachronism risk is high. You are putting a modern idea in a prehistoric context. The modern idea is "mental illness." The prehistoric context has no word for "mental illness." The prehistoric context has no category for "mental illness." The prehistoric context has no treatment for "mental illness." The anachronism risk is high because you are using a modern category to describe a prehistoric person.

The [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/) holds medical history materials, including early surgical instruments and their descriptions. These are not blogs. These are institutions that have been checking and preserving these sources for centuries.

The limit of inference is not a failure. It is the honest answer. The honest answer is: we do not know. We do not know why the cut was made. We do not know who made the cut. We do not know what the person felt. We do not know what the community thought. We know the hole. We know the survival. We do not know the story.

The story is an inference. The inference is labeled. The label tells you what the evidence does. The label tells you what the evidence cannot do. You can disagree with the inference. You can look at the skull yourself. That is the point.
