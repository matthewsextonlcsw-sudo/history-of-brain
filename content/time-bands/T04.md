## Draft

This is not a Renaissance. It is a set of regional syntheses — continuing Asian, African, American, Oceanic, Islamic, Jewish, and European worlds colliding with print, conquest, slavery, mission, and empire. The dates overlap because the worlds overlap. There is no single "early modern" moment. There are many, in many places, in many languages.

The [backbone's B4.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces Persianate and South Asian entanglements: Unani, Ayurveda, Siddha, Buddhist, court, shrine, household, and market practices. The ruling is explicit: follow the practices rather than inventing a single imported or indigenous line. The [backbone's B4.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) researches East and Southeast Asian continuities separately: Song–Qing changes, Kampo, Korean and Vietnamese lineages, and vernacular care. The [backbone's B4.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) reads the babaylan/katalonan through a colonial archive — and asks what the archive reveals, distorts, translates, and persecutes. The [Tremml-Werner account](https://doi.org/10.1093/pastj/gtz031) shows the archive's hand.

The [backbone's B4.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) places care, law, and coercion in specific European jurisdictions: Bethlem, family custody, charity, courts, medicine, religion, and alleged possession. The ruling is blunt: "everyone blamed demons" is false. The [backbone's B4.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) tracks *acedia* and *melancholy* through semantic change without turning either into a timeless depression label. The [backbone's B4.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt about print, anatomy, and the mechanical body: Vesalius, Burton, and Descartes are changes within European debates, not the moment humanity discovered the brain.

The [backbone's B4.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) treats conquest as a contact zone in the Americas: Nahua and other Indigenous concepts, colonial translation, mission, medicine, law, and the Hospital de San Hipólito. The [Ramos account](https://doi.org/10.1017/tam.2020.41) shows the contact zone in action. The [backbone's B4.8 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) tracks Atlantic Africa and the violent making of new healing worlds: African, European, and Indigenous knowledge under slavery and commerce. The [Kananoja account](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0E6627303E0A532A5C9A0232F5DD2D91/9781108491259c6_155-186.pdf/treating-their-symptoms.pdf) shows the violence without sanitizing "exchange." The [backbone's B4.9 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) builds evidence stories from letters, petitions, spiritual autobiography, poems, and court records — and shows the later cataloguing power that shapes what survives.

The transition is not a clean break. Empires increasingly connect classification to administration, property, labor, race, policing, and institutional confinement — but household and plural care persist. The next band picks up where these worlds leave off.
