## Draft

This is not one movement. It is a set of national and colonial variations, each with its own architecture, its own promise, its own violence. The dates overlap because the variations overlap. There is no single "asylum century." There are many, in many places, in many jurisdictions.

The [backbone's B5.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces moral treatment and its limits: Pinel, Tuke, and their successors. The regimen, the environment, the labor, the restraint — the promise was care, but the practice was control. The [backbone's B5.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) names the asylum as architecture, promise, workplace, and instrument of rule. The building is the argument. The architecture is not neutral. It is a statement about what the institution believes about the person inside it.

The [backbone's B5.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks the question the popular story skips: what happened to the people inside expanding asylums? Admissions, chronicity, work, restraint, medication, abuse, community ties, death, discharge, and patient writing. The [backbone's B5.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt: the body is the site of control. Restraint, seclusion, and the patient's body are not footnotes. They are the mechanism.

The [backbone's B5.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) centers the patient's own words: letters, poems, petitions, and testimony from inside the asylum. The [backbone's B5.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces colonial asylums and the export of a model: the asylum travels, and it changes as it travels. The [backbone's B5.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks who was believed, and who was not. The [R22 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R22-JAMAICA-OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTS-1860/FINDINGS.md) shows the archive's structural bias: no family-authored text survives directly. Every family trace is mediated. The [R26 packet](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/R26-GRAHAMSTOWN-WILBRAHAM-1908-1911/FINDINGS.md) shows the gap is specific, not general: the manuscript, casebook, ledgers, estate file, register, family objects, and annual report remain unopened. The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction.

The transition is not a clean break. The asylum century does not end. It changes. The next band picks up where these variations leave off — not where they end, because they do not end.
