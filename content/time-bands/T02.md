## Draft

This is not one classical age. It is many worlds, running in parallel, each with its own map of the self, its own healing, its own power. The dates overlap because the worlds overlap. There is no single "classical" moment. There are many, in many places, at many times.

The Greek-speaking Mediterranean offers one map. [Hippocrates' On the Sacred Disease](https://classics.mit.edu/Hippocrates/sacred.html) reframes epilepsy as a natural brain disorder — a move that sounds modern, but the tradition never escapes the entanglement of mind, spirit, and community. The [backbone's B2.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) pairs the naturalizing argument with Asclepian healing and competing philosophies. The brain is one answer. The sanctuary is another. The regimen is a third. They coexist. They do not replace each other.

The Roman imperial world offers a wider map. [Celsus 3.18](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:latinLit:phi0836.phi002.perseus-lat5:3.18/?right=perseus-eng2) shows household remedies alongside learned medicine, itinerant healers, temples, and law. The [backbone's B2.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces the pluralism: household, temple, learned, legal, and reported patient speech. The healing landscape is not a hierarchy. It is a market — but a market with power, status, and cost built into every transaction.

South Asia offers a layered map. The [backbone's B2.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) distinguishes Vedic and Upaniṣadic arguments, changing Ayurvedic corpora, Buddhist schools, and other philosophical traditions. *Manas* is not a single "mind." It is a faculty, a mediator, a site of choice. *Unmada* is not a single "madness." It is a cluster of conditions, each with its own etiology and treatment. The ruling is explicit: never equate *unmada* with a current label.

China offers a different map again. The [backbone's B2.4 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) compares named schools and changing uses of *qing* rather than inventing a single Chinese psychology. *Xin* is a heart-mind — emotion, conduct, and order in a single embodied locus. The [backbone's B2.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) traces premodern Chinese madness as medically plural: *dian*, *kuang*, wind, phlegm-fire, emotional disturbance, drugs, needling, talking, and ritual. The ruling is explicit: do not call the whole past "TCM."

The [backbone's B2.6 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) reserves a first-class lane for Mesoamerican and Andean concepts — locally grounded, material, and later contact-zone sources. The warning is explicit: do not reconstruct preconquest experience from colonial accounts alone. The [backbone's B2.7 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) asks the question the popular story skips: who had a choice of healer? Status, gender, enslavement, household authority, cost, and pilgrimage all shaped the answer. The "medical marketplace" is not a free market. It is a market with power built into every transaction.

The exchange routes connect these worlds: Egypt, the Persian empires, Central Asian corridors, Indian Ocean ports, pilgrimage, war, and diaspora. Similarity alone does not prove direct borrowing. The [backbone's exchange route](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) names the routes without claiming the connections.

The transition is not a clean break. Classical traditions do not disappear. They are translated, criticized, combined, ritualized, institutionalized, and made vernacular. The next band picks up where these worlds leave off — not where they end, because they do not end.
