# X10 — The Colonial Route

> *The colonial route is the story of how the mind became a colonial problem. The British Empire had its own asylums in its colonies. The records are in local archives and in the National Archives.*

## Draft

The British Empire had its own problem with mental illness. The colonies had their own asylums. The colonies had their own laws. The colonies had their own records. The Empire did not always deal with its colonial subjects the same way it dealt with its own.

The National Archives holds records of lunacy inquisitions taken in British colonies. The guide notes: "The later records, however, include copies of inquisitions taken in Ireland, and in some British colonies. The latter are concerned with the mental health of the person, and with getting them transported back to Britain."

The colonial route is a small part of the bigger story. But it is a real part. The Empire was a separate system. It had its own hospitals. It had its own records. It had its own rules. The colonial subject was not a British patient. The colonial subject was a colonial patient.

The records survive in local archives and in the National Archives. The inquisitions survive. The treatment records survive. The individual patient's story mostly does not. But the system is documented. You can search the catalogue. You can find the records. You can see what the Empire did.

The colonial route is not a story of progress. It is a story of a separate system doing its own thing. The Empire dealt with its own people. The records show it. The gaps show what the Empire did not want to record.
