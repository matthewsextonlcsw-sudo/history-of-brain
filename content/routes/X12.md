## Draft

The recovery route is the story of how the mind got a second chance. Before the 20th century, there was no concept of recovery. The patient was confined. The patient was treated. The patient was discharged, or the patient died. There was no third option.

The 20th century changed this. The concept of recovery emerged. The patient could get better. The patient could leave. The patient could live. The records of this shift are in the National Archives.

The Ministry of Health records (1798–2001) show the transition. The MH series holds correspondence, reports, and administrative records. The KB series holds records of related organisations. You can search them online.

The recovery route is not a story of progress. It is a story of change. The system changed. The records show the change. The gaps show what the system did not want to record.

Here is one more detail. The concept of recovery emerged in the 20th century. Before that, the patient was confined, treated, discharged, or died. After that, the patient could get better. The patient could leave. The patient could live. The recovery route is the story of that change. The records show it. The gaps show what the system did not want to record. The record is incomplete. That is the point.

The Ministry of Health records (1798–2001) show the transition in detail. The MH series holds correspondence, reports, and administrative records. You can search them by keywords like "mental," "lunatic," "insane," and "pauper lunatic." The records show the system at work: the correspondence, the reports, the administrative decisions. The recovery route is documented in these records.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from this period. They show the patient's perspective. They show the doctor's perspective. They show the system's perspective. The three perspectives are different. The record holds all three.
