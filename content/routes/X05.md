## Draft

The research route is the story of how the mind got studied. Before the 19th century, there was no systematic study of the mind. There were observations. There were case reports. There were medical texts. But there was no research program. No methodology. No peer review.

The Edwin Smith Papyrus is the oldest medical text we have. It dates to around 1300 BCE, though it was copied from an older text. It describes 48 cases of head, neck, and spine injuries. It is a surgical text, not a psychiatric one. But it shows that the ancient Egyptians were observing the body and recording what they saw. That is the beginning of the research route.

The 19th century changed this. The Lunacy Commission was established in 1845. It required asylums to submit reports. It sent inspectors. It collected data. For the first time, the mind was being studied systematically. The Wellcome Collection holds these reports. They are not pretty. They are bureaucratic. But they are real.

The 20th century continued the work. Psychiatric research became a field. The National Library of Medicine holds digitized editions of the key texts. You can read them online. You can check the sources. You can see what the researchers found. You can see what they did not find.

The research route is not a story of progress. It is a story of accumulation. Each generation added to the record. Each generation left gaps. The gaps are as important as the findings. The record is incomplete. That is the point.
