## Draft

This is the route of the body. Not one thing. Not one person. Not one time. Not one place. Not one record. It is a set of variations, each with its own body, its own promise, its own violence.

In Mesopotamia, the [Electronic Babylonian Library](https://www.ebl.lmu.de/) records diagnostic tablets that locate thought, emotion, and decision-making in the heart. The body was the heart. The promise was restoration. The violence was the ritual itself — the binding, the confinement, the public performance.

In Egypt, the [National Library of Medicine](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/ma10/ma10_hmd_reprint_papyrus.html) shows the Edwin Smith Papyrus: a surgical text that describes the brain as soft and vulnerable. The body was the brain. The promise was the brain's safety. The violence was the brain's vulnerability — the architecture, the regimen, the confinement.

In the Greek-speaking Mediterranean, the [backbone's B2.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the brain, soul, regimen, and sanctuary. The [Lloyd & Bowen account](https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520964800) shows the heart–brain debate in Greek medicine. The body was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

In the Islamic world, the [backbone's B3.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) maps the sciences of the soul in Arabic and Persian: perception, imagination, memory, sleep, emotion, body, ethics, and revelation. The body was not one thing. It was a set of faculties, each with its own function, its own promise, its own violence.

In the early modern period, the [backbone's B4.5 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) tracks *acedia* and *melancholy* through semantic change without turning either into a timeless depression label. The body was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

In the present, the [backbone's B10.3 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows neurodiversity, mad pride, recovery, and disability justice: separate and overlapping movements, each with its own arguments, its own internal disagreements, and its own institutional effects. The body was not one thing. It was a set of variations — the movement's promise, the movement's violence.

The [backbone's B0 lived-experience rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt: no direct voice survives. Say so prominently. The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction.

The answer depends on what you think a person is. If a person is a machine, the body is a part. If a person is a relation, the body is a network. If a person is a whole that cannot be taken apart, the question itself is the mistake.
