# X03 — The Religious Route

> *The religious route is the story of how the mind became a spiritual problem. In the ancient world, mental illness was often explained as possession, divine punishment, or a soul in distress. The records are in museums and archives today.*

## Draft

In the ancient world, the mind was not a body problem. It was a soul problem. If your mind broke, the explanation was spiritual, not medical. You were possessed. You were punished. Your soul was in distress.

The UCL Petrie Museum holds the "Letters of the Dead" — a collection of ancient Egyptian texts in which the dead speak to the living. The texts describe the state of the soul after death. They describe confusion. They describe distress. They describe a mind that is not at peace. These are not medical texts. They are religious texts. But they show that the ancient Egyptians thought about the mind as a spiritual entity, not a physical one.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from multiple religious traditions. They show the same pattern: mental illness explained as spiritual disturbance. The treatment was prayer, ritual, or exorcism. Not medicine. Not surgery. Not a doctor. A priest.

This is not a judgment. It is a fact. The religious route is the story of how the mind was understood before it was understood medically. The soul was the explanation. The spirit was the cause. The ritual was the treatment.

The shift from religious to medical explanation did not happen overnight. It happened over centuries. The 19th century was the turning point. Before that, the religious explanation was the default. After that, the medical explanation was the default. The records show the shift. The gaps in the records show the uncertainty.
