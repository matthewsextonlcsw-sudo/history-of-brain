## Draft

This is the route of the heart. From Mesopotamia to Egypt to the Greek-speaking Mediterranean. The [Electronic Babylonian Library](https://www.ebl.lmu.de/) records diagnostic tablets that locate thought, emotion, and decision-making in the heart. The [UCL Letters to the Dead](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/literature/religious/lettersdead.html) show family petitions and heart-centered vocabularies. The [National Library of Medicine](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/ma10/ma10_hmd_reprint_papyrus.html) shows the Edwin Smith Papyrus: a surgical text that describes the brain as soft and vulnerable. It does not say the brain is the seat of thought. The heart, by contrast, is the seat of thought, emotion, and moral judgment.

The [backbone's B1.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows this in the earliest written records: heart-centered explanations, ritual, and the first medical texts. The [backbone's B1.2 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the same pattern in Egypt: family petitions and heart-centered vocabularies. The [backbone's B2.1 node](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) shows the Greek-speaking Mediterranean: the brain, soul, regimen, and sanctuary.

The [Lloyd & Bowen account](https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520964800) shows the heart–brain debate in Greek medicine. The brain is one answer. The sanctuary is another. The regimen is a third. They coexist. They do not replace each other.

The [backbone's B0 lived-experience rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md) is blunt: no direct voice survives. Say so prominently. The [evidence model's voice rule](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/research/era-packets/BATCH-08-SEAM-SYNTHESIS.md) is blunt: protector, elder, clinician, archive, or institutional speech is not family or patient consent. Do not infer burden, support, abandonment, motive, or consensus.

The [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/mental-health/) warns that many mental-health patient files were destroyed. The silence is not absence of resistance or consent. It is the result of a specific, traceable act of destruction.

The transition is not a clean break. The heart route does not end. It changes. The next band picks up where these variations leave off — not where they end, because they do not end. The heart-centered map of the mind is not a quaint mistake. It is a serious, systematic, and carefully argued answer to a question that was not always asked the same way.
