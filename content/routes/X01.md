# X01 — The Asylum Route

> *The asylum is where the mind became a public problem. Before the 19th century, people with mental illness ended up in workhouses, prisons, or private madhouses. The County Asylums Act of 1808 changed that. The records are in the National Archives today.*

## Draft

Before the 19th century, there was no asylum. If you were poor and your mind broke, you ended up in a workhouse, a house of correction, or a prison. The National Archives guide says this plainly: pauper lunatics were dealt with under poor law, vagrancy law, or criminal law. If you were wealthy, your family made private arrangements. They sent you to a "madhouse" — a private house licensed by the justices of the peace.

The County Asylums Act of 1808 was the first step. It encouraged justices of the peace to build county lunatic asylums. But the response was slow. Many patients still ended up in prisons and workhouses. The 1808 Act was a suggestion, not a requirement.

The real change came in 1845. Two acts passed that year: the Lunacy Act and the County Asylums Act. Together, they made county lunatic asylums compulsory. The Lunacy Commission was established to regulate them. This is the moment the asylum became a public institution — not a private arrangement, not a prison, but a building the state was required to build and staff.

The 1890 Lunacy Act widened the role again. Wealthier patients began to be admitted. The asylum was no longer just for the poor.

Here is the hard part: most patient files have been destroyed. The National Archives guide says this directly. The records that survive are mainly administrative — admission registers, correspondence with poor law unions, quarterly returns. The individual patient's story is mostly gone. What survives is the system, not the person.

The Wellcome Collection holds photographs and documents from this period. The National Archives holds the admission registers (MH 94) and the criminal lunacy warrant books (HO 145). You can search them online through Ancestry.co.uk. The records are real. The gaps are real too.
