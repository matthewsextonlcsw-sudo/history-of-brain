# X08 — The Criminal Route

> *The criminal route is the story of how the mind became a criminal problem. Before the 19th century, people with mental illness who committed crimes were confined in prisons. The 1882 Criminal Lunatics Act changed that. The records are in the National Archives.*

## Draft

Before the 19th century, if you had a mental illness and you committed a crime, you went to prison. Not an asylum. A prison. The criminal law dealt with you. The mental illness was an afterthought.

The 1882 Criminal Lunatics Act changed this. It created a separate category: the "criminal lunatic." These were people who committed crimes while insane. They were confined at asylums, not prisons. Broadmoor was built specifically for them. Bethlem (Bethlehem) Hospital had a criminal wing.

The National Archives holds the records. HO 145 (1882–1921) covers criminal lunatics confined at Broadmoor and Bethlem. HO 20 (1820–1843) covers earlier registers. HO 144 (1869–1941) covers additional records. You can search them online through Ancestry.co.uk.

The criminal route is not a story of justice. It is a story of categorization. The state decided that some people were criminals. Some were insane. Some were both. The category determined where they went. The category determined what happened to them.

The records survive. The admission registers survive. The warrant books survive. The individual patient's story mostly does not. Most patient files have been destroyed. What survives is the system, not the person.
