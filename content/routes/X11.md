## Draft

The advocacy route is the story of how the mind became a rights problem. Before the 20th century, the patient had no rights. The patient was a case. The patient was a file. The patient was a legal subject. The patient was not a person with rights.

The 20th century changed this. The Mental Health Act of 1983 (in the UK) gave patients rights for the first time. The right to an independent mental health advocate. The right to appeal. The right to be treated with dignity. The records of this shift are in the National Archives.

The Ministry of Health records (1798–2001) show the transition. The MH series holds correspondence, reports, and administrative records. The KB series holds records of related organisations. You can search them online.

The advocacy route is not a story of progress. It is a story of struggle. The patients fought for their rights. The families fought for their rights. The advocates fought for their rights. The records show the fight. The gaps show what the system did not want to record.

The Wellcome Collection holds materials from this period. They show the patient's perspective. They show the advocate's perspective. They show the system's perspective. The three perspectives are different. The record holds all three.
