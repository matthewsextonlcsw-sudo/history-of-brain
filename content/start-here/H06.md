## Draft

A guide page is a tool. The tool shows how to read one type of page in the collection. It does not show what the record does not say.

The guide zone is where you go when you want to learn how to read a page type. The zone has twelve tools. Each tool shows you the method for one page type: time-band, place, route, lived-experience, evidence, or start-here.

Here is how to use the guides. Pick the page type you are trying to read. Open the matching guide. Read the method. Apply it to the page. Check the example.

Take the guide for time-band pages as an example. It shows you how to follow the dated facts and check each source. It walks through the 19th-century band. It names the County Asylums Act of 1808, the 1845 Acts, and the 1890 Act. It shows you how to follow the dated facts and check each source. That is the method, applied to one example.

The guides use the same evidence rules as every other page. They do not get a pass because they are guides. Each guide carries a Research section with sourced facts. Each guide carries a References section with full citations. You can check them just like any other page.

One more thing: the guides are not the same as the pages they describe. A guide shows you the method. The page applies the method. The guide for time-band pages shows you how to follow the dated facts. The 19th-century time-band page follows the dated facts. The guide is the manual. The page is the test. If the page does not follow the method, the page is not doing its job.

That is how you use the guides: pick the page type, open the matching guide, and apply the method.
