## Draft

The collection is a cited global history of the mind. The records are in museums and archives today. You can check them.

The collection is organized into seven zones. Each zone has a different job. The evidence zone is the method. It shows you how to read the evidence, how to check a citation, and how to label a claim. The start-here zone is the door. It shows you how to enter the collection. The time-band zone is the spine. It shows you what the record says about specific times, from prehistory to the present. The lived-experience zone is the voice. It shows you whose words survive and whose do not. The guide zone is the manual. It shows you how to read each type of page. The place zone is the map. It shows you what the record says about specific regions. The route zone is the path. It shows you how ideas and practices moved from one place to another.

Here is how the zones connect. A time-band page about the 19th century links to a place page about Britain. The place page links to a route page about the asylum. The route page links to a lived-experience page about the patient. The lived-experience page links to an evidence page about the voice rule. That is the collection in motion.

The National Archives guide is one of the sources the collection uses. It says lunatic asylums were first established in Britain in the mid-19th century. You can open the guide and check it. The collection does not pretend the record is complete. It names the gaps.

The zones are not separate. They connect. A time-band page links to a place page. A place page links to a route page. A route page links to a lived-experience page. A lived-experience page links to an evidence page. That is the collection in motion. You can follow the links and walk through the whole collection.

That is the collection: seven zones, connected, each with its own job.
