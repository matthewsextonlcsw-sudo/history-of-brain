# H03 — How to Read a Place Page

> *A place page is a map. The map shows what the record says about a specific place. The map does not show what the record does not say. That is the point of this page: the place page is a map, and the map is limited.*

## Draft

A place page is a map. The map shows what the record says about a specific place. The map does not show what the record does not say.

The places zone is where you go when you want to know what the record says about a region. The zone has eighteen doors, one for each region. Each door is a map. It shows what the record says about that region, and it names what the record does not cover.

Here is how to enter the places zone. Pick a region. Open the door. Read the Research section. Check each source. Then read the Draft. Follow the facts. Notice where the map runs out.

Take the Mesopotamia door as an example. It points to a diagnostic tablet. A doctor held the tablet, looked at a patient, and wrote down what he saw. The tablet says "if a man's head is heavy, and he sees dark, and his ears are blocked, then..." That is a medical observation. The door names the object, the place, and what the object says. It does not claim the tablet explains the whole mind of ancient Mesopotamia.

The places zone is bounded by geography. A door about Mesopotamia does not cover all of West Asia. A door about Egypt does not cover all of North Africa. Each door stays inside its region. That is the rule.

One more thing: the doors are not equal. Some doors are well-stocked with sources. Some doors are thin. A thin door is worse than an honest "research underway" marker. The collection does not pretend every door is complete. It names the gaps. A thin door is an honest marker, not a failure.

That is how you enter the places zone: pick a region, open the door, and notice where the map runs out.
