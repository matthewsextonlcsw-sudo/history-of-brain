## Draft

The lived-experience rule is the rule that says the record is limited. The record does not show what the person felt. The record does not show what the person thought. The record does not show what the person wanted.

The doctor wrote down what he saw. The doctor wrote down what he did. The person did not write down what they felt. The record is the doctor's words, not the person's words.

The National Archives guide says: "most patient files have been destroyed." That is a fact. The fact is bounded. The fact is limited. The limitation is the point.

The Wellcome Collection has photographs. The photographs show what the room looked like. The photographs do not show what the person felt. The photographs are a fact. The fact is limited.

That is the point of this page. The lived-experience rule is the rule that says the record is limited. The limitation is the point. You can check the record. You can see what it says. You can see what it does not say.
