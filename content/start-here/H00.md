## Draft

Welcome to the History of the Mind and Mental Health collection. This is a cited global history of the mind. The records are in museums and archives today. You can check them.

This is not a timeline of famous Western doctors. It is a connected public reference. You can ask: what did people think was happening? What did they call it then? What was it like for the person receiving care or control? Who had the power to define, help, confine, pay, refuse, or reform?

The collection is organized into seven zones. Each zone has its own purpose. The evidence zone shows you how to read the evidence. The start-here zone shows you how to enter the collection. The time-band zone shows you what the record says about specific times. The lived-experience zone shows you whose voice survives. The guide zone shows you how to read each type of page. The place zone shows you what the record says about specific regions. The route zone shows you how ideas and practices moved.

Here is a concrete fact to start with. The National Archives guide says lunatic asylums were first established in Britain in the mid-19th century. Before that, pauper lunatics were dealt with under poor law, vagrancy law, or criminal law. They ended up in workhouses, houses of correction, or prisons. That is a bounded, dated fact. You can open the guide and check it.

The collection does not pretend the record is complete. It names the gaps. It tells you whose voice survives and whose does not. It tells you what the record says and what it does not say. That is the promise of the collection: a cited global history of the mind, where every fact carries a source you can check.

The collection ends with open questions, not a victory screen. The record is what it is. You can check it. You can disagree with the interpretation. That is the point.
