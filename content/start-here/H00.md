## Draft

**The Human Mind Through Time**

A global, source-led history of how people have understood, experienced, cared for, changed, classified, and governed mental life — from the evidence left in ancient bodies and texts to the arguments unfolding around rights, wellness, data, and AI today.

Welcome. You do not need a background in medicine, psychology, or history to begin. This collection is built on one promise: every claim you read here is traced to a source, and every source is named. When historians disagree, we say so. When the evidence runs out, we say where it runs out.

**Four doors, one map.** You can enter by **time** — travel through eleven overlapping bands from prehistory to today, following the chronological spine [CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md](https://github.com/matthewsexton/history-of-brain/blob/main/history-of-brain/outline/CHRONOLOGICAL-BACKBONE.md). You can enter by **question** — follow twelve long-arc authority guides (Q01–Q12) that take a single question across centuries. You can enter by **experience** — hear lived-experience streams (V01–V06) about self-expression, family, care workers, movements, and archive silences. Or you can enter by **place** — open eighteen regional doors (P01–P18) plus twelve connection routes (X01–X12) that trace how ideas moved across borders. If you want the shortest route first, start with H01, the two-minute global history, which links out instead of pretending completeness.

**How to read this without modern labels taking over** — start with H02. **How we know what people felt in the past** — start with H03; its opening exercise (the healed skull) is the method of the whole collection in miniature. **Whose voice survives in the archive?** — start with H04. **Then and now are different questions** — start with H05 before judging any old treatment. For a fast orientation across all eleven bands at once, H01 is the compact route; the regional doors and connection routes are where you go deeper.

**A word on the end.** This collection does not end with a victory screen. The World Health Organization's current account still describes a large gap between mental-health needs and systems' responses, while rights-based guidance continues to press for community inclusion and less coercion. The open questions are still open — and now they are yours to explore.

For the fastest orientation across all eleven bands at once, H01 (the two-minute global history) is the route to take; it links forward into the spine rather than pretending completeness.
